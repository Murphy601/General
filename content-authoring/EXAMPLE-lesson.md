## PAGE 1: What is a plane figure?
A plane figure is a flat shape. You can draw it on paper and trace its edges with your finger. It has length and width, but it has no thickness. A mandazi is thick, so it is not a plane figure, but the top of a mandazi looks like a flat shape you could draw.

Look around your classroom. The face of your exercise book is a rectangle. The clock on the wall is a circle. A slice of a triangular sandwich shows a triangle. A window pane is often a square or a rectangle.

### Words you need
• Side: a straight line that forms the edge of a shape.
• Corner (vertex): the point where two sides meet.
• Curved edge: an edge that bends, like the edge of a coin.

### Try it
Find three flat shapes in your classroom. Say the name of each one and count its sides and corners. Write your findings in a small table in your exercise book.

## PAGE 2: Rectangles and squares
A rectangle has four straight sides and four corners. The corners are all square corners, which we also call right angles. The two long sides are equal to each other, and the two short sides are equal to each other.

A square is a special rectangle. All four of its sides are the same length, and it also has four square corners.

### How to tell them apart
• Rectangle: opposite sides are equal. Long and short sides are different.
• Square: all four sides are equal.
Every square is a rectangle, but not every rectangle is a square.

### Example
Wanjiku says her book cover is a square. Its sides measure 21 cm, 21 cm, 30 cm and 30 cm. The sides are not all equal, so it is a rectangle, not a square.

## PAGE 3: Triangles
A triangle has three straight sides and three corners. If you add up the number of sides and corners, you always get three of each. Roofs of houses, slices of pizza and the sail of a boat often have triangle shapes.

Triangles can look different. Some have three equal sides. Some have two equal sides. Some have three sides of different lengths. Some have one square corner. What makes each of them a triangle is that it has exactly three straight sides.

### Check your understanding
Draw a triangle using a ruler. Then count its sides and corners. What do you notice? Compare your triangle with your neighbour's. They may look different, but both have three sides.

## PAGE 4: Circles and ovals
A circle is a round shape with one curved edge and no corners. Every point on the edge of a circle is the same distance from the centre. A wheel, a coin and a plate are circles. An oval looks like a stretched circle, like an egg drawn from the side.

To draw a circle, you can trace around a round object such as a cup or a bottle top. You can also use a pair of compasses. Put the sharp point on the centre, open the compasses to the size you want, and turn them all the way around.

### Comparing circles and ovals
• A circle has the same width in every direction.
• An oval is longer in one direction than in the other.
• Neither has corners, and neither has straight sides.

### Activity
Trace the bottom of a cup to make a circle. Then make an oval by drawing the shape of an egg. Say how the two shapes are alike and how they are different.

## PAGE 5: Sorting and naming plane figures
Now you can name the four main plane figures: rectangle, square, triangle and circle. You can also spot an oval. To name a shape quickly, ask yourself two questions. Are the edges straight or curved? If they are straight, how many sides are there?

Use this simple guide. Curved edge and no corners: circle or oval. Three straight sides: triangle. Four straight sides with four square corners: rectangle. Four straight equal sides with four square corners: square.

### Worked example
A signboard has four straight sides. Two sides measure 50 cm and two measure 30 cm. All four corners are square. What is the shape?
Step 1: It has four straight sides, so it is a rectangle or a square.
Step 2: The sides are not all equal (50 cm and 30 cm), so it is not a square.
Answer: The signboard is a rectangle.

### Remember
Say the name, the number of sides and the number of corners each time you meet a new shape. This habit will make geometry easy in the next grades.

## QUIZ
1. How many straight sides does a triangle have?
   A) 2
   B) 3
   C) 4
   D) 5
   Answer: B
   Why: A triangle is a shape made from exactly three straight sides.
2. Which shape has no corners at all?
   A) Square
   B) Rectangle
   C) Circle
   D) Triangle
   Answer: C
   Why: A circle has one curved edge, so it has no corners.
3. A window has four equal sides and four square corners. What is its shape?
   A) Rectangle with unequal sides
   B) Triangle
   C) Circle
   D) Square
   Answer: D
   Why: Four equal sides with four square corners describe a square.
4. What is the difference between a square and a rectangle?
   A) A square has three sides
   B) A rectangle has no corners
   C) All the sides of a square are equal
   D) A rectangle is round
   Answer: C
   Why: In a square every side is the same length; in a rectangle opposite sides are equal but long and short sides differ.
5. Name two objects in your home that are circles. (2 marks)
   Answer: Any two correct answers, for example a plate and a coin.
   Why: Award 1 mark for each object that is truly round and flat.
6. A shape has four straight sides measuring 8 cm, 5 cm, 8 cm and 5 cm and four square corners. Name the shape and give a reason. (2 marks)
   Answer: A rectangle, because opposite sides are equal but not all sides are equal.
   Why: 1 mark for the name and 1 mark for the reason.
7. Draw a rectangle 6 cm long and 3 cm wide. Label the sides. (3 marks)
   Answer: A drawn rectangle with sides labelled 6 cm, 3 cm, 6 cm and 3 cm.
   Why: 1 mark for four straight sides, 1 mark for square corners, 1 mark for correct labels.
8. Explain why a football is not a plane figure but the drawing of its outline is. (2 marks)
   Answer: A football is a solid object with thickness, but its outline drawn on paper is flat.
   Why: A plane figure is flat, so only the drawing counts.
