## PAGE 1: Getting ready to paint
Painting means putting colour on paper, cloth or a wall with a brush, a sponge or your fingers. Paint is coloured powder or liquid mixed with water or oil. In school, we mostly use poster paint or watercolour paint.

### Tools and materials
• Paint in different colours, and white and black.
• Round brushes for lines and small areas, and flat brushes for wide areas.
• A palette. This can be a plastic lid or a piece of old cardboard covered in plastic.
• A container of clean water and a piece of cloth or rag.
• Thick paper, such as a sheet of manila paper.
• Old newspaper to cover the desk, and an apron or old shirt.

### Caring for your brush
1. Rinse the brush in water each time you change colour, and dab it on the rag.
2. Do not push the brush hard so that its hairs bend backwards.
3. After painting, wash the brush with soap and water and shape the tip with your fingers.
4. Store it with the hairs upwards, or lay it flat. Never leave a brush standing on its hairs in water.

### Keeping the place clean
Tidy up when you finish. Pour dirty water away carefully and do not pour paint down a place where animals drink. Wash your hands afterwards.

## PAGE 2: Primary and secondary colours
Colours can be sorted into groups. This sorting is called colour classification. It helps us know which colours we must buy or have, and how to mix others.

### Primary colours
The primary colours are red, yellow and blue. You cannot make them by mixing other colours. All other paint colours are made from them.

### Secondary colours
A secondary colour is made by mixing two primary colours in equal amounts.
• Red + yellow = orange
• Yellow + blue = green
• Blue + red = purple

## PAGE 3: Colour value, tints and shades
Colour value means how light or dark a colour is. Each colour can be made lighter or darker.

• To make a colour lighter, add white. A lighter colour is called a tint or a light tone.
• To make a colour darker, add black. A darker colour is called a shade or a dark tone.

Add only a little at a time. It takes a lot of white to change a dark colour, but a tiny bit of black makes a light colour much darker.

### Making a scale strip
1. Draw a long strip on paper and divide it into five equal boxes with a ruler.
2. Choose a secondary colour, for example orange.
3. In the first two boxes, mix orange with more and more white.
4. Paint the middle box with pure orange.
5. In the last two boxes, mix orange with a little and then a little more black.
6. Let it dry and label each box light, medium or dark.

## PAGE 4: Painting a single object with colour value
Colour value makes an object look round and solid. If you paint a mango in one flat colour, it looks like a flat shape. If you paint the light side lighter and the shadow side darker, it looks like a real fruit.

### Steps
1. Choose one simple object, such as a mango, an orange or a water bottle. Place it near a window so that light comes from one side.
2. Look carefully. Which side is brightest? Which side is darkest?
3. Draw the outline lightly with a pencil, making the object big enough to fill most of the paper.
4. Mix your main colour. For an orange mango, mix red and yellow.
5. Paint the whole object with the main colour and let it dry a little.
6. Mix a light tone by adding white, and paint it on the side facing the light.
7. Mix a dark tone by adding a little black, and paint it on the side away from the light. Add a soft shadow on the table under the object.
8. Blend the edges of the tones with a damp brush so that they join smoothly.

### Check your work
• Have you used light, medium and dark tones?
• Is the paint smooth without big drips?
• Did you keep the picture clean and neat?

Show your painting, and take turns to say what you like about each other's work.

## PAGE 5: What is a montage?
A montage is a picture made by joining many small pictures or cut pieces of paper, cloth or other flat materials on a background. The word means putting pieces together to make one whole picture. A montage can tell a story or show a theme, such as market day, our school or ball games.

### Subject matter
The subject matter is what the montage is about. In a montage about ball games, the pictures could be footballs, netballs, players running or teams cheering. All the cut-outs should fit the theme.

### Overlapping
Overlapping means placing one picture so that it partly covers another. Overlapping makes the montage look full and joined up, with no gaps.

### Neatness
A good montage has clean cut edges, no glue marks, no torn edges and pieces that are firmly stuck down.

## PAGE 6: Making a montage on ball games
### Materials
Old newspapers and magazines, scissors, a sheet of manila paper or cardboard for the background, glue and a brush, and a cloth for cleaning.

### Improvising glue
If you do not have glue, you can make flour paste.
1. Put two spoons of wheat flour in a cup.
2. Add a little cold water and stir until it is a smooth paste with no lumps.
3. An adult adds a little hot water while you stir until it thickens. Let it cool before use.
4. Apply it with a brush or a small stick. Use it on the day you make it, because it goes bad after a few days.

### Steps
1. Find pictures of ball games in old newspapers and magazines: football, netball, volleyball, basketball and others.
2. Cut around each picture with scissors. Cut slowly and keep your fingers away from the blade.
3. Lay the pieces on the background without gluing. Move them around until you like the arrangement. Overlap them so that there are no gaps.
4. Paste the back pieces first, then the middle ones, then the front ones. Press each piece down with a clean cloth.
5. Add a title, such as Ball Games, with paint or a marker.
6. Let it dry flat. Clean your working area and put the leftover paper in the bin.

### Talking about montages
Show your montage. Say what your theme is, how you arranged the pieces and which part you like best. Listen kindly to your friends' comments.

## QUIZ
1. Which group has only primary colours?
   A) Red, yellow, blue
   B) Orange, green, purple
   C) Red, green, blue
   D) Yellow, orange, blue
   Answer: A
   Why: Red, yellow and blue cannot be mixed from other colours, so they are the primary colours.
2. What colour do you get by mixing yellow and blue?
   A) Orange
   B) Purple
   C) Green
   D) Brown
   Answer: C
   Why: Yellow and blue mixed together give green, which is a secondary colour.
3. What do you add to a colour to make it lighter?
   A) Black
   B) White
   C) Water and soap
   D) A darker colour
   Answer: B
   Why: Adding white makes a tint, which is a lighter tone of the colour.
4. Which describes overlapping in a montage?
   A) Cutting pictures into tiny pieces
   B) Painting the pictures blue
   C) Placing one picture so that it partly covers another
   D) Leaving wide gaps between pictures
   Answer: C
   Why: Overlapping means pieces cover parts of one another, so the montage looks full.
5. Which is the best way to store a brush after painting?
   A) Standing on its hairs in a cup of water
   B) Washed and laid flat or with hairs upwards
   C) Left covered with paint
   D) Pushed into the bottom of a bag
   Answer: B
   Why: Washing and drying the brush keeps the hairs in good shape.
6. Name the three secondary colours and the primary colours that make each. (3 marks)
   Answer: Orange from red and yellow, green from yellow and blue, purple from blue and red.
   Why: 1 mark for each correct secondary colour with its two primary colours.
7. Explain how to make a dark tone of green. (2 marks)
   Answer: Mix green paint with a small amount of black, adding more black a little at a time until it is as dark as needed.
   Why: 1 mark for saying black is added, 1 mark for adding it a little at a time.
8. Describe how to make flour paste glue. (3 marks)
   Answer: Mix flour with a little cold water to a smooth paste. Add a little hot water with an adult while stirring until thick. Let it cool before using.
   Why: 1 mark for the smooth paste, 1 mark for the hot water added by an adult, 1 mark for cooling.
9. Give three qualities of a neat montage. (3 marks)
   Answer: Clean cut edges, pieces that overlap without gaps, and no glue marks or torn edges. Other correct answers are accepted.
   Why: 1 mark for each correct quality.
10. A learner paints a round orange with one flat colour and it looks flat. What should the learner do to make it look round? (3 marks)
   Answer: Paint a lighter tone on the side facing the light and a darker tone on the side away from the light, and blend the edges. Add a soft shadow under the fruit.
   Why: 1 mark for the light tone, 1 mark for the dark tone, 1 mark for blending or a shadow.
