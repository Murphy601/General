## PAGE 1: What is gymnastics?
Gymnastics is a sport in which people perform balances, rolls, jumps and turns with control. A gymnast moves the body in graceful ways and holds still positions called balances. In this topic, we learn two basic skills: the crab balance and the side roll.

A balance is a position you can hold still for a few seconds without falling. A roll is a movement in which your body turns over along the floor. Both need strength, control and courage, but they do not need special equipment. All you need is a soft mat, or a thick folded blanket on flat ground, and a clear space.

### Safety comes first
Gymnastics is safe when we follow the rules.
• Practise on a mat or on soft, clean ground. Never on concrete or a stony floor.
• Keep the space clear. Move desks, stones and bags away from your mat.
• Take off shoes, watches, bracelets, necklaces and hair clips. Wear loose, comfortable clothes.
• Tie back long hair.
• Do not chew sweets or gum while practising.
• Take turns, and never push or pull a friend.
• Stop at once and tell your teacher if something hurts.

### Warm up
Before you begin, warm up for five minutes. Jog gently on the spot, rotate your wrists, shoulders, hips and ankles, and stretch your arms and legs slowly.

## PAGE 2: The crab balance
The crab balance is a position in which your body is supported by both hands and both feet, with your tummy facing up. It looks like a table with four legs, the two hands and the two feet. It is called a crab balance because the body looks a bit like a crab walking sideways.

### Steps for the crab balance
1. Sit on the mat with your knees bent and your feet flat on the floor, about shoulder-width apart.
2. Put your hands on the mat behind you, shoulder-width apart, with your fingers pointing towards your feet.
3. Push down through your hands and feet, and lift your hips until your body is flat like a table. Your arms are straight and your thighs and tummy are level.
4. Keep your head in line with your body, and look towards your knees or up at the ceiling.
5. Hold the balance for three to five slow counts, and breathe normally.
6. Lower your hips gently back to the mat.

## PAGE 3: Practising the crab balance
The crab balance is easier if you know which muscles you use. Your arms and shoulders hold up the top half of your body. Your back and tummy muscles keep your body level. Your legs push the hips up. Each time you practise, these muscles get stronger.

### Practice plan
1. Start by lifting your hips only a little and putting them down again. Do this five times.
2. Lift higher and hold for two counts.
3. Hold the full balance for three, then five counts.
4. Try lifting one hand for one second, and put it back. Only try this when you are steady, and with a partner beside you.

### Common mistakes and how to fix them
• Hips sagging near the mat: push harder through your hands and feet, and squeeze your bottom muscles.
• Bent arms: straighten them and push the floor away.
• Head dropped back with a strain: keep the head in line, and look at the ceiling gently.
• Hands too close to your bottom: place your hands further back, level with your shoulders when you lift.

### Working in pairs
One learner performs and the other watches. The watcher stands beside the mat, checks the four points of the table, and says one thing that was good and one thing to improve. Then swap places. Being a kind partner is part of gymnastics.

### Steps for the side roll
1. Lie on your back at one end of the mat, with your body straight and your legs together.
2. Stretch your arms above your head, with your palms together and your head between your arms.
3. Point your toes and tighten your tummy muscles so that your body is like a stiff log.
4. Roll over onto your side, then on to your tummy, keeping the body long. Turn your head with your body.
5. Keep rolling until you reach the end of the mat, or roll back the other way.
6. Stop by keeping the body still, and get up slowly. Do not stand up fast, because you may feel dizzy.

### Points to remember
• Do not let your hands and legs come apart.
• Keep the roll in a straight line along the mat, and not in a curve.
• Roll slowly at first. Speed comes later.

## PAGE 5: Joining the crab balance and the side roll

### How to perform the sequence
1. Do a crab balance and hold for three counts.
2. Lower your hips slowly to the mat, and lie on your back.
3. Stretch your arms overhead and make your body long.
4. Do a side roll along the mat.
5. Finish by getting up slowly, standing tall with your arms out, and smile.

### Feedback
Watch your friends' sequences and give feedback. Use three questions. Was the balance still and steady? Was the roll straight? Was the movement smooth and controlled? Start with a good comment and be kind.

## PAGE 6: Gymnastics and a healthy body
Performing rolls and balances helps your body to stay healthy in many ways.
• Strength: holding a crab balance makes your arms, shoulders, tummy and legs stronger.
• Flexibility: stretching and rolling help your joints and muscles to bend and move freely.
• Balance and coordination: gymnastics teaches your body to stay steady and to move parts together.
• Heart and lungs: moving your whole body makes your heart beat faster, which keeps it strong.
• Confidence: when you master a skill, you feel proud and try new things more bravely.

### Keeping safe, always
Gymnastics is fun only when everyone is safe. Look after yourself and others. Check the mat, keep the space clear, wait your turn and stop when tired. When you support a partner, do it gently and only as your teacher shows you.

### Enjoy it
Gymnastics is not a race. Some learners will find balances easy and others will find rolls easy. Everyone is different. Praise each other's efforts and celebrate progress.

### Summary
The crab balance holds the body up on hands and feet, like a table. The side roll turns the body over sideways, like a log. Both need control, safe space and warm-up.

## QUIZ
1. Which body parts touch the floor in a crab balance?
   A) Both hands and both feet
   B) Head and knees
   C) Back and elbows
   D) Only the feet
   Answer: A
   Why: The crab balance is supported by two hands and two feet, with the tummy facing up.
2. In a crab balance, which way does your tummy face?
   A) Towards the floor
   B) Towards the ceiling
   C) Towards a wall on your side
   D) It does not matter
   Answer: B
   Why: In the crab balance the hips are lifted so that the tummy faces up like a table top.
3. For the side roll, how should your body be?
   A) Curled up in a ball
   B) Bent at the waist
   C) Straight and long like a log
   D) With arms and legs spread wide
   Answer: C
   Why: A straight body rolls smoothly along the mat in a line.
4. Which of these is a safety rule in gymnastics?
   A) Wear shoes and bracelets
   B) Practise on a stony floor
   C) Push a friend to help them roll
   D) Practise on a mat with a clear space
   Answer: D
   Why: A soft mat and clear space prevent injuries.
5. Which health benefit comes from gymnastics?
   A) Stronger muscles and better balance
   B) A weaker heart
   C) Taller buildings
   D) Less water needed
   Answer: A
   Why: Balances and rolls make muscles stronger and improve balance and coordination.
6. Describe how to move into a crab balance from sitting. (4 marks)
   Answer: Sit with knees bent and feet flat. Put hands behind you, shoulder-width apart, fingers pointing to the feet. Push through hands and feet to lift the hips until the body is level like a table. Hold for a few counts.
   Why: 1 mark for the sitting start, 1 mark for the hand position, 1 mark for lifting the hips, 1 mark for holding.
7. State two mistakes people make in the crab balance and how to correct each. (4 marks)
   Answer: Hips sagging, so push harder through hands and feet; bent arms, so straighten them. Other correct mistakes are accepted.
   Why: 1 mark for each mistake and 1 mark for each correction.
8. List three safety measures before doing a side roll. (3 marks)
   Answer: Remove jewellery and watches, warm up, and check that the mat is clear and flat. Other correct measures are accepted.
   Why: 1 mark for each correct measure.
9. Explain how you can combine the crab balance and the side roll into a sequence. (3 marks)
   Answer: Do a crab balance and hold for three counts, lower slowly to the mat and lie on your back with arms overhead, then do a side roll along the mat and finish by standing slowly.
   Why: 1 mark for crab balance and hold, 1 mark for lowering and lying straight, 1 mark for the side roll and finish.
10. How does performing rolls and balances improve body health? (3 marks)
   Answer: It makes muscles stronger, improves flexibility and balance, and exercises the heart and lungs.
   Why: 1 mark for each correct benefit.
