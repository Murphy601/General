## PAGE 1: Melody and the sol-fa syllables
A melody is the tune of a song. It is made of musical notes that go up and down in pitch, one after another. Pitch means how high or how low a sound is. A bird's whistle is a high pitch, and a lion's roar is a low pitch. When you hum a song, you are singing its melody.

To read and sing melodies, musicians use sol-fa. Sol-fa gives a name, called a syllable, to each note. The full set is doh, ray, me, fah, soh, lah, te, and doh again at the top. In Grade 4 we learn the first three syllables.

### The three syllables
• d is doh, the lowest of the three. It is the home note.
• r is ray, the middle one.
• m is me, the highest of the three.

Doh is the lowest, ray is one step above it and me is one step above ray. We can think of them as three steps on a small staircase. Going up the staircase, the sound rises. Going down the staircase, the sound falls.

### Sing the steps
Sing slowly with your teacher: d r m, then m r d. Notice how your voice rises and falls. Stand up on the rising notes and sit down slowly on the falling notes if it helps you to feel the pitch.

### How to make each sign
• d (doh): make a fist and hold it in front of you at about waist height, with the fist steady.
• r (ray): open the hand, keep the fingers together, and tilt the palm so that it slopes upward. Hold it a little above waist height.
• m (me): hold the hand flat, palm down, at about chest height, like a table top.

### Practise
1. Watch your teacher make the three signs and sing each note.
2. Make the sign and sing the note together.
3. In pairs, one learner shows a sign and the other sings the right note.
4. Try showing a short tune, such as d r m r d, only with signs while the class sings it.

## PAGE 3: Identifying d r m by ear and in songs
A good musician can hear a note and say its name. This skill is called listening or aural work. It takes practice.

### Listening game
1. Your teacher plays or sings one note, either d, r or m.
2. You listen carefully. Is it low, middle or high?
3. Show the hand sign for the note you heard.
4. Your teacher tells you if you are correct.

Start with only d and m. They are far apart and easy to tell. When you are good at it, add r.

### Songs that use only d r m
Some children's songs use only three notes. A well known example is Hot Cross Buns, which uses the notes m r d for the words Hot cross buns. It goes: m r d, m r d, and later d d d d, r r r r, m r d. Sing it with sol-fa syllables first. Then sing it with words.

### Sing by hand signs
Sing a familiar tune with your teacher leading the hand signs. Stop suddenly and ask the class to show the sign for the last note you sang.

### Sing in tune
When you sing, sit or stand tall, breathe in deeply and listen to yourself and others. If you sing too low or too high, listen again to the teacher's note and try to match it. Match means to sing the very same pitch.

## PAGE 4: Qualities of a good melody
Some melodies are easy to remember and enjoyable to sing. Others are dull. There are some qualities that make a melody good.

### Stepwise motion
Stepwise motion means the tune moves to the next note above or below, without jumping over any note. With d, r and m, moving d to r, r to m, m to r or r to d are steps. A stepwise tune is smooth and easy to sing.

### Variation
Variation means using different syllables and different lengths of notes. If you sing only d d d d, the tune is boring. Mix d, r and m, and mix crotchets, pairs of quavers and rests, so that the tune has interest.

### Ending on d
A tune sounds finished when it ends on d, the home note. If a tune stops on r or m, it feels like a story that has stopped in the middle.

## PAGE 5: Composing a short melody
To compose means to create something new. You can compose a short melody using only d, r and m and the rhythm you learned earlier: the crotchet, the pair of quavers and their rests.

### Steps to compose
1. Choose your rhythm first. For example, in two bars of four beats each: ta-te taa ta-te taa | taa ta-te taa taa.
2. Give each crotchet one syllable and each quaver one syllable, so a pair takes two syllables.
3. Use steps between notes. Do not jump from d straight to m.
4. Make sure the last note is d and is a crotchet.
5. Sing it with sol-fa and show the hand signs.
6. Write the syllables under the rhythm names.

### Worked example
Rhythm: ta-te taa ta-te taa | taa ta-te taa taa
Syllables: d r m m r m | r r m r d
Matching them beat by beat:
Beat 1 (ta-te): d r
Beat 2 (taa): m
Beat 3 (ta-te): m r
Beat 4 (taa): m
Beat 5 (taa): r
Beat 6 (ta-te): r m
Beat 7 (taa): r
Beat 8 (taa): d

Check: all moves are steps or repeated notes, the tune has variation, and the melody ends on d with a crotchet.

### Practise
Compose your own eight-beat melody. Write the syllables, sing it and show it to a friend. Ask your friend to sing it back. If they can sing it easily, it is a good melody.

## PAGE 6: Clay slabs and coils, getting ready
Now we turn the sol-fa syllables into clay models. We make three slabs and put the letters d, r and m on them using coils.

### Collecting and preparing clay
Clay is a fine, sticky soil that becomes soft when wet and hard when dry. Ask an adult where to dig clay soil in your area, for example a place near a stream. Never dig where it is dangerous or on someone's land without permission.
1. Remove stones, roots and leaves from the clay.
2. Add a little water and knead it, pressing and folding it with your hands, until it is smooth and does not stick to your fingers.
3. Keep the clay covered with a damp cloth or plastic so that it does not dry out.

### Two techniques
A slab is a flat piece of clay. To make one, flatten the clay with a rolling pin or a smooth bottle until it is about as thick as your little finger. Use two sticks of equal thickness on both sides so that the slab is even.

A coil is a long, thin roll of clay like a snake. To make one, roll a small piece of clay back and forth on a table with the palms of your hands until it is even and about as thick as a pencil.

### Making the slabs
Cut three rectangles of different sizes from your slab with a blunt knife or a strong piece of string. Neaten the edges with a little water on your finger. These will be the bases of your model.

### Steps
1. Make coils and lay one on the slab in the shape of the letter. For d, make a circle and a straight stem beside it. For r, make a short stem with a small curve. For m, make a stem with two small humps.
2. Before you press the coil on, scratch the slab and the coil where they will touch. Then dab a little water on the scratches. This helps the clay to stick and stops it falling off when it dries.
3. Press the coil gently onto the slab. Smooth the joins with a finger or a wooden stick.
4. Check that each letter is clear and about the same thickness.

### Drying and care
Leave the slabs to dry slowly in a shady place, not in strong sunshine, so that they do not crack. Wash your hands and tools, and put leftover clay in a covered container.

### Show and talk
Display your three slabs in order, d, r, m, from left to right. Take turns to talk about your model and someone else's. Sing the notes as you point to each slab.

## QUIZ
1. Which sol-fa syllable is the highest of d, r and m?
   A) d
   B) r
   C) m
   D) They are the same
   Answer: C
   Why: Doh is the lowest, ray is in the middle and me is the highest of the three.
2. On which note should a good short melody end?
   A) m
   B) r
   C) A rest
   D) d
   Answer: D
   Why: d is the home note. Ending on it makes the tune sound finished.
3. Which hand sign is a closed fist?
   A) d
   B) r
   C) m
   D) None of them
   Answer: A
   Why: In the hand signs, the closed fist stands for d, the sloping palm for r and the flat palm for m.
4. What is stepwise motion?
   A) Jumping over notes
   B) Moving to the next note above or below
   C) Playing only one note
   D) Singing very loudly
   Answer: B
   Why: In stepwise motion, each note is next to the one before it, such as d to r or r to m.
5. Why do we scratch the clay and dab a little water before joining a coil to a slab?
   A) To make the clay bigger
   B) To make the clay dry faster
   C) To help the two pieces stick together
   D) To change the colour
   Answer: C
   Why: Scratching and water make the surfaces rough and sticky, so that the coil holds to the slab.
6. State three qualities of a good melody. (3 marks)
   Answer: It uses stepwise motion, has variation of syllables and note lengths, and ends on d.
   Why: 1 mark for each correct quality.
7. Write the sol-fa syllables of a melody that goes up by steps from d to m and comes back down to d. (2 marks)
   Answer: d r m r d
   Why: 1 mark for going up d r m and 1 mark for coming back r d.
8. Explain how you make a coil and a slab from clay. (4 marks)
   Answer: For a slab, flatten the clay with a rolling pin or bottle until it is even and about as thick as a little finger. For a coil, roll a small piece between your palms on a table until it is long, thin and even, like a snake.
   Why: 2 marks for the slab method and 2 marks for the coil method.
9. Compose a four-beat melody using d, r and m that ends on d. Write the syllables and rhythm names. (4 marks)
   Answer: For example, rhythm: ta-te taa taa taa. Syllables: d r | m | r | d. Any four-beat melody is accepted if it uses only d, r and m, moves by steps and ends on d.
   Why: 1 mark for four beats, 1 mark for using only d, r and m, 1 mark for stepwise motion, 1 mark for ending on d.
