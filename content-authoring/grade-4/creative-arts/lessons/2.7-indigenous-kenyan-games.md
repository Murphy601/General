## PAGE 1: What are indigenous animal games?
Long before there were footballs, netballs and school playing fields, children in Kenya played games with what they had around them: open ground, stones, sticks, singing and their own bodies. These traditional games are called indigenous games. Indigenous means belonging to a people and a place from long ago. They are passed from grandparents and parents to children by playing together, and not by books.

Many indigenous games are inspired by animals. In an animal game, players move like an animal, copy its sounds, or act out a story about animals. Children watched the animals on the farm and in the bush, such as frogs at the river, hares in the grass, monkeys in the trees and elephants walking in a group. They copied how the animals moved and made them into games.

### Why are they called indigenous?
• They come from the communities and their environment.
• They use little or no equipment.
• They are learned by watching and joining in.
• They often go together with songs, clapping and drumming.

### Types of animal games
In this topic, we shall meet two types.
1. Animal movement games, where you move like an animal, such as the frog jump, crab walk or elephant walk.
2. Animal chasing games, where players act as animals that chase or run away, such as the hawk and the chicks.

Each community may have its own names and songs for such games. Ask your parents, grandparents or elders about the games they played, and share them with your class.

### Frog jump
1. Squat down with your knees bent and your hands on the ground in front of you.
2. Push up with your legs and jump forward, landing softly on your toes and hands.
3. Land with knees bent to absorb the shock.
4. Jump again, keeping the movement smooth. Say ribbit if you like.
This game makes the leg muscles strong.

### Crab walk
1. Sit down on the ground. Put your hands behind you and lift your hips, like the crab balance.
2. Walk on your hands and feet, with your tummy facing the sky.
3. You may walk forwards or backwards. Keep your head in line with your body.
This makes the arms, shoulders and tummy muscles strong.

### Elephant walk
Bend forward at the waist. Clasp your hands together and let your arms hang down like a trunk. Walk slowly with heavy steps, swaying your trunk from side to side. This game stretches the back and the back of the legs.

## PAGE 3: Animal movement games, part 2
### Snake slither
Lie on your tummy on soft, clean grass or a mat. Keep your arms by your sides. Move forward by wriggling your body from side to side, without using your hands or legs much. Take care that your face and clothes stay clean. This exercises the tummy and back muscles.

### Hare hop
Squat with your hands on your knees. Hop forward with both feet, quickly, like a hare in the grass. Keep your hops small and light and land on the balls of your feet. It builds leg strength and speed.

### Crocodile crawl
Lie on your tummy with your hands beside your shoulders. Push up so that your body is straight, resting on your hands and toes. Crawl forward slowly, moving your left hand and right foot together, and then the right hand and left foot. Do not let your tummy sag. This builds strong arms and shoulders.

### Giraffe stretch
Stand tall on your toes and stretch your arms high above your head, as if you are reaching leaves at the top of a tree. Walk slowly with long, smooth steps. Then bend down as if drinking water, keeping your legs a little apart. This stretches the whole body.

### Monkey swing
Stand with your feet apart, bend your knees and swing your arms loosely from side to side. Then move like a monkey, hopping side to side with your hands low. It teaches balance and coordination.

### What the games teach
These games strengthen the muscles, make the joints flexible, and improve balance and coordination. They also teach us about the animals we share the land with.

## PAGE 4: An animal chasing game, the hawk and the chicks
This game is played in many places. It teaches teamwork, quick thinking and fast movement.

### Players
• One player is the hen. The hen is at the front and protects the chicks.
• Three to six players are the chicks. They stand behind the hen in a line, each one holding the waist or shoulders of the player in front.
• One player is the hawk. The hawk tries to tag the last chick.

### How to play
1. The hawk stands about five metres from the hen and chicks.
2. The hawk calls that it is hungry. The hen spreads her arms and moves left and right to block the hawk.
3. The chicks stay in line and hold on gently, moving with the hen.
4. If the hawk tags the last chick, the chick becomes the hawk, and the hawk joins the chicks at the front. Choose a new hen if you like.
5. Play for a set time, and give everyone a turn as the hawk and the hen.

### Rules
• Hold, but do not pull, the player in front.
• The hawk must tag gently, only on the back or shoulders.
• Nobody may push, trip or grab clothes.
• Stop at once when the teacher blows the whistle.

## PAGE 5: Animal games to a musical rhythm
Many indigenous games are played with songs, clapping or drumming. The rhythm helps players to move together and makes the game more fun.

### Using the rhythm names
Use the rhythm names you learnt: taa is a long sound that lasts one beat, and ta-te is two short sounds in one beat.
• Elephant walk: slow, heavy steps. Step on each beat: taa taa taa (rest).
• Hare hop: quick hops. Two small hops per beat: ta-te ta-te ta-te ta-te.
• Frog jump: jump on the first beat, rest on the others: taa (rest) (rest) (rest).

### Steps
1. Choose a game and a rhythm. Clap the pattern together first.
2. One learner plays the beat on a drum or claps, and the others move to it.
3. Change the tempo. A faster beat makes hares hop faster, and a slow beat suits elephants.
4. Add a song. Sing the names of the animals and perform actions.
5. Take turns to lead, so that everyone can make up a pattern.

Counting the beats also connects to Mathematics, because you count steps and jumps as you play.

## PAGE 6: Safety, fitness and appreciation
### Safety rules for animal games
• Play on soft, clear ground such as short grass, a mat or clean sand. Check for stones, thorns, broken glass and holes.
• Warm up before you play and cool down after.
• Wear light clothes that allow you to move, and shoes that fit or none if the teacher allows.
• Keep a safe distance from other players. Do not push, trip or pull.
• Drink water after playing, and rest when you are tired.
• Follow the leader and the whistle.
• Tell the teacher if you feel pain.

### How the games make us fit and healthy
• Muscles: jumping, crawling and walking on hands and feet make the arms, legs and tummy muscles stronger.
• Stamina: playing for a long time makes the heart and lungs stronger.
• Flexibility: stretching like a giraffe makes the body bend easily.
• Balance and coordination: moving like a crab or monkey teaches the body to move together.
• Mood: playing with friends makes us happy and less stressed.

### Roles and responsibility
In an animal game, each player may play a different role, such as leader, hen, hawk or drummer. Take your role seriously. Be fair, and give others turns.

### Appreciation
Indigenous games are part of our heritage, which means something valuable that we get from earlier generations. They cost nothing and they can be played anywhere. Ask an elder to teach you a game from your community and share it at school. When you play a game from another community, respect its rules and songs.

   Answer: B
   Why: In the crab walk, players move on hands and feet like a crab.
2. Why are traditional games such as animal games called indigenous?
   A) They cost a lot of money
   B) They belong to a people and were passed down from long ago
   C) They were invented last year
   D) They need special machines
   Answer: B
   Why: Indigenous games belong to the communities and are passed on from older to younger people.
3. In the hawk and the chicks game, what does the hawk try to do?
   A) Tag the last chick
   B) Catch the hen's hat
   C) Push the hen over
   D) Run away from the chicks
   Answer: A
   Why: The hawk tries to tag the last chick in the line while the hen blocks.
4. Which is a safety rule for animal games?
   A) Play on ground with sharp stones
   B) Push others to make them jump
   C) Play on soft, clear ground
   D) Play without warming up
   Answer: C
   Why: Soft, clear ground prevents cuts and injuries.
5. Which animal game exercises the arms and shoulders by moving on the hands and toes?
   A) Giraffe stretch
   B) Crocodile crawl
   C) Snake slither
   D) Elephant walk
   Answer: B
   Why: In the crocodile crawl the body is held straight on hands and toes, which builds arm and shoulder strength.
