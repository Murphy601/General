## PAGE 1: Beat and rhythm
Every song has a steady beat, like your heart beating or a person walking at an even pace. When you tap your foot or nod your head to music, you are keeping the beat. The beat does not change speed unless the singers change the tempo on purpose.

Rhythm is different from the beat. Rhythm is the pattern of long and short sounds that fit over the beat. When you clap the words of a song, you clap its rhythm. Some sounds are short, and some sounds last longer.

### Feel the difference
Sing a song you know well, such as a topical song about washing hands. Tap the beat on your knees with a steady pulse. Then clap the words of the song. You will notice that some claps come quickly, one after another, and some come slowly. The knee taps are the beat. The claps are the rhythm.

### Why we write rhythm down
If we could not write rhythm, a song would be lost when the singer forgot it. Writing lets people far away play the same rhythm. In music, we write rhythm with note symbols and with rhythm names. In this topic, we use two note types and their rests.

### Try it
Say your own name and clap each syllable. For example, Wanjiku is Wan-ji-ku, which has three claps. Compare the number of claps with your friends' names. Names are full of rhythm.

## PAGE 2: The crotchet and the pair of quavers
Notes have different lengths. We measure the length of a note in beats. Two notes are your first ones.

### Crotchet
A crotchet is a note that lasts for one beat. It looks like a filled-in oval with a straight line, called a stem, going up from it. Its French rhythm name is taa. You say it slowly, as a long sound: taa.

### Pair of quavers
A quaver is a shorter note. It lasts for half a beat. Quavers usually come in pairs, with a thick line called a beam joining their stems. A pair of quavers together lasts for one beat, the same as one crotchet. Its French rhythm name is ta-te. You say it quickly, both sounds in the same beat.

### Number link
One quaver is half a beat. Two quavers make 1/2 + 1/2 = 1 whole beat, which is the same as one crotchet.

## PAGE 3: Rests, the silent beats
A rest is a sign for silence. In music, silence is as important as sound. A rest tells you to keep the beat but make no sound. Every note has a rest that lasts as long as the note.

### Crotchet rest
The crotchet rest lasts for one beat of silence. It looks like a squiggle, a bit like a lightning bolt. When you see it, you must not clap. Keep the beat by opening your hands wide or tapping your knees silently.

### Quaver rest
The quaver rest lasts for half a beat of silence. It looks like a small number 7 with a dot on the left. A beat can have one quaver followed by a quaver rest: you make one short sound, then stay silent for the second half of the beat.

### Counting with rests
Count the beats in your head: 1 2 3 4. Clap on the beats with notes. On the beat with a crotchet rest, do not clap.

Example with four beats:
Beat 1: taa (clap)
Beat 2: taa (clap)
Beat 3: rest (no clap, hands apart)
Beat 4: taa (clap)

Say it with rhythm names: taa taa (rest) taa.

### Common mistake
Some learners hurry over a rest and start the next note early. Remember that a rest takes as long as its note. The silence is a real beat.

### Steps to read a pattern
1. Find the four beats. Each beat has either a crotchet, a pair of quavers or a rest.
2. Say the rhythm names slowly: ta-te ta-te taa (rest).
3. Say it again, tapping your knee on the beat.
4. Clap the notes and open your hands on the rest.
5. Speed up a little but keep the beat steady.

### Writing from dictation
Dictation means that your teacher claps a pattern and you write it down. Listen twice. Count the beats. For each beat, decide if it was one long sound, two short sounds or silence. Write taa, ta-te or a rest sign for each beat.

Example: your teacher claps a pattern, and you hear two quick sounds, then one long sound, then silence, then one long sound. Write: ta-te taa (rest) taa.

## PAGE 5: Free hand lettering
To write the rhythm names neatly, we use free hand lettering. This means writing letters with your hand, without a ruler or stencil. We use lowercase letters, also called small letters, for the rhythm names taa and ta-te.

### The four guide lines
When we practise lettering, we draw four light lines.
• Ascender line: the top line for tall letters.
• Top of small letters: the line where the small letters end.
• Baseline: the line on which all the letters sit.
• Descender line: the bottom line for letters with tails.

### Ascenders and descenders
An ascender is the part of a letter that goes up above the small letters, like the stem of b, d, f, h, k, l and t. A descender is the part that goes down below the baseline, like the tail of g, j, p, q and y. Letters like a, e, n, o, r, s, u and x do not have either.

### Writing taa and ta-te
The word taa has one ascender, the letter t. The word ta-te has two, the two letter t's. Write every t so that its stem reaches the ascender line, and keep the letters a and e between the baseline and the top of the small letters. Leave an even space between letters, and write a short hyphen in the middle of ta-te.

## PAGE 6: Qualities of a good rhythmic pattern
When you compose a rhythm, you make up a new pattern and write it down. A good rhythmic pattern has some clear qualities.

### Variation of note values
A pattern that uses only one note value, such as taa taa taa taa, sounds dull. Mix crotchets, pairs of quavers and rests. Long and short sounds together make the pattern interesting.

### Repetition of patterns
Repeating a small group of notes helps listeners remember your rhythm. For example, ta-te ta-te taa (rest) can be played twice. Repetition gives shape to the music. But too much repetition can be boring, so use it wisely.

### Ending on a long note
A rhythm feels finished when it ends on a long note. In your patterns, end on a crotchet (taa). If you end on a pair of quavers or a rest, the listener may feel that the music has not finished.

### Steps to compose
1. Decide on the number of beats, for example four or eight.
2. Choose what goes on each beat: a crotchet, a pair of quavers or a rest.
3. Check that each beat is full. Quaver pairs fill one beat and rests fill their own time.
4. Clap the pattern and adjust it.
5. Make sure it ends on a crotchet.
6. Write it using rhythm names and note symbols.

### Example
Four beats: ta-te taa ta-te taa. It uses two kinds of notes, has some repetition (the ta-te taa group comes twice) and ends on a long note.

## PAGE 7: Compose, perform and critique
Let us put everything together. Work in a group of three or four learners.

### Task
1. Agree on a four-beat pattern. Write it using rhythm names.
2. Draw the notes and rests on a single line, as in Figure 2. Write the rhythm name under each beat.
3. Clap your rhythm to a steady beat while another group member taps the beat on a drum.
4. Change your pattern. Add a rest, or swap a crotchet with a pair of quavers.
5. Perform your pattern for the class.

### Critique with respect
A critique is an honest, kind talk about someone's work. Use these questions.
• Was the beat steady?
• Was there variation, and were the notes and rests clapped correctly?
• Was there a repeated group?
• Did it end on a long note?

Say one good point first, and then give one idea that could make it better. Listen to others' views and accept them politely.

Your rhythm should be your own idea. Copying a friend's pattern is not fair. Original work shows honesty.

### Summary
A crotchet is taa and lasts one beat. A pair of quavers is ta-te and also lasts one beat. Rests are silent beats. A good pattern has variation, some repetition and a long note at the end.

## QUIZ
1. How many beats does a crotchet last?
   A) Half a beat
   B) One beat
   C) Two beats
   D) Four beats
   Answer: B
   Why: A crotchet lasts one whole beat and is named taa.
2. What is the French rhythm name for a pair of quavers?
   A) taa
   B) ta-te
   C) rest
   D) te-taa
   Answer: B
   Why: A pair of quavers fills one beat with two short sounds, called ta-te.
3. How should you show a crotchet rest when clapping?
   A) Clap twice quickly
   B) Clap loudly
   C) Do not clap for one beat
   D) Stop the song
   Answer: C
   Why: A rest is a silent beat. You keep the beat but make no sound.
4. Which of these letters has a descender?
   A) b
   B) t
   C) p
   D) a
   Answer: C
   Why: The letter p has a tail that goes below the baseline, so it is a descender. The letters b and t go up.
5. Which ending makes a rhythmic pattern feel finished?
   A) A crotchet, a long note
   B) A pair of quavers
   C) A rest
   D) A very fast note
   Answer: A
   Why: Ending on a long note, the crotchet, makes the pattern sound complete.
6. Explain why two quavers make the same length as one crotchet. (2 marks)
   Answer: Each quaver is half a beat. Half a beat and half a beat together make one whole beat, which is the length of a crotchet.
   Why: 1 mark for saying a quaver is half a beat, 1 mark for adding to make one beat.
7. Write the rhythm names for a four-beat pattern made of a pair of quavers, a crotchet, a pair of quavers and a crotchet. (2 marks)
   Answer: ta-te taa ta-te taa
   Why: 1 mark for the correct order and 1 mark for correct spelling of the names.
8. Describe three qualities of a good rhythmic pattern. (3 marks)
   Answer: It has variation of note values, some repetition of a group of notes, and it ends on a long note.
   Why: 1 mark for each quality.
9. Compose a four-beat rhythm using a crotchet, a pair of quavers and a crotchet rest. Write it using rhythm names and say how you would clap it. (4 marks)
   Answer: For example: taa ta-te (rest) taa. Clap one long clap, two quick claps, keep silent on the third beat with hands open, then finish with one long clap. Any correct four-beat pattern using all three kinds is accepted.
   Why: 1 mark for four beats in total, 1 mark for using all three kinds, 1 mark for correct rhythm names, 1 mark for the explanation of how to clap it including the silent beat.
10. Name the four guide lines used for free hand lettering. (2 marks)
   Answer: Ascender line, top of small letters, baseline and descender line.
   Why: 2 marks for all four, 1 mark for two or three.
