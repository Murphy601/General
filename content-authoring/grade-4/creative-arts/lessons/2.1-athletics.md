## PAGE 1: Sprinting in athletics
Athletics is a group of sports that include running, jumping and throwing. Kenya is well known around the world for its runners. In this topic we learn about sprints. A sprint is a short race run at full speed from start to finish. In school we may sprint 50 metres. In big competitions, the sprints are 100 metres, 200 metres and 400 metres.

A sprinter needs two things: a quick start and good running form. A quick start means leaving the line fast when the race begins. Good form means running with the right posture, which is the way you hold your body.

### Warm up first
Sprinting uses many muscles at once. Cold muscles can get hurt. Always warm up for about five to ten minutes before you run.
1. Jog slowly around the field.
2. Swing your arms in big circles, forwards and then backwards.
3. Do gentle leg swings, holding on to a friend's shoulder.
4. Stretch your legs by touching your toes gently, without bouncing.
5. Run a few short, easy strides.

### Why start well?
In a short race, one second lost at the start is hard to win back. Practising the start helps you to move quickly, keep balance and learn control.

## PAGE 2: The commands and the medium start
Sprint races start with three signals from the starter. The starter is the person who begins the race.
1. On your marks: you go to the start line and get into position.
2. Set: you lift your hips slightly and stay still.
3. Go, or the sound of a whistle or a gun: you run.

Athletes must not move before the last signal. Moving too early is called a false start.

### The medium start
A medium start is a crouch start. It is used a lot in school. The sprinter is on one knee at the start line.

### How to do it
1. Stand with your toes just behind the start line.
2. Place your stronger foot, the one you would kick a ball with, as the front foot about one and a half foot lengths behind the line.
3. Kneel on the other knee, so that your rear knee is level with the middle of your front foot.
4. Put your hands on the ground just behind the line, shoulder-width apart, with your thumbs and fingers making a small arch.
5. Look down at the ground a short distance ahead, with your neck relaxed.
6. On set, lift your hips a little above your shoulders and lean forward, with weight on your hands.

## PAGE 3: The elongated start
The elongated start is like the medium start, but the feet are further apart. Elongated means stretched out and long. The back knee is placed further back, so the start position is longer.

### What is different
• The rear knee is level with the heel of the front foot, and not with the middle.
• The distance between the front foot and the start line is about the same.
• The push is a little longer and stronger, but it can feel less steady at first.

### Steps
1. Put your front foot about one and a half foot lengths behind the line.
2. Step the other foot back and kneel so that your knee is level with the heel of the front foot.
3. Put your hands behind the line, arms straight and shoulder-width apart.
4. On the set command, lift your hips a little higher than your shoulders.
5. On go, push hard with both legs. Drive your back leg forward first, and swing your arms.

### Which start is better?
Try both. Some learners feel more balanced in the medium start. Others get a stronger push from the elongated start. The right start is the one that lets you stay balanced and leave the line fast. Ask a classmate to watch your feet and tell you what they notice.

### Remember
Do not look up too soon, because this raises your body too fast. Come up gradually over the first few steps.

### Key points
• Head: keep it in line with your body, eyes looking ahead, not down at your feet.
• Body: lean forward a little, and keep your back straight.
• Arms: bend your elbows to about a right angle. Swing the arms forward and back, not across your chest, and keep your hands relaxed.
• Legs: lift your knees high and push off the ground strongly. Land on the front part of your foot, not the heel.
• Face and shoulders: stay relaxed. Tight shoulders make you slower.
• Finish: keep running through the finish line, and slow down gradually afterwards.

### Why posture matters
When you keep the right posture, your body moves forward in a straight line with no wasted effort. Leaning too far back acts like a brake. A bent body wastes energy and can hurt your back.

## PAGE 5: Practising safely and racing
Practise each part of sprinting separately, then join the parts together.

### Practice steps
1. Practise the medium start ten times, slowly. Focus on your foot and knee places.
2. Practise the elongated start ten times.
3. Run 20 metres from a start, focusing on lifting your knees and swinging your arms.
4. Run a full 50 metre race with a friend.
5. Ask a friend to watch and tell you one thing you did well.

### Safety rules
• Run only in your own lane, or the space given to you, so that you do not knock others.
• Check that the track is free of stones, holes and animals.
• Wear light clothes and shoes that fit and are tied.
• Stop at the finish gradually. Do not stop suddenly or turn around fast.
• Never push or trip another runner.
• Drink water after running and rest when tired.
• Tell your teacher at once if you feel pain.

### Sprint games
Play a game such as relay races. In a relay, a team of four runners each run a short distance, and pass on a baton to the next runner. Cheer for each other. The winner is not always the first runner but often the team who cooperate best.

## PAGE 6: Painting the Kenyan flag
The Kenyan flag flies at national athletics events. We can make our own flag to cheer with.

### The flag
The Kenyan flag has three big colours: black at the top, red in the middle and green at the bottom. Thin white stripes separate them. In the centre is a shield with two crossed spears. The shape of the flag is a rectangle about one and a half times as long as its height.

### What the colours mean
• Black stands for the people of Kenya.
• Red stands for the blood shed in the struggle for freedom.
• Green stands for the land and its natural wealth.
• White stands for peace and honesty.
• The shield and spears stand for the defence of freedom.

### Steps to paint your flag
1. Cut a piece of white paper or cloth in the ratio of three to two, for example 30 cm long and 20 cm high.
2. With a ruler, lightly draw the three main stripes and the thin white lines between them.
3. Sketch the shield and the crossed spears in the middle.
4. Paint black at the top, red in the middle and green at the bottom. Leave the white lines unpainted.
5. Paint the shield and spears, neatly and carefully.
6. Let it dry. Then glue or sew one edge to a stick as a handle.

## PAGE 7: Why we fly the Kenyan flag at athletics events
The national flag is a symbol of our country. A symbol is something that stands for an idea. When athletes carry the flag or the flag is raised at an athletics event, everyone sees the country they represent. Athletes feel proud, and supporters feel united.

### The value of flying the flag
• It shows patriotism, which is love for our country.
• It reminds us of our freedom and the people who worked for it.
• It brings people from different communities together to cheer for one team.
• It shows respect for Kenya to the visitors from other countries.

### Respect for the flag
Treat the flag with care. Do not let it touch the ground, do not write on it and do not use torn or dirty flags. When the national anthem is played and the flag is raised, stand up straight and be quiet.

### Cheering with the flag
At your class sprint races, wave your flags to cheer for all the runners, and not just your friends. Cheer kindly and do not tease anyone who comes last. Hold your flag high above the heads of others so that no one is hit by the stick, and keep well away from the track.

### Summary
Sprints are quick races. Good starts and good running form make a runner faster. The medium and elongated starts differ in where the back knee is placed. Always warm up, run in your lane and cheer for all runners.

## QUIZ
1. What are the three signals at the start of a sprint, in order?
   A) Set, go, on your marks
   B) On your marks, set, go
   C) Go, on your marks, set
   D) On your marks, go, set
   Answer: B
   Why: First you get to the line, then you lift your hips at set, and you run at go.
2. In an elongated start, where is the back knee?
   A) Level with the toes of the front foot
   B) Level with the heel of the front foot
   C) Far in front of the line
   D) Touching the front foot
   Answer: B
   Why: In the elongated start, the rear knee is placed level with the front heel, which is further back than in the medium start.
3. Which of these is good sprinting technique?
   A) Keeping arms straight and stiff
   B) Landing on the heels
   C) Bending elbows and lifting knees high
   D) Looking at your feet all the time
   Answer: C
   Why: Bent elbows swinging back and forth, and high knees, give a strong, fast stride.
4. What does the green colour on the Kenyan flag stand for?
   A) The people
   B) Blood shed for freedom
   C) Peace
   D) The land and its natural wealth
   Answer: D
   Why: Black is for the people, red for the blood shed for freedom, green for the land and white for peace.
5. Why do sprinters warm up before a race?
   A) To make their muscles ready and avoid injury
   B) To tire out other runners
   C) To let the starter rest
   D) To make the track smooth
   Answer: A
   Why: Warm muscles work better and are less likely to be hurt.
6. Describe how to do the medium start. (4 marks)
   Answer: Place the front foot about one and a half foot lengths behind the line. Kneel with the rear knee level with the middle of the front foot. Put both hands behind the line, shoulder-width apart. On set, raise the hips a little above the shoulders and lean forward.
   Why: 1 mark for the front foot, 1 mark for the knee position, 1 mark for the hands, 1 mark for the set position.
7. State three safety rules to follow during sprint races. (3 marks)
   Answer: Run only in your own lane; check the track for stones and holes; slow down gradually after the finish; never push or trip another runner.
   Why: 1 mark for each correct rule, up to 3 marks.
8. Explain why the Kenyan flag is flown at national athletics events. (2 marks)
   Answer: It is a symbol of the country and shows which nation the athletes represent. It makes people feel proud, united and patriotic.
   Why: 1 mark for the symbol of the country, 1 mark for feelings of pride or unity.
9. Explain why it is important to keep good posture during a sprint. (3 marks)
   Answer: Good posture keeps the body moving forward in a straight line, saves energy, gives balance and helps the runner to be fast. Leaning back or bending the body slows the runner and can cause pain or injury.
   Why: 1 mark for forward movement or balance, 1 mark for saving energy or speed, 1 mark for avoiding injury.
10. List the steps you would follow to paint a Kenyan flag on paper. (4 marks)
   Answer: Cut a rectangle of paper in the ratio three to two; draw the stripes lightly with a ruler; sketch the shield and spears; paint black at the top, red in the middle and green at the bottom, leaving white lines; let it dry and attach a handle.
   Why: 1 mark for measuring the rectangle, 1 mark for drawing and sketching, 1 mark for the correct colour order, 1 mark for drying and attaching the handle.
