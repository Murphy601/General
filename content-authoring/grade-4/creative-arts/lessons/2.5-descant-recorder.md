## PAGE 1: Meet the descant recorder
The descant recorder is a small wind instrument. It is played by blowing air into it. It is made of plastic or wood, and it has a sweet, clear and quite high sound. The word descant tells us that it plays the higher notes. Another name for it is the soprano recorder. Many schools use the descant recorder to teach children how to play a melody.

### Parts of the recorder
The recorder comes in three pieces that fit together. Each piece is called a joint.
• Head joint: the top piece. It has the mouthpiece, which you put between your lips, and the small opening where the air goes through.
• Middle joint: the middle piece. It has most of the finger holes.
• Foot joint: the bottom piece. It has the last finger holes and it ends in a wider opening called the bell.
• Finger holes: the holes that you cover with your fingers. There are seven on the front and one for your thumb at the back.

### How the sound is made
When you blow gently into the mouthpiece, air runs through a narrow channel and hits a sharp edge. This makes the air inside the recorder vibrate, and we hear a note. Covering more finger holes makes the vibrating air longer, and this gives a lower note. Uncovering holes makes the note higher.

## PAGE 2: Caring for your recorder
A recorder that is looked after can last many years. It also keeps the sound sweet. Because you blow into it, it can carry germs from your mouth, so hygiene matters a lot.

### Hygiene measures
• Wash your hands before you play.
• Never share your recorder with another person unless it has been cleaned first. Germs that cause colds and other illnesses can pass on through the mouthpiece.
• Do not eat or chew sweets while playing.
• After playing, wipe the outside of the mouthpiece with a clean, dry cloth.
• Clean the recorder from time to time. If it is made of plastic, your teacher may wash the joints in warm, soapy water, rinse them, and let them dry fully. Do not use very hot water, as it can bend plastic. Wooden recorders should not be soaked in water.
• Keep your own recorder marked with your name.

### Handling
• Hold it with both hands and do not drop it.
• When you put the joints together, twist them gently, and do not force them.
• Do not bite or chew the mouthpiece.
• Do not tap the recorder against things.

### Storage
• Put it in its bag or box after you finish.
• Keep it away from very hot places, such as near a fire or in direct sun, because heat can bend it.
• Do not put anything heavy on it.
• Keep it away from small children and from animals.

## PAGE 3: Playing techniques
Good playing begins with the right posture, hold and breath.

### Posture
Sit or stand tall with your back straight and your shoulders relaxed. Hold your head up. If you slouch, you cannot breathe well.

### Holding the recorder
1. Hold the recorder in front of you, pointing down at about a 45 degree angle away from your body.
2. Put your left hand at the top, and your right hand at the bottom. The left thumb covers the thumb hole at the back.
3. Your right thumb goes under the recorder to support it. It does not cover a hole.
4. Curve your fingers, and cover the finger holes with the soft pads of your fingers, not the tips. The holes must be fully covered, or you will hear a squeak.

### Blowing
1. Put the mouthpiece between your lips, not between your teeth. It should sit just inside the mouth.
2. Take a slow, deep breath.
3. Blow gently, as if you were blowing on hot tea to cool it. A soft, steady breath makes a warm tone.
4. Start each note by saying the sound too or doo quietly with your tongue. This is called tonguing, and it makes the notes clear and separate.

### Problems and fixes
• A squeaky, very high sound: you are blowing too hard. Blow more gently.
• A breathy, weak sound: a finger is not covering a hole, or your breath is too weak. Check your fingers.
• A muffled sound: your lips are covering the small opening. Move the mouthpiece a little.

### The fingerings
• B: cover the thumb hole and hole 1 (the first hole near the top, with your left index finger).
• A: cover the thumb hole and holes 1 and 2 (left index and middle fingers).
• G: cover the thumb hole and holes 1, 2 and 3 (left index, middle and ring fingers).

Notice the pattern. B uses the fewest fingers, and each note below adds one more finger. More fingers down means a longer column of air, and a lower sound. So B is the highest of the three and G is the lowest.

### Practise
1. Say the fingerings aloud as you place your fingers: B, thumb and one; A, thumb, one and two; G, thumb, one, two and three.
2. Play each note for four slow counts. Listen for a clear, steady tone.
3. Play B A G slowly, going down, and then G A B going up.

A special sign called the treble clef is written at the start of the staff. It tells us the names of the lines. With it, the second line is G and the third line is B, and A is the space between them.

### A link with sol-fa
If we call G doh, then A is ray and B is me. So the notes G A B on the recorder are the same as d r m in sol-fa. Any melody you sang with d r m can be played on the recorder with G, A and B.

## PAGE 6: Playing a simple melody
A melody on the recorder needs correct notes, correct rhythm and a steady beat. You can use the rhythm names you already know.

### Tune 1: three-note tune
This tune is the familiar song Hot Cross Buns. It uses only B, A and G.
Line 1: B A G (rest)
Line 2: B A G (rest)
Line 3: G G G G A A A A
Line 4: B A G (rest)
Lines 1, 2 and 4 are crotchets: taa taa taa, then a crotchet rest. Line 3 is made of pairs of quavers: ta-te ta-te ta-te ta-te. Each pair is one beat, so line 3 has four beats.

### Steps to learn a tune
1. Sing it in sol-fa first: m r d, m r d, d d d d r r r r, m r d.
2. Clap the rhythm and say the rhythm names.
3. Finger the notes without blowing, moving from note to note slowly.
4. Play very slowly, and make each note clear.
5. Speed up a little, keeping the beat steady. Do not hurry the rests.
6. Play it with a friend, and then in a group with one learner tapping the beat on a drum.

### Making your own tune
Make a four-beat tune using B, A and G. Write the notes and the rhythm names, for example B A G G. Play it many times until it is smooth. End on G, the lowest note, so that the tune sounds finished.

## PAGE 7: Performing and giving feedback
When you play for others, remember your posture, breathing and tone. Tone is the quality of a sound. A good tone is clear, warm and steady. It comes from gentle, steady breath.

### Before you play
1. Wash your hands and check that your recorder is clean and put together well.
2. Sit or stand tall. Hold the recorder correctly.
3. Count in silently, one two three four, so that the group starts together.

### Playing in a group
• Listen to the others as you play. If you are louder than the group, blow more softly.
• Keep looking at your music and at the conductor, who may be your teacher.
• Do not stop if you make a mistake. Carry on with the beat and get back in.

### Feedback
Watch your friends play and give feedback that is kind and helpful. Ask these questions.
• Were the notes B, A and G correct?
• Was the rhythm steady, with rests in the right places?
• Was the tone clear, without squeaks?
• Was the posture good?

Say one thing you liked, and then one thing to improve. When you receive feedback, listen quietly and try the advice.

### Summary
The descant recorder has a head joint, a middle joint and a foot joint, with finger holes. We keep it clean and safe, sit tall, blow gently and cover the holes fully. B, A and G use the left hand only, and with G as doh they match d r m.

## QUIZ
1. Which part of the recorder goes between your lips?
   A) Foot joint
   B) Bell
   C) Thumb hole
   D) Mouthpiece
   Answer: D
   Why: The mouthpiece is at the top of the head joint, and you blow into it.
2. Which fingers cover holes when you play G?
   A) The thumb and hole 1 only
   B) The thumb and holes 1 and 2
   C) The thumb and holes 1, 2 and 3
   D) No holes are covered
   Answer: C
   Why: G is the lowest of the three notes, so the thumb hole and holes 1, 2 and 3 are covered.
3. What does a squeaky, very high sound usually mean?
   A) You are blowing too hard
   B) The recorder is too clean
   C) You are playing very softly
   D) The recorder is too long
   Answer: A
   Why: Blowing too hard makes the recorder squeak. A soft, steady breath gives a good tone.
4. Why should you not share a recorder without cleaning it?
   A) It will grow bigger
   B) Germs from the mouth can spread illnesses
   C) It will play louder
   D) It will change colour
   Answer: B
   Why: Saliva on the mouthpiece can carry germs, and sharing can spread diseases such as colds.
5. Which of these is the best way to store a recorder?
   A) In its bag or case, away from heat
   B) In the sun on a windowsill
   C) In water
   D) Under a heavy book
   Answer: A
   Why: A bag protects it from dust and knocks, and heat can bend it.
6. Name the three joints of a descant recorder. (3 marks)
   Answer: The head joint, the middle joint and the foot joint.
   Why: 1 mark for each correct joint.
7. Describe how you should hold the recorder and place your fingers. (4 marks)
   Answer: Hold the recorder pointing down and away from the body. The left hand is at the top and the right hand at the bottom. The left thumb covers the thumb hole. Use the soft pads of curved fingers to cover the holes completely.
   Why: 1 mark for the angle, 1 mark for the left hand above the right, 1 mark for the thumb, 1 mark for the pads and covering holes fully.
8. Explain why the note gets lower when more finger holes are covered. (2 marks)
   Answer: Covering more holes makes the column of vibrating air longer inside the recorder, and a longer column gives a lower note.
   Why: 1 mark for a longer column of air and 1 mark for lower pitch.
9. State three ways of caring for your recorder. (3 marks)
   Answer: Wipe the mouthpiece after use, keep it in a bag, and keep it away from heat. Other correct answers such as not sharing, not biting it and washing hands are accepted.
   Why: 1 mark for each correct way.
10. Explain how to produce a good tone on the descant recorder. (3 marks)
   Answer: Sit tall, blow a gentle steady breath, and cover the holes fully with the finger pads. Do not blow too hard.
   Why: 1 mark for good posture, 1 mark for gentle steady breath, 1 mark for fully covered holes.
