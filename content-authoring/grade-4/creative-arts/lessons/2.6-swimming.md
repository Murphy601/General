## PAGE 1: Swimming as a life skill and pool safety
Swimming is moving through water by using your arms and legs. It is fun and it keeps your body fit. It is also a life skill. A person who can swim and float is much safer near a river, a lake, the ocean or a swimming pool. Every year, many people drown in Kenya, and learning to swim helps to prevent this.

Before you enter the water, you must know the safety rules. Water can be dangerous if we are careless.

### Safety rules for the pool
• Swim only when a teacher or a lifeguard is watching you.
• Never swim alone.
• Walk on the pool side. Wet floors are slippery.
• Stay in the shallow end until your teacher says you can go deeper.
• Do not push, dunk or jump on others.
• Do not dive into shallow water. You can hit the bottom and hurt your head or neck.
• Do not eat or chew sweets in the pool.
• Listen to the whistle and follow instructions at once.
• If you are in trouble, raise your arm and shout for help.

### Getting ready
1. Wear a proper swimming costume and, if you have long hair, a swimming cap.
2. Shower or wash with clean water before entering.
3. Do a short warm-up: swing your arms, stretch your legs and jog gently.
4. Listen to the teacher's instructions before entering the water.

### Orientation
When you first visit the pool, walk around the edge with your teacher. Look at the shallow end, the deep end, the steps and the ladders. Ask where the rescue equipment is kept.

### Ways to enter
1. By the steps or ladder: hold the rails with both hands and walk down feet first, one step at a time, facing the steps.
2. Sit and slide: sit on the edge of the pool with your feet in the water. Put both hands on the edge beside your hips. Turn your body to face the wall, and slide gently into the water, holding the edge.
3. Step or jump in: in water that is deep enough, and only when your teacher allows it, step off the edge feet first. Keep your arms out for balance and bend your knees on landing.

### Points to remember
• Always look before you enter. Make sure nobody is below you.
• Enter feet first. Never enter head first unless you have been trained.
• Keep one hand on the wall until you are steady.
• Move away from the edge so that the next person can enter.

## PAGE 3: Water games
Water games help you to feel comfortable in the water. They are fun, and they teach you to breathe, move and stay calm. Play them only in shallow water with your teacher.

### Games to play
• Bubble blowing: put your mouth in the water and blow bubbles slowly. Then put your nose and mouth in and blow out through both. Breathe in above the water, and try to blow ten bubbles.
• Follow the leader: line up and walk, hop and jump in the water behind a leader who changes the movement.
• Walk through the tunnel: one team stands with legs apart and the others pass under, holding their breath.
• Treasure hunt: throw small plastic rings or sinking toys into the shallow water. Take turns to pick them up from the bottom.
• Circle walk: hold hands in a circle and walk one way, then the other way. Then all crouch and put shoulders under water together.
• Water tag: one player is it and tries to touch others, walking fast but not running. No pushing.

### Rules of the games
1. Only play when your teacher says so.
2. Do not hold anyone under water.
3. Take turns and let everyone join.
4. Stop at once when you hear the whistle.

### Enjoying it
Water games help shy learners to get used to water. Encourage friends who are afraid, and never laugh at them. Everyone starts somewhere.

## PAGE 4: Floating
Floating means staying at the top of the water without swimming. Your body can float because water pushes up on it. When you relax, your lungs are full of air, and you float more easily. Floating helps you rest in water and teaches confidence.

### Mushroom float
1. Stand in shallow water, take a deep breath and put your face in.
2. Bend your knees and hug them to your chest with both arms. Tuck in your chin.
3. Your back will rise to the surface, like the top of a mushroom. Hold for three to five seconds.
4. To stand, put your feet down gently and lift your head.

### Prone float
Prone means face down. Hold the wall, take a breath, put your face in and stretch your arms forward. Push gently off the wall or the bottom, and keep your body long and relaxed. Keep your tummy soft. Do not lift your head.

### Supine float
Supine means face up. Lie back with your arms stretched out and your ears in the water. Look at the sky. Push your tummy up gently, keep your legs together and breathe slowly. Your teacher or a friend can hold your back at first.

### Practise safely
Practise floating only in shallow water where you can stand, with your teacher watching. Stay calm. If you get tired, stand up and rest.

## PAGE 5: Gliding
Gliding means moving through the water without kicking or pulling, after a push from the wall. It is done in a streamlined body shape, a long thin shape that slips through water easily. Gliding shows how a good body shape helps you move.

### Steps for the glide
1. Stand in shallow water with your back to the wall, and your arms stretched above your head, palms together.
2. Put one foot on the wall behind you, and then the other. Bend your knees.
3. Take a breath and put your face in the water, with your head between your arms.
4. Push off gently with your legs. Keep your body straight and your toes pointed.
5. Hold the position until you slow down, and then put your feet down and stand up.

### Common mistakes
• Lifting the head: this makes your legs sink.
• Bent arms or legs: they slow you down.
• Pushing too hard from the wall: you may lose control. A gentle push is enough.

### Practise
Try to glide farther each time. Mark where each learner stops, and see who improves by the end of the week. Compare your glide with a friend's, and talk about what helped.

## PAGE 6: Feedback, hygiene and respect
When you finish, take a moment to review your progress. Watch each other perform the water entry, the float and the glide, and give feedback in a kind way.

### Questions for feedback
• Did the entry follow the safe steps, feet first?
• Was the body relaxed and long during the float?
• Was the glide straight, with arms stretched and face in the water?
• Did the swimmer follow safety rules and listen to the teacher?

Start with what was good. Then suggest one thing to do better. Encourage each other, since it takes time to learn to swim.

### Pool hygiene
• Shower before and after swimming.
• Do not spit or pass urine in the pool. This keeps the water clean for everyone.
• Do not drink the pool water.
• Dry yourself well afterwards and dress in dry clothes. Wash your costume and hang it to dry.
• Keep the pool area tidy and put litter in the bin.

### Water safety outside the pool
The skills you learn in the pool help you in other water places, but rivers, dams and the sea are much more dangerous. Water can be deep or fast. Never go in without a trusted adult who can swim. If someone is drowning, do not jump in to rescue them. Shout for an adult and throw something that floats, such as a jerrycan with a lid, for them to hold.

### Summary
Swimming is an essential life skill. We enter feet first, play games to get used to water, learn to float in mushroom, prone and supine positions, and glide in a streamlined shape. Safety comes first, in and out of the pool.

## QUIZ
1. How should you enter the swimming pool at first?
   A) Head first from the edge
   B) Feet first with the teacher watching
   C) By running and jumping
   D) By pushing a friend in
   Answer: B
   Why: Feet-first entry is safe, because you cannot hit your head on the bottom.
2. Which float is done face down?
   A) Supine
   B) Prone
   C) Both are face down
   D) Neither is face down
   Answer: B
   Why: Prone means face down, and supine means face up.
3. What is the mushroom float?
   A) A float where you hug your knees and your back rises to the surface
   B) A float on your back with your arms out
   C) A float with your feet on the pool floor
   D) A swimming race
   Answer: A
   Why: In the mushroom float the body is curled up, with the back at the top like a mushroom.
4. What should your body be like during a glide?
   A) Curled in a ball
   B) Straight and long, in a streamlined shape
   C) Sitting upright
   D) Kicking hard
   Answer: B
   Why: A long, straight shape passes through the water easily.
5. Why do we shower before entering the pool?
   A) To get wet
   B) To make the water warmer
   C) To keep the pool clean
   D) To learn to float
   Answer: C
   Why: Washing removes dirt and sweat, so the water stays clean for everyone.

   Why: 1 mark for each correct rule.

8. Explain the steps of a glide. (4 marks)
   Answer: Stand with the back to the wall and arms stretched above the head. Put the feet on the wall and bend the knees. Take a breath and put the face in with the head between the arms. Push off gently, keep the body straight and glide.
   Why: 1 mark for arms stretched, 1 mark for the feet on the wall, 1 mark for the breath and face in, 1 mark for the push and straight body.
9. Explain why swimming is an essential life skill. (2 marks)
   Answer: It helps a person to stay safe near water and reduces the risk of drowning. It also keeps the body fit.
   Why: 1 mark for safety or drowning prevention and 1 mark for one other benefit.
