## PAGE 1: What is photography?
Photography is the art of taking pictures with a camera. A picture taken with a camera is called a photograph, or a photo for short. Photographs capture moments so that we can look at them later: a school trip, a family celebration or a baby's first steps.

Long ago, cameras used film that had to be developed with chemicals. Today most photographs are taken with digital devices. A digital device stores pictures as numbers in its memory, so we can see them at once on a screen, keep hundreds of them and delete the ones we do not like.

## PAGE 2: Parts, care and safe use
### Parts of a digital device for photography
• Lens: the glass eye that lets light in and shows the picture. Keep it clean.
• Screen: shows what you are about to photograph and the pictures you have taken.
• Button: a button on the body or on the screen that you press to take the photo.
• Flash: a small light that flashes in dark places.
• Memory: the place inside where the photos are stored.
• Battery: gives the power. A device with a flat battery will not work.

### Caring for the device
1. Hold the device with both hands, and use a strap if there is one, so that it does not drop.
2. Clean the lens gently with a soft, dry cloth. Do not touch it with your fingers.
3. Keep it away from water, dust, sand and hot sun.
4. Switch it off and put it in its case when you finish.
5. Charge the battery as your teacher shows you.

### Using the device responsibly
• Ask permission before taking a photograph of someone. Some people do not want their photograph taken.
• Never take photographs in private places such as toilets or changing rooms.
• Do not share photographs of others without asking them and an adult.
• Use the device only for the task set, and only while an adult knows what you are doing.

Being polite and honest is a part of being a good photographer.

## PAGE 3: The element of light
Light is the most important element in a photograph. The word photography actually comes from words that mean drawing with light. Without enough light, the picture is dark and dull. With too much or the wrong kind of light, the picture can be too bright or full of harsh shadows.

### Good lighting
• Outdoors, natural sunlight is best. Early morning and late afternoon give soft, gentle light.
• Stand so that the light falls on the front of your subject. The subject is the person or thing you are photographing.
• Indoors, stand near a window or an open door so that daylight falls on the subject.
• The flash can help in dark rooms, but do not stand too close because it may be too bright.

### Poor lighting
• Do not point the camera directly at the sun. It can hurt your eyes and it will spoil the picture.
• Avoid taking a photo with the bright sky or a bright window behind your subject. The subject becomes dark, and you cannot see the face.
• Strong midday sun can cast dark shadows on faces.

## PAGE 4: The centre of interest
The centre of interest is the main thing in a photograph that you want people to look at first. It might be a friend's smiling face, a flower, a football or the school gate. A photograph with a clear centre of interest tells the viewer what it is about.

### How to make a clear centre of interest
• Decide what your main subject is before you press the button.
• Move closer, or use the zoom, so that the subject fills a good part of the picture.
• Look behind the subject. Remove things that distract, such as a bin, a stranger or a bright object.
• Place the subject in the middle, or slightly to one side. Make sure it is sharp and not blurred.
• When photographing a child or an animal, bend down to their eye level.

## PAGE 5: Taking photographs step by step
Now you can take your own photographs. Work in a group of three or four and take turns, so that everyone has a chance to use the device.

### Before shooting
1. Ask your teacher for a device and check that its battery is charged and its memory has space.
2. Wipe the lens gently with a soft, dry cloth.
3. Decide what you will photograph: a portrait of a friend, a plant, a classroom object or a view of the school compound.

### Taking the photo
1. Check the light. Where is the sun? Is the subject well lit?
2. Choose the centre of interest. What is the most important thing?
3. Hold the device with both hands and keep your elbows close to your body to stay steady.
4. Look at the screen. Move closer or farther away until the subject looks right. Tap on the subject on the screen if the device lets you, so that it is sharp.
5. Hold your breath for a second and press the button gently. Do not shake.
6. Check the photograph. If it is dark, blurred or the subject is too small, take it again.

### Ideas to try
• A portrait of yourself or a friend against a plain wall.
• A close-up of a leaf, a flower or a stone.
• A photograph of a school building from a low angle.

## PAGE 6: Selecting and talking about photographs
After taking many photos, we choose the best ones. This is called selecting. Keep only the good photographs and delete the poor ones, so that the memory does not fill up.

### Questions for selecting a photograph
• Is there enough light? Can you see the subject clearly?
• Is there a clear centre of interest?
• Is the picture sharp, and not blurred?
• Is there anything distracting at the edges or in the background?
• Does the picture make you feel or think something?

### Sharing and feedback
Show your best photograph to the class and explain how you took it. Where was the light coming from? What was your centre of interest? Listen to feedback from your friends. Use polite words. Say what you like first. Then suggest one improvement, such as getting closer or moving to better light.

### Respect and responsibility
Only share pictures when everyone in them agrees. Return the device to the teacher in good condition, and thank the group.

### Summary
Photographers use digital devices such as a smartphone, tablet or camera. Good light and a clear centre of interest make a good photograph. We take care of the device, ask permission and keep safe.

## QUIZ
1. Which one is a digital device for taking photographs?
   A) A smartphone
   B) A ruler
   C) A pair of scissors
   D) A drum
   Answer: A
   Why: A smartphone has a camera lens and stores photographs digitally.
2. What is the part of a camera that lets light in called?
   A) The strap
   B) The battery
   C) The lens
   D) The case
   Answer: C
   Why: The lens lets light in so that the picture can be formed.
3. Where should the sun be for a well-lit photo of a person?
   A) Behind the person
   B) Behind the photographer
   C) In front of the camera lens
   D) It does not matter
   Answer: B
   Why: When the sun is behind the photographer, light falls on the front of the subject.
4. What is a centre of interest?
   A) The middle of the room
   B) The main subject that the viewer should look at
   C) The button used to take the photo
   D) The battery in the camera
   Answer: B
   Why: The centre of interest is the main thing in the photograph that draws the eye.
5. What should you do first before taking a photograph of a classmate?
   A) Ask for permission
   B) Use the flash
   C) Run away
   D) Delete other photos
   Answer: A
   Why: It is polite and safe to ask permission before photographing anyone.
6. Give two ways of caring for a digital camera or tablet. (2 marks)
   Answer: Hold it with two hands and use a strap; clean the lens with a soft dry cloth; keep it away from water and dust. Any two.
   Why: 1 mark for each correct way.
7. Explain why you should not point a camera towards the sun. (2 marks)
   Answer: The sun can hurt your eyes, and the very bright light spoils the photograph by making the subject dark.
   Why: 1 mark for danger to the eyes and 1 mark for a spoiled picture.
8. List three ways to make a clear centre of interest. (3 marks)
   Answer: Move closer to the subject; remove distracting things in the background; place the subject in the middle or slightly to the side; keep it sharp; bend to eye level for children. Any three.
   Why: 1 mark for each correct way.
9. Describe how to choose the best photograph from several that you have taken. (3 marks)
   Answer: Check that there is enough light, that the subject is clear and sharp, and that the centre of interest is easy to see. Keep the best and delete the poor ones.
   Why: 1 mark for light, 1 mark for sharpness or clarity, 1 mark for centre of interest or deleting poor photographs.
10. Why is light important in photography? (3 marks)
   Answer: Light lets the camera record the picture. With good light the subject can be seen clearly. With poor light the picture is too dark, or the face is in shadow.
   Why: 1 mark for light recording the picture, 1 mark for clear subject with good light, 1 mark for the effect of poor light.
