## PAGE 1: Different kinds of songs
People sing for many reasons. Some songs praise God, some show love for our country and some teach us about things happening around us. Listening carefully to a song tells us what kind of song it is and what message it carries.

### Sacred songs
Sacred songs are songs of worship. They praise God, give thanks, ask for help or teach about faith. Hymns and choruses sung in church, mosque songs and prayer songs are examples. They are usually sung in a calm, respectful way.

### Patriotic songs
Patriotic songs show love for one's country. They talk about the beauty of the land, unity, peace, freedom and hard work. The Kenya National Anthem is our best known patriotic song. We sing patriotic songs at school assemblies and national days.

### Topical songs
Topical songs are about a topic that matters at a certain time, such as keeping the environment clean, road safety, washing hands, saving water or going to school. They give a message in a form that is easy to remember.

### Talk about the message and mood
Every song has a message, which is the main idea that the singers want you to hear. It also has a mood, which is the feeling it gives, such as joy, calm, sadness or pride. Before you sing, ask: What is this song about? How should it feel? The way you sing should show the message and mood.

## PAGE 2: Accurate singing and performance directions
To sing well, you must sing accurately.

### Accuracy in pitch
Pitch is how high or low a note is. Singing with accurate pitch means singing the right notes, not too high or too low. Listen to the teacher or a recording, then hum the tune softly before singing the words.

### Accuracy in rhythm
Rhythm is the pattern of long and short sounds. Sing every word at the right time, and keep together with the beat. Clap the rhythm of the words first if a line is hard.

### Performance directions
A composer writes directions to tell us how to perform a song. Two important ones are tempo and dynamics.
• Tempo means the speed of the music. It can be fast, moderate or slow.
• Dynamics mean how loud or soft the music is. Soft is written as p, and loud as f. A sign that grows wider, like an opening mouth, means getting louder. A sign that closes means getting softer.

## PAGE 3: Performance techniques and etiquette
A good performance needs more than the right notes. It needs a good voice and a good presence.

### Posture
Stand tall with feet slightly apart, shoulders relaxed and head up. Hold your song sheet, if you have one, at chest level. When you sit, sit upright and away from the back of the chair.

### Breathing
Take a deep breath through your nose and mouth so that your tummy moves out gently. Sing out on the breath. Do not shout, as this hurts your voice.

### Diction
Diction means saying the words clearly. Open your mouth well and pronounce every word and ending. Then the audience can understand the message.

### Gestures and facial expressions
Gestures are hand and body movements that fit the song. A gesture such as raising your hands for praise or waving for greeting helps to tell the story. Facial expressions show the mood: smile for happy songs, and a calm, serious face for sad or prayerful songs. Look at your audience.

### Etiquette
Etiquette means good manners in performing.
• Walk on and off the stage quietly.
• Listen to the leader, and start and stop together.
• Do not push, talk or laugh during a performance.
• Take a small bow when you finish, and clap for others.
• As an audience member, be silent and clap at the right time.

## PAGE 4: Singing in unison and the East African Community anthem
To sing in unison means that everyone sings the same notes at the same time. There are no different parts. Unison singing is simple, strong and good for anthems, because everyone can join in.

### The East African Community
The East African Community is a group of countries in East Africa that work together on trade, travel, peace and other shared matters. It has its own anthem. An anthem is a special song that represents a country or a group of countries.

### Learning the anthem
1. Listen to a live or recorded performance of the anthem several times.
2. Read the words slowly with your teacher, line by line. Talk about what they mean and the message they give about unity and peace.
3. Clap the rhythm of the words.
4. Sing one line at a time after the teacher, listening carefully for pitch.
5. Put the lines together and sing the whole song slowly.
6. Sing in unison, together, at the same speed and volume.

### How to stand and behave
An anthem is sung standing straight and quietly, with respect. Keep your face calm and serious, and avoid talking, playing or moving around. Sing clearly so that your voice joins others to make one strong voice.

### Why it matters
Singing the anthem shows that we care about neighbours and that we want to live in peace with them. Singing it with pride helps us to feel part of a bigger family.

## PAGE 5: Kenyan folk songs and their types
A folk song is a traditional song that belongs to a community. It is passed on from parents and elders to children by singing, not by writing. Each Kenyan community has its own folk songs in its own language and style.

To identify a folk song, we give its name and the community it comes from. We can also say the occasion when it is sung, which tells us its type.

### Types and what they do
• Work songs: sung while working, such as farming, fetching water or grinding grain. They give the workers a rhythm, and make the work seem lighter.
• Funeral songs: sung when someone has died. They comfort the family, remember the person and are usually slow and sad.
• Naming songs: sung when a baby is given a name. They welcome the child and are usually joyful.
• Initiation songs: sung when young people move from childhood to adulthood. They teach courage and community values.
• Marriage songs: sung at weddings. They praise the couple and give them advice and good wishes.
• Worship songs: sung to praise or pray to God.

## PAGE 6: Performing a folk song with roles
A folk song performance often has many people, and each has a role.
• Soloist: one person who sings the lead line, or the verse. The soloist often starts the song.
• Chorus or singers: the group that sings the answer or the repeated part. This pattern is called call and response.
• Instrumentalists: people who play instruments such as drums and shakers to keep the beat.
• Dancers: people who move to the music when the community's style includes dancing.

### Steps to perform
1. Choose a folk song from your community, or one taught by your teacher. Talk about the community it belongs to, its type and its message.
2. Share the roles: soloist, singers and instrumentalists. Let everyone have a turn over several practices.
3. Practise with accurate pitch and rhythm. Decide on tempo and dynamics.
4. Add gestures and facial expressions that show the message.
5. Perform for the class. Sing clearly, respect each other's roles and take a bow.

### Using your own language
Many folk songs are in the languages of the communities, such as Kikuyu, Luo, Luhya, Kamba, Kalenjin and Kiswahili. Sing in the original language if you can, and listen with respect when others sing in theirs. If you do not know what the words mean, ask an elder or a friend from that community to explain.

### Respect and value
Folk songs carry the history, feelings and wisdom of a people. When we sing songs from different communities, we learn about each other and become closer as Kenyans. Never laugh at a song because it is not from your own community.

### Posture check
Before you sing, check your posture. Stand tall, feet slightly apart, shoulders relaxed and head up, with your eyes on the audience.

## QUIZ
1. A song that praises God is called
   A) a patriotic song
   B) a sacred song
   C) a topical song
   D) a work song
   Answer: B
   Why: Sacred songs are songs of worship that praise God or ask for his help.
2. What does the sign f mean in a song?
   A) Fast
   B) Finish
   C) Loud
   D) Soft
   Answer: C
   Why: The sign f stands for loud, and p stands for soft.
3. What does singing in unison mean?
   A) Everyone sings different notes
   B) One person sings alone
   C) Everyone hums quietly
   D) Everyone sings the same notes together
   Answer: D
   Why: In unison, all the singers sing the same notes at the same time.
4. Which song is normally sung when a baby is given a name?
   A) A funeral song
   B) A naming song
   C) A work song
   D) An initiation song
   Answer: B
   Why: Naming songs welcome the baby and are sung at the naming ceremony.
5. What does diction mean?
   A) Singing loudly
   B) Walking on to the stage
   C) Clapping the rhythm
   D) Saying the words clearly
   Answer: D
   Why: Good diction means pronouncing all the words clearly so that the audience can understand.
6. Explain the difference between tempo and dynamics. (2 marks)
   Answer: Tempo is the speed of the music, fast or slow. Dynamics are how loud or soft the music is.
   Why: 1 mark for each correct meaning.
7. Name three of the six types of Kenyan folk songs and say when each is sung. (3 marks)
   Answer: For example, work songs are sung during work, funeral songs when someone has died, and marriage songs at a wedding. Other correct answers are accepted.
   Why: 1 mark for each correct type with its occasion.
8. State four good performance techniques for singers. (4 marks)
   Answer: Stand tall with good posture, breathe deeply, sing words clearly with good diction, and use gestures and facial expressions that fit the song. Other correct answers such as good etiquette are accepted.
   Why: 1 mark for each correct technique.
9. Describe how you would learn to sing the East African Community anthem in unison. (3 marks)
   Answer: Listen to it several times, read and understand the words with the teacher, then practise line by line before singing the whole song together at the same speed and volume.
   Why: 1 mark for listening, 1 mark for learning the words, 1 mark for practising line by line and singing together.
10. List the roles that people can play in a folk song performance. (3 marks)
   Answer: A soloist who leads, a chorus who answer, instrumentalists who play drums or shakers, and dancers.
   Why: 1 mark for each of three correct roles.
