## PAGE 1: What is appreciation in Creative Arts?
To appreciate something means to notice it, understand it and value it. When you appreciate a painting, a song, a photograph or a sports performance, you look or listen carefully, think about it and say what you feel and learn from it. Appreciation is not just saying that something is nice. It is giving reasons.

Creative Arts includes drawings, paintings, montages, models, photographs, songs, music, dance and sports. All of them are made or performed by people who want to say something or to show a skill. We learn a lot by studying and analysing them. To analyse means to look closely at the parts of something to understand how it works.

### Four things to think about
1. Aesthetic: how beautiful or pleasing it is. For a picture, look at the colours, shapes and tones. For music, listen to the tune and rhythm. Aesthetic is about what is pleasant to see or hear.
2. Skills: what the artist has done well. Did they use smudging? Did they mix colours well? Did they sing in tune, keep the beat or balance well?
3. Message: what the artist or performer wants us to learn or feel. A song may urge us to keep the environment clean. A painting of a flag may show love for our country.
4. Behaviour: how we should behave as an audience or a viewer. We are quiet and respectful, and we do not laugh at others' work.

### Why appreciate?
Appreciation helps us to learn from others, to improve our own work and to respect people from different backgrounds. Every community has its own art, and each one is valuable.

## PAGE 2: How to analyse and give feedback
Analysing a work of art is a step by step process.

### Steps
1. Look or listen without speaking. Take your time.
2. Describe what you see or hear. For example: I see a drum drawn with dark and light tones.
3. Say what is skilful. For example: the smudging makes the drum look round.
4. Say what the message or mood is. For example: the song is joyful and invites us to dance.
5. Say what you would improve. For example: the background could be darker to show the drum more clearly.

### Giving feedback
Feedback is what you say to someone about their work to help them. Good feedback is:
• Kind: use polite, gentle words. Do not laugh or insult.
• Honest: say what you really think, but with care.
• Specific: point to a particular thing. Do not just say nice or bad.
• Helpful: give a suggestion for the next time.

A good way is to say two good things and one idea to improve. This is sometimes called two stars and a wish. For example: Two stars: I like your bright colours and your neat cutting. A wish: I wish you would overlap the pictures more so that there are no gaps.

### Receiving feedback
When others comment on your work, listen quietly. Do not argue. Say thank you and think about the ideas. Feedback helps you to grow.

## PAGE 3: Making a portfolio
A portfolio is a folder in which we keep our creative works together. It protects them and helps us to show them to other people. There are two kinds.
• Working portfolio: it holds work in progress, such as sketches, trials and drafts. It shows how your work developed.
• Display portfolio: it holds your best finished works for showing to teachers, parents and visitors.

### Steps for making a portfolio
1. Get two large pieces of manila paper or cardboard, a little bigger than your artwork.
2. Place them together and fold in the middle to form a folder. Trim the edges to make them even.
3. Make two holes in the open edges with a punch or a pencil, and thread a piece of string or ribbon to use as a tie.
4. Decorate the cover by pasting coloured paper or pieces of fabric in a pattern. Do not use too many colours, and keep the edges neat.
5. Write your name, class and the title, for example My Creative Arts Portfolio, on a label and paste it on the cover.
6. Put your works inside. Write the date on each one and add a short note about what it is and how you made it.

## PAGE 4: Presenting and caring for displayed works
### Presenting your artwork
When you present a work from your portfolio, you show it and explain it to others.
1. Hold the work carefully by the edges.
2. Say the title and what it is: a drawing, a painting or a montage.
3. Say what materials and skills you used, such as charcoal and smudging.
4. Explain the idea or message.
5. Say what you liked most, and what you would change.
6. Invite questions and listen to feedback politely.

### Displaying work
A display is a place where works are shown for others to see. A classroom wall, a table or a notice board are good places. Each work should have a label with the title and the artist's name.

### Caring for displayed works
• Do not touch works unless allowed. Fingers can spoil paint and charcoal.
• Do not lean on tables or walls where works are displayed.
• Keep works away from water, direct sun and dust.
• Handle the edges of pictures gently, and use tape or pins carefully.
• Return the works to the portfolio afterwards, and store the portfolio flat in a dry place.
• Do not write on or tear someone's work.

### Behaviour as a viewer
Walk quietly around the display. Look, think and speak softly. Say only kind and helpful things. Respect every child's work equally.

## PAGE 5: The Kenya National Anthem
An anthem is a special song that represents a country. The Kenya National Anthem is called Ee Mungu Nguvu Yetu, which means O God of all creation in the English version. It is often sung in Kiswahili.

### Role of the anthem
• It is a national symbol, like the flag. It stands for the whole nation.
• It unites Kenyans from all communities.
• It shows our love and respect for our country.
• It reminds us of our values and our promises to each other.

### The three verses
The anthem has three verses. Each verse has its own message.

### Values in the anthem
Unity, peace, justice, hard work, service, patriotism and thankfulness to God.

### Occasions
The anthem is sung at school assemblies, on national days such as Madaraka Day, Mashujaa Day and Jamhuri Day, at sports events and at other official ceremonies. Depending on the occasion, one verse or all three verses may be sung.

### How to behave
Stand straight and still and face the flag if there is one. Remove your cap or hat. Sing clearly and with respect. Do not talk, play or move about.

## PAGE 6: Analysing a Kenyan folk song
When you listen to or watch a folk song, you can analyse it by asking questions about its components, or parts.

### Components of a folk song
1. Community of origin: which community does the song come from?
2. Type or occasion: is it a work song, funeral song, naming song, initiation song, marriage song or worship song?
3. Message: what does the song say or teach? What is the mood?
4. Participants and their roles:
• Soloist: one singer who leads.
• Singers or chorus: a group who answer and repeat.
• Instrumentalists: people who play the instruments.
5. Instruments: which instruments are played, such as drums, shakers or rattles?

### Using the right words
Use correct words when you describe a performance. Say soloist, chorus, call and response, tempo (speed), dynamics (loud or soft), pitch (high or low) and rhythm. For example: The soloist sang the call and the chorus answered in a fast tempo, with loud drums.

### Practise
Listen to or watch a folk song performed live or recorded. Fill in the five components in a short table. Compare your answers with a friend's, and note anything you missed.

## PAGE 7: Appreciating games and sports
Games and sports are also part of Creative Arts. In this course, you have learnt netball, athletics, gymnastics, and either swimming or indigenous animal games. We take part in them for fun, health and friendship. Some schools also hold inter-class or inter-school competitions.

### What to look for when you watch or take part
• Teamwork: do the players pass, help and encourage each other?
• Skill: is the passing accurate, is the sprint start quick, is the balance steady, is the glide straight?
• Safety: do players follow the rules and look after themselves and others?
• Sportsmanship: do the players play fairly, respect the referee and accept the result?

### Sportsmanship
Sportsmanship means being fair and kind in sports. A good sportsperson:
• Follows the rules, even when nobody is watching.
• Respects the umpire, the referee and the teacher.
• Congratulates the winners and encourages the losers.
• Does not cheat, bully or tease.
• Shakes hands and thanks the other team.

### Analysing a sports performance
After a mini-game or race, sit in a group and discuss. Say one skill that you saw done well, such as a catch in netball. Say one way the team can improve. Remember to talk about your own performance too.

### Summary
We appreciate Creative Arts works by looking at beauty, skill and message, and by behaving respectfully. We keep works in a portfolio, care for them and give kind feedback. We respect the Kenya National Anthem and folk songs from all communities, and play sports fairly and safely.

   Answer: B
   Why: Good feedback is kind, specific and helpful, and it includes a suggestion.

   A) One
   B) Two

   Answer: C
