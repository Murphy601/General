## PAGE 1: Hearing and listening are not the same
Hearing happens by itself. If a matatu hoots outside, you hear it whether you want to or not. Listening is different. Listening means you choose to give your ears, your eyes and your mind to the speaker. A pupil who listens attentively can repeat what was said, follow the instructions and answer questions afterwards.

In this course you will use your own mother tongue, the language you speak at home. Your teacher will guide you on which language your class uses. The lessons are written in English so that every class can use them, and then you apply each idea to your own language.

### How to be an attentive listener
• Face the speaker and keep your eyes on them.
• Sit still and keep your hands and feet quiet.
• Do not talk while someone else is speaking.
• Think about the words. Ask yourself what the speaker wants you to know.
• Wait for your turn before you ask a question.

### Try it
Your teacher will say four short sentences about a cultural food. Do not write anything down. Then say what you remember. Notice how much more you remember when you listen carefully.

## PAGE 2: Nouns, the naming words
A noun is a word that names a person, an animal, a place or a thing. In a song about cultural foods you may hear the names of foods such as maize, beans, millet, sweet potato, banana and mango. All of these are nouns.

When we listen to a song, we can pick out the nouns. A good way is to ask, "Which words are names of things I can see, touch, eat or count?" Sing or say the song slowly. Every time you hear a name, tap the desk once.

### Nouns in an English food song
Imagine this short song:
"We cook the beans, we roast the maize, we boil the potatoes, we share the food."
The nouns are beans, maize, potatoes and food.

### Activity
Work with a partner. Recall one cultural food song from home or from your community. Say it slowly, one line at a time. List every noun you hear on a chart. Then find out how each noun is said in your mother tongue and write it next to the English word if your teacher allows it.

## PAGE 3: Singular and plural
Singular means one. Plural means more than one. One mango is singular. Three mangoes are plural. A song often uses plural nouns because a meal usually has many foods.

In English we make most plurals by adding s, for example bean and beans. Some words add es, for example mango and mangoes, or potato and potatoes. Some words change inside, like loaf and loaves. A few do not change at all, such as maize when we mean the grain in general.

### Every language does this in its own way
Languages do not all make plurals the same way. In some languages the beginning of the word changes. In others the ending changes. In others a separate word shows that there are many. Do not guess. Ask an elder, a parent or your teacher how your language shows one and many.

### Practice with English
Change these to plural: cup, plate, egg, cabbage, tomato.
Answers: cups, plates, eggs, cabbages, tomatoes.

Now do the same job in your mother tongue. Choose five food nouns. Write the singular and the plural side by side on a chart.

## PAGE 4: Responding to spoken instructions
An instruction tells you what to do. Songs and singing games often carry instructions. In a singing game, one pupil is the soloist. The soloist sings a line that tells the group what to do, and the group answers with actions or with a song response.

Here is a game in English. The soloist sings: "Stir the pot!" The group pretends to stir and sings back, "We stir the pot!" The soloist sings: "Clap two times!" The group claps twice and answers. To do well, you must wait for the whole instruction, understand it, and only then act.

### Listening steps for instructions
1. Listen to the whole instruction before you move.
2. Picture what you must do.
3. Do it in the right order.
4. If you did not understand, ask politely.

### Why it matters
If you do not respond correctly, the game breaks down. In the same way, in real life, wrong actions can waste food or hurt someone. This is why our key question is: how should we respond to instructions? We respond by listening fully and acting carefully.

## PAGE 5: Collecting and keeping cultural songs
Every community has songs about food, farming and cooking. Elders often know songs that were sung when grinding grain, harvesting or cooking for visitors. These songs carry our culture. If nobody writes them down or records them, they may be forgotten.

### A class song portfolio
A portfolio is a folder where you keep your work. Your class can make a song portfolio.
1. Ask a parent, grandparent or neighbour for a song about food or work.
2. Listen carefully. Ask them to say it slowly.
3. Write it down or, with permission, record it.
4. Write the name of the person who shared it, and the date.
5. Underline the nouns and circle any instructions in the song.
6. Place the song in the class portfolio.

### Good manners when collecting
Greet the elder first. Explain why you want the song. Listen without interrupting. Say thank you at the end. Remember to keep the recordings safe and to return borrowed materials.

## PAGE 6: Putting it together
You have learned four things. First, listening means choosing to pay attention. Second, nouns are naming words and we can pick them out of a song. Third, nouns have singular and plural forms and each language forms them in its own way. Fourth, we respond to spoken instructions by listening to the end and then acting.

### Common mistakes
• Acting before the instruction has ended.
• Talking to a friend while the soloist is singing.
• Adding s to every noun in your mother tongue without asking how the language really forms plurals.

### Summary activity
Sing or say a food song from your community. Point out two nouns and give the plural of each. Then let a classmate give you an instruction from the song and carry it out.

## QUIZ
1. What is the main difference between hearing and listening?
   A) Hearing needs paper and a pen
   B) Listening is done on purpose with your mind and attention
   C) Hearing is only done at night
   D) Listening is done only with your hands
   Answer: B
   Why: Hearing happens by itself. Listening is a choice to give attention and think about what is said.
2. Which of these words is a noun?
   A) roast
   B) sweet
   C) banana
   D) quickly
   Answer: C
   Why: A banana is a thing, so it is a naming word. Roast is an action, sweet describes and quickly tells how.
3. What is the plural of the English noun potato?
   A) potatos
   B) potatoes
   C) potatoe
   D) potatoies
   Answer: B
   Why: Potato adds es to make the plural, potatoes.
4. In a singing game, what should you do first when the soloist gives an instruction?
   A) Move at once
   B) Listen to the whole instruction
   C) Sing louder than the soloist
   D) Ask a friend to do it for you
   Answer: B
   Why: You must hear the full instruction so that you act correctly.
5. List the nouns in this sentence: We boil the beans and roast the maize. (2 marks)
   Answer: beans and maize
   Why: One mark for each noun. Boil and roast are actions, not nouns.
6. Give the plural of each: cup, egg, tomato. (3 marks)
   Answer: cups, eggs, tomatoes
   Why: One mark for each correct plural.
7. Why should you not guess how plurals are made in your mother tongue? What should you do instead? (3 marks)
   Answer: Languages make plurals in different ways, so a guess may be wrong. I should ask an elder, a parent or my teacher.
   Why: One mark for saying languages differ, one for saying guessing may be wrong, one for a correct source to ask.
8. Explain three good manners to show when you collect a song from an elder. (3 marks)
   Answer: Greet the elder, listen without interrupting, and say thank you at the end. Asking permission before recording is also correct.
   Why: One mark for each polite action explained.
