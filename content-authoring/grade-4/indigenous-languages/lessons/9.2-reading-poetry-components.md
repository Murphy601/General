## PAGE 1: Poems about leadership
Poems can advise, encourage and warn. A poem about good leadership might tell pupils to be honest, fair and kind when they lead others. Many communities have poems and songs that praise good leaders and warn against bad ones.

To understand a poem, we read it slowly, more than once. We look at the words, the lines and the message.

### A model poem in English
Lead with honesty
A leader should be fair and true,
Listen well to all,
Share the work with all the crew,
And answer when they call.

If you lead, be kind and strong,
Make the rules clear,
Put right every wrong,
And keep your friends near.

### First reading
Read the poem silently, then aloud. What is it about? It is advice to leaders to be fair, listen, share work and be kind.

### Key questions
How do we describe a poem? We talk about its title, lines, stanzas, rhyme, rhythm, images and message. Why do we read poems? To enjoy language, learn lessons and share feelings.

## PAGE 2: Components of a poem
A poem has parts that we can name.

### The parts
• Title: the name of the poem.
• Line: one row of words. Poems are written in lines, not full-width paragraphs.
• Stanza: a group of lines. Stanzas are separated by a space.
• Rhyme: words that end with the same sound, such as all and call.
• Rhythm: the beat or flow of the poem when read aloud.
• Repetition: repeating words or lines for effect.
• Message or theme: the main idea the poet wants us to understand.
• Images: words that make pictures in our minds.

### Find them
In the model poem, how many lines are in the first stanza? Four. Which words rhyme? true and crew; all and call.

## PAGE 3: Understanding a poem
To understand a poem, follow these steps.

1. Read the title. Guess what the poem will say.
2. Read the whole poem aloud.
3. Read it again. Look at each stanza.
4. Find words you do not know and guess their meaning from the lines around them.
5. Ask: What is the poet telling us? What feeling does the poem give?
6. Say the main message in your own words.

### The message
For the model poem: A good leader is fair and honest, listens to others, shares work and is kind.

### Words in context
• "crew" means a group of people working together.
• "answer when they call" means respond when people need you.

### Meaning of lines
"Listen well to all" means a leader does not listen only to a few friends but to everybody.

### Discuss
In a group, discuss the main issues raised in the poem. Do you agree with the advice? Give an example from your class or community of a leader who did these things. Give reviews: say what you liked about the poem and why.

## PAGE 4: Answering questions on a poem
Poems can be tested with questions. Here are questions on the model poem.

### Questions and answers
1. What is the title? (Lead with honesty)
2. How many stanzas does the poem have? (two)
3. Which words rhyme in the first stanza? (true and crew; all and call)
4. What does the poet say a leader should do? (be fair, listen, share the work, be kind and strong)
5. Which line tells leaders to share work? (Share the work with all the crew)
6. What is the mood of the poem, serious or funny? (serious, but friendly)

### Tips for answering
• Read the question carefully.
• Go back to the poem.
• Use words from the poem in your answer.
• Answer in a complete sentence.
• If a question asks why, give a reason.

### In your mother tongue
Ask your teacher or an elder for a short poem or song in your language about being a good leader. Listen to it. What is the message? Which parts are repeated? Are there rhymes? Tell your partner, but do not try to translate it word for word if you are not sure.

### Vocabulary game
Make word cards with leadership words: leader, order, choose, rule, honesty, law. Play a matching game with meanings.

## PAGE 5: Watching performances and composing a poem
### Watching poems
If your school has a screen, or if a poet visits, watch a poem being performed. Notice:
• The reciter's voice, pace and pauses.
• Facial expression and hand movements. These are called non-verbal cues.
• How the audience responds.

### Composing a poem
Compose your own poem on leadership with your group.
1. Choose a title, such as "A Good Leader".
2. Decide on the message.
3. Write two stanzas of four lines each.
4. Try to make lines two and four rhyme.
5. Add repetition of a strong line.
6. Read it aloud and improve.

### Example
A good leader listens,
Speaks the truth each day,
Helps the weak and lonely,
And shows the proper way.

### Perform
Recite your poem as a choral poem with your group. Use non-verbal cues such as nodding, smiling, or open hands. Speak clearly and keep the same pace. The class can hold a poetry festival where classes perform for each other.

## PAGE 6: Summary and common mistakes
A poem has a title, lines, stanzas, rhyme, rhythm and a message. We read it aloud, look for meaning, answer questions using the text and enjoy performing.

### Remember
• A stanza is a group of lines.
• Rhyme is the same ending sound.
• Read the poem more than once.
• The message is the main idea.
• Use the poem's words in your answers.

### Common mistakes
• Reading only once and answering from memory.
• Thinking that every poem must rhyme. Some poems do not.
• Mixing up a line and a stanza.
• Ignoring the title.
• Making fun of a classmate's poem.

### Final activity
Choose any short poem. Name its title, count its stanzas, find one pair of rhyming words and say its message in one sentence. Share with a partner.

## QUIZ
1. What is a stanza?
   A) A group of lines in a poem
   B) A single word
   C) A title
   D) A rhyme
   Answer: A
   Why: A stanza is a group of lines that belong together.
2. Which pair of words rhyme?
   A) all and call
   B) leader and rule
   C) poem and song
   D) fair and strong
   Answer: A
   Why: All and call end with the same sound.
3. What is the message of a poem?
   A) The main idea the poet wants to tell us
   B) The number of lines
   C) The name of the printer
   D) The date it was written
   Answer: A
   Why: The message or theme is the main idea.
4. What is rhythm?
   A) The beat or flow of a poem when read aloud
   B) The page number
   C) The colour of the ink
   D) The title
   Answer: A
   Why: Rhythm is the pattern of beats in the poem.
5. Read the lines: A good leader listens, Speaks the truth each day. What does the poet say a good leader does? (2 marks)
   Answer: A good leader listens and speaks the truth every day.
   Why: One mark for listens and one for speaks the truth.
6. Name three components of a poem. (3 marks)
   Answer: Title, line, stanza, rhyme, rhythm, message (any three).
   Why: One mark for each component.
7. Give the steps you would follow to understand a poem you have just met. (3 marks)
   Answer: Read the title, read the poem aloud more than once, find and work out meanings of unknown words, and say the message in your own words.
   Why: One mark for each of three valid steps.
8. Why is it useful to use words from the poem when answering questions? (2 marks)
   Answer: It shows that your answer comes from the poem and makes it accurate.
   Why: One mark for showing the source and one for accuracy.
