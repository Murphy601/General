## PAGE 1: Two ways to find information fast
You do not need to read every word to find out what a text is about, or to find one fact. Good readers use two quick strategies: skimming and scanning.

Skimming means glancing quickly over a text to get the main idea. You look at the title, the first sentence and any headings, and you let your eyes run across the lines. You do not stop for details.

Scanning means running your eyes over a text to find one specific piece of information, such as a name, a number or a certain word. You know what you are looking for before you begin.

### Everyday examples
• Skimming: looking through a newspaper to see what the stories are about.
• Scanning: looking for the price of maize flour in a shop advert.
• Skimming: flipping through a book to see if it is interesting.
• Scanning: running your finger down a list to find your name.

### Key questions
How do we read to get information? We choose the right strategy for our purpose. Why is it important to understand words in context? Because a word can have different meanings in different places.

## PAGE 2: A model text about money
Read this passage. It is a short text on money.

"Mama Neema sells fruit at the market. Every morning she buys mangoes, bananas and oranges from a farmer. She pays 300 shillings for a box of mangoes. She sells the mangoes for 450 shillings. The extra money is her profit. Mama Neema puts part of her profit in a savings group. She uses the rest to buy food and pay school fees. She says, 'Saving a little every day helps me plan for hard times.'"

### Skim it
Look at it for ten seconds. What is the main idea? It is about how Mama Neema earns and manages money.

### Scan it
Scan for these facts.
1. What does she pay for a box of mangoes? (300 shillings)
2. What does she sell it for? (450 shillings)
3. What is the extra money called? (profit)
4. Where does she put part of her profit? (a savings group)

### Reading in your mother tongue
Practise skimming and scanning in a short text in your own language. Look for the main idea first. Then scan for a name or a number.

## PAGE 3: How to skim
Follow these steps.

1. Read the title. It tells you the topic.
2. Read any headings and captions.
3. Read the first sentence of each paragraph. It usually gives the main idea.
4. Look at the last sentence.
5. Glance over the rest without stopping.
6. Ask: what is this text mostly about?

### Practise
Skim this passage and give the main idea.
"A shop sells many things. Some people buy food. Others buy clothes and books. The shopkeeper counts the money at the end of the day."
Main idea: A shop sells goods and the shopkeeper counts the money.

### Time limits
Give yourself only twenty seconds to skim. This trains your eyes to move quickly. Then ask a partner to check your main idea.

### When to skim
Skim when you are deciding if a book or article is useful, when you want a quick idea before reading properly, or when you have very little time. Skimming does not replace careful reading. If you need to study a text, read it slowly afterwards.

## PAGE 4: How to scan
Scanning is for finding facts.

1. Decide what you are looking for, such as a name, price, date or word.
2. Keep that word or number in your mind.
3. Move your eyes quickly down or across the text. You may use your finger.
4. Stop when you see it and read the sentence around it.
5. Check it answers your question.

### Tips
• Numbers and names stand out. Look for digits and capital letters.
• Bold words and headings can help.
• Do not read every word. Ignore the rest.

### Practise
Scan the Neema passage for "school fees". Which sentence includes it? "She uses the rest to buy food and pay school fees."

### Scanning game
Write a word on the board. The first pupil to find it in the text and read the sentence wins a point. Play with different passages. Then play with a passage in your mother tongue, using the words your teacher chooses.

## PAGE 5: Building vocabulary on money
Money has its own words. Learning them helps you read and speak about business.

### Money words in English
• trade: buying and selling
• profit: money left after paying the cost
• loss: when you get less money than you spent
• customer or buyer: the person who buys
• seller: the person who sells
• products: things made or grown for sale
• services: work done for others, such as cutting hair

### Vocabulary bank
Make a class chart with these words and their meanings. Write them in English and add the words in your mother tongue with the help of your teacher.

### Using a dictionary
A simple dictionary lists words in order from A to Z. Find the word by its first letter, then its second. Read the meaning. Notice that some words have more than one meaning. Choose the one that fits the text.

### Word in context
Many English words have more than one meaning. The word change can mean coins given back: "The seller gave me change." It can also mean to become different: "The weather may change." Only the sentence around the word shows which meaning is right.

### Check
Choose three words. Write a sentence for each. Read them to a partner.

## PAGE 6: Comprehension and summary
After reading, answer questions to show you understood. Work with a partner and look back at the text for answers.

### Model comprehension
Text: "A conman pretended to sell cheap phones. He took money from people and ran away. The police caught him and returned the money."
1. What did the conman pretend to do? (sell cheap phones)
2. What did he do with the money? (ran away with it)
3. Who caught him? (the police)
4. What lesson can we learn? (Be careful with strangers who promise cheap goods.)

### Summary
Skimming is quick reading for the main idea. Scanning is quick searching for one detail. Vocabulary in context helps us understand.

### Common mistakes
• Reading every word when you only need one fact.
• Scanning without knowing what to look for.
• Skimming and then answering detailed questions without going back to the text.
• Choosing a dictionary meaning that does not fit the sentence.

## QUIZ
1. What is skimming?
   A) Reading quickly to get the main idea
   B) Reading slowly to remember every word
   C) Copying a text
   D) Reading aloud
   Answer: A
   Why: Skimming means glancing over a text for its general idea.
2. What is scanning?
   A) Looking for one specific fact
   B) Skipping the whole text
   C) Drawing a picture
   D) Reading with a friend
   Answer: A
   Why: Scanning is searching for a particular detail.
3. You want to find the price of a bag of flour in a shop advert. Which strategy do you use?
   A) Scanning
   B) Skimming
   C) Singing
   D) Copying
   Answer: A
   Why: You know exactly what you are looking for, so you scan for it.
4. What is profit?
   A) Money left after the cost is paid
   B) Money lost in business
   C) A type of market
   D) A shop
   Answer: A
   Why: Profit is the gain after costs.
5. Mama Neema buys a box of mangoes for 300 shillings and sells it for 450 shillings. How much is her profit? (2 marks)
   Answer: 150 shillings
   Why: 450 minus 300 equals 150. One mark for the working and one for the answer with units.
6. Give the steps of skimming a passage. (3 marks)
   Answer: Read the title, read headings, read the first sentence of each paragraph, glance over the rest, and state the main idea.
   Why: One mark for each correct step, up to three.
7. Explain the difference between skimming and scanning. (3 marks)
   Answer: Skimming is quick reading to get the main idea. Scanning is searching for one fact. In skimming we do not know a specific fact to find, but in scanning we do.
   Why: One mark for each definition and one for the difference.
8. Why should you look at the context when choosing a dictionary meaning? (2 marks)
   Answer: Many words have more than one meaning and the context shows which meaning fits.
   Why: One mark for words having several meanings and one for context showing the right one.
