## PAGE 1: Listening to understand
Comprehension means understanding. When you listen for comprehension, you do more than hear words. You follow the story or message, remember the main points and are able to answer questions about it.

Oral texts are all around us. A resource person, such as a nurse or a school first aider, might visit your class and talk about personal safety. A radio programme might explain what to do in case of a burn. Your grandmother might tell you a story about a child who was careful near a river.

### Why listen to oral texts?
We listen to oral texts to learn, to enjoy and to keep culture alive. Many important lessons in our communities have been passed on by word of mouth for generations. When you listen well, you become a good learner and a good storyteller too.

### Before you listen
• Know the topic. Ask what you expect to hear.
• Sit where you can see and hear the speaker.
• Be ready to remember: who, what, where, when, why, how.

### Try it
Your teacher will read a short story aloud. Do not write. Afterwards, tell your partner two things you remember.

## PAGE 2: A model oral passage on safety
Listen to, or read aloud, this passage. Your teacher may read it twice.

"Otieno was helping in the kitchen when the pot of hot water began to boil. His mother told him, 'Stay away from the fire, Otieno.' But he wanted to see the steam. He moved closer and touched the handle. The pot tipped and hot water splashed on his arm. He shouted in pain. His mother put his arm under cool running water for many minutes. Then she took him to the clinic. The nurse gave him a bandage and said, 'Next time, listen to your mother. Fire and hot water can hurt.' Otieno learned his lesson."

### What to remember
Who is in the story? Otieno, his mother and the nurse.
What happened? Otieno touched the pot and got a burn.
What did the mother do? She used cool water and took him to a clinic.
What is the lesson? Obey safety instructions.

This is a story in English. In class you can retell it in your mother tongue.

## PAGE 3: Answering questions from an oral text
After listening, we answer questions. The type of question tells us what kind of answer to give.

### Question words
• Who: asks about a person.
• What: asks about a thing or event.
• Where: asks about a place.
• When: asks about time.
• Why: asks for a reason.
• How: asks about the way something is done.

### Examples from the passage
1. Who told Otieno to stay away from the fire? (his mother)
2. Why did he move closer? (he wanted to see the steam)
3. What did his mother do first? (she put his arm under cool water)
4. Where did they go? (to the clinic)

### How to answer well
Listen for the key word in the question. Give a short, complete answer. If you did not hear, politely ask, "Please could you repeat that?" Never make up an answer.

### Practice
Answer in a full sentence: "Why did the pot tip over?" Answer: The pot tipped over because Otieno touched the handle.

## PAGE 4: Verbs in an oral text
A verb is a word that shows an action or something that happens. In the passage about Otieno, some verbs are: helping, boil, told, wanted, moved, touched, tipped, splashed, shouted, put, took, gave, said, learned.

### How to pick verbs out when listening
As you listen, ask, what are they doing? What happened? Every answer will contain a verb. Each time you hear a verb, raise a finger or tap softly.

### Team task
In small groups, listen again to the passage. Each pupil listens for verbs in one part. Make a list on a chart: touched, tipped, splashed.
Verbs about the past sometimes have endings such as ed in English. In your mother tongue, a verb may change in another way. Listen and note whether the verbs change and how.

### Verb charades
This is the guessing language game. One pupil acts out a verb, such as jump, wash, carry or cut. The rest guess the verb. The pupil who guesses first acts out the next verb. Do it in English and in your mother tongue.

## PAGE 5: Completing sentences with verbs
Verbs can be missing from a sentence, and we fill the gap. This is called completing a sentence. Doing it orally helps us know how verbs fit.

### Gap-filling in English
Choose a verb from the list: wash, cut, carry, tie, cool.
1. Always ______ your hands with soap. (wash)
2. Be careful when you ______ vegetables with a knife. (cut)
3. Put a burn under water to ______ it. (cool)
4. Do not ______ heavy loads on your head if you are too small. (carry)
5. ______ your shoelaces so that you do not fall. (tie)

### In pairs
One pupil reads a sentence with a pause where the verb should be. The partner says the missing verb. Then swap. After that, make up three sentences of your own on safety at school, say them in your mother tongue, and let your partner fill in the gaps.

### Sharing experiences
In your group, talk about how you keep safe at school: on the playground, on the road and in the kitchen. Everyone should have a turn, and everyone should listen with respect.

## PAGE 6: Summary and common mistakes
Listening for comprehension means listening to understand and remember. We answer who, what, where, when, why and how questions. We can pick out verbs, which are the action words of a sentence.

### Remember
• Get ready before listening.
• Listen for the key details.
• Answer in complete sentences.
• Verbs show actions.

### Common mistakes
• Talking while the text is being read.
• Answering without listening to the whole question.
• Guessing instead of asking for a repeat.
• Thinking that a verb is only a word for big actions. Verbs such as is and are, sleep and think are verbs too.

### Final activity
Ask an adult at home to tell you a short safety story or advice. Bring the story to class and retell it in your own words. Your classmates listen and ask two questions each.

## QUIZ
1. What does comprehension mean?
   A) Understanding
   B) Speaking loudly
   C) Writing fast
   D) Singing
   Answer: A
   Why: Comprehension is understanding what you hear or read.
2. Which question word asks about a reason?
   A) Where
   B) When
   C) Why
   D) Who
   Answer: C
   Why: Why asks for the reason something happened.
3. Which word in this sentence is a verb: Otieno touched the handle?
   A) Otieno
   B) touched
   C) the
   D) handle
   Answer: B
   Why: Touched shows an action.
4. What should you do if you did not hear a question well?
   A) Guess an answer
   B) Say nothing and leave
   C) Politely ask the speaker to repeat it
   D) Laugh
   Answer: C
   Why: Asking politely for a repeat helps you to give a correct answer.
5. Use the story of Otieno. Why did his mother put his arm under cool running water? (2 marks)
   Answer: To cool the burn and reduce the pain.
   Why: One mark for cool the burn and one for the reason of helping the injury.
6. Complete with a verb: Always ______ your hands before you eat. (2 marks)
   Answer: wash
   Why: Award marks for wash or another verb that fits the sentence, such as clean, and for correct spelling.
7. Name the six question words used to check comprehension. (3 marks)
   Answer: Who, what, where, when, why and how.
   Why: Award 3 marks for all six, 2 marks for four or five, 1 mark for two or three.
8. Explain two ways of being a good listener when a resource person visits your class. (3 marks)
   Answer: Sit where you can see and hear, stay quiet, keep your eyes on the speaker, and ask questions at the end politely.
   Why: One mark for each listening behaviour, and one mark for explaining why it helps.
