## PAGE 1: Leisure time and how we use it
Leisure time is the free time you have when you are not in class, doing homework or helping with chores. What do you like to do in your free time? Some pupils play football, some draw, some read, some sing or dance, and some help their parents with small tasks.

A hobby is something you enjoy doing regularly in your free time. A talent is a skill you are good at, such as drawing or running. Leisure time can help us discover and grow our talents.

### Constructive leisure activities
Constructive means useful and helpful. A constructive activity builds you up and does not harm you or others.
• Playing sports and games
• Reading books
• Drawing and painting
• Playing music, singing and dancing
• Community service, such as cleaning the compound
• Learning a new skill, such as sewing or gardening

### Key questions
How do you spend your leisure time? Why should we express our opinions? We express opinions so that others know what we think, and so that we can learn from each other.

### Listen and list
Listen to your teacher or a recording describing children's free-time activities. List the activities you hear. Say which ones are constructive and which are not. Give a reason.

## PAGE 2: Describing leisure activities
To describe an activity, tell what it is, where and when it is done, who takes part and why you like it.

### Model in English
"My favourite hobby is football. I play with my friends on the school field after lessons. We make two teams of six. It keeps me fit and helps me make friends. I also learn to work with others."

### Speaking frame
Complete these sentences about yourself.
• My favourite leisure activity is ______.
• I do it at ______ with ______.
• I like it because ______.
• It helps me to ______.

### Vocabulary
Some words for talking about leisure: free time, hobby, talent, creative, peers, responsibility, community service.
Peers are people of your own age or class.
Responsibility means a duty you are trusted to do.

### Practise in your mother tongue
Describe a leisure activity in your mother tongue for one minute. Your partner listens and tells you one thing they learned. Then swap. Be careful to speak clearly and to listen without interrupting.

## PAGE 3: Interjections
An interjection is a short word or sound that shows a sudden feeling. It stands apart from the rest of the sentence and often ends with an exclamation mark.

### Examples in English
• "Wow! That was a great goal."
• "Oh no! I have lost my ball."
• "Hurray! We won the match."
• "Ouch! That hurt."
• "Yuck! This milk is sour."
• "Oops! I dropped my pencil."

### What feelings do they show?
Wow: surprise or joy. Oh no: worry. Hurray: happiness. Ouch: pain. Yuck: disgust. Oops: a small mistake.

### How to say them
Say an interjection with the right feeling in your voice. If you say "Hurray!" in a sad voice, it does not sound true.

### In your community
Every language has its own interjections. They are learned by listening to people. Ask elders and friends which sounds and short words your community uses for surprise, pain, joy, disgust and worry. Note them on a chart with the feeling they show. Do not invent them. Copy exactly what you hear, and check with your teacher.

## PAGE 4: Using interjections when giving opinions
When you give an opinion, an interjection can show your feeling.

### Model discussion
Pupil A: I think reading is the best way to spend free time.
Pupil B: Hmm, I prefer football because it keeps me fit.
Pupil A: Oh, that is a good point!
Pupil C: Wow! I like both reading and football.

### Tips
• Use interjections sparingly. Too many make your speech tiring.
• Match the interjection to your real feeling.
• Never use an interjection to make fun of someone.
• Keep listening. Give others time to speak.

### Practice
Write which feeling each interjection shows.
1. Hurray! Our team won. (happiness)
2. Yuck! This food is rotten. (disgust)
3. Ouch! The thorn pricked me. (pain)
4. Wow! What a big rainbow. (surprise)

### Discussion
In groups of four, discuss the question: Which is a better use of leisure time, reading or playing outside? Take turns. Each pupil gives an opinion and a reason, and includes one interjection.

## PAGE 5: Poems and debates
### A poem about leisure
Compose a short poem on leisure as a group.
Example:
Wow, it is time to play!
Hurray, no lessons today!
We run and read and sing,
Free time is a lovely thing.
Present the poem to the class, then discuss its message. What does it say about how we should use our free time?

### How to debate
A debate is a formal discussion in which two sides give opposite views on a topic. Each side speaks in turn and gives reasons.

Steps:
1. Choose a motion, for example, "Reading is a better use of leisure time than watching television."
2. Divide into two teams: those who agree (the proposers) and those who disagree (the opposers).
3. Each team plans three reasons.
4. Speakers take turns. Each speaks for about one minute.
5. Listeners note down good points.
6. The class votes on which team spoke best.

### Rules of debate
• Listen without interrupting.
• Speak politely. Say "I respectfully disagree" instead of "You are wrong."
• Give reasons and examples.
• Accept the result gracefully.

## PAGE 6: Summary and common mistakes
Leisure activities are what we do in our free time. Constructive activities help us grow. Interjections show sudden feelings. A debate lets us share opinions with reasons and respect.

### Common mistakes
• Choosing a leisure activity that harms yourself or others.
• Using interjections all the time.
• Shouting during a debate.
• Interrupting the speaker.
• Making up sounds and saying they belong to your language instead of asking an elder.

### Final activity
Give a one-minute talk on your favourite leisure activity. Include one interjection at a suitable place. Your partner tells you what feeling it showed.

## QUIZ
1. What is leisure time?
   A) Free time when you are not working or in class
   B) Lesson time
   C) Sleeping time
   D) Exam time
   Answer: A
   Why: Leisure is free time for rest and enjoyment.
2. Which of these is an interjection?
   A) Quickly
   B) Hurray!
   C) Table
   D) Under
   Answer: B
   Why: Hurray is a short word that shows sudden happiness.
3. Which feeling does Ouch! show?
   A) Pain
   B) Disgust
   C) Joy
   D) Sleepiness
   Answer: A
   Why: People say ouch when something hurts.
4. What is a debate?
   A) A discussion where two sides give opposite views with reasons
   B) A song
   C) A game of football
   D) A type of poem
   Answer: A
   Why: A debate is an organised discussion of a motion with opposite sides.
5. Give two examples of constructive leisure activities. (2 marks)
   Answer: Reading, playing sports, drawing, singing, gardening, community service (any two).
   Why: One mark for each.
6. Write two sentences with interjections that show happiness and pain. (2 marks)
   Answer: Hurray! We won the match. Ouch! I hurt my foot.
   Why: One mark for each correct sentence with a suitable interjection.
7. State three rules to follow in a debate. (3 marks)
   Answer: Listen without interrupting, speak politely, give reasons, accept the result gracefully.
   Why: One mark for each rule, up to three.
8. Why should we ask an elder about interjections in our mother tongue instead of inventing them? (2 marks)
   Answer: Interjections are learned from real speakers of the language. Inventing them may make mistakes and give wrong ideas about the language.
   Why: One mark for learning from speakers and one for avoiding wrong information.
