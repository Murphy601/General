## PAGE 1: Where is it? Words of position
When we describe where something is, we use small words called prepositions. A preposition shows the position of one thing in relation to another, and sometimes shows direction or time.

Imagine a hen. Where is the hen? It can be in the yard, on the fence, under the tree, behind the house, next to the well or between two goats. Each small word tells the listener something different. Without these words, a description would be confusing.

### Common prepositions of position
• on: touching the top of something
• in: inside something
• under: below something
• in front of: before something, facing it
• behind: at the back of something
• beside or next to: at the side of something
• between: with something on each side
• outside: not inside

### Key question
What is the importance of prepositions in description? They help the listener to picture exactly where something is. A person searching for a lost goat needs to know whether it was behind the shed or beside the river.

### Try it
Look around the classroom. Say where your bag is, using a preposition. Then say where the teacher's chair is.

### Describe the picture
1. The ball is on the table.
2. The bag is under the table.
3. The box is beside the table.

### Practice
Use a preposition to complete:
1. The book is ______ my desk. (on)
2. The cat sleeps ______ the bed. (under)
3. The teacher stands ______ the class. (in front of)
4. The goat is ______ the fence. (behind)
Other correct answers are possible, so check that your sentence makes sense.

### In your mother tongue
Words of position may be separate words in your language, or they may be parts joined to other words. Ask your teacher or an elder how to say on, under, beside and between, and write them on a chart.

## PAGE 3: Role playing positions
Working with prepositions is fun when we move objects.

### Position game
1. Put several objects on a table: a pencil, a book, a cup and a ball.
2. One pupil reads an instruction: "Put the pencil on the book."
3. Another pupil follows it.
4. The class checks together whether it was done correctly.

### More instructions
• "Put the cup under the table."
• "Place the ball between the book and the pencil."
• "Stand behind your chair."
• "Put the book in the bag."
• "Sit next to your friend."

### Turn the game around
A pupil places the objects, and the others describe the positions using full sentences. Example: "The ball is in front of the book."

### Listen carefully
The game works only if you listen to the whole instruction. If you hear "between", you know there must be two things, one on each side. You can play this game in English and in your mother tongue.

### Find prepositions in a text
Read: "The dog slept under the tree. The birds sat on the branches. The cow ate grass beside the river." The prepositions are under, on and beside.

## PAGE 4: Listening to information about animals
Animals are part of our lives. Some are domestic animals, such as cows, goats, hens and dogs, kept by people. Others are wild animals, such as lions, elephants and zebras, which live freely in the forest and on the plains.

### Listening for animals
Your teacher may play a recording or a resource person may talk about animals. Listen carefully and note the names of the animals you hear. Work with a partner and make a list of domestic animals and a list of wild animals.

### Answer oral questions
After listening, answer questions such as:
1. Which animals were named?
2. Where do these animals live?
3. What do they eat?
4. How do people care for them?

### Model listening passage
"Chege has a goat named Kali. Every morning Chege takes Kali to the field beside the river. In the evening he puts her in the shed behind the house. He gives her clean water and checks that she is not sick. If she is sick, he calls the veterinary doctor."

Questions: Where does Chege take the goat in the morning? (to the field beside the river) Where does she sleep? (in the shed behind the house) Who does he call if the goat is sick? (the veterinary doctor).

## PAGE 5: Animal welfare
Animal welfare means taking good care of animals so that they are healthy, safe and not hurt. Animals feel pain and hunger, just like people.

### Good animal care
• Give food and clean water every day.
• Provide a safe shelter from sun, rain and thieves.
• Do not beat animals or make them carry too heavy loads.
• Take sick animals to a veterinary doctor.
• Do not throw stones at animals.
• Do not take eggs or chicks from wild birds.
• Protect wild animals and their homes, such as forests.

### Speaking for animals
To advocate means to speak in support of something. You can advocate for animals by telling others how to treat them well. Make a short speech using prepositions.
Example: "Animals must have a shelter behind the house. Water should be placed next to the food. Do not put a heavy load on a donkey."

### Make a mosaic
An artistic mosaic is a picture made from small pieces of paper or seeds. Choose an animal and make a mosaic. Write one sentence about caring for it, and use a preposition. Display the mosaic at your Indigenous Languages exhibition.

### Listen to a resource person
If a farmer or animal health officer visits, prepare two questions to ask, such as "What do you feed your cows?" and "How do you protect them from disease?"

## PAGE 6: Summary and common mistakes
Prepositions show the position of things. We can describe places, follow instructions and give information about animals using them. Animal welfare means caring for animals well.

### Remember
• Prepositions: on, in, under, in front of, behind, beside, between, outside.
• Listen to the whole instruction before acting.
• Answer questions with full sentences.
• Treat animals kindly.

### Common mistakes
• Confusing in and on. A book in a bag is inside it. A book on a table is on top.
• Mixing up beside and between. Between needs two things.
• Answering questions before hearing them fully.
• Being unkind to animals.

### Final activity
Describe your home compound to a partner using at least five prepositions. Where is the house? Where is the well or tap? Where do the animals stay? Let your partner draw what they hear. Compare the drawing with your idea.

## QUIZ
1. What does a preposition show?
   A) The position of one thing in relation to another
   B) The colour of a thing
   C) The sound of an animal
   D) The size of a bag
   Answer: A
   Why: Prepositions such as on, under and beside show position.
2. The bag is below the table. Which preposition fits?
   A) On
   B) Under
   C) Beside
   D) Between
   Answer: B
   Why: Under means below something.
3. Where is the ball if it is touching the top of the table?
   A) Under the table
   B) Behind the table
   C) On the table
   D) Outside the table
   Answer: C
   Why: On shows that something is touching the top of another thing.
4. Which of these is an example of animal welfare?
   A) Giving clean water and shelter
   B) Beating a donkey
   C) Throwing stones at a dog
   D) Leaving a sick goat alone
   Answer: A
   Why: Welfare means looking after animals' needs and safety.
5. Complete with a preposition: The cat sleeps ______ the bed. (2 marks)
   Answer: under (or on, if the cat sleeps on top)
   Why: One mark for a preposition and one for a sentence that makes sense.
6. Name three prepositions used to describe position. (3 marks)
   Answer: on, in, under, behind, beside, between, in front of (any three).
   Why: One mark for each correct preposition.
7. List three ways of taking good care of a domestic animal. (3 marks)
   Answer: Give food and clean water, provide a safe shelter, and take it to a veterinary doctor when it is sick.
   Why: One mark for each valid way.
8. Write two sentences describing where objects are in your classroom, using a different preposition in each. (2 marks)
   Answer: The board is in front of the class. My bag is under my desk.
   Why: One mark for each correct sentence with a different preposition.
