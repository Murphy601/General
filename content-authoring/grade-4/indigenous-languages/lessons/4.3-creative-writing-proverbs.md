## PAGE 1: Proverbs as treasure
Proverbs and sayings are treasures of language. Each one holds the wisdom of many people over many years. Elders often use them in speeches, in teaching and even in solving disagreements. A proverb can say in a few words what would take a long explanation.

Communities across Kenya and the world have proverbs about work, honesty, friendship, patience, respect and the environment. Some are similar from one community to another, for example, about working together. Others come from a particular way of life.

### Key question
Why are proverbs and sayings important? They teach values, keep culture alive, make speech colourful and help us remember lessons.

### Difference between a proverb and a saying
A proverb is usually a complete, well-known sentence that gives advice, such as "Look before you leap." A saying is a common expression, such as "It is raining cats and dogs," which means heavy rain. A saying may describe something rather than advise.

### Try it
Tell your partner one proverb or saying you have heard from a grandparent. What do you think it means?

## PAGE 2: Collecting proverbs from the community
A collection of proverbs is a valuable class resource. You can make a group portfolio, a folder where you keep them.

### How to collect
1. Ask elders, parents and neighbours, politely, for proverbs and sayings.
2. Listen to the whole proverb. Ask them to say it slowly.
3. Write it carefully in your mother tongue.
4. Ask what it means and when it is used.
5. Write the meaning in your own words.
6. Note the name of the person who shared it.

### Organise the portfolio
Sort proverbs by theme, such as work, honesty, respect, patience and friendship. Write each proverb on a sentence strip and pin it on the wall.

### Model entry
Proverb: Many hands make light work.
Theme: Working together.
Meaning: A task is easier when people share it.
Told by: (name of person).

### Kiswahili example
"Ukiona vyaelea, vimeundwa." Meaning: when you see things floating, someone has made them float. Success comes from effort and planning.

## PAGE 3: Guessing and explaining meaning
Understanding a proverb means asking what lesson lies behind the words.

### Meaning game
One group reads out a proverb. The other groups have one minute to discuss what it means. Each group gives its answer. The group that first explains it correctly wins a point.

### Example
"A stitch in time saves nine."
Words: one stitch now prevents nine stitches later.
Meaning: deal with small problems quickly before they grow.
Situation: A small hole in your uniform is easy to mend today. If you wait, it becomes a big tear.

### Another example
"Honesty is the best policy."
Meaning: telling the truth is always the wisest choice.
Situation: A pupil finds a pencil case, takes it to the teacher, and its owner is happy.

### Check your answer
If two pupils give different meanings, ask an elder or a teacher. Proverbs may have more than one meaning, but the lesson should be sensible and respectful.

## PAGE 4: Planning an imaginative composition
An imaginative composition is a story that comes from your imagination. It is not a true report. It may have made-up characters and events. The best imaginative stories are still believable and teach or delight.

### Elements of a composition
• Title: a short name for the story.
• Characters: who is in it.
• Setting: where and when it happens.
• Beginning: introduce characters and setting.
• Middle: a problem or adventure.
• Ending: how the problem is solved.
• Lesson or proverb: what the story shows.

### Plan before you write
Draw a simple plan like this.

Title: The Lazy Farmer
Characters: Mzee Juma, his neighbour.
Setting: A village in the dry season.
Problem: Juma does not plant on time.
Ending: He goes hungry while his neighbour has food.
Proverb: Little by little fills the measure, or, A stitch in time saves nine.

Choose your own theme related to school or home. Fill in a plan with each of these headings.

## PAGE 5: Writing with a proverb
Now write a composition using a proverb. The proverb can come at the end to sum up the lesson or in a character's words.

### Model composition in English
"Baraka Keeps His Promise
Baraka was a pupil who came late to school every day. His teacher told him, 'A stitch in time saves nine. If you get up early today, you will avoid trouble tomorrow.' But Baraka did not listen. One day the class went on a trip, and the bus left without him. Baraka stood alone at the gate, sad. After that, he set his alarm and woke up early. He never missed anything again. Now, whenever he sees a friend late, he says, 'A stitch in time saves nine.'"

### Steps
1. Use your plan.
2. Write the title.
3. Begin with a sentence that catches attention.
4. Write in paragraphs.
5. Use the proverb in a suitable place.
6. Write the ending.
7. Read it aloud and check punctuation.

You can write your composition in your mother tongue. If you meet a difficulty, ask your teacher or an elder for help.

## PAGE 6: Sharing, reviewing and improving
A writer improves through feedback. Swap your composition with a partner. Read and answer these questions.

### Peer review checklist
• Does the story have a beginning, middle and end?
• Is the proverb used in the right way?
• Are the characters and setting clear?
• Are capital letters and full stops correct?
• What did you like best?

### Giving feedback
Start with something good. Then suggest one improvement. Be kind and clear. Example: "I like the way you described the village. Perhaps you could add what Juma thought at the end."

### Publishing
Copy the final version neatly. Place the best compositions in the class reading club or on the class notice board. Read one aloud to the class.

### Common mistakes
• Using a proverb without knowing its meaning.
• Writing a proverb in the story that has nothing to do with the plot.
• Forgetting the ending.
• Not checking spelling or punctuation.

## QUIZ
1. What is an imaginative composition?
   A) A story that comes from the imagination
   B) A list of proverbs
   C) A news report
   D) A bill
   Answer: A
   Why: Imaginative compositions are made-up stories.
2. Which is a proverb?
   A) Look before you leap.
   B) It is raining.
   C) Good morning.
   D) Bring the book.
   Answer: A
   Why: It is a short saying that gives advice.
3. What does A stitch in time saves nine mean?
   A) Sewing is difficult
   B) Fix small problems early to avoid bigger ones
   C) Nine is a lucky number
   D) Cloth costs a lot
   Answer: B
   Why: Acting early prevents bigger trouble later.
4. Which of these is NOT part of a story plan?
   A) Setting
   B) Characters
   C) Problem
   D) A shopping bill
   Answer: D
   Why: A shopping bill has nothing to do with planning a story.
5. Name three headings you would use in a plan for an imaginative composition. (3 marks)
   Answer: Title, characters, setting, problem, ending (any three).
   Why: One mark for each correct heading.
6. Explain what the proverb Honesty is the best policy means. (2 marks)
   Answer: It means telling the truth is always the wisest choice, because it brings trust.
   Why: One mark for the meaning and one for the reason or example.
7. Describe how you would collect proverbs from an elder politely. (3 marks)
   Answer: Greet the elder, ask politely for proverbs, listen to the whole proverb, write it down, ask its meaning, and say thank you.
   Why: One mark for each of three correct steps.
8. Write a two-sentence ending to a story about a pupil who kept the proverb Many hands make light work. (3 marks)
   Answer: The whole class helped to clean the compound. It took only ten minutes, and they all learned that many hands make light work.
   Why: One mark for a clear ending, one mark for using the proverb correctly, one mark for correct punctuation.
