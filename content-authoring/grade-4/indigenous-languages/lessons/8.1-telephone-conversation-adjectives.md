## PAGE 1: Digital devices around us
A digital device is a machine that works with electronic signals to help us communicate, learn or do work. Examples are mobile phones, computers, tablets, televisions, radios, cameras and calculators. Many people now use a mobile phone every day to call family, send messages and listen to news.

Each device has a use. A phone is for calling and messaging. A radio is for listening to news, music and programmes. A camera takes photographs. A computer helps us to write, draw and search for information. A television shows pictures with sound.

### Matching devices to uses
Match each device with its use.
1. Mobile phone: to make calls
2. Camera: to take photographs
3. Radio: to hear news and music
4. Computer: to write and search for information
5. Television: to watch programmes

### Care of devices
• Keep them dry and clean.
• Handle them gently.
• Ask permission before using someone else's device.
• Use them for good purposes only.
• Take breaks so your eyes and ears rest.

### Key question
How do we respond during a telephone conversation? We greet, speak clearly, listen carefully and use polite words.

## PAGE 2: Describing a device with adjectives
An adjective is a word that describes a noun. It tells us more about a person, animal, place or thing, such as its colour, size, shape or height.

### Examples in English
• a small mobile phone
• a red radio
• a tall television stand
• a round clock
• a heavy computer
• a new tablet

### Describing by aspect
Colour: red, black, silver, blue
Size: big, small, large, tiny
Shape: round, square, flat, thin
Height: tall, short
Weight: heavy, light

### Sentence examples
"My aunt has a black mobile phone." (black describes the phone)
"The radio is small and silver." (small and silver describe the radio)

### Activity
Work with a partner. Choose a device in the classroom, such as a clock or a calculator. Describe it using at least three adjectives. Your partner guesses what it is.
Example: "It is round. It is white. It is on the wall." Answer: a clock.

### Adjectives in your mother tongue
Ask your teacher how words that describe are used in your language. In some languages, a describing word comes after the noun, and in some it changes to agree with the noun. Note down how your language does it.

### Model telephone conversation
Ring, ring.
Mr. Kimani: Hello, this is Mr. Kimani speaking.
Neema: Good afternoon, Mr. Kimani. This is Neema from Grade 4. May I speak to my classmate Baraka, please?
Mr. Kimani: Certainly. Please hold on a moment.
Baraka: Hello, Baraka speaking.
Neema: Hello, Baraka. I am calling about tomorrow's homework.
Baraka: Thank you for calling, Neema. It is on page ten.
Neema: Thank you very much. Goodbye.
Baraka: Goodbye.

Notice the polite words. In the next page we will list them.

## PAGE 4: Polite expressions
Polite expressions are words and phrases that show respect and kindness.

### Common polite expressions in English
• Good morning, good afternoon, good evening
• Please
• Thank you
• You are welcome
• May I speak to ...?
• Sorry, I did not hear you. Could you repeat that, please?
• Excuse me
• Please hold on
• Goodbye, take care

### Other telephone manners
• Speak clearly and not too loudly.
• Do not eat while talking.
• Give your name at the start.
• Do not interrupt.
• If you dialled a wrong number, say sorry and end the call politely.
• Do not call late at night unless it is urgent.
• Respect the other person's time; keep the call short.

### In your mother tongue
Every language has ways of greeting and thanking. Ask an elder how to greet politely on a phone, how to ask for someone and how to say goodbye. Write down three polite phrases in your language.

### Answer questions
Read the model conversation again. Who called? (Neema) Who did she want to speak to? (Baraka) Why did she call? (About homework) What polite words did she use? (Good afternoon, please, thank you, goodbye)

## PAGE 5: Role playing a call
Now practise a telephone conversation with a partner. Sit back to back so you cannot see each other, as on a real call. You must rely on your voice.

### Situation 1: Calling a call centre
A call centre is a place where trained staff answer phone calls from customers. Suppose a call centre helps people who want to know about the weather or bus times.
Caller: Good morning. I would like to know the bus time for Kisumu.
Agent: Good morning. Thank you for calling. The bus leaves at eight o'clock.
Caller: Thank you. Could you please repeat that?
Agent: Certainly. The bus leaves at eight o'clock.
Caller: Thank you very much. Goodbye.
Agent: You are welcome. Goodbye.

### Situation 2: Speaking to a relative
Call your grandmother to greet her. Ask how she is. Tell her one piece of news. Say goodbye politely.

### Peer feedback
After each role play, the listeners say: did they greet? Did they speak clearly? Did they use please and thank you? Did they end politely?

### Questions to ask during a call
Use polite question words: Could you please tell me ...? May I ask ...? When does ...? Where is ...? How much is ...?
Speak in English and in your mother tongue.

## PAGE 6: Summary and common mistakes
Digital devices help us communicate. Adjectives describe them. A telephone call has four parts, and polite expressions make it pleasant.

### Remember
• Greet, say who you are, talk and listen, say goodbye.
• Use please and thank you.
• Adjectives describe nouns by colour, size, shape and more.
• Ask politely if you did not hear.

### Common mistakes
• Starting to talk without greeting.
• Speaking too fast or too loudly.
• Forgetting to give your name.
• Ending the call suddenly without saying goodbye.
• Using a phone without permission.

### Final activity
Choose one device. Write three adjectives that describe it. Then role play a call in which you ask a friend to help you find it. Use polite expressions.

## QUIZ
1. What is an adjective?
   A) A word that describes a noun
   B) A word that names a person
   C) A word that shows action
   D) A word that joins sentences
   Answer: A
   Why: Adjectives describe nouns, telling their colour, size, shape and so on.
2. Which word is an adjective in the phrase a small silver radio?
   A) radio
   B) a
   C) small
   D) in
   Answer: C
   Why: Small describes the radio. Silver also does, but radio is the noun.
3. What should you do first when someone answers your phone call?
   A) Greet them politely
   B) Hang up
   C) Shout the message
   D) Ask for money
   Answer: A
   Why: A polite greeting begins the call.
4. Which of these is a polite expression?
   A) Give me that now
   B) Please could you repeat that
   C) I am not interested
   D) Stop talking
   Answer: B
   Why: Please and could you are polite forms of asking.
5. Name three digital devices and give one use of each. (3 marks)
   Answer: Mobile phone for calling, radio for hearing news, camera for taking photographs.
   Why: One mark for each device with a correct use.
6. Describe a mobile phone using three adjectives. (3 marks)
   Answer: For example: It is small, black and thin.
   Why: One mark for each adjective that fits.
7. List the four parts of a telephone conversation. (4 marks)
   Answer: Greet, say who you are and why you called, talk and listen, say goodbye.
   Why: One mark for each part.
8. Give two rules of good telephone manners. (2 marks)
   Answer: Speak clearly, give your name, do not interrupt, do not call late at night without a reason.
   Why: One mark for each valid rule.
