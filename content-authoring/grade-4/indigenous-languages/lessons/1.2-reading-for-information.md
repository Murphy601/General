## PAGE 1: Why we read for information
We read for many reasons. We read stories for fun and notices to know what to do. When we read to learn something new, we say we are reading for information. A text about cultural foods can teach us what people eat, how food is cooked and why some foods are kept for special days.

To find information in a text you must read with a question in your mind. Before you begin, ask: What is this text about? Who is it for? What do I want to find out?

### A short model text
Read this text about a food. Your teacher may ask you to adapt it into your mother tongue.

"Ugali is a stiff food made from maize flour. People boil water, add flour and stir until it is thick. It is often eaten with vegetables, meat or fish. Many families eat it for lunch or supper."

### What does the text teach?
It tells us what ugali is made from, how it is cooked and when it is eaten. That is the information.

## PAGE 2: Vocabulary, the words we know
Vocabulary means all the words you know and can use. When you read a text about cultural foods you meet many new words, such as flour, boil, stir, roast, preserve and dry. The more words you know, the easier it is to understand what you read.

### How to build vocabulary from a text
1. Read the text once for the general meaning.
2. Read again and underline words that are new or important.
3. Write each word on a flashcard. Write the word on one side and its meaning or a picture on the other.
4. Say the word aloud and use it in a sentence.
5. Keep the flashcards in a box or envelope.

### Word bank
A word bank is a class list of useful words on one theme. With your group, make a vocabulary bank called Cultural Foods. Add the words from the text above, such as flour, boil, stir, thick, vegetables. Then add the same words in your mother tongue beside each one, after asking your teacher or an elder.

## PAGE 3: Nouns in a text: singular and plural
A text about food is full of nouns. Some are singular and some are plural. Finding them helps you read carefully.

Read this sentence: "Mama Wanjiru buys tomatoes, an onion and three cabbages at the market."
Nouns: Mama Wanjiru, tomatoes, onion, cabbages, market.
Singular: onion, market. Plural: tomatoes, cabbages.

### Ways to spot a plural
• A number word in front, such as three or many.
• A word such as some, few or several.
• The ending or the shape of the noun itself.

### Compile a list
With your group, copy a short passage about food and make a two-column list.
Singular: onion, market, banana
Plural: tomatoes, cabbages, bananas
Note that the noun banana appears in both forms. In your mother tongue, check with your teacher how the plural is shown, since it may not be by an ending like s.

## PAGE 4: Reading aloud together
Reading a text aloud helps you use new words. In a small group, practise reading the passage fluently. Fluent reading means reading smoothly without stopping at every word and with a voice that is easy to follow.

### Steps for group reading
1. Read silently first.
2. Point to hard words and help each other say them.
3. Take turns. Each pupil reads one or two sentences.
4. Listen to each other. Do not laugh at mistakes.
5. Read the whole passage again, this time faster and more smoothly.

### Peer review
After each group reads, the other pupils give feedback. Say one thing that was good and one thing to improve. For example: "You read the words clearly. Try to pause at the full stops." Be kind and honest. This shows respect.

### Reading game
Use your flashcards. One pupil holds up a card and the others read the word quickly. The group that reads the most cards correctly in one minute wins.

## PAGE 5: Answering questions and listing foods
After reading, check that you understood. Ask and answer questions about the text.

### Model text 2
"Millet porridge is a warm drink. It is made by mixing millet flour with water and boiling it. Some people add a little sugar or lemon. It is good for breakfast."

Questions:
1. What is millet porridge made from? (millet flour and water)
2. How is it cooked? (by boiling)
3. What may some people add? (sugar or lemon)
4. When is it good to drink? (at breakfast)

### List the cultural foods
Read a passage from a book, magazine or a page your teacher gives you. Write a list of the foods you find in it. Then compare your list with your partner. Did you find the same foods? If not, go back to the text and check.

### Tip
Always look back at the text to find your answer. Do not guess from memory only.

## PAGE 6: Summary and common mistakes
Reading for information means reading with a question, finding key words, and understanding what the text tells us. You have learned to build vocabulary using flashcards and word banks, to identify singular and plural nouns, and to read aloud with a group.

### Remember
• Read once for the main idea and once for details.
• Underline new words and find their meaning.
• Use a word bank to remember them.
• Notice if a noun is one or many.

### Common mistakes
• Reading too fast without understanding.
• Skipping hard words instead of asking about them.
• Answering from memory without checking the text.
• Copying a word in your mother tongue without asking the teacher if you are not sure it is right.

### Final activity
Choose one short passage about a cultural food. Write down three new words, the singular and plural of two nouns and one question with its answer. Read your work to a partner.

## QUIZ
1. What does vocabulary mean?
   A) All the words you know and can use
   B) Only the long words in a book
   C) A kind of song
   D) The pictures in a text
   Answer: A
   Why: Vocabulary is the set of words a person knows and uses.
2. Which of these is a plural noun?
   A) onion
   B) market
   C) cabbages
   D) porridge
   Answer: C
   Why: Cabbages names more than one cabbage. Onion and market name one.
3. What should you do first before reading a text for information?
   A) Ask yourself what you want to find out
   B) Close your eyes
   C) Copy the last line
   D) Guess the answers
   Answer: A
   Why: Reading with a question in mind helps you find useful information.
4. Which is a good way to remember new words?
   A) Write them on flashcards with their meanings
   B) Hide the text
   C) Never repeat them
   D) Read only the title
   Answer: A
   Why: Flashcards let you practise the word and meaning again and again.
5. Read this: "Mother buys three bananas and a mango." List the singular and plural nouns. (3 marks)
   Answer: Plural: bananas. Singular: mango (also mother).
   Why: One mark for bananas as plural, one for mango as singular, one for mother as singular.
6. Give two ways to know that a noun is plural in a sentence. (2 marks)
   Answer: A number word such as three in front of it, or a word such as many or some. The word may also change its shape.
   Why: One mark for each valid clue.
7. Describe how to give kind and useful feedback after a group reads aloud. (3 marks)
   Answer: Say one thing that was good, then one thing to improve, and speak politely.
   Why: One mark for a good point, one for an improvement point, one for politeness.
8. Read Model text 2 about millet porridge. What is it made from, and how is it cooked? (2 marks)
   Answer: Millet flour and water, cooked by boiling.
   Why: One mark for the ingredients and one for the cooking method.
