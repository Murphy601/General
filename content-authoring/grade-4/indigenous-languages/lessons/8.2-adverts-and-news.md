## PAGE 1: What is an advertisement?
An advertisement, or advert, is a message that tells people about a product or service so that they will want to buy or use it. A product is a thing made or grown for sale, such as a phone, a bag of flour or a pair of shoes. A service is work done for others, such as a haircut or a bus ride.

We meet adverts in newspapers, on posters, on radio, on television and on phones. Their job is to attract attention and give information.

### Parts of an advert
• Headline: a short, eye-catching title.
• Picture or drawing of the product.
• Description: words that tell about it.
• Price, if any.
• Contact details, such as a place or phone number.

### Key question
Why are advertisements important? They tell people what is for sale, where to find it and how much it costs. They also help sellers to reach customers.

### A model advert
"BRIGHT BAG SALE. Strong school bags in red, blue and green. Big and light. Only 600 shillings. Buy now at Neema Shop, next to the market."

### Discuss
What is the product? (school bags) What colours? (red, blue, green) How much? (600 shillings) Where can you buy it? (Neema Shop, next to the market)

## PAGE 2: Describing objects in adverts
Adverts use adjectives to describe products. An adjective describes a noun by colour, size, shape and other qualities.

### Aspects to describe
• Colour: red, green, yellow
• Size: big, small, medium
• Shape: round, square, long
• Feel: soft, smooth, rough
• Quality: strong, new, cheap, modern
• Taste: sweet, tasty, fresh

### Practice
Read the advert: "Fresh sweet oranges. Big, round and juicy. Ten oranges for 100 shillings."
Adjectives: fresh, sweet, big, round, juicy.

### Vocabulary game
Play a Pictionary vocabulary game. One pupil draws an item on the board without speaking or writing. The class guesses and gives adjectives to describe it. For example, if a pupil draws a round clock, the class says: round, small, old, black.

### Personal advert booklet
Collect adverts from newspapers and magazines. Paste them into a booklet. Under each, write what it is selling and two adjectives that describe it. Keep the booklet clean and tidy.

### In your mother tongue
Describe an object in your language using colour, size and shape words. Ask your teacher to help you check that the describing words are right.

## PAGE 3: Interpreting an advert
To interpret means to explain what something means. When you read an advert, do not simply believe every word. Ask questions.

### Questions to ask
1. What is being sold?
2. Who is it for?
3. What is the price?
4. Where can I get it?
5. Is it really as good as the words say?

### Word check
Words like best, cheapest and amazing are used to persuade. Persuade means to try to make someone agree or buy. It is wise to check the product before buying.

### Model advert
"SUPER SOAP. The best soap in the world. Makes clothes white. Only 50 shillings. Buy it at Kamau's shop, near the bus stop. Free calendar with every ten bars!"

### Interpretation
1. Product: soap.
2. Price: 50 shillings.
3. Place: Kamau's shop, near the bus stop.
4. Offer: a free calendar with every ten bars.
5. Persuading words: best in the world. A careful shopper may test the soap first.

### Be careful of fake adverts
Some adverts trick people, especially ones that promise to make money quickly or sell things at prices that seem too good to be true. Never send money to strangers. Ask an adult first.

## PAGE 4: What is news?
News is information about recent events. It tells us what happened, where, when, who was involved and why. News comes from newspapers, radio, television and websites.

### A news item has
• A headline: a short title. Example: "School wins football cup."
• The lead: the first sentence that gives the main facts.
• Details: more information.
• Sometimes a quote: the exact words of a person.

### Model news item
"MWANGAZA PRIMARY WINS CUP
Pupils of Mwangaza Primary School won the county football cup on Saturday. They beat Lakeview Primary by two goals to one. The captain, Achieng, said, 'We trained hard every day.' The school will receive a trophy on Monday."

(This is an invented example for practice.)

### Answer questions
1. Who won? (Mwangaza Primary School)
2. What did they win? (the county football cup)
3. When? (Saturday)
4. What was the score? (two goals to one)
5. What did the captain say? (We trained hard every day.)

### Describe objects in news
News sometimes describes objects, such as "a shiny gold trophy" or "a large red truck". Find adjectives in a news story and list them.

## PAGE 5: Reading and presenting news and adverts
### Role play a news bulletin
In groups, act as a news team.
1. Choose a news story about your school or community.
2. One pupil is the newsreader. Others are reporters or interview people.
3. The newsreader gives the headline and reads the item clearly.
4. Describe objects and products using adjectives.
5. Audience listens and asks questions.

### Newsreader tips
• Speak clearly at a steady pace.
• Look at the camera or audience.
• Use a serious but friendly voice.
• Pause at the end of each sentence.

### Make a poster advert
Work with a group to make an advert for a local product, such as fruit, woven baskets or hand-made soap. Include a headline, a picture, adjectives, a price and where to buy. Display the poster.

### Digital devices
If your school has a tablet or a computer, recreate the content of an advert using a simple drawing or word program. If not, use paper and coloured pencils. Present it to the class.

### Read and answer
Read an advert and a news item from a newspaper. Answer four questions on each. Check your answers with a partner.

## PAGE 6: Summary and common mistakes
Adverts tell about products and services and try to persuade us. News informs us about events. We read them with questions in mind, describe objects using adjectives and check facts carefully.

### Remember
• Adverts: headline, picture, description, price, contact.
• News: headline, lead, details.
• Adjectives describe colour, size and shape.
• Ask who, what, where, when, why.

### Common mistakes
• Believing everything an advert says.
• Ignoring the price or the place in an advert.
• Reading news without noticing the date.
• Confusing adverts with news. Adverts try to sell. News tells facts.
• Cutting from a newspaper without permission.

### Final activity
Choose one advert and one news item. Say two ways in which they are different. Then describe the object in the advert using three adjectives.

## QUIZ
1. What is the purpose of an advertisement?
   A) To tell people about a product or service
   B) To give a homework
   C) To end a story
   D) To teach a song
   Answer: A
   Why: Adverts present products and services to buyers.
2. Which of these is an adjective in the advert: Fresh sweet oranges?
   A) oranges
   B) sweet
   C) buy
   D) shop
   Answer: B
   Why: Sweet describes the oranges. Fresh does too, but oranges is a noun.
3. What is a headline?
   A) A short eye-catching title
   B) The last line of a story
   C) The price of a book
   D) A picture
   Answer: A
   Why: A headline is the short title at the top of a news item or an advert.
4. Which is the best thing to do before buying a product that an advert calls the best in the world?
   A) Check the product and the price first
   B) Buy it at once
   C) Send money to a stranger
   D) Ignore everything
   Answer: A
   Why: Adverts use strong words to persuade, so we should check carefully.
5. Read: Bright Bag Sale. Strong school bags in red and blue. Only 600 shillings. Name the product and the price. (2 marks)
   Answer: The product is school bags and the price is 600 shillings.
   Why: One mark for each.
6. List three parts of an advert. (3 marks)
   Answer: Headline, picture, description, price, contact details (any three).
   Why: One mark for each part.
7. Explain one difference between an advert and a news item. (2 marks)
   Answer: An advert tries to persuade people to buy, while a news item reports facts about events.
   Why: One mark for each side of the difference.
8. Write a short advert of three sentences for fresh mangoes. Use two adjectives. (3 marks)
   Answer: For example: Fresh mangoes for sale. They are big, sweet and juicy. Only 100 shillings for five, at the market.
   Why: One mark for a clear product, one mark for two adjectives, one mark for price or place.
