## PAGE 1: Telling about something that happened to you
A personal experience is something that happened to you. It might be the day you first went to the market alone, the time you saved money for a gift, or the day you lost a coin and found it again. When we tell others about these events, we describe experiences.

Describing an event well means the listener can picture it. They should know who was there, where it happened, when it happened, what took place and how you felt.

### Key question
How do we describe events and experiences? We use clear sentences, put the events in order, use words that show time, and speak with feeling.

### Example in English
"Last Saturday I went to the market with my aunt. I had fifty shillings from my birthday. I wanted to buy a notebook. At the shop I counted the money carefully. I bought the notebook and had ten shillings left. I felt proud because I had saved the money."

### Try it
Tell your partner about a time you handled money, such as buying sweets, paying a fare or keeping savings. Keep your story to five sentences.

## PAGE 2: Six questions for a good story
Before you tell a story, ask yourself six questions.

1. Who was there?
2. What happened?
3. Where did it happen?
4. When did it happen?
5. Why did it happen?
6. How did you feel?

### Model
Who: Achieng and her brother.
What: They sold vegetables at a stall.
Where: Near the bus stop.
When: On Saturday morning.
Why: To raise school fees money.
How they felt: Tired but pleased.

### Good storytelling habits
• Start with a clear opening such as "One day" or "Last week".
• Tell events in the order they happened.
• Speak clearly and not too fast.
• Look at your listeners.
• Use your hands and face a little.
• End with what you learned or felt.

### Practise
Choose one experience. Answer the six questions in your head. Then tell your story to a group of four. Fluency means speaking smoothly, so practise once before your turn.

## PAGE 3: The conjunction when
A conjunction is a joining word. It links parts of a sentence. The word when joins two ideas by showing time.

### Examples in English
• "When I reached the shop, it was closed."
• "I felt happy when I counted my savings."
• "When the bell rang, we ran to the market."

### How it works
In each sentence, when connects two events, and shows that one happened at the time of the other. We can put when at the start or in the middle.
"When it rained, we ran home." = "We ran home when it rained."
Notice the comma after the first part when when comes at the start.

### Practice
Join the sentences with when.
1. I was counting money. The phone rang.
Answer: When I was counting money, the phone rang.
2. The trader gave me change. I said thank you.
Answer: When the trader gave me change, I said thank you.

### In your mother tongue
Your language has a way to say when. It may be a single word or a part added to the verb. Ask your teacher or an elder to show you how time is joined in sentences, and write two examples on a chart.

## PAGE 4: The language train game
The language train is a game that builds sentences one after another. It helps you to use when and to keep a story going.

### How to play
1. Sit or stand in a line or circle.
2. The first pupil says a starting sentence, such as "I went to the market."
3. The next pupil continues, beginning with the word when: "When I got there, I saw many people."
4. The next adds: "When I bought tomatoes, the seller gave me one extra."
5. Keep going until the story ends.

### Rules
• Begin each sentence with when.
• Listen to the pupil before you.
• Keep the story making sense.
• Do not laugh at others' ideas.
• If you cannot think of anything, ask your neighbour for a hint.

### Play again in your mother tongue
Use the way your language joins events in time. Your teacher or a classmate who knows the language well can help everyone check the sentences.

### Extend
Try a version where each sentence uses a different joining word, such as and, but or because.

## PAGE 5: Narrating an event with when
Now put together a longer story with several uses of when.

### Model narration
"When I woke up on Saturday, I remembered the market. When I reached the stall, my mother was already selling. When a customer came, I greeted him politely. When he paid me, I counted the money twice. When the day ended, we had sold all the vegetables. I was very happy."

### Recording narrations
If your school has a phone, tablet or recorder, and your teacher agrees, record each pupil's narration. Listen together and say one thing that was good and one thing to improve. Keep the recordings safe.

### Skit about the market
Work in groups to act a short skit about something interesting that happens in a market, such as a customer bargaining or a lost child being found. Plan the characters, the setting and what happens. Use at least two sentences with when. Perform in your mother tongue if you can.

### Money and honesty
In your skits, show good values. For example, a seller who gives the right change or a buyer who is honest.

## PAGE 6: Summary and common mistakes
Describing experiences means telling who, what, where, when, why and how. The word when is a conjunction that joins two events in time. Games and skits help us practise.

### Remember
• Tell events in order.
• Use when to link events.
• Put a comma after a when part at the start of a sentence.
• Speak clearly and with feeling.

### Common mistakes
• Telling the story in the wrong order.
• Starting several sentences with and then over and over.
• Forgetting to say where or when.
• Speaking too quietly.
• Laughing at someone else's story.

### Final activity
Tell a two-minute story about a time you were surprised by something to do with money. Use when at least three times.

## QUIZ
1. What is a personal experience?
   A) Something that happened to you
   B) A story from a book
   C) A rule
   D) A number
   Answer: A
   Why: Personal experiences are events you have lived through.
2. What kind of word is when in the sentence When I reached the shop, it was closed?
   A) A conjunction
   B) A noun
   C) A verb
   D) A number
   Answer: A
   Why: When joins two ideas by showing time.
3. Which sentence is punctuated correctly?
   A) When the bell rang we ran home.
   B) When the bell rang, we ran home.
   C) When, the bell rang we ran home.
   D) When the bell, rang we ran home.
   Answer: B
   Why: A comma follows the when part at the start of a sentence.
4. Which question does NOT help you plan to tell an experience?
   A) Where did it happen?
   B) Who was there?
   C) How did I feel?
   D) How many pages are in my book?
   Answer: D
   Why: The number of pages in a book has nothing to do with the event.
5. Join with when: The trader gave me change. I said thank you. (2 marks)
   Answer: When the trader gave me change, I said thank you.
   Why: One mark for using when correctly, and one mark for the comma.
6. Give three good storytelling habits. (3 marks)
   Answer: Begin with a clear opening, tell events in order, speak clearly, look at listeners, end with what you learned.
   Why: One mark for each habit, up to three.
7. Describe how the language train game is played. (3 marks)
   Answer: One pupil says a starting sentence. Each pupil in turn continues the story with a sentence that starts with when, until the story ends.
   Why: One mark for a starting sentence, one for taking turns, one for using when.
8. Why is it good to record and listen to your own narration? (2 marks)
   Answer: You can hear where you spoke well and what to improve, such as speed or clarity.
   Why: One mark for hearing your voice and one for finding improvements.
