## PAGE 1: Talking about a real experience
Leadership is about guiding others, and good leaders know how to talk and listen. In this lesson you will learn how to take part in a conversation properly and how to describe something that happened to you.

A real-life experience is something that truly happened to you or someone you know. Describing it well means the listener can picture the event.

### Model experience in English
"Last term our class chose a class monitor. The teacher asked us to nominate someone honest and responsible. We wrote names on small papers and put them in a box. Then the teacher counted the votes. Achieng was chosen. I felt happy because I voted for her, and she promised to help everyone."

### What to include
• Who was there
• What happened
• When and where
• How you felt
• What you learned

### Leaders in our communities
Every community has leaders, such as the chief, elders, teachers and class monitors. They make decisions, settle disagreements and guide others. A good leader listens, tells the truth and treats people fairly. People obey leaders because they need order and peace. This links to our key question: Why is it important to obey leaders?

### Try it
Tell your partner about a time you saw a leader do something good. Use four sentences.

## PAGE 2: Turn taking
Turn taking means speaking one person at a time in a conversation and waiting for your chance. It shows respect. If everyone speaks at once, no one can understand anything.

### Rules for turn taking
1. Wait until the speaker finishes before you speak.
2. Raise your hand or signal politely if you want to speak.
3. Keep your turn short so others can also speak.
4. Do not always speak first or longest.
5. Listen to others without making faces or noises.
6. If you are chairing the discussion, invite quiet pupils to speak.

### A simple tool
Use a talking object, such as a small stick or ball. Only the person holding it may speak. When finished, pass it to the next person. In many communities elders use such customs to keep order in meetings. Ask an elder how turns to speak are given in community meetings in your area.

### Model dialogue
Chair: Let us talk about how to keep our classroom clean. Baraka, please begin.
Baraka: I think everyone should pick up litter.
Chair: Thank you. Wanjiku, your turn.
Wanjiku: We could also make a duty roster.
Chair: Good idea. Amina?

### Practise
In groups of five, discuss "How can our class be led well?" Use a talking object.

## PAGE 3: Polite interruption
Sometimes you need to interrupt. Perhaps you did not understand, or something urgent has come up. Interrupting rudely can hurt feelings. Polite interruption means asking for a chance to speak in a courteous way.

### Polite phrases in English
• "Excuse me, may I say something?"
• "Sorry to interrupt, but could you repeat that?"
• "May I add a point, please?"
• "Pardon me, I did not understand. Could you explain?"

### Rude phrases to avoid
• "Shut up!"
• "That is wrong!" (shouted)
• Speaking loudly over the person.

### Steps
1. Wait for a small pause.
2. Raise your hand or make eye contact.
3. Use a polite phrase.
4. Speak briefly.
5. Say thank you and let the first speaker continue.

### Culture
In your community, there are respectful ways of asking to speak, especially to elders. Ask your parents or an elder what is polite in your language. Write down two polite phrases of interruption.

### Listen to a recording
If possible, listen to a recording of a conversation with turn taking and polite interruption. Notice which phrases the speakers use and how they handle silence.

## PAGE 4: Verbs
A verb is a word that shows an action, a happening or a state. Verbs are the heart of a sentence.

### Kinds of verbs
• Action: run, choose, vote, count, lead, listen.
• State: is, are, was, feel, seem.

### Verbs in the audio or passage
From the experience on page 1, the verbs are: chose, asked, nominate, wrote, put, counted, was chosen, felt, voted, promised.

### Verb charts
Listen to a short recording about a leader, or read a short passage. Work with a partner and write every verb you hear on a chart.

### Verbs and time
English verbs show time: walk (now), walked (before), will walk (later). Your mother tongue also shows time, but in its own way, sometimes by changing part of the verb. Listen to a sentence about yesterday and one about tomorrow in your language. What changes?

### Practice
Choose a verb to complete each sentence: listen, lead, vote, share.
1. A good class monitor should ______ the class well. (lead)
2. Pupils ______ for their leader by raising hands or writing names. (vote)
3. We must ______ carefully when the teacher speaks. (listen)
4. A fair leader will ______ duties among all pupils. (share)

## PAGE 5: A panel discussion
A panel discussion is a planned conversation where a small group of speakers, called the panel, discusses a topic in front of an audience. A moderator guides the discussion.

### Steps
1. Choose a topic, such as "How can leaders be chosen fairly in our school?"
2. Choose a moderator and four panel members.
3. The moderator welcomes everyone and introduces the topic.
4. Each panel member gives an opinion in turn.
5. Members may add points using polite interruption.
6. The audience asks questions.
7. The moderator thanks everyone and summarises.

### The moderator says
"Welcome, everyone. Today we will discuss how leaders should be chosen. Please give your opinion in turn and respect each other."

### Assess
Listeners note: Did speakers wait for their turn? Did they use polite phrases? Did they use verbs to describe real experiences?

## PAGE 6: Summary and common mistakes
Good conversation means taking turns, listening and interrupting politely. We describe experiences using verbs. Respect for leaders and each other keeps order in our school and community.

### Remember
• One speaker at a time.
• Use polite phrases to interrupt.
• Verbs show actions and states.
• Keep your turn short.
• Thank the speaker.

### Common mistakes
• Speaking while someone else is speaking.
• Interrupting with rude words.
• Dominating the discussion.
• Not listening because you are planning what to say next.
• Making fun of someone's opinion.

### Final activity
Role play a classroom meeting to choose a class monitor. One pupil chairs, others speak in turn. Each one interrupts politely once. Afterwards list five verbs you used.

## QUIZ
1. What does turn taking mean?
   A) Speaking one person at a time and waiting for your chance
   B) Speaking loudest
   C) Speaking all at once
   D) Refusing to speak
   Answer: A
   Why: Turn taking is speaking in an orderly way, one person after another.
2. Which is a polite way to interrupt?
   A) Excuse me, may I say something?
   B) Stop talking!
   C) You are wrong!
   D) Be quiet!
   Answer: A
   Why: It is courteous and asks permission.
3. Which word in this sentence is a verb: The teacher counted the votes?
   A) teacher
   B) counted
   C) the
   D) votes
   Answer: B
   Why: Counted shows the action.
4. What does a moderator do in a panel discussion?
   A) Guides the discussion and gives turns
   B) Sleeps
   C) Speaks all the time
   D) Leaves the room
   Answer: A
   Why: The moderator welcomes, introduces the topic and keeps order.
5. Give two rules for good turn taking. (2 marks)
   Answer: Wait until the speaker finishes; keep your turn short; do not always speak first.
   Why: One mark for each valid rule.
6. Write two polite phrases you can use to interrupt. (2 marks)
   Answer: Excuse me, may I say something? Sorry to interrupt, but could you repeat that?
   Why: One mark for each polite phrase.
7. Choose a verb: A good class monitor should ______ the class well. Then explain why obeying leaders is important. (3 marks)
   Answer: lead. Obeying leaders keeps order, peace and safety.
   Why: One mark for the verb lead and two marks for a reasoned explanation.
8. Describe how a panel discussion is organised. (4 marks)
   Answer: Choose a topic, choose a moderator and panel members, panel members give opinions in turn, the audience asks questions, and the moderator summarises and thanks everyone.
   Why: One mark for each of four correct steps.
