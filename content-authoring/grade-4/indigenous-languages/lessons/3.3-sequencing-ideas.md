## PAGE 1: Ideas in order
A sequence is the order in which things happen or are told. When you tell how to bake a cake, you cannot say "Eat the cake" before "Mix the flour". The steps would not make sense. Good writing puts ideas in order so that the reader can follow.

A paragraph is a group of sentences about one main idea. Each sentence adds something to that idea. The first sentence often tells the main idea, the next give details and the last may close the topic.

### Key question
Why should we sequence ideas in a story or paragraph? Because a reader who meets ideas in a muddle gets lost.

### Try it
These sentences are in the wrong order. Number them in the right order.
___ Then he put on his shoes.
___ Kamau woke up early.
___ Finally he left for school.
___ He washed his face and had breakfast.
Correct order: Kamau woke up early (1), He washed his face and had breakfast (2), Then he put on his shoes (3), Finally he left for school (4).

## PAGE 2: Finding the main idea
The main idea is what the paragraph is mostly about. The other sentences give details.

### Model paragraph
"Fire can be dangerous, so we must handle it with care. Never play with matches. Keep the paraffin lamp away from the curtains. Ask an adult to light the stove. If clothes catch fire, stop, drop and roll on the ground."

Main idea: Fire is dangerous and we must handle it with care.
Details: never play with matches, keep the lamp away from curtains, ask an adult to light the stove, stop drop and roll.

### How to find the main idea
1. Read the paragraph twice.
2. Ask: what is every sentence about?
3. Look at the first and last sentences, which often carry the main idea.
4. Say the main idea in one short sentence.

### Peer review
In pairs, cut out a short paragraph from a newspaper or magazine. Underline the main idea and write it on a strip of paper. Show your partner. Do you agree? Talk about it politely.

### Common sequence words
• First, to begin with
• Next, then, after that
• Later, meanwhile
• Finally, at last, in the end

### Use with commas
Put a comma after most starting words: "First, boil the water." "Finally, serve the tea."

### In your mother tongue
Your language also has words for first, next and finally. Ask your teacher or an elder how they are said and how they fit into sentences. Make a list of them on a chart.

## PAGE 4: Verbs in sentences
Verbs are the action words. Every full sentence needs a verb. To sequence ideas well, you need to choose the right verbs.

### Finding verbs
In the sentence "Kamau washed his face", the verb is washed. In "The children clap and sing", the verbs are clap and sing.

### Newspaper hunt
With a partner, take a short news article. Underline all the verbs. Write them on a list. Notice how many verbs appear in one paragraph.

### Verbs and order
Verbs also show when something happens: past, present or future. In English we say walk, walked, will walk. In a story about what happened, we usually use the past. Keep the same time all through the paragraph. Do not mix them without reason.

### Digital or card game
If you have a computer or tablet, use a drag and drop game to join word parts into sentences. Without a device, cut cards with word parts such as "The boy / runs / to school" and join them in the correct order.

## PAGE 5: Reordering and writing a paragraph
Now practise with a jumbled paragraph.

### Jumbled sentences
a. She rolled the dough flat and cut it into small pieces.
b. Mama Achieng mixed flour, water and sugar to make dough.
c. The mandazi were golden and ready to eat.
d. She fried the pieces in hot oil until they turned brown.

Correct order: b, a, d, c.
Rewritten: "Mama Achieng mixed flour, water and sugar to make dough. She rolled the dough flat and cut it into small pieces. She fried the pieces in hot oil until they turned brown. The mandazi were golden and ready to eat."

Notice how each sentence depends on the one before it. You cannot fry the pieces before you cut them, and you cannot eat the mandazi before they are cooked.

### Write your own
Write a paragraph of five sentences on how to keep safe near a fire, or how to prepare a simple food.
1. Begin with a main idea sentence.
2. Add steps in order using first, next, then, finally.
3. Check that each sentence has a verb.
4. Read it aloud to see if it flows.

### Publishing
Swap your paragraph with a friend for comments. Then copy the final version neatly for the class or school magazine.

## PAGE 6: Summary and common mistakes
A paragraph has a main idea and details in a sensible order. Sequence words and correct verbs help the reader to follow. Checking and rearranging make writing better.

### Remember
• One paragraph, one main idea.
• Use words such as first, next, then and finally.
• Every sentence needs a verb.
• Read aloud to check the order.

### Common mistakes
• Putting the ending before the beginning.
• Mixing two topics in one paragraph.
• Forgetting the commas after sequence words.
• Changing from past to present by accident.

### Final activity
Write four sentences about your morning routine. Cut them into strips and mix them up. Give them to a partner to arrange. If the partner puts them in the wrong order, ask yourself if the sequence words were clear enough.

## QUIZ
1. What is a paragraph?
   A) A group of sentences about one main idea
   B) A single word
   C) A picture
   D) A long title
   Answer: A
   Why: A paragraph develops one main idea with several sentences.
2. Which word shows the last step in a sequence?
   A) First
   B) Next
   C) Then
   D) Finally
   Answer: D
   Why: Finally is used for the last step.
3. Which sentence should come first in a paragraph about washing hands?
   A) Then dry your hands with a clean cloth.
   B) Finally, put the towel away.
   C) First, wet your hands with clean water.
   D) After that, rinse well.
   Answer: C
   Why: First begins the sequence and wetting the hands starts the process.
4. Which word in this sentence is a verb: The children sing and clap?
   A) The
   B) children
   C) and
   D) sing
   Answer: D
   Why: Sing shows an action, so it is a verb. The other options are a naming word and two small joining or pointing words.
5. Put these sentences in order: (a) Finally, serve the tea. (b) First, boil the water. (c) Next, add the tea leaves. (2 marks)
   Answer: b, c, a
   Why: One mark for starting with b and one mark for ending with a.
6. Write the main idea of this paragraph: Matches are dangerous. Children must not play with them. Keep them out of reach. Ask an adult to light the fire. (2 marks)
   Answer: Matches are dangerous and must be handled with care.
   Why: One mark for naming matches, one mark for the idea of danger or care.
7. List three sequence words and use one in a sentence. (3 marks)
   Answer: First, next, then, finally. Example: First, I wash my hands.
   Why: Two marks for three words, one mark for a correct sentence.
8. Explain why sequencing ideas is important in writing. (3 marks)
   Answer: It helps the reader follow the ideas, prevents confusion and makes the writing clear and logical.
   Why: One mark for each valid point.
