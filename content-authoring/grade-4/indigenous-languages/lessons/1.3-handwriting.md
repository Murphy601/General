## PAGE 1: Why neat handwriting matters
Writing is a way of sending a message to someone who is not with you. If the letters are messy, the reader may not understand your message. A shopkeeper who cannot read a customer's list may give the wrong goods. A teacher who cannot read your answer cannot give you the marks you deserve.

Legible writing means writing that can be read easily. Neat writing means writing that is clean, tidy and well arranged on the page. Good handwriting shows respect for the reader and for yourself. Pupils who write neatly also feel proud of their work, and this builds confidence.

### Key question
Why is it important to write legibly and neatly? Talk with a partner and give two reasons. Then compare your reasons with those of another pair.

### Facts to remember
• A message has value only if it can be read.
• Neat writing saves time because you do not need to rewrite.
• Everyone can improve with practice.

## PAGE 2: The letters of your language
Every written language uses letters or signs to show its sounds. Many Kenyan languages use the same Roman alphabet as English, but some add extra marks or special letters, and each language has its own order and sounds. Your teacher will show you the letters of the language your class is learning.

### Getting to know each letter
For every letter, learn three things.
1. Its shape: where you start, and which way the pencil moves.
2. Its size: tall letters, short letters and letters that go below the line.
3. Its sound: what you say when you see it.

### Ways to practise
• Trace the letter in the air with your finger.
• Trace it on a card with a pencil.
• Model it using clay or strings.
• Paint it with a brush or with a finger dipped in paint.
• Write it many times on a line and choose your best one.

Use the alphabet chart in your classroom. Point to a letter and say its sound. Let your partner check if you are right.

### Rules for tidy writing
• Tall letters touch the top line. Short letters reach the middle line.
• Letters that hang down go below the base line.
• Leave a small gap between letters that is the same each time.
• Leave the width of one finger between words.
• Start each sentence on the left and keep your writing on the line.

### Sitting and holding
Sit up straight. Hold the pencil lightly between your thumb and first finger, resting on the middle finger. Do not press so hard that the page tears.

## PAGE 4: Forming simple words and sentences
When you can write letters well, join them into words and words into sentences. Choose words on a theme, such as cultural foods, so the writing has meaning.

### Steps
1. Choose a word, for example the English word maize.
2. Say the sounds slowly.
3. Write the word carefully, checking each letter.
4. Read it aloud to be sure it is correct.

A sentence is a group of words that makes complete sense. It begins with a capital letter and ends with a full stop. Here are examples in English that you can copy neatly and then write in your mother tongue with the help of your teacher.

"We eat maize." "Mother cooks beans." "The food is hot."

### Fill in the blank
Complete each sentence with a word from the box: (rice, water, market)
1. We buy vegetables at the ______.
2. Father boils ______ for the tea.
3. The children eat ______ for lunch.
Answers: market, water, rice.

## PAGE 5: Sentence cards, copying and gallery walks
Copying well is a useful skill. When you copy a sentence, look at the whole word, not letter by letter, then write it from memory, then check.

### Sentence card game
Your teacher prepares cards, each with one simple sentence. Pupils fish out a card, read it, and copy it neatly in their books. A partner checks the copy: are the letters the right size? Are the words spaced? Is there a capital letter and a full stop?

### Gallery walk
Write your best sentence on a strip of paper. Pin the strips on the wall. Walk around quietly and look at each one. Leave a small note that says something kind and helpful, such as "Your spacing is very even."

### Writing competition
In a friendly class competition, everyone copies the same sentence. The judges look at four things: size, spacing, straight lines and cleanliness. Everyone who improves gets a clap, not only the winner.

## PAGE 6: Summary and common mistakes
Good handwriting begins with knowing the letters and how to form them. It grows with practice. You have learned to trace, model and paint letters, to keep letters the right size, to leave spaces between words, and to write and copy sentences neatly.

### Checklist for neat writing
• Am I sitting well and holding my pencil properly?
• Do my letters sit on the line?
• Are tall and short letters different?
• Are my spaces even?
• Did I begin with a capital letter and end with a full stop?

### Common mistakes
• Making letters too big or too small.
• Writing on top of other words when you make a mistake. Rub out gently or draw one neat line through it.
• Crowding words together.
• Rushing at the end of a page.

### Practice
Copy this sentence three times, each time a little better: "Good handwriting helps others to read my work." Then choose your best line and colour a star beside it.

## QUIZ
1. What does legible writing mean?
   A) Writing that can be read easily
   B) Writing very quickly
   C) Writing in coloured ink
   D) Writing only capital letters
   Answer: A
   Why: Legible means easy to read.
2. Where do tall letters such as b reach in a lined book?
   A) Below the base line
   B) The top line
   C) The margin
   D) They stay in the middle
   Answer: B
   Why: Tall letters go up to the top line while short letters stay near the middle line.
3. About how big should the space between words be?
   A) No space at all
   B) The width of one finger
   C) The width of your hand
   D) A full line
   Answer: B
   Why: A finger space is enough to separate words clearly without leaving big gaps.
4. Which two things should every sentence have?
   A) A picture and a title
   B) A capital letter at the start and a full stop at the end
   C) Two commas
   D) A number in front
   Answer: B
   Why: A sentence begins with a capital letter and ends with a full stop.
5. Name three ways of practising the shape of a letter before writing it in your book. (3 marks)
   Answer: Trace it in the air, trace it on a card, model it with clay or strings, or paint it.
   Why: One mark for each valid way.
6. Give two reasons why neat handwriting is important. (2 marks)
   Answer: It helps the reader to understand the message, and it shows respect and builds confidence.
   Why: One mark for each sensible reason.
7. Copy this sentence neatly and correctly: we eat maize. (2 marks)
   Answer: We eat maize.
   Why: One mark for a capital W and one mark for the full stop.
8. Describe what to check when your partner marks a copied sentence. (3 marks)
   Answer: Check that the letters are the right size, the words are spaced, and there is a capital letter and full stop.
   Why: One mark for each of three correct checks.
