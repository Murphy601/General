## PAGE 1: Reading about leisure
When we read a passage, we look for ideas, feelings and information. Leisure activities are a good topic because everyone has some experience of them.

### Model passage
"Saturday was a special day for Wanjiru and her brother, Kamau. After finishing their chores, they went to the field near the river. 'Hurray! No school today!' shouted Kamau. They flew a kite made from paper and sticks. 'Wow! Look how high it goes!' cried Wanjiru. Suddenly the string broke. 'Oh no! The kite is falling!' shouted Kamau. They ran after it and found it in a tree. 'Oops, it is torn,' said Wanjiru sadly. Their grandfather laughed and helped them mend it. 'Aha! Now it is stronger than before,' he said."

### First reading
Read the passage silently. What is it mainly about? It is about a brother and sister enjoying a kite on a free day.

### Second reading
Read it again and answer:
1. Where did the children go? (to the field near the river)
2. What did they make the kite from? (paper and sticks)
3. What went wrong? (the string broke)
4. Who helped them? (their grandfather)

### Try it
Ask your teacher to read the passage aloud. Notice the changes in the voice when the interjections come.

## PAGE 2: Taking notes on the main ideas
Notes are short records of important points. They help you remember what you read.

### How to take notes
1. Read the passage.
2. Underline important words.
3. Write short phrases, not whole sentences.
4. Number the points in order.

### Notes on the kite passage
1. Saturday, no school.
2. Children go to field near river.
3. Fly a paper kite.
4. String breaks, kite falls.
5. Kite torn and mended by grandfather.

### Main idea
The main idea is: Children enjoy a kite on Saturday, and their grandfather helps when it breaks.

### Practise
Take a short passage about leisure from a book or from your teacher. Write four note points. Use them to tell the passage aloud to your partner in your own words.

### Note-taking in your mother tongue
You can also take notes in your mother tongue. Make sure to write only key words and to use correct spelling for the words you know. Ask for help with spelling if you are not sure.

## PAGE 3: Finding interjections in a text
Interjections in writing are usually followed by an exclamation mark or a comma. Often they appear at the start of a sentence or in speech.

### Find them in the kite passage
• Hurray!
• Wow!
• Oh no!
• Oops
• Aha!

### Punctuation
An exclamation mark shows strong feeling: "Wow!" A comma after a mild interjection shows a softer feeling: "Oops, it is torn."

### Practise
Find the interjections in these sentences.
1. "Ah! I forgot my book."
2. "Well, we will try again."
3. "Bravo! You have done it."
4. "Ugh! The smell is terrible."
Answers: Ah, Well, Bravo, Ugh.

### Underline and read
Choose a passage. Underline each interjection. Read it aloud and say each with the feeling it shows.

## PAGE 4: What do interjections mean?
Interjections show feelings. Here is a chart of common English interjections and the feelings they show.

Wow: surprise or amazement
Hurray: joy
Oh no: worry or disappointment
Ouch: pain
Yuck or Ugh: disgust
Oops: a small mistake
Aha: sudden understanding
Bravo: praise
Shh: a request for silence

### In your community
People in your community also use sounds and short words to show feelings. These may differ from English ones. Listen to elders and friends. When are they used? What feelings do they show? On flashcards, write each interjection on one side, and the feeling it shows on the other side. Do not guess a meaning. Ask.

### Reading with the right intonation
Read the interjections with the right voice. "Ouch!" is sharp. "Hurray!" is loud and bright. "Aha!" rises as you understand something.

### Discuss
Talk with a partner about why interjections are used more in speech than in formal writing. They show quick feelings, and in a news report or a letter we usually use full sentences instead.

## PAGE 5: Using interjections in oral paragraphs
Now use interjections in speaking a short paragraph.

### Oral paragraph model
"Yesterday, I went fishing with my uncle. Hurray! I caught my first fish. Wow, it was big! Then Oops, I dropped it back in the water. Oh no! But my uncle laughed and helped me try again. Aha! I caught another one."

### How to prepare
1. Choose a leisure activity.
2. Think of three moments: something exciting, something surprising and something that went wrong.
3. Choose an interjection for each.
4. Say your paragraph aloud.
5. Ask your partner if the feelings sounded real.

### Reading competition
In a reading competition, each pupil reads a paragraph with interjections. The judges give marks for clear reading, correct use of intonation for each interjection, and steady pace. Everyone gets a turn.

### Be careful
Interjections should not be rude. Do not use ones that insult or hurt anyone. Use polite, cheerful words.

## PAGE 6: Summary and common mistakes
Reading a passage involves understanding the main ideas, taking notes and noticing special words like interjections. Interjections show feelings and should be read with the right intonation.

### Remember
• Read once for the main idea and again for details.
• Notes are short.
• Interjections show feelings.
• Exclamation marks show strong feelings.

### Common mistakes
• Reading an interjection in a flat voice.
• Writing whole sentences as notes.
• Using interjections that do not match your feeling.
• Ignoring the exclamation mark.

### Final activity
Read a short passage about a leisure activity. Underline the interjections. Say the feeling each shows. Then rewrite one sentence without the interjection and see how the meaning changes.

## QUIZ
1. What does an interjection show?
   A) A sudden feeling
   B) A number
   C) A place
   D) A time
   Answer: A
   Why: Interjections show sudden emotions such as joy or pain.
2. Which punctuation mark often follows an interjection that shows strong feeling?
   A) Exclamation mark
   B) Question mark
   C) Full stop
   D) Comma
   Answer: A
   Why: An exclamation mark shows strong feeling.
3. Which interjection would you use when something you smell is disgusting?
   A) Hurray
   B) Yuck
   C) Bravo
   D) Aha
   Answer: B
   Why: Yuck shows disgust.
4. In the sentence Oops, it is torn, what is the interjection?
   A) Oops
   B) it
   C) is
   D) torn
   Answer: A
   Why: Oops is the small word showing a mistake.
5. Write two note points from the kite passage. (2 marks)
   Answer: Children go to the field near the river; they fly a paper kite; the string breaks; grandfather mends the kite (any two).
   Why: One mark for each short and correct point.
6. Choose the correct interjection for each: (a) a big surprise (b) a small mistake. (2 marks)
   Answer: (a) Wow (b) Oops
   Why: One mark for each match.
7. Why do we read interjections with the right intonation? (2 marks)
   Answer: Because the voice shows the feeling, so the listener understands the emotion.
   Why: One mark for feeling and one for listener understanding.
8. Write an oral paragraph of four sentences on a free day, using two interjections. (4 marks)
   Answer: Any four sentences about a leisure activity with two suitable interjections, such as: Hurray! Today we played football. Wow, I scored a goal. Then Oops, I fell down. My friends helped me up.
   Why: One mark for each sentence, and full marks only if two interjections fit the feelings.
