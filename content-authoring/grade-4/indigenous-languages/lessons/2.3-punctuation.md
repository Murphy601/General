## PAGE 1: Why we need punctuation
Punctuation marks are small signs that help the reader understand our writing. When we speak, we use our voice to show where a sentence stops and where to pause. When we write, we use punctuation.

Read this without any punctuation: "it is raining mama has brought umbrellas we are wet"
It is hard to read. Now read it with punctuation: "It is raining. Mama has brought umbrellas. We are wet."
The second version is clear.

### The marks in this lesson
• The full stop: .
• The comma: ,
• The capital letter: A B C

Most Kenyan languages that are written with the Roman alphabet use these same marks in much the same way as English. Your teacher will show you any special rules used in the writing of your mother tongue.

### Key question
What is the importance of punctuation in writing? Discuss with a partner. Give an example of a sentence that is confusing without punctuation.

## PAGE 2: The full stop
A full stop is a small dot that shows the end of a statement. It tells the reader to stop and take a breath. Every sentence that tells us something must end with a full stop.

### Examples about the weather
"The sun is hot." "Clouds cover the sky." "A cool wind blows."

### Where not to put a full stop
Do not put a full stop in the middle of a sentence. Wrong: "The wind. Blows the leaves." Right: "The wind blows the leaves."

### Two more uses
• A full stop can follow a short form, such as Mr. or Dr., in English writing.
• Questions and exclamations use other marks, the question mark and the exclamation mark, but a full stop is right for ordinary statements.

### Practice
Add the missing full stops.
1. The rain fell all night
2. We wore warm clothes
3. The river is full
Answers: put a full stop after night, clothes and full.

## PAGE 3: The comma
A comma is a small mark that shows a short pause. It helps us separate things in a list and keep parts of a sentence apart so that the meaning is clear.

### Commas in a list
"In the rainy season we plant maize, beans, peas and potatoes."
Place a comma after each item except the last two. The word and joins the last two.

### Comma after a starting word or phrase
"In the morning, the sky was clear."
"However, it rained later."

### Comma when speaking to someone
"Wanjiku, please bring the umbrella."
"Thank you, Baraka."

### Practice
Add commas: "The weather can be hot cold windy or rainy."
Answer: "The weather can be hot, cold, windy or rainy."

### Check in your language
Ask your teacher if your mother tongue uses commas in the same places. Copy three sentences from a book in that language and underline every comma.

### More on names
Names of people, towns, rivers, mountains, counties and days all begin with a capital letter. Ordinary nouns such as boy, town and river do not, unless they start a sentence.

### Practice
Rewrite with correct capitals: "on monday i will visit my aunt in kisumu."
Answer: "On Monday I will visit my aunt in Kisumu."

### Capitals in your mother tongue
Your mother tongue may follow the same rules or slightly different ones. Look at a written text in your language and discover where capital letters are used.

## PAGE 5: Checking and correcting a passage
Good writers check their work. Read the passage below and find the mistakes.

"it was a cold morning in nyeri the children wore sweaters scarves and socks their mother made hot porridge"

### Steps to correct
1. Read the passage aloud slowly and listen for natural pauses.
2. Put a capital letter at the start of each sentence and on names.
3. Put a full stop at the end of each sentence.
4. Put commas between items in a list.

Corrected: "It was a cold morning in Nyeri. The children wore sweaters, scarves and socks. Their mother made hot porridge."

### Group activity
Work in groups. Write a short paragraph of four sentences about the weather today. Swap your paragraph with another group. They circle every punctuation mark used and put a tick beside correct uses and a cross beside errors. Return the paragraphs and discuss the corrections kindly.

### Create your own
Write a short paragraph on the weather in your area using at least two capital letters for names, three full stops and one comma in a list.

## PAGE 6: Summary and common mistakes
Punctuation makes writing clear. The full stop ends a statement. The comma separates items in a list and shows short pauses. Capital letters begin sentences and names.

### Remember
• Every sentence begins with a capital letter.
• Every statement ends with a full stop.
• Use commas between items in a list.
• Names of people and places start with a capital letter.

### Common mistakes
• Forgetting the full stop at the end.
• Using a capital letter in the middle of a word or for ordinary nouns.
• Putting a comma where a full stop is needed, which joins two sentences wrongly.
• Using too many commas.

### Final check

## QUIZ
1. Which mark shows the end of a statement?
   A) Comma
   B) Full stop
   C) Capital letter
   D) Colon
   Answer: B
   Why: A full stop is used at the end of a statement.
2. Which sentence is punctuated correctly?
   A) We went to Mombasa on Friday
   B) We went, to Mombasa on Friday.
   C) We went to Mombasa on Friday.
   D) We went to Mombasa. on Friday.
   Answer: C
   Why: It begins with a capital, has capitals for Mombasa and Friday, and ends with one full stop. The others have a missing full stop or misplaced marks.
3. Where should a comma go in this sentence: We bought rice beans and flour?
   A) After bought
   B) After rice
   C) After and
   D) After flour
   Answer: B
   Why: In a list the comma separates rice from beans, so it is We bought rice, beans and flour.
4. Which word should begin with a capital letter?
   A) river
   B) Kenya
   C) rain
   D) school
   Answer: B
   Why: Kenya is the name of a country, so it starts with a capital letter.
5. Rewrite with full stops and capitals: the sun is hot we wear hats (2 marks)
   Answer: The sun is hot. We wear hats.
   Why: One mark for capitals and one for full stops.
6. Add commas: The market sells tomatoes onions cabbages and kale. (2 marks)
   Answer: The market sells tomatoes, onions, cabbages and kale.
   Why: One mark for the comma after tomatoes and one for the comma after onions. No comma is needed before the word and.
7. Give three uses of capital letters. (3 marks)
   Answer: The start of a sentence, names of people and places, and the word I and days of the week.
   Why: One mark for each use.
8. Correct this passage: it rained on tuesday the roads were muddy (3 marks)
   Answer: It rained on Tuesday. The roads were muddy.
   Why: One mark for the capitals on It and Tuesday, one for the capital on The, and one for the two full stops.
