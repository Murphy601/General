## PAGE 1: Why clear instructions matter
Instructions tell people what to do and how to do it. We follow instructions when we cook, when we use a machine, when we cross the road and when we take medicine. If instructions are unclear, people may make mistakes or get hurt.

Leaders give instructions too. A class monitor may say, "Line up quietly." A teacher may say, "Open your books to page ten." A road sign tells drivers to stop or to slow down.

### Key question
Why should we give clear instructions? Because people will do the right thing only if they understand exactly what to do. Clear instructions save time, prevent accidents and avoid arguments.

### A poor instruction and a good one
Poor: "Do the thing with the papers."
Good: "Collect the exercise books from every desk and place them on the teacher's table."

### Try it
Give a partner an instruction to draw a simple house. Use only words. Does the drawing match what you imagined? If not, how could your instruction be clearer?

## PAGE 2: Writing clear instructions
Follow these rules.

1. Say what the task is at the top.
2. List what is needed, such as materials.
3. Write the steps in the correct order.
4. Number the steps or use words such as first, then, next, finally.
5. Begin each step with a verb.
6. Use short sentences.
7. Say only one action in each step.
8. Add a warning if something is dangerous.

### Example: How to wash hands
Title: How to wash your hands
You need: clean water, soap.
1. Wet your hands with clean water.
2. Rub soap on your hands.
3. Rub your hands together for twenty seconds.
4. Rinse the soap off.
5. Dry your hands with a clean cloth.

### Verbs
Each step starts with an action word: wet, rub, rinse, dry. These are verbs. A verb used to give a command is often called an instruction verb. Write the verbs in the steps above on a chart.

### In your mother tongue
Say the steps aloud in your mother tongue. Notice which word tells the action in each step.

## PAGE 3: Road signs and picture instructions
Some instructions use pictures and shapes, not words. Road signs help drivers and walkers to stay safe.

### Common signs
• A red eight-sided sign: stop.
• A triangle with a red border: warning.
• A blue round sign: an order you must follow.
• A zebra crossing: a place where people can cross the road.

### Giving instructions about signs
"When you see a zebra crossing, wait at the edge. Check both sides. Cross carefully."
Practise giving such instructions in pairs.

## PAGE 4: Speaking instructions and role play
### Role play giving instructions
In groups, act out situations in which a leader gives instructions.
• A prefect leading pupils to assembly.
• A teacher explaining a safety drill.
• A parent teaching a child to cook porridge.
• A traffic police officer directing a driver.

Each speaker should:
• Use polite, firm words.
• Say each step slowly.
• Use words like first, next and finally.
• Check that the listener understands.

### Model
"Please listen carefully. First, line up in twos. Then walk quietly to the hall. Finally, sit on the mat and wait for the teacher. Do you all understand?"

### Find the verbs
Underline the verbs in the model instruction: listen, line up, walk, sit, wait, understand.

### Check the audience
Let listeners repeat the instructions in their own words. If they cannot, the instructions were not clear enough, and the speaker should try again more slowly.

## PAGE 5: Creating an instruction chart
An instruction chart is a poster that shows steps for a task, using short words and pictures.

### Steps for making a chart
1. Choose a task that people in your school need to know, such as how to keep the classroom clean, how to wash hands, or how to cross a road.
2. Write a big title.
3. List the steps in order.
4. Number the steps.
5. Begin each step with a verb.
6. Add simple drawings or symbols.
7. Use large clear letters so people can read it from a distance.
8. Use colour but keep the text easy to read.
9. Check it for spelling and punctuation.

### Model chart text
HOW TO KEEP OUR CLASS CLEAN
1. Sweep the floor every morning.
2. Wipe the desks.
3. Put waste in the bin.
4. Wash your hands.

### Treasure hunt game
Place a chain of instruction notes around the school. Each note tells the next place to go: "Walk to the big tree. Look under the red stone." The last note leads to a hidden object. Groups follow the chain and see how important clear instructions are.

### Share and assess
Show your chart to another group. They check: Are the steps in order? Is each step clear? Is the writing neat? They give kind feedback.

## PAGE 6: Summary and common mistakes
Clear instructions are short, in order and start with verbs. An instruction chart puts steps on a poster for everyone to read. Leaders should give clear instructions so that people are safe and work is done well.

### Remember
• Title, materials, numbered steps.
• Start each step with a verb.
• One action per step.
• Use first, next, then, finally.
• Check that others understand.

### Common mistakes
• Missing a step.
• Putting steps in the wrong order.
• Giving two actions in one step.
• Writing too small for people to read.
• Forgetting a warning where a task is dangerous.

### Final activity
Make an instruction chart of five steps on how to make a cup of tea, or how to plant a seed. Give it to a friend and ask them to follow it. Were they successful? Improve your chart.

## QUIZ
1. What should the first word of each step in an instruction usually be?
   A) A verb
   B) A number
   C) A colour
   D) A name
   Answer: A
   Why: Instructions tell people what to do, so each step starts with an action word.
2. Which order is correct for washing hands?
   A) Dry, rinse, wet, rub soap
   B) Wet, rub soap, rinse, dry
   C) Rinse, dry, wet, rub soap
   D) Rub soap, dry, wet, rinse
   Answer: B
   Why: You must wet, apply soap, rinse and then dry your hands.
3. What is an instruction chart?
   A) A poster that shows steps for a task
   B) A story about a hero
   C) A list of names
   D) A song
   Answer: A
   Why: It presents steps in order for people to follow.
4. Which word helps show the order of steps?
   A) Finally
   B) Green
   C) Table
   D) Yesterday
   Answer: A
   Why: Finally shows the last step in a sequence.
5. Write the verbs in this instruction: Open your book and read page ten. (2 marks)
   Answer: open and read
   Why: One mark for each verb.
6. Give three rules for writing clear instructions. (3 marks)
   Answer: Put steps in order, begin each step with a verb, use short sentences, write one action per step.
   Why: One mark for each valid rule, up to three.
7. Write four numbered steps for crossing a road safely. (4 marks)
   Answer: 1. Stop at the edge. 2. Look right, left and right. 3. Listen for vehicles. 4. Cross when the road is clear.
   Why: One mark for each correct step in a sensible order.
8. Why should leaders give clear instructions? (2 marks)
   Answer: So that people know exactly what to do, which keeps them safe and saves time.
   Why: One mark for each reason.
