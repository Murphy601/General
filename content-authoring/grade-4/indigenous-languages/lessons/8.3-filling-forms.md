## PAGE 1: What is a form?
A form is a printed or typed page with spaces for people to write information. Forms help organisations and people collect the same kind of information from many people in a neat, fast way.

You may have seen forms at a clinic, a bank, a post office, a shop or a school. When you join a library or a club, you may fill in a form. On a phone or computer, there are forms too, called digital or online forms.

### Why do we fill forms?
• To give information about ourselves.
• To help an office keep records.
• To register for something, such as a club or a competition.
• To order or apply for something.
• To make sure everyone gives the same kind of information in the same place.

### Key question
Why do we fill forms? So that important information is given clearly and kept in order, and so that the right people can find it later.

### Look at a form
Ask your teacher to show you a real form, for example a school admission form or a library card form. Find the title, the boxes and the instructions.

### The parts
1. Title: tells what the form is for.
2. Instructions: tell how to fill it in, for example use a pen, write in capital letters.
3. Fields or spaces: places for information such as name, age and class.
4. Signature: your own handwriting of your name to show the information is true.
5. Date: the day you filled it.

### Common fields
Name, age, date of birth, class, school, address, telephone number, and a parent's or guardian's name.

## PAGE 3: Filling a form correctly
Follow these rules.

1. Read the whole form first.
2. Read the instructions.
3. Use a pen, unless told to use a pencil.
4. Write clearly and neatly inside the spaces.
5. Give true information only.
6. Do not leave a needed space empty. If a question does not apply to you, write N/A, which means not applicable.
7. Check your spelling.
8. Sign and date at the end.
9. Read the whole form again before handing it in.

### Practice with the Class Club Form
Copy the form into your book. Fill it in with your own information. Use capital letters as asked.

### Forms and safety
Do not share your home address, phone number or other personal details on a form unless a trusted adult, such as a parent or teacher, says it is safe. For online forms, always ask an adult first.

### Format
Format means how a form is arranged on the page. A good format has a clear title, enough space for writing, spaces lined up neatly and a logical order, for example, name first, then details, then signature last.

## PAGE 4: Creating a form
You can design your own form. Ask what information you want to collect and why.

### Steps
1. Decide the purpose. Example: to find out classmates' favourite cultural foods.
2. Write a title. Example: Favourite Food Survey.
3. List the questions or fields: name, class, favourite cultural food, how it is cooked.
4. Leave enough space for each answer.
5. Write short instructions at the top.
6. Add a place for a signature and date if needed.
7. Check that it is neat and easy to read.
8. Test it with a friend. Can they fill it in easily?

### Model
FAVOURITE FOOD SURVEY
Instructions: Write neatly in ink.
Name: ______
Class: ______
Favourite cultural food: ______
Who cooks it at home? ______
Date: ______

### Keep in a portfolio
Collect different forms in a folder. Look at how each is arranged. Notice what makes a form easy or hard to fill.

### In your mother tongue
You may also create a form in your mother tongue. Write the field names in that language after checking spellings with your teacher.

## PAGE 5: Sharing and digital forms
### Fill each other's forms
Swap the forms you made with friends. Each friend fills in the information following the instructions. Then return the forms and review them.

### Review questions
• Were the instructions clear?
• Was there enough space?
• Did the questions make sense?
• Is the format neat?
Adjust your form according to feedback.

### Digital forms
An online or digital form works the same way. You type your answers into boxes, choose from lists and click a button to send. If your school has computers, your teacher may help you create a simple digital form using a school program. Remember:
• Only use school-approved programs.
• Do not enter personal information without permission.
• Check your answers before pressing send.

### Real-life forms
Look at real examples: a clinic card form, a library card form or a school competition entry. Discuss why each asks for its information and which fields matter most.

## PAGE 6: Summary and common mistakes
A form is a page with spaces to give information. It has a title, instructions, fields, a signature and a date. We fill forms carefully and truthfully, and we can create forms of our own.

### Remember
• Read before writing.
• Follow the instructions.
• True information only.
• Neat writing and correct spelling.
• Sign and date.

### Common mistakes
• Writing before reading the instructions.
• Leaving needed spaces blank.
• Writing outside the spaces.
• Guessing an answer you do not know.
• Giving private details to a stranger.

### Final activity
Create a form with five fields to collect information about pupils' favourite games. Give it a title and instructions. Ask two friends to fill it in and tell you whether it was clear.

## QUIZ
1. What is a form?
   A) A page with spaces to give information
   B) A kind of song
   C) A type of story
   D) A picture
   Answer: A
   Why: A form has spaces for people to write information.
2. What does a signature show?
   A) Your own handwriting of your name showing the information is true
   B) The price of a form
   C) The colour of the pen
   D) The form number
   Answer: A
   Why: A signature confirms that you agree the information is yours and correct.
3. What should you do first before filling a form?
   A) Read the whole form and the instructions
   B) Fill any space
   C) Sign it
   D) Fold it
   Answer: A
   Why: Reading first helps you to fill it correctly.
4. What does N/A mean when written in a form?
   A) Not applicable
   B) New address
   C) No answer needed always
   D) Next assignment
   Answer: A
   Why: N/A means the question does not apply to you.
5. Name the five parts of a simple form. (3 marks)
   Answer: Title, instructions, fields (spaces for information), signature and date.
   Why: Award 3 marks for all five, 2 marks for three or four, 1 mark for two.
6. Give two rules for filling in a form. (2 marks)
   Answer: Write clearly inside the spaces and give true information only; use a pen; sign and date it.
   Why: One mark for each valid rule.
7. Why should you not share personal details such as your phone number on a form without asking an adult? (2 marks)
   Answer: Someone could misuse the information and it may not be safe.
   Why: One mark for the risk and one mark for asking a trusted adult.
8. Create a form of four fields to find out your classmates' favourite games. (4 marks)
   Answer: Title: Favourite Games. Instructions: Write neatly. Fields: Name, Class, Favourite game, Date.
   Why: One mark for a title, one for instructions, one for at least three fields, one for neat layout.
