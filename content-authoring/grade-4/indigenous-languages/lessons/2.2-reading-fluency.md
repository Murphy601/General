## PAGE 1: What is fluent reading?
A fluent reader reads smoothly, at a steady speed, and with a voice that makes the meaning clear. Imagine listening to a friend who reads every word slowly and flatly, one by one, like a machine. It is hard to follow. Now imagine a reader who reads as if they are telling you a story. You want to listen.

To read fluently you need three things: you must know the words, you must read at the right pace, and you must use your voice well. Voice includes intonation and expression, which we will learn in this lesson.

### Two key questions
• How can we ensure we read fluently?
• Why is reading fluently important?

Fluent reading matters because it helps listeners to understand and enjoy a story. It also helps the reader to understand, because a reader who does not stop at every word can think about the meaning.

### Try it
Read this sentence in a flat robot voice: "The rain has come." Now read it as if you are happy. Then as if you are worried. How did your voice change?

## PAGE 2: Intonation, the music of speech
Intonation is the rise and fall of the voice as we speak. All languages use intonation, and each language has its own patterns. In English, a question often goes up at the end, and a statement usually goes down.

### Examples in English
"You are coming to school." (voice falls at the end, a statement)
"You are coming to school?" (voice rises at the end, a question)
"Come here!" (voice is strong, a command)

The same words can mean different things when the intonation changes. This is why we say intonation carries meaning.

### In your own language
Ask your teacher or an elder to say a statement and a question in your mother tongue. Listen. Does the voice go up, down or stay the same? Some languages show questions with special words as well as with the voice, so listen for both.

## PAGE 3: Reading with expression
Expression means showing feelings and meaning in your voice as you read. Happy, sad, angry and afraid feelings each sound different.

### Ways to read with expression
• Change the loudness: softer for a secret, louder for a shout.
• Change the speed: slower for a sad moment, faster for an exciting one.
• Pause at commas and full stops.
• Make different voices for different characters.
• Use your face and hands a little to help.

### Short text to practise
"Suddenly the wind became strong! The clouds turned dark. Everyone ran home. 'Oh no, my clothes are on the line!' cried Amina. 'Quick, help me!'"

Read the first two sentences in a rising, excited voice. Read Amina's words as if she is in a hurry. Use a calm voice for the narrator.

### Important
Reading with expression does not mean shouting. Do not make the voices so strange that the listeners cannot understand. Clear and natural is best.

## PAGE 4: Reader's theatre
In reader's theatre, pupils read a short script aloud without acting out every movement. Each one has a part. The audience enjoys the story through the voices.

### How to do it
1. Choose a short passage about weather.
2. Give each pupil a role: narrator, wind, rain, farmer, child.
3. Read your part silently three times.
4. Mark the words to stress with an underline.
5. Stand or sit in front of the class and read with expression.
6. Listeners give feedback.

### Model passage
Narrator: The sky is grey.
Farmer: I hope the rain will come.
Child: Look, the first drops are falling!
Rain: Tap, tap, tap.

Use your voice to show hope, surprise and the sound of raindrops. You can read it in English first, and then help your group to write and read a version in your mother tongue.

## PAGE 5: Role play: the weather bulletin
Radio and television give weather forecasts. A newsreader speaks clearly, at a steady pace, and with a serious and friendly tone.

### A model bulletin
"Good evening. Here is the weather for tomorrow. In the morning it will be cool and cloudy. By noon the sun will shine and it will be warm. In the evening we expect light rain. Please carry an umbrella. Good night."

### Your task
In groups, take turns to be the newsreader. Read the bulletin with expression. If you have a phone, tablet or recorder, with permission from your teacher you can record it. Play it back and listen. Did the newsreader:
• Speak clearly?
• Pause at full stops?
• Sound friendly and confident?
• Look up at the audience now and then?

Then create a new bulletin for your own area and read it in your mother tongue.

### Share experiences
Talk in your group about a time when you read a story aloud. What went well? What was hard? What would you do differently now?

## PAGE 6: Summary and common mistakes
Fluent reading has three parts: knowing the words, reading at a good pace, and using your voice through intonation and expression. Practice makes it better.

### Remember
• Intonation is the rise and fall of the voice.
• Expression shows feeling.
• Pause at punctuation marks.
• Practise before you read to an audience.

### Common mistakes
• Reading too fast, so the listeners cannot follow.
• Reading word by word with no pauses.
• Reading in a flat voice.
• Shouting or making fun voices that hide the meaning.

### Final activity
Choose two sentences from a story you like. Read each in three ways: happy, sad and angry. Ask your partner to guess the feeling each time.

## QUIZ
1. What is intonation?
   A) The rise and fall of the voice
   B) The spelling of a word
   C) The number of pages in a book
   D) A kind of poem
   Answer: A
   Why: Intonation is the rise and fall of the voice as we speak.
2. In English, what usually happens to the voice at the end of a question?
   A) It rises
   B) It disappears
   C) It stays low and flat
   D) It becomes a whisper
   Answer: A
   Why: Many English questions rise at the end, while statements usually fall.
3. Which is an example of reading with expression?
   A) Reading each word in the same flat voice
   B) Reading very fast without stopping
   C) Reading a sad part slowly and softly
   D) Refusing to read aloud
   Answer: C
   Why: Changing speed and loudness to show feeling is expression.
4. What should a reader do at a full stop?
   A) Pause briefly
   B) Shout
   C) Skip the next word
   D) Start again
   Answer: A
   Why: Punctuation marks guide pauses that help the listener follow.
5. Why is reading fluently important? Give two reasons. (2 marks)
   Answer: It helps listeners understand and enjoy the story, and it helps the reader to understand too.
   Why: One mark for each reason.
6. List three ways of reading with expression. (3 marks)
   Answer: Change the loudness, change the speed, pause at punctuation, use different voices, use your face and hands.
   Why: One mark for each valid way, up to three.
7. Explain how a weather newsreader should sound. (3 marks)
   Answer: The newsreader should speak clearly, at a steady pace and in a friendly and confident tone, pausing at full stops.
   Why: One mark for each of three qualities.
8. What would you do to improve after your classmates review your reading aloud? (2 marks)
   Answer: Practise the parts they mentioned, such as reading more slowly or pausing at full stops, and then read again.
   Why: One mark for using feedback, one for a suitable action.
