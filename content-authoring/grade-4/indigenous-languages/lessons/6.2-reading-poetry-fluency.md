## PAGE 1: What is a poem?
A poem is a piece of writing or speech that uses carefully chosen words, often arranged in short lines, to express feelings, ideas or pictures. Poems can be about anything: people, animals, seasons, school, love, or what happens in your neighbourhood.

Poems are made to be heard as well as read. In many communities, poems are recited or sung at ceremonies, at work and during play. A poem is like music made of words.

### Some features of poems
• Short lines instead of long paragraphs.
• A group of lines is called a stanza.
• Sometimes the ends of lines have the same sound. This is called rhyme.
• Words often paint pictures in our minds.
• Often a strong beat or rhythm.

### A short model poem in English
Be kind to animals
Give them food and water,
Give them shade from the sun,
Every hen and goat and cow
Needs a caring one.

### Key questions
How do we read poems fluently? By knowing the words, using the right pace and using intonation. Why are poems important? They share feelings, teach lessons, entertain and keep language alive.

## PAGE 2: Pace, the speed of reading
Pace is how fast or slow you read. A good reader chooses the pace to suit the poem.

### Guide to pace
• A sad or calm poem is read slowly.
• A happy or exciting poem can be read a little faster.
• Do not rush so that listeners cannot follow.
• Do not read so slowly that the poem drags.
• Pause briefly at commas and a little longer at the end of a stanza.

### Practise
Read the model poem twice.
First, read too fast. Notice how words run together.
Second, read at a steady pace. Notice how much clearer it is.

### Measuring pace
Work in pairs. One pupil recites while the other times how many seconds it takes. Repeat and try to keep the same steady time. A phone or a clock with a second hand will help.

### Note
Good pace is not always the same. If a line says "The rain falls slowly," you may slow down your voice, which makes the meaning felt.

## PAGE 3: Intonation in poems
Intonation is the rise and fall of the voice. In a poem, it helps to show feeling and meaning.

### Ways to use your voice
• Raise your voice on important words.
• Lower your voice for sad or serious lines.
• Use a questioning rise if the line is a question.
• Stress the rhyming words gently.

### Try marking a line
Take one line from a poem. Underline the words you will stress. Draw a slash where you will pause. Read it aloud using your marks.

### Poems in your mother tongue
Every language has poems and songs with their own beat and intonation. Listen to an elder or a recording of a poem in your language. Notice where the voice rises, where it drops and where it pauses. Do not try to copy words from another language into it; the beat belongs to the language.

## PAGE 4: Reciting and performing
To recite means to say a poem aloud, usually from memory.

### Steps for reciting
1. Read the poem many times.
2. Learn a few lines at a time.
3. Mark the pauses and stressed words.
4. Practise in front of a mirror or a friend.
5. Stand straight, look at the audience and speak clearly.
6. Use your hands and face a little.

### Choral recitation
In a choral recitation, a group says the poem together. Everyone must keep the same pace. One pupil may say a line alone, and the group joins on the refrain, which is a line repeated.

### Watching performances
Watch recitations by others, either live in class or on a video if your school has a screen. Notice:
• How does the reciter use the voice?
• Is the pace steady?
• Does the voice change to show feeling?
• Does the reciter look at the audience?

### Peer assessment
After a recitation, classmates say one good thing and one thing to improve. Use kind words.

## PAGE 5: Writing and answering questions on a poem
You can write a short poem of your own on a theme such as animal welfare.

### Steps to compose a simple poem
1. Choose the theme, such as caring for a pet.
2. Think of pictures: what do you see, hear, feel?
3. Write short lines.
4. Try to make some lines end with the same sound, such as day and play.
5. Read it aloud and fix words that do not sound right.

### Model
Feed the hen at break of day,
Give her water, let her play,
Kind hands keep her safe and well,
That is how we care, we tell.

### Answering questions on a poem
Read the poem again and answer these questions.
1. What is the poem about? (caring for a hen)
2. Which words rhyme? (day, play; well, tell)
3. What does the poet want us to do? (feed and care for animals kindly)
4. What is the mood, happy or sad? (happy)

### Spoken word
Spoken word is poetry performed for an audience with strong voice and feeling. Practise and, if you can, record your poem. Keep your recordings in a folder called your portfolio.

## PAGE 6: Summary and common mistakes
Reading poems fluently needs a steady pace, good intonation and clear pronunciation. Recite from memory with expression. Answer questions by looking back at the poem.

### Remember
• Read for meaning first.
• Choose the pace to suit the poem.
• Stress the important words.
• Pause at the end of lines with punctuation.

### Common mistakes
• Reading a poem like a shopping list.
• Rushing through the lines.
• Ignoring the punctuation.
• Forgetting to look at the audience.
• Laughing at another pupil who forgets a line.

### Final activity
Choose four lines of a poem. Mark them with underlines for stress and slashes for pauses. Recite them to a partner. Ask: was my pace too fast, too slow or just right?

## QUIZ
1. What is a group of lines in a poem called?
   A) A stanza
   B) A sentence
   C) A paragraph
   D) A chapter
   Answer: A
   Why: A group of lines in a poem is a stanza.
2. What is pace?
   A) How fast or slow you read
   B) How loud you read
   C) The colour of the page
   D) The number of poems
   Answer: A
   Why: Pace refers to reading speed.
3. Which two words rhyme?
   A) day and play
   B) hen and goat
   C) water and shade
   D) food and cow
   Answer: A
   Why: Day and play end with the same sound.
4. How should you read a sad poem?
   A) Slowly, with a softer voice
   B) Very fast and loud
   C) In a shout
   D) Without stopping
   Answer: A
   Why: A slower, softer voice suits a sad mood.
5. What is a refrain? (2 marks)
   Answer: A line or group of words that is repeated in a poem or song.
   Why: One mark for repeated and one mark for line or words in a poem or song.
6. Give three tips for reciting a poem well. (3 marks)
   Answer: Learn it well, mark pauses and stress, speak clearly, look at the audience, keep a steady pace.
   Why: One mark for each tip, up to three.
7. Read: Feed the hen at break of day, Give her water, let her play. Name the rhyming words and say what the lines ask us to do. (3 marks)
   Answer: The rhyming words are day and play. The lines ask us to feed and give water to the hen and let her play.
   Why: One mark for day and play, and two marks for the meaning of the lines.
8. Explain what peer assessment of a recitation should include. (2 marks)
   Answer: One thing that was good and one thing to improve, said kindly.
   Why: One mark for each part.
