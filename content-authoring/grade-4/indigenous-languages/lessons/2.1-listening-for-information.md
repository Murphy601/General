## PAGE 1: What is an instruction?
An instruction is a set of words that tells someone what to do. "Carry your umbrella" is an instruction. So is "Close the window because it is raining." We hear instructions every day: at home, at school, on the road and on the radio.

When it rains, a good instruction can keep you safe and dry. A weather forecast on the radio may say, "Heavy rain is expected this afternoon. Carry a raincoat and keep away from rivers." Two instructions are hidden in this short news item: carry a raincoat, and keep away from rivers.

### Why clear instructions matter
If the speaker mumbles, or gives the steps in a muddled order, the listener may do the wrong thing. Clear speech makes clear action. This is our key question: why should we give clear instructions in speech?

### Think and share
Talk with a partner. What instructions do you hear each morning before you leave home for school? Say them aloud, using your mother tongue. Then say what might go wrong if the instructions were not clear.

## PAGE 2: Picking instructions out of an oral text
An oral text is anything spoken, such as a story, a message or a radio programme. It may hold facts, opinions and instructions all mixed together. To select instructions, listen for words that tell you to do something. They usually begin with an action word.

### Listen to this model text
"Today the sky is cloudy. Grandmother says the rain will come soon. First, bring the clothes from the line. Then close the doors and windows. After that, put a bucket under the roof to collect water. Remember to stay indoors when it thunders."

Pick out the instructions:
1. Bring the clothes from the line.
2. Close the doors and windows.
3. Put a bucket under the roof.
4. Stay indoors when it thunders.

The sentence "Today the sky is cloudy" is information but not an instruction, because it does not tell anyone to do anything.

### Signal words
Words such as first, then, after that, next and finally show the order of the steps. When you hear them, listen extra carefully.

## PAGE 3: Verbs, the action words
A verb is a word that shows an action or something that happens. Run, bring, close, put and stay are all verbs. Instructions nearly always begin with a verb, so verbs are the key to finding them.

### Finding the verb
Ask: what is the doing word? In "Close the doors" the doing word is close. In "The rain falls" the verb is falls.

### Verbs in the weather text
From the text on page 2, the verbs are: bring, close, put, remember, stay. We can write them on a chart under the heading Verbs.

### Verbs in your mother tongue
Every language has verbs, but they may be built in different ways. In many languages the verb carries extra parts that tell who is acting or when. Do not worry about that yet. For now, your task is only to spot the doing word in a spoken sentence in your language and say it aloud.

### Activity
Work in a group. Take three instructions in your mother tongue about what to do when it rains. Say them slowly. Point at the doing word in each. List the verbs on a chart.

## PAGE 4: Games for giving and following instructions
Games make listening fun. They also show how easily a message can change if we do not listen well.

### Simon says
One pupil is the leader. When the leader says "Simon says, touch your head," everyone touches their head. If the leader gives an instruction without saying "Simon says," nobody should move. Anyone who moves is out. The game teaches you to listen to every word.

### Chinese whisper
Pupils sit in a line. The first pupil whispers a short instruction to the next, such as "Bring the red umbrella to the door." Each pupil whispers it on. The last pupil says it aloud. Is it the same as the first? Usually it has changed. This shows why we must speak clearly and listen closely.

### Rules of both games
• Speak clearly. Do not shout.
• Say the instruction only once.
• Do not repeat it for the person before you.
• Talk about what went wrong after the game, not during it.

Play these games in your mother tongue and in English.

## PAGE 5: Giving instructions well
To give instructions, first know exactly what you want done. Then say the steps in the right order using signal words. Use short sentences. Check that the listener has understood.

### Example: what to do when it is raining
1. First, take your washing indoors.
2. Next, switch off your radio if there is thunder.
3. Then, wait inside until the rain stops.
4. Finally, wear shoes before you walk out.

### Speaking tips
• Look at your listener.
• Speak at a steady pace.
• Use a strong but friendly voice.
• Pause between steps.
• Ask, "Did you understand?"

### Peer review
In pairs, one pupil gives instructions for a simple task, such as how to fold a paper boat or draw a house. The partner does it. Then both discuss: was the instruction clear? What could be improved? Use kind words such as "Your first step was very clear. Please slow down at the end."

## PAGE 6: Summary and common mistakes
Instructions are spoken words that tell us what to do. To select them from an oral text we listen for verbs and signal words. We respond by doing the steps in order. We appreciate clarity because it keeps everyone safe and saves time.

### Remember
• Verbs are the action words.
• Instructions usually begin with a verb.
• Signal words tell the order: first, then, next, finally.

### Common mistakes
• Giving many instructions at once with no pauses.
• Leaving out the order words.
• Doing steps in a different order from what was said.
• Making fun of a classmate who mishears an instruction.

## QUIZ
1. Which of these is an instruction?
   A) The sky is cloudy.
   B) Bring the clothes from the line.
   C) It rained yesterday.
   D) Grandmother is old.
   Answer: B
   Why: It tells someone to do something and begins with the verb bring.
2. What is a verb?
   A) A naming word
   B) A word that shows an action
   C) A word that describes a colour
   D) A joining word
   Answer: B
   Why: Verbs are doing words such as run, close and put.
3. Which words show the order of steps in an instruction?
   A) first, then, next
   B) big, small, tall
   C) mother, father, sister
   D) red, blue, green
   Answer: A
   Why: Signal words show the sequence in which steps should be done.
4. What does the Chinese whisper game teach us?
   A) That whispering is rude
   B) That messages can change if we do not speak and listen clearly
   C) That only teachers give instructions
   D) That games are a waste of time
   Answer: B
   Why: The message changes as it passes on, showing how important clear speech and careful listening are.
5. Write down the verbs in this instruction: Close the window and sit down. (2 marks)
   Answer: close and sit
   Why: One mark for each verb.
6. Give two reasons why instructions should be clear. (2 marks)
   Answer: So the listener does the right thing, and so that people stay safe and no time is wasted.
   Why: One mark for each sensible reason.
7. Give three instructions for what a pupil should do when it starts to rain at school. Use signal words. (3 marks)
   Answer: First, go into the classroom. Then close the windows. Finally, wait for the rain to stop.
   Why: One mark for each clear instruction; the signal words must be used.
8. Explain what to do when you give instructions to a partner and want to check that they understood. (3 marks)
   Answer: Speak clearly and slowly, use short sentences in the right order, and ask if they understood or ask them to say it back.
   Why: One mark for each valid technique.
