# Indigenous Languages Grade 4: notes for human checking

## Approach
- The platform is language-neutral, so lessons teach the language-skill concepts (listening, reading fluency, punctuation, sentence types, oral literature, functional writing) in English, with activities where pupils apply each concept to their own mother tongue with help from the teacher or elders.
- No words or sentences in any specific Kenyan language were written. Where the lesson says how a language forms plurals, questions, interjections, adjectives and so on, it only says that languages differ and tells pupils to ask elders or the teacher.
- 27 lessons cover every sub-strand in the design (1.1 to 9.3, numbering follows the design's theme numbers). The design has no sub-strand 4.1 in the old manifest, so 4.1 (Self-expression: oral narratives, similes, metaphors) was written as a new lesson.
- The sixty-lesson design also lists a 6-lesson "Showcasing" block; it has no content of its own, so no lesson was written for it.

## Please double-check
- Kiswahili proverbs used in lessons 4.2 and 4.3: "Haba na haba hujaza kibaba", "Kidole kimoja hakivunji chawa", "Asiyesikia la mkuu huvunjika guu", "Ukiona vyaelea, vimeundwa". The English glosses are standard, but the gloss for "Ukiona vyaelea, vimeundwa" (success comes from effort and planning) is the least certain and a Kiswahili teacher should confirm it.
- Names in model texts (Otieno, Amina, Mama Neema, Mwangaza Primary and so on) are invented; the news item in 8.2 is labelled as an invented example.
- Road-sign descriptions in 9.3 are simplified (red eight-sided stop sign, blue round order sign, red-bordered triangle warning sign).
- Lesson 7.3 tells pupils not to invent spellings for interjections; teachers should supply the accepted orthography for their language.
- Some short-answer bank items award marks for "any valid" answers; markers should accept sensible alternatives.
