## PAGE 1: Writing tone marks correctly
In pinyin the tone mark is part of the spelling. If you leave it out or put it in the wrong place, the word changes or looks wrong. Let us practise writing tone marks neatly.

### The four marks
ā (first tone): a straight line, like a flat road: ˉ
á (second tone): a line going up from left to right: ˊ
ǎ (third tone): a small V shape: ˇ
à (fourth tone): a line going down from left to right: ˋ

### Write the four tones of "ma"
Write these in your exercise book, three times each.
mā má mǎ mà
Draw the mark clearly above the vowel a, and do not let it touch the letter above or below.

### Where do I put the mark?
Remember the rules.
1. If there is an a, put the mark on a: hǎo.
2. If there is no a, look for e or o: xiè, lóu.
3. If there is iu or ui, mark the second letter: liù, duì.
4. Otherwise, mark the single vowel: nǐ, dà.
When the letter i has a tone mark, do not write its dot: nǐ.

### Check yourself
Copy these words and check with a partner: nǐ, hǎo, jiào, mā, bà, dà.

## PAGE 2: Writing greetings in pinyin
Now write the greetings in pinyin with tone marks. Look at each one carefully before you write.

nǐ hǎo: hello
nín hǎo: hello (polite)
wǒ jiào ______: my name is ______
nǐ ne?: and you?

### A note about capital letters and spaces
• Write pinyin syllables in small letters, except for names.
• The first letter of a name is a capital: wǒ jiào Amina.
• Leave a space between words.
• End a sentence with a full stop, or a question mark for a question.

### Write your introduction
Write this conversation in pinyin, with your own name in the blank.
A: nǐ hǎo!
B: nǐ hǎo! wǒ jiào ______. nǐ ne?
A: wǒ jiào ______.

### Common mistakes
• Leaving out the tone marks.
• Putting the mark above the wrong vowel, for example hāo instead of hǎo.
• Writing the dot on i together with the mark.
Read your work aloud when you finish. If it sounds right, it is probably spelled right.

### Extra practice
Write your friend's name and yours in pinyin letters next to the phrase wǒ jiào. Swap books and check each other's tone marks.

### Practise the strokes
Trace each stroke in the air with your finger, then on your desk, and then on paper. Say the name as you write it.
• 横 héng: from left to right.
• 竖 shù: from top to bottom.
• 撇 piě: from top right down to the left.
• 捺 nà: from top left down to the right.
• 点 diǎn: a short dot pressed downward.
• 提 tí: a short line flicked upward to the right.
• 横折 héngzhé: go across, then turn down.
• 竖钩 shùgōu: go down, then flick a hook to the left.

### Look for strokes in characters
The character 十 has two strokes: 横 and 竖. The character 人 has two strokes: 撇 and 捺. Find these strokes in the characters 你好 too.

## PAGE 4: Stroke order
Every character has a correct order for writing its strokes. Writing in order makes your characters neat and helps you write quickly. The rules are simple.

### Rules of stroke order
1. Top to bottom: write 三 with the top line first.
2. Left to right: write 好 with 女 on the left before 子 on the right.
3. Horizontal before vertical when they cross: write the 横 of 十 first.
4. 撇 before 捺: in 人 write the left stroke first.
5. Outside before inside, and close the box last: in 日, write the left and top first, then the inside line, then the bottom.

Write each character in the figure five times, saying the strokes aloud in order. Count the strokes and compare: 十 has 2, 人 has 2, 大 has 3, 口 has 3 and 日 has 4. Check that the picture has the same number of strokes as you wrote.

## PAGE 5: Writing the greeting characters
Now write the characters for the greetings. Look at the meaning and pinyin of each.

你 nǐ (you): 7 strokes
好 hǎo (good): 6 strokes
我 wǒ (I, me): 7 strokes
叫 jiào (to be called): 5 strokes
呢 ne (question word): 8 strokes

### Write the greeting
你好 nǐ hǎo: hello
我叫 wǒ jiào: my name is
你呢? nǐ ne?: and you?

好 is built from two smaller parts: 女 on the left and 子 on the right. Write the left part first, then the right part. This follows the left-to-right rule.

### Model the characters
Use materials you can find near you to make the characters big. You can use pebbles, sand, cereal grains such as maize, plasticine, clay or thread. Lay out the strokes in the correct order. Take a photo or put your work on display. Then look at your friends' work, and tell them what you like. Say something kind and honest.

### Compose a sentence
Write a full sentence in characters: 你好!我叫____。你呢? Write your own name in pinyin letters in the blank. Use good stroke order.

## PAGE 6: Punctuation and summary
Chinese uses punctuation marks like the ones you know, but they look a little different.

。 the full stop, a small circle, used at the end of a statement.
? the question mark, used at the end of a question.
! the exclamation mark, used after a greeting or a strong feeling.
, the comma, a short pause.
、 the list comma, used between items in a list.
In Chinese handwriting these marks are wider than ours and take the space of one character, for example ？ ！ ，.

### Fill in the missing marks
Add the correct punctuation.
1. 你好 (!)
2. 我叫小明 (。)
3. 你呢 (?)
4. 爸爸、妈妈、哥哥 (、)

### Why handwriting matters
Neat writing helps other people understand you. If your tone marks and strokes are clear, your reader will know what you mean. The person who reads your work may not be there to ask you questions, so you must write carefully.

### Common mistakes
• Writing strokes in the wrong order.
• Joining strokes that should be separate.
• Forgetting punctuation at the end of a sentence.

### Summary
• Tone marks belong above the correct vowel.
• There are eight basic strokes.
• Write from top to bottom and from left to right.
• Use punctuation to end your sentences.

## QUIZ
1. On which vowel does the tone mark go in hǎo?
   A) h
   B) a
   C) o
   D) No vowel
   Answer: B
   Why: When there is an a in the syllable, the mark is placed on the a.
2. Which basic stroke goes from left to right?
   A) 竖 shù
   B) 撇 piě
   C) 横 héng
   D) 点 diǎn
   Answer: C
   Why: 横 héng is the horizontal stroke.
3. How many strokes are in the character 大?
   A) 2
   B) 3
   C) 4
   D) 5
   Answer: B
   Why: 大 has three strokes: 横, 撇 and 捺.
4. In the character 人, which stroke do you write first?
   A) 捺 nà
   B) 点 diǎn
   C) 竖 shù
   D) 撇 piě
   Answer: D
   Why: The rule is 撇 before 捺, so the left stroke comes first.
5. Which mark ends a Chinese statement?
   A) 。
   B) ?
   C) ,
   D) 、
   Answer: A
   Why: The small circle 。 is the Chinese full stop.
6. Write the pinyin for "hello" and "and you?" with the correct tone marks. (2 marks)
   Answer: nǐ hǎo and nǐ ne?
   Why: 1 mark for each correct phrase with tones.
7. Write the stroke order rule for 十 and say how many strokes it has. (3 marks)
   Answer: Horizontal before vertical. First the 横, then the 竖. It has two strokes.
   Why: 1 mark for the rule, 1 mark for the correct order and 1 mark for the number of strokes.
8. Punctuate the sentence: 我叫小明 (statement) and 你呢 (question). (2 marks)
   Answer: 我叫小明。 你呢?
   Why: 1 mark for 。 and 1 mark for ?.
9. Explain why writing tone marks correctly is important. (2 marks)
   Answer: The tone changes the meaning of the word, so a wrong or missing tone mark can give a different word or make the reader confused.
   Why: 1 mark for saying the tone changes the meaning and 1 mark for the effect on the reader.
