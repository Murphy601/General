## PAGE 1: Writing family words in pinyin
You have heard and read the family words. Now you will write them. When we write pinyin, the tone marks must be in the right place. Let us copy each word with care.

爸爸 bàba: father
妈妈 māma: mother
哥哥 gēge: older brother
姐姐 jiějie: older sister
弟弟 dìdi: younger brother
妹妹 mèimei: younger sister
儿子 érzi: son
女儿 nǚ'ér: daughter

### Tone placement practice
Check where the mark goes.
• bàba: the mark is on the a in the first syllable. The second syllable has no mark, because it is light.
• jiějie: the mark is on the e in jiě, because there is an e and no a.
• mèimei: the mark is on the e in mèi, because there is an e.
• nǚ'ér: ü keeps its two dots, and the mark goes above them: ǚ.

### Listening and writing
Your teacher reads the family words aloud in any order. You write each one in pinyin. Then you swap books with a partner, and check that the tone marks are correct.

### Remember
In the words with a light second syllable, we do not write a tone mark on the second syllable. We only write it on the first. That is why bàba and māma each have only one mark.

## PAGE 2: Filling in a family tree
A family tree is a diagram of the family. Write the missing words in the tree.

Imagine that the tree looks like this:
Top: ______ (father) and ______ (mother)
Below: ______ (older brother), ______ (older sister), me, ______ (younger brother), ______ (younger sister)

### Answers
Father: bàba
Mother: māma
Older brother: gēge
Older sister: jiějie
Younger brother: dìdi
Younger sister: mèimei

### Draw your own tree
Draw your own family tree in your exercise book. Write pinyin and, if you like, the characters under each person. Only include the family members who really live with you or whom you want to show. Some families do not have a sister or a brother, and that is fine. Some families have grandparents, cousins and other loved ones as part of the home. Write the names of your family members in your own language beside each person.

### Check your work
Look at your tree. Does each label have the correct tone marks? Ask a partner to check.

## PAGE 3: Stroke by stroke: family characters
Let us look at some family characters and how they are built.

妈 mā (mother): 6 strokes
爸 bà (dad): 8 strokes
哥 gē (older brother): 10 strokes
姐 jiě (older sister): 8 strokes
弟 dì (younger brother): 7 strokes
妹 mèi (younger sister): 8 strokes
家 jiā (home, family): 10 strokes

### A secret in the characters
Look at 妈, 姐 and 妹. All three have the same part on the left: 女. This part means "woman" or "female". You will also see it in the word 好 hǎo. Words for female family members often have 女 in them.
妈 = 女 + 马 (the part 马 mǎ gives a clue to the sound)
姐 = 女 + 且
妹 = 女 + 未
Also, 女儿 (daughter) has 女 at the start.

### Join the strokes
Work in groups. Use strips of paper, straws or sticks for the strokes. Join them to make the characters, following the stroke order. Start with the left part. Display your character for other learners to admire.

### Stroke order reminder
Write from left to right and from top to bottom. When a character has a left part and a right part, write the left part first.

## PAGE 4: Writing sentences with 这是
You can write simple sentences about your family using the pattern:
这是 + 我 + family word。

这是我爸爸。zhè shì wǒ bàba. This is my father.
这是我妈妈。zhè shì wǒ māma. This is my mother.
这是我姐姐。zhè shì wǒ jiějie. This is my older sister.
那是我弟弟。nà shì wǒ dìdi. That is my younger brother.

### New characters
这 zhè: this (7 strokes)
是 shì: is (9 strokes)
我 wǒ: I, my (7 strokes)
那 nà: that (6 strokes)

### Steps for writing a sentence
1. Write the pinyin first.
2. Write the characters under it.
3. Put 。 at the end.
4. Read your sentence aloud to check.

### Try it
Write two sentences about your own family, using 这是 or 那是. Take turns to read them aloud with your partner. Ask your partner: is the sentence correct? Are the tone marks right?

### Word order reminder
In Chinese, the words come in this order: this + is + my + person. There is no word for "a" or "the". So do not write extra words.

## PAGE 5: Writing a paragraph about my family
A paragraph is a group of sentences about one idea. Let us write a short paragraph about a family. Read this model.

我家有五口人。这是我爸爸,这是我妈妈。这是我哥哥,这是我妹妹,这是我。
wǒ jiā yǒu wǔ kǒu rén. zhè shì wǒ bàba, zhè shì wǒ māma. zhè shì wǒ gēge, zhè shì wǒ mèimei, zhè shì wǒ.
There are five people in my family. This is my father, this is my mother. This is my older brother, this is my younger sister and this is me.

New words: 有 yǒu (there is), 口 kǒu (a word used when counting family members), 人 rén (people, person).

### How is it built?
1. The first sentence tells how many people are in the family. This is the main idea.
2. The other sentences give the details, one person at a time.
3. Each sentence ends with 。

### Arrange sentence strips
Your teacher gives strips with these sentences mixed up. Put them in order to make a good paragraph.
这是我妈妈。
我家有四口人。
这是我爸爸。
这是我。
The correct order starts with 我家有四口人。 Then 这是我爸爸。 这是我妈妈。 这是我。

### Write your own
Write a paragraph about your own family. Count the people and use 我家有___口人 with the right number. Use only words that you know.

## PAGE 6: Pinyin, characters and our own writing
Chinese uses characters. Pinyin uses the same letters as we use in Kiswahili and English. Compare them.

### Compare
爸爸 = bàba in pinyin = baba in Kiswahili.
妈妈 = māma in pinyin = mama in Kiswahili.

The sounds are similar, but the writing is very different. A character is a picture-like symbol. A Kiswahili word is written with letters that show sounds. Pinyin helps us to bridge the two systems.

### Why learn characters?
• Characters keep the same meaning across many spoken Chinese languages.
• Each character has its own history and meaning.
• You can recognise characters even before you know how to say them.
• Chinese written texts, such as books and street signs, use characters.

### Talk about it
Which do you find easier: characters or pinyin? Why? Which is more useful? Remember, both are important. Pinyin helps us say the sounds, and characters give us the words.

### Common mistakes
• Putting a tone mark on the light second syllable in bàba.
• Writing the strokes of 妈 in the wrong order.
• Forgetting the full stop 。 at the end.

### Summary
You can write family words in pinyin with correct tone marks and in characters with correct strokes. You can write sentences and a short paragraph about your family.

## QUIZ
1. Which part is common to the characters 妈, 姐 and 妹?
   A) 马
   B) 女
   C) 且
   D) 未
   Answer: B
   Why: 女 means woman or female and appears in all three characters.
2. Where does the tone mark go in mèimei?
   A) On the m
   B) On the i
   C) On the e of mèi
   D) On the last syllable
   Answer: C
   Why: In mèi the vowels are e and i. There is an e, so the mark goes on the e. The second syllable is light and has no mark.
3. Which sentence means "This is my older sister"?
   A) 这是我姐姐。
   B) 这是我弟弟。
   C) 那是我妈妈。
   D) 我家有五口人。
   Answer: A
   Why: 姐姐 is older sister.
4. What does 我家有五口人 mean?
   A) I have five brothers
   B) There are five people in my family
   C) My house has five rooms
   D) I am five years old
   Answer: B
   Why: 有 is there is, 五口人 is five people and 我家 is my family.
5. How many strokes does 妈 have?
   A) 4
   B) 5
   C) 6
   D) 8
   Answer: C
   Why: 妈 has 6 strokes: 女 has three and 马 has three.
6. Write the pinyin for 爸爸, 哥哥 and 妹妹. (3 marks)
   Answer: bàba, gēge, mèimei.
   Why: 1 mark for each word with correct tones and no mark on the second syllable.
7. Write a sentence in Chinese and pinyin: "This is my mother." (2 marks)
   Answer: 这是我妈妈。 zhè shì wǒ māma.
   Why: 1 mark for the characters and 1 mark for the pinyin.
8. Say one similarity and one difference between writing 妈妈 and writing mama in Kiswahili. (2 marks)
   Answer: Similarity: they sound alike and mean the same. Difference: 妈妈 is written with characters but mama is written with letters that show the sounds.
   Why: 1 mark for the similarity and 1 mark for the difference.
9. Give two reasons why it is important to learn Chinese characters. (2 marks)
   Answer: For example, they keep the same meaning across regions, they appear on books and signs, and they have their own meaning and history.
   Why: 1 mark for each sensible reason.
