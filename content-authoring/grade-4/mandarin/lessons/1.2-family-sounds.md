## PAGE 1: Members of my family
Your nuclear family is the group of people closest to you at home, usually parents and children. In Mandarin the words for family members are easy to hear and say. Listen and repeat each one after your teacher.

爸爸 bàba: father, dad
妈妈 māma: mother, mum
哥哥 gēge: older brother
姐姐 jiějie: older sister
弟弟 dìdi: younger brother
妹妹 mèimei: younger sister
儿子 érzi: son
女儿 nǚ'ér: daughter
家 jiā: home, family

Notice something special. Mandarin has different words for an older brother and a younger brother, and for an older sister and a younger sister. In English we just say "brother" or "sister". In Chinese families it matters whether the person is older or younger than you.

### Sing along
Choose a tune you know well, such as "Twinkle, Twinkle, Little Star". Put the family words into the song: bàba, māma, gēge, jiějie, dìdi, mèimei. Sing slowly at first and make sure each tone is clear.

## PAGE 2: Same letters, different tones
When you listen to Mandarin, you must catch the tone as well as the sounds. The family words teach this well, because two very common family words differ only in tone.

Group 1: the syllable "mā"
mā 妈 mother
má 麻 hemp (a plant)
mǎ 马 horse
mà 骂 to scold

Group 2: the syllable "bā"
bā 八 eight
bá 拔 to pull out
bǎ 把 a word used for things with handles
bà 爸 dad

### Listening game: choose the right pinyin
Your teacher says a word, and you choose the correct syllable. If you hear the word for "mother", you point to mā. If you hear "dad", you point to bà. If you hear "horse", it is mǎ.

Try this with a partner. One says a syllable aloud with a tone, and the other writes down or points to the right one. Change roles after five turns.

### Sort by sound
Cut small cards with the syllables bā, mā, dì, mèi, gē, jiě on them. Sort them by initial (b, m, d, g, j) and then by final. Say each card aloud as you place it.

### What to notice
Two words can have the same initial and final and still be totally different words. Only the tone tells them apart.

## PAGE 3: Doubling syllables in Chinese and Kiswahili
Did you notice that many family words repeat a syllable? 爸爸 bàba, 妈妈 māma, 哥哥 gēge, 姐姐 jiějie, 弟弟 dìdi, 妹妹 mèimei. In Mandarin the second syllable is said in the light neutral tone. The first syllable keeps its tone.

Kiswahili also uses repeated syllables for family words.

Chinese          Kiswahili
bàba (dad)       baba
māma (mum)       mama
gēge, dìdi       kaka (brother)
jiějie, mèimei   dada (sister)

### Compare and contrast
• Alike: both languages double a syllable in words for close family, and both make the words easy for small children to say.
• Different: Kiswahili baba and mama are said with equal beats, while in Mandarin we hear a tone and then a light neutral syllable.
• Different: Mandarin has separate words for older and younger brothers and sisters, but Kiswahili uses kaka and dada for both.

### Think about it
Which family words in your mother tongue repeat a syllable? Write two of them. You may find that many languages of the world use "ma" and "ba" or "pa" for parents. Language is a bridge between people.

## PAGE 4: Who is this? Asking with 这 and 那
Now we can make sentences. Two small words help us point.
这 zhè: this (near me)
那 nà: that (far from me)
是 shì: is, are
谁 shéi: who

Question: 这是谁? zhè shì shéi? (Who is this?)
Answer: 这是我妈妈。zhè shì wǒ māma. (This is my mum.)
Question: 那是谁? nà shì shéi? (Who is that?)
Answer: 那是我哥哥。nà shì wǒ gēge. (That is my older brother.)

### How the sentence is built
Order: 这 + 是 + who or what.
Notice that the question word 谁 stays in the same place as the answer will be. Just swap 谁 for the person.
我 wǒ means "my" here. For example 我妈妈 means "my mum".

### Practice with pictures
Hold up a picture of a family member. Your partner asks: 这是谁? You answer with the correct sentence. Then point to a picture far away and ask 那是谁?

### Sound check
Watch the tones: zhè and nà are both falling (fourth tone). shì is falling too. shéi rises. Say the question a few times, and your voice will fall, fall, fall, rise.

## PAGE 5: Introducing my own family
You can now introduce your family in a short speech. Here is a model. Read it, then change the words to match your own family.

我家有爸爸、妈妈、哥哥和我。
wǒ jiā yǒu bàba, māma, gēge hé wǒ.
My family has dad, mum, an older brother and me.

New words: 有 yǒu (to have), 和 hé (and).

### Steps for a clear introduction
1. Practise each word slowly, with its tone.
2. Say the whole sentence at a steady pace.
3. Look at your listener and speak clearly.
4. Ask the listener to repeat back what you said.

### Take turns
Stand in pairs. Person A says who is in their family. Person B says "这是谁?" while pointing at a picture card of a family member. Then swap.

### Respect for every family
Families in Kenya can be big or small. Some children live with grandparents, aunts or uncles. Every family is valuable. When friends introduce their family, listen politely and clap for them.

## PAGE 6: Speaking Chinese clearly
What can you do to speak Chinese clearly? Here are strategies that help.

• Listen first. Hear the word several times before you say it.
• Copy the tone with your hand. Move it flat, up, down-up or down.
• Say it slowly, then speed up.
• Exaggerate the puff of air for p, t, k.
• Record yourself and compare with your teacher.
• Ask a friend for kind feedback.

### Review the words
爸爸 bàba, 妈妈 māma, 哥哥 gēge, 姐姐 jiějie, 弟弟 dìdi, 妹妹 mèimei, 儿子 érzi, 女儿 nǚ'ér.

### Common mistakes
• Saying mā (mother) when you mean mǎ (horse).
• Forgetting the neutral tone in the second syllable, for example saying bà-bà with two strong tones.
• Mixing up gēge (older brother) and dìdi (younger brother).

### Summary
Chinese family words teach us about tones and doubled syllables. Kiswahili has doubled syllables too, so you already know a similar idea. Speak clearly, listen carefully and respect how others speak.

## QUIZ
1. What does 妹妹 mèimei mean?
   A) Older sister
   B) Mother
   C) Younger sister
   D) Daughter
   Answer: C
   Why: mèimei is a younger sister. Older sister is jiějie, mother is māma and daughter is nǚ'ér.
2. Which syllable means "horse"?
   A) mā
   B) má
   C) mǎ
   D) mà
   Answer: C
   Why: mǎ 马 is horse. The third tone dips and rises.
3. How do you ask "Who is this?" in Mandarin?
   A) 这是什么?
   B) 那是我妈妈。
   C) 我家有爸爸。
   D) 这是谁?
   Answer: D
   Why: 这是谁? zhè shì shéi? asks who a person is. 谁 means who.
4. Which Kiswahili word is like Mandarin 爸爸 bàba?
   A) baba
   B) dada
   C) kaka
   D) mama
   Answer: A
   Why: Baba means father in both languages. Mama matches māma and dada matches sister words.
5. What is one difference between Chinese and Kiswahili family words?
   A) Kiswahili has no family words
   B) Chinese has separate words for older and younger brothers
   C) Chinese never repeats a syllable
   D) Kiswahili uses tones on every word
   Answer: B
   Why: Mandarin has gēge and dìdi, but Kiswahili uses kaka for both.
6. Write the answer to "这是谁?" when you point to your mother. Give the pinyin as well. (2 marks)
   Answer: 这是我妈妈。 zhè shì wǒ māma.
   Why: 1 mark for the correct sentence and 1 mark for correct pinyin with tone marks.
7. Name the four family words that repeat a syllable and give their English meanings. (3 marks)
   Answer: Any four of bàba (dad), māma (mum), gēge (older brother), jiějie (older sister), dìdi (younger brother), mèimei (younger sister). Marks for three or four correct.
   Why: 1 mark for two correct, 2 marks for three, 3 marks for all four with meanings.
8. Explain two ways Chinese and Kiswahili family words are alike or different. (2 marks)
   Answer: Alike: both repeat syllables in words for close family. Different: Chinese has older and younger words for siblings but Kiswahili uses kaka and dada.
   Why: 1 mark for each correct comparison.
9. Give two strategies you can use to speak Chinese clearly. (2 marks)
   Answer: For example listen first, use your hand to copy the tone, speak slowly then faster, or record yourself.
   Why: 1 mark for each sensible strategy.
