## PAGE 1: Taking words apart
When you read a Mandarin word in pinyin, you can take it apart into an initial, a final and a tone. Doing this makes reading much easier, because you can sound out any new syllable in the same way that you sound out an English word.

### Read the figure
Read across each row. In the first row, n + i + third tone makes nǐ, which is the character 你 and means "you". In the second row, h + ao + third tone makes hǎo, the character 好, meaning "good".

### Highlight the parts
Take a sentence and mark the initials in one colour and the finals in another. Look at this sentence.
nǐ hǎo, wǒ jiào Lǐ Huá.
Initials: n, h, w, j, l, h.
Finals: i, ao, o, iao, i, ua.
The last two syllables are a name, so you may see finals that you have not yet learnt. Do not worry. The rule is the same: say the initial, then the final, and let the tone shape your voice.

### Read aloud slowly
Always begin slowly. Point to each syllable with your finger, and say it clearly. Once you can read the sentence smoothly, read it faster. Reading aloud helps you hear yourself and fix your mistakes.

## PAGE 2: Combining jumbled pieces
Here is a puzzle. Your teacher gives you cards with initials, finals and tone marks all mixed up. You must join them to make correct syllables.

Cards: h, n, ǐ, ǎo, j, iào, m, ā

### Solve it
• n + ǐ = nǐ
• h + ǎo = hǎo
• j + iào = jiào
• m + ā = mā

Now join two syllables to make a word.
nǐ + hǎo = nǐ hǎo (hello)
mā + mā = māma (mum)

### Rules to remember while joining
1. Every syllable needs a final. Some syllables do not need an initial.
2. Put the tone mark on the right vowel. Choose a if there is one, otherwise e or o. With i and u together, mark the second one.
3. If two third tones meet, say the first one as a second tone: nǐ hǎo is said like ní hǎo.

### Group activity
In groups of three, one learner holds initials, one holds finals and one holds tones. Stand together to build a syllable. The class reads it aloud. If it is correct, you score a point. Then swap your cards.

## PAGE 3: Greeting each other
Now let us read simple greetings in characters and pinyin.

你好! nǐ hǎo! Hello!
您好! nín hǎo! Hello! (polite)
老师好! lǎoshī hǎo! Hello, teacher!
同学们好! tóngxuémen hǎo! Hello, classmates!
早上好! zǎoshang hǎo! Good morning!
再见! zàijiàn! Goodbye!
谢谢! xièxie! Thank you!

New words: 老师 lǎoshī (teacher), 同学们 tóngxuémen (classmates), 早上 zǎoshang (morning), 再见 zàijiàn (goodbye, literally "see you again"), 谢谢 xièxie (thank you).

### Read in pairs
Read each greeting to your partner with a friendly face. Your partner answers with the same greeting. Try greeting your teacher with 老师好 and hearing the teacher answer 同学们好.

### Culture note
In China, students often stand and greet the teacher at the start of the lesson. In Kenya we may say "Good morning, teacher." Greeting nicely is a way of showing respect everywhere in the world.

### Read with expression
Smile when you read greetings. A friendly face changes your voice. Try reading "nǐ hǎo!" as if you were meeting a good friend.

## PAGE 4: Introducing yourself
Read this short conversation aloud with a partner. Take turns reading each part.

A: 你好!
   nǐ hǎo!
B: 你好!我叫安娜。你呢?
   nǐ hǎo! wǒ jiào Ānnà. nǐ ne?
A: 我叫小明。
   wǒ jiào Xiǎomíng.
B: 再见!
   zàijiàn!
A: 再见!
   zàijiàn!

Meaning:
A: Hello!
B: Hello! My name is Anna. And you?
A: My name is Xiaoming.
B: Goodbye!
A: Goodbye!

### How to read a conversation
• Read your part clearly, not too fast.
• Wait for your partner to finish.
• Keep your voice steady, and let questions end a little higher.
• Each time you finish, praise your partner and say one thing they did well.

### Make it yours
Change the names to your own name and your friend's name. You may say your name in pinyin letters: wǒ jiào Kevin, wǒ jiào Zawadi. The names are written in our own alphabet, but the rest of the sentence is Chinese.

## PAGE 5: Rhymes with different tones
Mandarin rhymes often use the same syllable in different tones. Reading them helps you notice the differences. Read this popular one slowly, then faster.

妈妈骑马,
马慢,
妈妈骂马。

Pinyin:
Māma qí mǎ,
mǎ màn,
māma mà mǎ.

Meaning: Mum rides a horse. The horse is slow. Mum scolds the horse.

New words: 骑 qí (to ride), 慢 màn (slow), 骂 mà (to scold).

### Tone spotting
Find every "ma" in the rhyme.
• māma has a high tone then a light tone.
• mǎ is a dipping tone.
• màn has a falling tone.
• mà is a falling tone.
Listen to how the same sound "ma" changes the meaning. With the wrong tone, the rhyme sounds like nonsense.

### Read like this
Read the first line quietly, the second line with a pause after the comma, and the last line with a cross voice, as if you were scolding. Reading with feeling makes the rhyme more fun.

## PAGE 6: Reading aloud well
When you read aloud in Mandarin, your listeners must be able to hear each sound and tone. Use this checklist.

### Checklist
1. Look at the whole line before you read.
2. Find the tones in each syllable.
3. Read slowly and clearly.
4. Pause at commas (,) and stop at full stops (。).
5. Keep your voice loud enough for everyone to hear.

### Full stops and commas
In Chinese the full stop is a little circle: 。 The comma looks like our comma: , and the question mark is the same as ours: ?. You will learn to write them later.

### Wait for your turn
When a friend is reading, listen patiently. Do not interrupt. When you give feedback, be kind and truthful. For example: "Your hǎo was clear. Next time, make nǐ a little higher."

### Common mistakes
• Reading every syllable with the first tone.
• Forgetting that the second syllable of māma is light.
• Reading too fast, so the tones disappear.

### Summary
You can now take syllables apart into initial, final and tone, join them, and read greetings and a short dialogue. You have also read a rhyme that uses the same sound in different tones.

## QUIZ
1. Which initial and final make the syllable hǎo?
   A) n + i
   B) j + iao
   C) h + ao
   D) m + a
   Answer: C
   Why: h is the initial and ao is the final, with a third tone on a.
2. What does 再见 zàijiàn mean?
   A) Thank you
   B) Goodbye
   C) Hello
   D) Good morning
   Answer: B
   Why: 再见 means goodbye, literally "see you again".
3. How is 你好 nǐ hǎo actually said?
   A) Like nǐ nǐ
   B) Like hǎo hǎo
   C) Like ní hǎo
   D) Like nì hǎo
   Answer: C
   Why: When two third tones come together, the first one changes to a second tone.
4. In the rhyme, which word means "to scold"?
   A) mǎ
   B) qí
   C) màn
   D) mà
   Answer: D
   Why: mà 骂 means to scold. mǎ is horse and màn is slow.
5. On which vowel does the tone mark go in jiào?
   A) i
   B) a
   C) o
   D) u
   Answer: B
   Why: When there is an a, the tone mark goes on a: jiào.
6. Join these pieces to make a syllable and give one meaning: m + ā. (2 marks)
   Answer: mā, meaning mother (妈).
   Why: 1 mark for the syllable and 1 mark for the meaning.
7. Read the conversation part: 你好!我叫安娜。你呢? Write it in pinyin and say what it means. (3 marks)
   Answer: nǐ hǎo! wǒ jiào Ānnà. nǐ ne? Hello! My name is Anna. And you?
   Why: 1 mark for the pinyin of the first sentence, 1 mark for the rest, 1 mark for the meaning.
8. List three things to do to read aloud well. (3 marks)
   Answer: Any three of: look at the whole line first, find the tones, read slowly and clearly, pause at commas and stop at full stops, speak loudly enough for everyone.
   Why: 1 mark for each correct point.
9. Explain why reading rhymes with the same syllable in different tones helps you learn Mandarin. (2 marks)
   Answer: It helps you notice that the tone changes the meaning, so you learn to say each tone carefully.
   Why: 1 mark for noticing tone difference and 1 mark for linking it to meaning.
