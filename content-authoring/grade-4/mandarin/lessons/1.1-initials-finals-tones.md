## PAGE 1: Meet Mandarin and pinyin
Mandarin is the most widely spoken language in China, and millions of people around the world speak it. It is written with Chinese characters, called hanzi. Each character stands for one syllable and carries a meaning. Because the characters do not show the sound directly, we use a helper system called pinyin. Pinyin writes Mandarin sounds with the same letters we use for English and Kiswahili.

Every Mandarin syllable has three parts.
• An initial: the sound at the start, like b, m or h.
• A final: the vowel sound that follows, like a, o or ao.
• A tone: the pitch pattern of the voice, shown by a mark over the vowel.

Look at how the greeting is built.
你好 = nǐ hǎo = hello
n + ǐ = nǐ (you)
h + ǎo = hǎo (good)

So nǐ hǎo means "you good", which is how Chinese people say "hello". Some syllables have no initial at all, such as ā or ài, and that is fine.

### Try it
Say "nǐ hǎo" to the person next to you. Then tell each other how you greet people at home. Do you say "Habari?", "Jambo!" or a greeting in your mother tongue? Every language has its own special sounds, and Mandarin's special feature is its tones.

## PAGE 2: The initials
The initials for this grade are b p m f d t n l g k h j q x. Learn them in small groups, the way many Chinese children do.

Group 1, lips: b p m f
Group 2, tongue tip: d t n l
Group 3, back of the tongue: g k h
Group 4, smiling sounds: j q x

### Sounds to copy
• b is like the p in "spin". No puff of air.
• p is like the p in "pin". Add a puff of air.
• m and f are like English m and f.
• d is like the t in "stop". t has a puff of air.
• n and l are like English n and l.
• g is like the k in "sky". k has a puff of air.
• h is like English h, said a little rougher in the throat.
• j, q and x are said with the lips spread in a smile. q has a puff of air. x is like a soft "sh".

### Feel the puff
Hold a small strip of paper in front of your mouth. Say bā, then pā. The paper should move a lot more for pā. The pairs b/p, d/t, g/k differ mainly by this puff of air. Working with a partner, say the pairs and watch the paper.

## PAGE 3: The finals
The simple finals you need are a, o, e, i, u and ü. Then there are two joined finals: ao and iao.

• a: open your mouth wide, like saying "ah" at the doctor.
• o: round your lips, like the "aw" in "law".
• e: say "uh" with your mouth relaxed, in the back of the mouth.
• i: like the "ee" in "see".
• u: like the "oo" in "moon".
• ü: keep your lips round like u, but try to say "ee". Your lips stay pushed forward.
• ao: like the "ow" in "cow". Example: hǎo.
• iao: like the "yow" in "meow". Example: xiǎo (small).

### A spelling rule to remember
After j, q and x, the sound ü is written as plain u. So the word 去 (to go) is written qù, and it is still said with the ü sound. After n and l we keep the two dots, as in nǚ (girl) and lǜ (green).

Say these syllables aloud: bā, pō, mǐ, fǔ, dà, tā, nǐ, lǎo, gē, kǒu, hǎo, jiā, qù, xiǎo. Each one is initial + final + tone.

## PAGE 4: The four tones
Mandarin has four main tones. The same syllable said with a different tone becomes a different word. The tone marks are placed above the vowel: ā á ǎ à.

### The four tones with mā
• Tone 1 (ˉ) mā 妈: mother. Say it high and steady, like singing one long note.
• Tone 2 (ˊ) má 麻: hemp. Rise, like asking "What?"
• Tone 3 (ˇ) mǎ 马: horse. Dip down and come up again.
• Tone 4 (ˋ) mà 骂: to scold. Drop sharply, like saying "No!"

Some syllables are said short and light with no tone mark. This is the neutral tone, as in the second syllable of māma (mum).

## PAGE 5: Marking the tone and the tone change
Where does the tone mark go? Follow these rules.
1. If there is an a, put the mark on a: hǎo, bào.
2. If there is no a, but there is an e or o, put it there: méi, lóu.
3. If the final has i and u together (iu or ui), put it on the second one: liú, duì.
4. Otherwise, mark the only vowel: nǐ, dà, tā.
When a tone mark goes on the letter i, it takes the place of the dot, as in nǐ.

### Two third tones in a row
When two third tones stand together, the first one is said as a second tone. So 你好 is written nǐ hǎo but said like "ní hǎo". You still write the third-tone mark on both.

### Tone practice pairs
bā (eight) and bà (dad)
bá (to pull) and bǎ (a handle)
Try saying each pair while moving your hand: flat, up, down-up, down. Moving your hand helps your voice follow the tone.

### Why tones matter
If you say mǎ when you mean mā, you have said "horse" instead of "mother". Clear tones make sure the listener understands you.

## PAGE 6: Saying hello and your name
Now you can say a full greeting with the correct tones.
你好 nǐ hǎo: hello (to a friend or classmate)
您好 nín hǎo: hello (polite, to a teacher or an older person)
我 wǒ: I, me
叫 jiào: am called
你呢? nǐ ne: and you?

### A short conversation
Amina: 你好! nǐ hǎo!
Brian: 你好! wǒ jiào Brian. nǐ ne?
Amina: 我叫 Amina.

For your own name, say wǒ jiào and then your name. The word "ne" is neutral tone: say it light and short.

### Compare languages
In Kiswahili we say "Habari?" and the answer is "Nzuri". In Mandarin we say "nǐ hǎo". Talk with your classmates: how do you say hello and your name in your home language? Notice how each language uses its own special sounds. Respect every language your friends speak.

### Remember
• A syllable = initial + final + tone.
• Four tones plus a neutral tone.
• Say nǐ hǎo with the tones flat, rising and falling clearly.

## QUIZ
1. What are the three parts of a Mandarin syllable?
   A) Initial, final and tone
   B) Noun, verb and adjective
   C) Stroke, radical and character
   D) Letter, word and sentence
   Answer: A
   Why: A syllable is made of an initial, a final and a tone, as in h + ǎo = hǎo.
2. Which tone is drawn as a high, flat line?
   A) Tone 2
   B) Tone 4
   C) Tone 3
   D) Tone 1
   Answer: D
   Why: The first tone is high and level, as in mā.
3. What does mǎ 马 mean?
   A) Mother
   B) Horse
   C) Hemp
   D) To scold
   Answer: B
   Why: mǎ with the third tone means horse. Mother is mā, hemp is má and to scold is mà.
4. Which pair of initials differs mainly by a puff of air?
   A) m and f
   B) n and l
   C) b and p
   D) h and x
   Answer: C
   Why: p is said with a puff of air, b is not. The same is true for d/t and g/k.
5. Where does the tone mark go in hǎo?
   A) On h
   B) On a
   C) On o
   D) Nowhere
   Answer: B
   Why: When a syllable has an a, the tone mark goes on the a.
6. Say what nǐ hǎo and nín hǎo mean, and explain when to use nín hǎo. (2 marks)
   Answer: Both mean hello. Nín hǎo is the polite form used for a teacher or an older person.
   Why: 1 mark for the meaning and 1 mark for the polite use of nín.
7. Write the four tone marks over the vowel a, and say which tone each belongs to. (3 marks)
   Answer: ā (tone 1), á (tone 2), ǎ (tone 3), à (tone 4).
   Why: 1 mark for two correct marks, 2 marks for three, 3 marks for all four with the correct tone numbers.
8. How is nǐ hǎo actually said, and why? (2 marks)
   Answer: It is said like ní hǎo. When two third tones come together, the first changes to a second tone.
   Why: 1 mark for the changed sound and 1 mark for the rule.
9. Explain why saying the tone correctly is important in Mandarin. Give one example. (3 marks)
   Answer: The tone changes the meaning of the word. For example mā means mother but mǎ means horse.
   Why: 1 mark for saying tone changes meaning, 2 marks for a correct pair of examples.
