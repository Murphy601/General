## PAGE 1: A tiny seed
Jesus told the parable of the mustard seed to explain the kingdom of God. It is found in Luke 13:18-19. The story is also told in Matthew 13 and Mark 4.

Jesus asked, "What is the kingdom of God like? What shall I compare it to?" Then He said, "It is like a mustard seed, which a man took and planted in his garden. It grew and became a tree, and the birds perched in its branches."

### Words to know
• Parable: a story with a meaning.
• Kingdom of God: God's rule in the hearts of people who obey Him.
• Mustard seed: a very tiny seed.
• Evangelise: to tell others the good news about Jesus.

### The size of the seed
A mustard seed is one of the smallest seeds that farmers plant. In Matthew 13:32 Jesus says that it is the smallest of all seeds. Yet, when it is planted and grows, it becomes a large plant, large enough for birds to rest in its branches.

### Think about it
Have you ever planted a seed? How long did it take to grow?

### Steps of growth
1. A farmer plants the seed in the soil.
2. It is watered and warmed by the sun.
3. Roots grow down and a shoot grows up.
4. Over time, it becomes big and strong.

Growth is slow, and you cannot see it happening every minute, but it does happen. The lesson of the parable is that something very small can become very great.

## PAGE 3: What the parable means
Jesus used the seed to describe how God's kingdom grows.

### The meaning
• The small seed stands for the beginning of God's kingdom. It began small: Jesus and a few followers.
• The big tree stands for the growth of God's kingdom. Today, the church is found all over the world, including Kenya.
• The birds stand for the people who find shelter, peace and care in God's kingdom.

### From a small beginning
After Jesus went back to heaven, the disciples began to tell people about Him. On the day of Pentecost, about three thousand people believed and were baptised (Acts 2:41). Then the church grew and spread to many towns and countries. This is how God's kingdom grew from a small beginning.

### A lesson
Do not despise small beginnings. A small kind act, a short prayer or a little help can grow into something great.

## PAGE 4: Our part in the growth of God's kingdom
The kingdom of God grows when people hear the good news, believe in Jesus and live as He taught. Each of us can help.

### How can children help God's kingdom to grow?
• Be an example. Be kind, honest, and obedient at home and school.
• Pray for others and for the church.
• Invite friends to church, Sunday school or Bible club.
• Share Bible stories with others.
• Give your time and gifts to serve in church, by singing, cleaning, ushering or helping.
• Care for God's creation.

### Responding to salvation
Salvation is being saved from sin through Jesus Christ. We respond by believing in Jesus, repenting of our sin, and living as His followers.

### Activity: plant a seed
Collect a small seed from the school compound and plant it in a container. Water it every day and watch how it changes. Draw the seed as it sprouts and grows. What did you learn about patience and care?

## PAGE 5: Growing in faith and service
Just as a seed needs water, soil and sunshine to grow, our faith needs care as well.

### What helps our faith to grow
• Prayer talking to God each day.
• Bible reading learning about God.
• Worship with other believers.
• Obedience doing what God says.
• Friends who encourage us to do good.

### Being patient
Seeds do not become trees overnight. In the same way, we grow as Christians step by step, and we should be patient with ourselves and others.

### Caring for the environment
When we plant trees and care for plants, we look after God's creation. A tree gives shade, fruit, firewood and homes for birds. Planting a tree is a good way to serve God and our community.

### Summary
• Jesus compared the kingdom of God to a tiny mustard seed that grows into a large tree (Luke 13:18-19).
• God's kingdom began small and has grown across the world.
• We help it grow by good example, prayer, sharing the good news and serving.
• Our faith grows with care, like a plant.

## QUIZ
1. Which parable compares the kingdom of God to a tiny seed that grows into a tree?
   A) The lost coin
   B) The mustard seed
   C) The good Samaritan
   D) The lost sheep
   Answer: B
   Why: Luke 13:18-19 compares God's kingdom to a mustard seed.
2. In the parable, what happens to the seed?
   A) It is eaten by birds
   B) It grows into a tree where birds perch in its branches
   C) It dies in the ground
   D) It is thrown away
   Answer: B
   Why: The man planted it, and it grew and became a tree.
3. What does the small mustard seed stand for?
   A) The small beginning of God's kingdom
   B) A rich king
   C) A big house
   D) The end of the world
   Answer: A
   Why: God's kingdom began small, with Jesus and a few followers, and grew large.
4. Which of these helps the kingdom of God to grow?
   A) Setting a good example and telling others about Jesus
   B) Keeping the good news secret
   C) Never going to church
   D) Being unkind to others
   Answer: A
   Why: We help by living as Christians and sharing the good news.
5. What does a seed need in order to grow?
   A) Soil, water and sunshine
   B) Only darkness
   C) Only noise
   D) Nothing
   Answer: A
   Why: Plants need soil, water and light to grow.
6. Explain what Jesus meant by comparing the kingdom of God to a mustard seed. (3 marks)
   Answer: Just as a tiny seed grows into a large tree, God's kingdom began small but grows big and provides shelter for many.
   Why: 1 mark for small beginning, 1 mark for growth, 1 mark for shelter or many people.
7. Give three ways in which a child can help God's kingdom grow. (3 marks)
   Answer: Set a good example, invite friends to church, pray, share Bible stories, serve in church.
   Why: 1 mark for each valid way.
8. Describe how you would plant and care for a seed in the school compound. (2 marks)
   Answer: Dig a small hole, place the seed, cover it with soil and water it regularly; protect it from animals and watch it grow.
   Why: 1 mark for planting correctly and 1 mark for watering and caring.
