## PAGE 1: Israel and the birth of Samson
Samson's story is found in the book of Judges, chapters 13 to 16. Judges is the book that tells about leaders whom God raised up to rescue Israel before Israel had kings. At the time of Samson, the Philistines were ruling the Israelites. The Philistines were a strong neighbouring people who oppressed Israel.

An angel of the Lord told Samson's parents that they would have a son. The angel said the child must be set apart for God from birth and must never have his hair cut. Such a person was called a Nazirite. This showed that Samson belonged to God in a special way and was meant to help save Israel from the Philistines. The Bible says the Lord blessed Samson as he grew.

### Words to know
• Judge: a leader whom God raised up to rescue and guide Israel.
• Philistines: a people who ruled over Israel at that time.
• Nazirite: someone set apart for God.

## PAGE 2: Samson and the lion
Judges 14:5-9 tells us that Samson went down to Timnah with his father and mother. As they came to the vineyards of Timnah, suddenly a young lion came roaring towards him.

The Spirit of the Lord came upon Samson with power. With his bare hands he tore the lion apart as one might tear a young goat. He had no weapon. He did not tell his father or mother what he had done.

### What the story says
• The lion attacked Samson, not the other way round.
• Samson's strength came from the Spirit of the Lord, not from himself.
• Samson was alone when it happened, and he did it without any weapon.

### What the story does not say
The Bible does not tell us to fight lions or dangerous animals. Nobody today should try to do what Samson did. The story is about God's power, not about children being brave in dangerous situations. If you ever meet a dangerous animal, stay away and call for help from adults.

## PAGE 3: The honey
Some time later Samson returned along the same road to marry the woman he had chosen. He turned aside to look at the body of the lion. Inside it a swarm of bees had made honey. Samson scooped some honey into his hands and ate as he walked. He gave some to his father and mother, and they ate it. But he did not tell them that he had taken the honey from the body of the lion (Judges 14:8-9).

### Notes
• This story shows that God can bring something sweet, like honey, from a place of danger.
• Samson kept a secret from his parents. Later in his life he made some bad choices and got into trouble. It is best to be open and honest with parents and trusted adults.
• Judges tells us that Samson's life had strong points and weak points. He was strong in body, but he did not always choose wisely.

## PAGE 4: God's power in our lives
Samson's story is often used to teach that God is powerful. Nothing is too hard for Him. He gave Samson strength for the work that He had for him. The strength was a gift, and Samson was meant to use it to serve God and help his people.

### How God's power works today
God does not usually give people the strength of Samson. But He helps us in many other ways.
• He gives us courage to face our fears, like the first day at a new school.
• He gives us wisdom to make good choices.
• He gives us the ability to forgive.
• He heals and protects us.
• He gives us talents to help others.

### Acknowledge God
To acknowledge God's power means to admit and to say thank you that our strength and abilities come from Him. When you win a race, pass an exam or overcome a difficulty, you can say, "Thank you, God, for helping me."

### Activity
In pairs, tell each other about one time when you felt afraid and asked God for help.

## PAGE 5: Trusting God in life's challenges
Children face many challenges. Some are bullied. Some struggle with reading or sums. Some come from families that have little money. Some lose someone they love. Trusting God does not mean that problems disappear. It means that we believe God is with us and helps us.

### How to trust God
1. Pray and tell God what worries you.
2. Read the Bible to remember His promises.
3. Ask for help from parents, teachers or pastors.
4. Do your best, and do not give up.
5. Thank God for helping you.

### What Samson's story teaches
• God is powerful, and His power can help His people.
• We should use our gifts to serve God, not to boast.
• We must trust in God when challenges come.
• We should be open and honest with our parents.

### Summary
• Samson was a judge of Israel, set apart for God.
• The Spirit of the Lord gave him strength to tear a lion apart with his hands (Judges 14:5-9).
• God's power is greater than any danger.
• We should acknowledge God's power and trust Him in life's challenges.

## QUIZ
1. In which book of the Bible do we read that Samson killed a lion?
   A) Genesis
   B) Judges
   C) Luke
   D) Psalms
   Answer: B
   Why: Judges 14:5-9 tells the story of Samson and the lion.
2. What did Samson use to kill the lion?
   A) A sword
   B) A spear
   C) His bare hands
   D) A stone
   Answer: C
   Why: The Bible says he tore the lion apart with his bare hands.
3. What gave Samson the strength to kill the lion?
   A) His long hair only
   B) His father
   C) The Spirit of the Lord
   D) Special food
   Answer: C
   Why: Judges 14:6 says that the Spirit of the Lord came upon him powerfully.
4. What did Samson find in the lion's body some time later?
   A) A snake
   B) A swarm of bees and honey
   C) Gold
   D) Nothing
   Answer: B
   Why: Judges 14:8 says he found a swarm of bees and honey in the body.
5. What should a child do if they meet a dangerous animal?
   A) Try to fight it like Samson
   B) Stay away and call adults for help
   C) Go closer to look
   D) Throw stones at it
   Answer: B
   Why: The story is about God's power, not about children fighting dangerous animals.
6. Name two people or groups mentioned in the story of Samson's time. (2 marks)
   Answer: Samson, his parents, the Philistines, the Israelites, the angel of the Lord. Any two.
   Why: 1 mark for each valid answer.
7. Explain how the story of Samson shows God's power. (3 marks)
   Answer: Samson had no weapon, but the Spirit of the Lord gave him the strength to tear a lion apart. This shows God's power is greater than any danger. The strength came from God, not Samson.
   Why: 1 mark for no weapon or ordinary man, 1 mark for the Spirit of the Lord giving strength, 1 mark for the conclusion that God is powerful.
8. Give three ways in which a child can trust God when facing a challenge. (3 marks)
   Answer: Pray, read the Bible, ask help from trusted adults, do their best, thank God.
   Why: 1 mark for each valid way, up to 3.
