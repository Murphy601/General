## PAGE 1: What are thoughts and feelings?
A thought is an idea in your mind. A feeling is what you sense inside, such as happiness, sadness, fear or anger. Thoughts and feelings are invisible, but they affect what we say and do.

Suppose a friend shares half of her mandazi with you. You may think, "She is kind." You may feel happy and thankful. Then you may say "Thank you" and smile. Your thought led to a feeling, and the feeling led to your action.

### Good thoughts
Good thoughts help us and other people. Examples are:
• Thinking of how to share with others.
• Thinking of how to help your parents at home.
• Thinking, "I am thankful for my food."
• Thinking of how to respect others.

### Bad thoughts
Bad thoughts lead to wrong actions. Examples are wanting to take something that is not yours, wishing to hurt someone who annoyed you, or thinking, "I hate her." God wants us to fill our minds with good things. Philippians 4:8 encourages believers to think about what is true, pure, lovely and worthy of praise.

## PAGE 2: Different feelings
Everyone has different feelings. Feelings themselves are not bad. What matters is what we do with them.

### Talk about it
• Joy: when your team wins, or you pass a test.
• Sadness: when a pet dies or a friend moves away.
• Anger: when someone breaks your pencil on purpose.
• Fear: when you hear thunder at night.
• Thankfulness: when someone helps you.

It is healthy to name your feeling. Say to yourself, "I am feeling angry," or "I am feeling sad." When you name a feeling, it is easier to decide what to do about it.

## PAGE 3: The importance of being joyful
Joy is a deep, happy feeling that comes from knowing God loves you. Philippians 4:4 says, "Rejoice in the Lord always." This means that a Christian can be glad because God cares for them, even when life is hard.

### Why joy is important
• It makes you healthy in mind and body. A joyful child plays, studies and shares more easily.
• It makes other people happy. A smile can cheer up a tired parent.
• It helps you face problems without giving up.
• It shows others that you trust God.

### How to be joyful
1. Thank God each day for small things, like food and friends.
2. Sing songs of praise, alone or with others.
3. Play and laugh with your friends in kind ways.
4. Help others. Giving often makes the giver joyful.

### Activity
Write Philippians 4:4 on a flash card. Decorate it and stick it where you will see it every day. Say the verse aloud each morning.

## PAGE 4: Anger and Ephesians 4:26
Everyone feels angry sometimes. Ephesians 4:26 teaches, "In your anger do not sin. Do not let the sun go down while you are still angry." The Bible does not say we must never feel angry. It says we must not do wrong things when we are angry, and we must not keep the anger for days.

### Lessons from Ephesians 4:26
• Anger is a feeling, but hitting, insulting or breaking things is sin.
• Deal with anger quickly. Do not carry it to bed.
• Make peace with the person who upset you.

### How to cope with anger
1. Stop and take three deep breaths.
2. Count slowly to ten before you speak.
3. Walk away from the situation if you need to.
4. Tell a trusted adult, teacher or friend how you feel.
5. Pray and ask God for calmness.
6. Forgive the person and, if you were wrong, say sorry.

### Role play idea
Act out a scene where a classmate accidentally spills your water. Show the wrong reaction, then show the calm reaction using the steps above.

## PAGE 5: Coping with sadness and living out the lessons
Sadness is a normal feeling. You may feel sad when you lose something dear, when others leave you out, or when someone in the family is sick. You are not alone. God cares for you, and so do good people around you.

### What to do when you feel sad
• Tell a parent, guardian, teacher or pastor how you feel.
• Pray and tell God about it.
• Do something helpful, such as drawing, singing, walking, or playing with a friend.
• Remember that sad feelings usually pass.

### Being kind to sad friends
If you see a friend sitting alone and looking sad, sit next to them. Ask kindly, "Are you okay?" Listen. Do not laugh at them.

### Summary
• Thoughts lead to feelings and actions, so we should think good thoughts.
• All feelings are normal, but our actions must be right.
• Rejoicing in the Lord always gives us joy (Philippians 4:4).
• When angry, we should not sin, and we should make peace quickly (Ephesians 4:26).

## QUIZ
1. Which is a good thought?
   A) I will share my lunch with a hungry friend
   B) I will take my friend's pen when she is not looking
   C) I hope he fails his test
   D) I will not greet anyone today
   Answer: A
   Why: Thinking of sharing leads to kindness. The other thoughts lead to wrong actions.
2. What does Philippians 4:4 tell Christians to do?
   A) Rejoice in the Lord always
   B) Keep silent always
   C) Never feel sad
   D) Take revenge
   Answer: A
   Why: Philippians 4:4 says, "Rejoice in the Lord always."
3. According to Ephesians 4:26, what should a person do when angry?
   A) Shout at others
   B) Not sin and make peace before the day ends
   C) Keep the anger for a week
   D) Pretend nothing happened and avoid everyone forever
   Answer: B
   Why: The verse teaches that anger should not lead to sin and should not be held onto until sunset.
4. Which is the best way to calm down when angry?
   A) Hit the person
   B) Break something
   C) Take deep breaths and count to ten
   D) Say unkind words
   Answer: C
   Why: Breathing and counting slows the body and gives time to think before acting.
5. A friend is sitting alone and looks sad. What should you do?
   A) Walk past quickly
   B) Laugh at the friend
   C) Tell others to avoid the friend
   D) Sit with the friend and kindly ask what is wrong
   Answer: D
   Why: Kindness and listening are ways to care for others.
6. Give two reasons why being joyful is important. (2 marks)
   Answer: It keeps us healthy and happy, it cheers up others, it helps us face problems, and it shows that we trust God. Any two.
   Why: 1 mark for each valid reason.
7. Explain how you would cope with anger if a classmate broke your ruler by mistake. (3 marks)
   Answer: I would stop and breathe, count to ten, and remember that it was an accident. I would tell my classmate calmly how I feel, forgive, and ask God to help me not to sin in my anger.
   Why: 1 mark for calming down, 1 mark for speaking calmly or forgiving, 1 mark for prayer or applying Ephesians 4:26.
8. Write down two lessons you learn from Ephesians 4:26. (3 marks)
   Answer: (1) Being angry is not itself a sin, but we must not do wrong things when angry. (2) We should settle anger and make peace quickly, not keep it for days.
   Why: 1 mark for each lesson, and 1 mark for expressing them clearly in the child's own words.
