## PAGE 1: Jesus chooses twelve disciples
A disciple is a follower and learner. Jesus had many followers, but He chose twelve of them to be with Him in a special way. They are also called apostles, which means "those who are sent". Jesus sent them to preach, to heal and to lead the people after He returned to heaven.

Matthew 10:1 says that Jesus called His twelve disciples and gave them authority to drive out evil spirits and to heal every disease and sickness. They were ordinary men: fishermen, a tax collector and others. Jesus chose them, taught them and prepared them for the work ahead.

### Words to know
• Disciple: a follower and learner of Jesus.
• Apostle: someone sent out with a message.
• Church: the community of believers in Jesus.
• Repent: to be sorry for sin and turn away from it.

### Think about it
Why do you think Jesus chose twelve people to be His close friends and helpers?

### Notes
• Simon was called Peter, and Andrew was his brother.
• James and John were brothers, the sons of Zebedee.
• Matthew had been a tax collector before he followed Jesus.
• Judas Iscariot later betrayed Jesus. After his death, the disciples chose Matthias to take his place (Acts 1:26).

### Memory game
Write each name on a card. Mix them up and put them in the order of the list. Then time yourself to see how fast you can do it.

## PAGE 3: The Holy Spirit comes
Before Jesus went back to heaven, He told His disciples to wait in Jerusalem for the Holy Spirit. On the day of Pentecost, the disciples were together in one place. The Holy Spirit came upon them, and they began to speak in other languages, so that visitors from many countries heard about God's wonders in their own languages (Acts 2:1-12).

### Peter preaches
Peter stood up and spoke boldly to the crowd. He told them about Jesus, who had died and risen again. Many people were deeply moved. They asked, "Brothers, what shall we do?"

### Peter's answer: Acts 2:38
"Repent and be baptised, every one of you, in the name of Jesus Christ for the forgiveness of your sins. And you will receive the gift of the Holy Spirit."

This verse tells us what to do: repent, be baptised and receive the Spirit. Repent means to turn away from sin.

## PAGE 4: How the early church began and lived
Acts 2:41 says that about three thousand people accepted Peter's message and were baptised that day. They were added to the group of believers. This was the start of the church in Jerusalem.

### How the first Christians lived
Acts 2:42 says that they devoted themselves to the apostles' teaching, to fellowship, to the breaking of bread and to prayer. They met together often, ate together and shared what they had with those who were in need.

### What we can learn
• They listened to God's word.
• They prayed together.
• They shared with the poor.
• They were united and glad.
• They praised God, and others were drawn to join them.

### Role play
In groups, act out how the early church began: the disciples waiting, the Holy Spirit coming, Peter preaching and the people believing.

## PAGE 5: Turning away from sin today
Acts 2:38 still speaks to us. Repenting is not just for adults. Children can also turn away from sin.

### How to repent
1. Admit what you have done wrong.
2. Tell God that you are sorry.
3. Ask forgiveness from anyone you hurt.
4. Make a decision to do better.
5. Ask the Holy Spirit to help you.

### Being part of the church today
The church today continues the work of the early church. We can join by attending, praying, learning the Bible and sharing with others. Every believer, young or old, is part of the church.

### Summary
• Jesus chose twelve disciples (Matthew 10:1-4).
• On Pentecost the Holy Spirit came and Peter preached. About three thousand were baptised (Acts 2).
• The early church prayed, learnt, shared and worshipped together.
• Acts 2:38 calls us to repent and be baptised.

## QUIZ
1. How many disciples did Jesus choose?
   A) Seven
   B) Ten
   C) Twelve
   D) Twenty
   Answer: C
   Why: Matthew 10:1 speaks of the twelve disciples.
2. Which disciple was a tax collector before following Jesus?
   A) Matthew
   B) Peter
   C) Andrew
   D) Philip
   Answer: A
   Why: Matthew 10:3 calls him "Matthew the tax collector".
3. Who preached on the day of Pentecost?
   A) Judas
   B) Thomas
   C) Peter
   D) Philip
   Answer: C
   Why: Peter stood up and spoke to the crowd (Acts 2).
4. About how many people were baptised on the day of Pentecost?
   A) 30
   B) 300
   C) 3,000
   D) 30,000
   Answer: C
   Why: Acts 2:41 says about three thousand.
5. What does Acts 2:38 tell people to do?
   A) Repent and be baptised
   B) Stay at home
   C) Build a boat
   D) Count the days
   Answer: A
   Why: Peter told the crowd to repent and be baptised.
6. List any six of the twelve disciples of Jesus. (3 marks)
   Answer: Simon Peter, Andrew, James, John, Philip, Bartholomew, Thomas, Matthew, James son of Alphaeus, Thaddaeus, Simon the Zealot, Judas Iscariot.
   Why: 3 marks for six names, 2 marks for four or five names, 1 mark for two or three names.
7. Describe how the early church began. (3 marks)
   Answer: The Holy Spirit came upon the disciples at Pentecost; Peter preached; about three thousand people believed and were baptised.
   Why: 1 mark for the Holy Spirit coming, 1 mark for Peter's preaching, 1 mark for the people believing and being baptised.
8. State two ways in which the first Christians lived together. (2 marks)
   Answer: They studied the apostles' teaching, prayed and met together, shared with the needy.
   Why: 1 mark for each valid way.
