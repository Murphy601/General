## PAGE 1: Joseph before he met Pharaoh
Joseph was one of the twelve sons of Jacob. His father loved him and gave him a special coat. His brothers grew jealous, and they sold him to traders who took him to Egypt. In Egypt Joseph worked in the house of an officer named Potiphar. He was later accused of something he did not do and was put in prison.

Even in prison, Joseph remained faithful to God. The Bible says that the Lord was with him. In prison he met two of Pharaoh's officials, the cupbearer and the baker. Each had a dream, and Joseph told them what the dreams meant. He said, "Interpretations belong to God." The cupbearer was later freed, as Joseph had said. Joseph asked him to remember him, but the cupbearer forgot for two years (Genesis 40).

### Words to know
• Pharaoh: the title of the king of Egypt.
• Interpret: to explain what a dream means.
• Famine: a long period when there is not enough food.

### Think about it
How do you think Joseph felt while he waited in prison? What helped him to keep going?

## PAGE 2: Pharaoh's two dreams
One night, Pharaoh had a dream. He was standing by the River Nile, and seven fat, healthy cows came up from the river and began to eat the grass. Then seven other cows came up. They were thin and ugly. The thin cows ate up the fat cows. Pharaoh woke up.

He fell asleep again and had a second dream. Seven full, good heads of grain grew on one stalk. Then seven thin heads of grain, scorched by the east wind, sprouted up. The thin heads swallowed up the full ones. Pharaoh woke up again, and it was a dream (Genesis 41:1-7).

Pharaoh was troubled. He called all the magicians and wise men of Egypt. Nobody could tell him what the dreams meant.

### Think about it
Have you ever had a dream that troubled you? Share a dream with a partner, but remember that not every dream has a meaning.

## PAGE 3: Joseph is called
Then the cupbearer remembered Joseph. He told Pharaoh about the young Hebrew in prison who had explained his dream. Pharaoh sent for Joseph. Joseph was quickly brought out of the prison. He shaved and changed his clothes and came to Pharaoh.

Pharaoh said, "I had a dream, and no one can interpret it. But I have heard that when you hear a dream you can interpret it." Joseph answered, "I cannot do it, but God will give Pharaoh the answer he desires" (Genesis 41:16). Joseph did not boast. He gave the credit to God.

### What Joseph explained
God showed Pharaoh what He was about to do. The seven fat cows and the seven full heads of grain meant seven years of plenty in Egypt. The seven thin cows and the seven thin heads meant seven years of famine that would follow. The famine would be so severe that the years of plenty would be forgotten. The dream came twice because God had firmly decided it and would soon do it (Genesis 41:25-32).

## PAGE 4: Joseph's wise advice
Joseph did not stop after the interpretation. He gave Pharaoh a plan. He suggested that Pharaoh should find a wise and discerning man and put him in charge of Egypt. This person should take a fifth of the harvest during the seven good years and store it in the towns. Then, during the famine, there would be food (Genesis 41:33-36).

Pharaoh and his officials liked the plan. Pharaoh said, "Can we find anyone like this man, one in whom is the spirit of God?" He put Joseph in charge of the land of Egypt, second only to himself. Joseph was thirty years old.

### Note
Joseph later carried out this plan, and many people from other lands came to Egypt to buy grain during the famine.

## PAGE 5: Wisdom and the fear of God
Joseph could interpret the dream because God gave him wisdom. Joseph knew God, trusted Him and gave Him the honour. Pharaoh saw that the spirit of God was in Joseph.

### The fear of God
Proverbs 1:7 says, "The fear of the Lord is the beginning of knowledge." Proverbs 9:10 says it is the beginning of wisdom. To fear God does not mean to be terrified of Him. It means to respect Him deeply, to love Him and to obey Him. People who fear God ask Him for help and follow His ways.

### Lessons we learn from Joseph
1. Depend on God, not on your own cleverness. Joseph said, "I cannot do it, but God will give the answer."
2. Do not give up when life is unfair. Joseph stayed faithful in prison.
3. Use your gifts to serve others. Joseph's plan saved many people from hunger.
4. Plan and save for hard times. Save some money or food when you have plenty.
5. Be a good leader. Joseph was honest, wise and hardworking.

### Summary
• Pharaoh had two dreams about seven good years followed by seven years of famine.
• God gave Joseph the meaning, and Joseph gave the credit to God.
• Joseph advised Pharaoh to store grain and became the second most powerful person in Egypt.
• The fear of the Lord is the beginning of wisdom.

## QUIZ
1. What did Pharaoh's seven fat cows stand for?
   A) Seven years of plenty
   B) Seven years of famine
   C) Seven enemies
   D) Seven servants
   Answer: A
   Why: Joseph explained that the seven fat cows stood for seven years of plenty.
2. Who first told Pharaoh about Joseph?
   A) The baker
   B) The cupbearer
   C) A magician
   D) Potiphar
   Answer: B
   Why: The cupbearer remembered Joseph, who had explained his dream in prison.
3. Whom did Joseph give the credit for the interpretation?
   A) Himself
   B) The cupbearer
   C) God
   D) The magicians
   Answer: C
   Why: Joseph said, "I cannot do it, but God will give Pharaoh the answer."
4. What advice did Joseph give about the harvest in the good years?
   A) Sell all the grain to other lands
   B) Store a fifth of it for the years of famine
   C) Burn the extra grain
   D) Eat as much as possible
   Answer: B
   Why: Genesis 41:34 says to take a fifth of the harvest during the seven years of plenty.
5. Which verse tells us that the fear of the Lord is the beginning of knowledge?
   A) Proverbs 1:7
   B) John 3:16
   C) Luke 1:37
   D) Matthew 5:39
   Answer: A
   Why: Proverbs 1:7 says, "The fear of the Lord is the beginning of knowledge."
6. Explain what it means to "fear God" in the Bible. (2 marks)
   Answer: It means to respect Him deeply, to love Him and to obey Him.
   Why: 1 mark for respect or reverence, 1 mark for love or obedience.
7. State three lessons that we can learn from the story of Joseph interpreting the dream. (3 marks)
   Answer: Depend on God's wisdom; do not give up in hard times; use your gifts to serve others; plan and save for hard times.
   Why: 1 mark for each valid lesson.
8. A child receives Ksh 100 pocket money each week. Using Joseph's advice, how could the child plan? (3 marks)
   Answer: Save part of the money for hard times or future needs, spend carefully on needs first, and share a little with someone in need.
   Why: 1 mark for saving, 1 mark for careful spending, 1 mark for sharing or giving.
