## PAGE 1: What is trust?
To trust someone is to believe that they are honest, reliable and will not harm you. You trust your parents to feed you and keep you safe. You trust the driver of your school bus to drive carefully. You trust a friend who keeps your secret. Trust is like a bridge between people. It takes a long time to build and can break quickly.

Trust is also important in our relationship with God. We can trust God because He is faithful and loving. Psalm 125:1 says, "Those who trust in the Lord are like Mount Zion, which cannot be shaken but endures for ever." A mountain does not move in a storm. A person who trusts God stays steady when troubles come.

### Words to know
• Trust: believing that someone is reliable and will not harm you.
• Temptation: something that pulls you to do wrong.
• Assertive: able to say what you think or feel, and say no, politely but firmly.
• Stranger: a person you do not know.

### Think about it
Who do you trust most? What has that person done to earn your trust?

## PAGE 2: Joseph says no
The Bible tells how Joseph, who lived in Egypt as a young man, was tempted to do wrong. He worked in the house of his master, Potiphar. Potiphar's wife kept asking Joseph to do something wicked with her. Joseph refused. He said, "How then could I do such a wicked thing and sin against God?" (Genesis 39:9).

Day after day she spoke to him, but he would not listen. One day when he was in the house, she grabbed his cloak, but he ran out of the house, leaving his cloak in her hand (Genesis 39:7-12). She lied about him, and Joseph was thrown into prison, though he had done nothing wrong. Yet God was with him.

### Lessons from Joseph's story
• Joseph knew that doing wrong would be a sin against God.
• He said no clearly.
• He ran away from the temptation. Sometimes the best way to avoid sin is to leave the place.
• He remained faithful even when it cost him.
• God was with him and later raised him up.

### Note
Even when we do the right thing, people may treat us unfairly. God sees and cares.

## PAGE 3: Being assertive and resisting temptation
Being assertive means standing firmly for what is right, in a calm and polite way. It is not being rude or aggressive. Children can be assertive when someone tries to persuade them to do wrong.

### How to say no
1. Say "No!" in a strong, clear voice.
2. Give a short reason: "My parents say I must not."
3. Walk away quickly, or run if you feel unsafe.
4. Tell a trusted adult about what happened.

### Examples of temptation
• A friend wants you to copy in a test.
• Older children ask you to take something from a shop.
• Someone tells you to keep a bad secret.
• Someone touches you in a way that makes you feel uncomfortable, or asks you to do something that you know is wrong.

### Avoiding temptation
• Stay away from places and people that may lead you to sin.
• Do not play alone in isolated places.
• Choose friends who do right.
• Pray for strength.

### Role play
In pairs, act out saying no to a friend who wants you to break a rule. Practise the four steps above.

## PAGE 4: People we can trust and strangers
A trusted person is one who cares about you, tells the truth, keeps you safe and gives good advice.

### Trusted people
• At home: parents, guardians, grandparents, an older sibling.
• At school: class teacher, head teacher, school counsellor, matron.
• In the community: pastor, Sunday school teacher, nurse, police officer, a neighbour your parents know.

### Staying safe from strangers
A stranger is someone you do not know. Most people are good, but a few are not. To stay safe:
• Do not go anywhere with a stranger, even if they say your parents sent them. Your family should agree on a family password.
• Do not accept sweets, money or lifts from strangers.
• Do not give your name, address or school to a stranger.
• If someone tries to grab you, shout, "Help! I do not know this person!" and run to a safe place.
• Tell a trusted adult straight away.

### If you are in danger
Say what happened to a trusted adult or call the Childline Kenya helpline on 116. Someone will help you.

## PAGE 5: Trusting God always
Trusting people is important, but our deepest trust is in God. People can sometimes fail us, but God never does.

### Why trust God?
• He made us and knows what is best for us.
• He is loving and faithful.
• He has helped people in the Bible again and again.
• He hears our prayers.

### How to show that we trust God
1. Pray every day and tell Him your worries.
2. Read the Bible to learn His promises.
3. Obey what He teaches, even when it is hard.
4. Thank Him for His help.

### Poem idea
Write a short poem called "People I trust". List two or three people and say why you trust them.

### Summary
• Trust means believing someone is reliable and will not harm you.
• Joseph said no to temptation and ran away from it (Genesis 39:7-12).
• We must be assertive, know trusted people, and avoid strangers.
• We trust God always (Psalm 125:1).

## QUIZ
1. What did Joseph do when Potiphar's wife tempted him?
   A) He agreed
   B) He refused and ran out of the house
   C) He told everyone in the town
   D) He hid in a cupboard
   Answer: B
   Why: Genesis 39:12 says that he left his cloak in her hand and ran out of the house.
2. What does being assertive mean?
   A) Being rude to everyone
   B) Saying no to wrong in a firm and polite way
   C) Keeping quiet always
   D) Fighting anyone who disagrees
   Answer: B
   Why: Being assertive means standing for what is right calmly and firmly.
3. A stranger offers you sweets and a lift home. What should you do?
   A) Accept and go
   B) Say no, move away and tell a trusted adult
   C) Give him your address
   D) Ask him for more sweets
   Answer: B
   Why: You should never go with strangers, and you should tell a trusted adult.
4. Which verse tells us that those who trust in the Lord are like Mount Zion?
   A) Psalm 125:1
   B) Genesis 1:1
   C) John 3:16
   D) Luke 1:37
   Answer: A
   Why: Psalm 125:1 says this about those who trust the Lord.
5. Who is a person you can trust at school?
   A) Any stranger who visits
   B) Your class teacher or school counsellor
   C) A person who tells you to keep bad secrets
   D) A person who offers you gifts to go with them
   Answer: B
   Why: Teachers and counsellors are trained to keep children safe.
6. List three people whom you can trust at home, school or in the community. (3 marks)
   Answer: Parent or guardian, class teacher, pastor, nurse, grandparent.
   Why: 1 mark for each valid person.
7. Describe how you can say no to a friend who wants you to do something wrong. (3 marks)
   Answer: Say no clearly, give a short reason, walk away, and tell a trusted adult.
   Why: 1 mark for saying no, 1 mark for giving a reason or walking away, 1 mark for telling a trusted adult.
8. Explain why we should trust God always. (3 marks)
   Answer: God made us and knows what is best; He is loving and faithful; He hears our prayers and has helped many people in the Bible.
   Why: 1 mark for each valid reason.
