## PAGE 1: What is a family?
A family is a group of people who are related by birth, marriage or adoption and who care for one another. God gave us families so that children can be loved, fed, taught and kept safe. In a family we learn to share, to say sorry and to pray.

Families are not all the same. Some children live with both parents. Some live with one parent, a grandmother, an aunt or a guardian. Some live with a foster family. Every kind of family should be respected, and every child deserves love and care.

### Songs and Bible
The Bible shows that God cares for families. He wants parents to teach their children and children to honour their parents.

### Try it
Say the names of the people you live with. Tell your partner one thing you enjoy doing with them, such as eating together, praying together or telling stories at night.

## PAGE 2: The nuclear family
The nuclear family is the smaller group of people who live together in one home. It is usually made up of parents and their children. It can also include a guardian in place of a parent.

### Members of the nuclear family
• Father
• Mother
• Sons (brothers)
• Daughters (sisters)

Guardians, step-parents and adopted children are also true family members.

### Roles
• Parents provide food, clothing and school fees, and they guide the children.
• Children obey, help with chores and respect everyone in the home.
• Older children help to look after the younger ones.

### Example
Achieng lives with her mother, her older brother Otieno and her little sister. They form a nuclear family. Achieng washes the plates, Otieno fetches water, and their mother cooks and goes to work. Each person has a part to play.

## PAGE 3: The extended family
The extended family is bigger than the nuclear family. It includes relatives who may not live in the same house but who are still part of the family.

### Members of the extended family
• Grandparents: father and mother of your parents.
• Uncles: brothers of your parents, and husbands of your aunts.
• Aunts: sisters of your parents, and wives of your uncles.
• Cousins: children of your uncles and aunts.

### Why the extended family matters
• Grandparents share wisdom and stories about the past.
• Aunts and uncles may help with school, food or advice.
• Cousins are friends and playmates.
• In hard times relatives support one another.

In many Kenyan communities, the extended family gathers for weddings, funerals, festivals and holidays. These meetings keep the family united.

### Steps
1. Write your own name at the bottom of a page.
2. Write the names of your parents or guardians above yours.
3. Write your grandparents above them, if you know them.
4. Add brothers, sisters, aunts, uncles and cousins in the right places.
5. Join the names with lines and colour your tree.

Knowing your family tree helps you understand who your relatives are and how to greet them.

## PAGE 5: Honouring and respecting family members
Ephesians 6:1-3 says, "Children, obey your parents in the Lord, for this is right. Honour your father and mother, which is the first commandment with a promise: that it may go well with you and that you may enjoy long life on the earth." Exodus 20:12 also gives this commandment.

To honour means to respect, to obey and to show thankfulness. Parents work hard to feed, clothe and educate us. Honouring them is one way of saying thank you.

### Ways to respect family members
• Greet them politely each morning.
• Listen when they speak and follow good instructions.
• Help with chores without being asked.
• Speak kindly. Do not shout or use rude words.
• Share food and toys fairly.
• Respect grandparents by helping them carry loads or fetch water.
• Pray for the family.

### Why it matters
Respect brings peace at home. When everyone honours each other, the home becomes a happy place where children can grow well.

### Summary
• The nuclear family includes parents and children.
• The extended family includes grandparents, aunts, uncles and cousins.
• A family tree shows how family members are related.
• We honour parents and respect all family members.

## QUIZ
1. Which group is part of the extended family?
   A) Father, mother and children living in one house
   B) Grandparents, aunts, uncles and cousins
   C) Teachers and classmates
   D) Neighbours only
   Answer: B
   Why: The extended family includes relatives beyond the nuclear family, such as grandparents, aunts, uncles and cousins.
2. Who is a cousin?
   A) Your father's brother
   B) Your mother's mother
   C) The child of your aunt or uncle
   D) Your teacher
   Answer: C
   Why: Cousins are children of your uncles and aunts.
3. In a family tree, who is normally placed at the top?
   A) The youngest child
   B) A friend
   C) The oldest generation, such as grandparents
   D) A neighbour
   Answer: C
   Why: A family tree begins with the oldest generation at the top and moves down to the youngest.
4. Which Bible passage tells children to honour their parents?
   A) Ephesians 6:1-3
   B) Genesis 1:1
   C) Luke 19:1
   D) John 3:16
   Answer: A
   Why: Ephesians 6:1-3 tells children to obey and honour their parents.
5. Which is a good way to respect your grandmother?
   A) Ignore her when she speaks
   B) Help her carry her load and greet her politely
   C) Shout when she asks for help
   D) Laugh at her old age
   Answer: B
   Why: Helping and greeting politely shows honour and respect.
6. Name two members of the nuclear family and two of the extended family. (4 marks)
   Answer: Nuclear: father, mother, brother, sister. Extended: grandmother, uncle, aunt, cousin. Any two of each.
   Why: 1 mark for each correct member, four in total.
7. Explain why it is important to honour parents. (3 marks)
   Answer: The Bible commands it (Exodus 20:12). Parents work hard to care for us. Honouring them brings peace at home and God's blessing.
   Why: 1 mark each for the Bible command, for the parents' care, and for the peace or blessing that follows.
8. Draw a simple family tree showing yourself, your parents and your grandparents. (3 marks)
   Answer: A tree with the child at the bottom, parents above, grandparents at the top, connected by lines and labelled.
   Why: 1 mark for correct levels, 1 mark for connecting lines, 1 mark for labels.
