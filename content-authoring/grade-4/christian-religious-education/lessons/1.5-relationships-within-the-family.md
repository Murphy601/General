## PAGE 1: My body belongs to me
Your body is a gift from God, and it belongs to you. Nobody has the right to touch you in a way that makes you feel unsafe, uncomfortable or confused. This lesson helps you to know how to stay safe and what to do if someone breaks these rules. It is not meant to frighten you. It is meant to protect you.

The Bible teaches that our bodies are precious. In 1 Corinthians 6:19 Christians are told that the body is a temple of the Holy Spirit. A temple is a holy place, so we must respect and care for our bodies.

### Private parts
The parts of your body that are covered by underwear or swimming costume are private. They are for you only. A doctor or nurse may need to examine you, but this should be done with a parent or trusted adult present and with your permission.

### Good touch and unsafe touch
• Good touch: a hug from a parent, a handshake, a pat on the back, or a nurse checking your temperature. It feels safe.
• Unsafe touch: any touching of your private parts, or any touch that hurts or makes you feel scared or uncomfortable.

## PAGE 2: Taking care and knowing the rules
Most adults and relatives are kind and safe. But sometimes a person, even someone you know, may try to hurt a child. That is why we learn safety rules. Following these rules is being wise, not being rude.

### Safety rules
• Be careful when you are alone with anyone, especially an older person or someone of the opposite sex. Try to play in open places where other people are nearby.
• Do not go alone to a quiet or hidden place with someone, even someone you know.
• Do not accept gifts, sweets, money or rides from strangers. Someone who tries to tempt you with gifts and then asks you to keep a secret is not safe.
• Do not keep secrets that make you feel bad or scared.
• Do not be alone with anyone who asks you to take off your clothes or touches your private parts.
• Do not share your pictures or private information online with people you do not know.

### Trust your feelings
If a situation makes your stomach feel tight or you feel uneasy, this is a warning. Move away from the situation and tell a trusted adult.

## PAGE 3: Saying No and getting away
You have the right to say NO to any touch or request that feels wrong, even if it comes from someone bigger, older or well known to you.

### How to protect yourself
1. Say NO loudly and clearly. Say, "Stop! I do not like that!"
2. Move away quickly. Run to a safe place where there are people.
3. Shout for help if you need to.
4. Tell a trusted adult straight away, even if the person told you to keep it secret.

### A story from the Bible
2 Samuel 13 tells a sad story. A young woman named Tamar was harmed by her half-brother Amnon. Tamar told him no and pleaded with him, but he did not listen. This story shows that wrong things can be done even by someone in the family. It also shows why we must tell a trusted grown-up early and never blame ourselves. What happened to Tamar was not her fault. The person who did wrong is always the one who is guilty.

### Remember
If someone hurts or touches you wrongly, it is never your fault.

## PAGE 4: Whom can I tell?
The most important thing is to tell someone who can help you. Choose a person you trust. Keep telling until someone listens and helps.

### People you can tell
• A parent, guardian or grandparent.
• Your class teacher or head teacher.
• A school counsellor or matron.
• A pastor, Sunday school teacher or another church leader.
• A nurse or doctor at a clinic.
• The police, or the Childline Kenya helpline. The free number is 116, and children and adults can call it for help.

### If the first person does not help
If you tell one person and they do not help, tell another one. Do not stop until you are safe. You can also write the problem down and give it to a teacher.

### What helpers should do
A good helper will listen, believe you, keep you safe and make sure the person doing wrong is stopped. You will not be in trouble for telling the truth.

### Activity
In pairs, name three trusted people you could tell. Write their names in your exercise book.

## PAGE 5: A holy life and healthy relationships
Healthy relationships in the family and community are built on respect, kindness and honesty. Family members should protect one another. Older relatives should set a good example. Younger ones should feel safe.

### The body as a temple
1 Corinthians 6:19 says the body is a temple of the Holy Spirit. Christians therefore try to live holy lives. For a child, this means keeping your body clean, dressing modestly, avoiding harmful places and people, and refusing to take part in anything that dishonours God or hurts others. As you grow older, you should wait until marriage before having a sexual relationship. For now, your job is to learn, play and stay safe.

### Being a good friend
• Do not tease others about their bodies.
• If a friend tells you they were touched wrongly, believe them and help them tell a trusted adult.
• Do not spread a friend's private matters as gossip.

### Summary
• Your body is a temple of the Holy Spirit and belongs to you.
• Say NO to unsafe touch, get away and tell a trusted adult.
• It is never a child's fault if they are hurt.
• Call 116 in Kenya if you need help.

## QUIZ
1. What does 1 Corinthians 6:19 say about the body?
   A) It is a temple of the Holy Spirit
   B) It belongs to strangers
   C) It should be hidden away
   D) It is not important
   Answer: A
   Why: The verse teaches that the body is a temple of the Holy Spirit, so we respect it.
2. Which is an example of unsafe touch?
   A) A handshake from a teacher
   B) A hug from your mother
   C) Someone touching your private parts
   D) A nurse checking your temperature with your parent present
   Answer: C
   Why: Touching private parts is unsafe and wrong, and must be reported.
3. Someone offers you sweets and money if you will go with them alone. What should you do?
   A) Go with them quickly
   B) Say NO, move away and tell a trusted adult
   C) Accept but keep it a secret
   D) Ask them for more sweets
   Answer: B
   Why: Gifts used to tempt children are a warning. Saying no and telling a trusted adult keeps you safe.
4. Which is a trusted person to tell if you are touched wrongly?
   A) A stranger on the road
   B) Someone who told you to keep secrets
   C) A parent, teacher or church leader
   D) Nobody at all
   Answer: C
   Why: Trusted adults such as parents, teachers and church leaders can protect you.
5. What is the Childline Kenya helpline number given in the lesson?
   A) 100
   B) 116
   C) 999
   D) 1000
   Answer: B
   Why: 116 is the free child helpline in Kenya.
6. State three ways of protecting yourself from sexual abuse. (3 marks)
   Answer: Say no loudly, run away to a safe place, do not accept gifts or rides from strangers, do not be alone in hidden places, and tell a trusted adult.
   Why: 1 mark for each correct way, up to 3.
7. A child says, "It was my fault because I did not run fast enough." Explain what you would tell the child. (3 marks)
   Answer: It is never the child's fault. The person who did wrong is guilty. The child was brave to speak and should tell a trusted adult who can help.
   Why: 1 mark for saying it is not the child's fault, 1 mark for who is guilty, 1 mark for telling a trusted adult.
8. Why should Christians respect their bodies? (2 marks)
   Answer: The body is a temple of the Holy Spirit, and it is a gift from God, so we should keep it holy and safe.
   Why: 1 mark for temple of the Holy Spirit, 1 mark for gift from God or living a holy life.
