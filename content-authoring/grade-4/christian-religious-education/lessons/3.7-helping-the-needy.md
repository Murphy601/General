## PAGE 1: Who are the needy?
A needy person is someone who lacks something important, such as food, clothing, shelter, health care, school things or a friend. They may be poor, sick, elderly, orphaned, disabled or in trouble because of drought, floods or fire.

Needy people live in every community, and they may be in your class. A child who has no pencil, a child who has no lunch, or a child who is new and lonely can all be in need.

### Types of need
• Physical need: food, water, clothing, a place to sleep.
• School need: books, pencils, uniform, fees.
• Health need: medicine, treatment, care when sick.
• Emotional need: comfort, friendship, someone to listen.

### Think about it
Can you think of a person near your home or school who is in need? How might you help them?

## PAGE 2: What Jesus taught
In Matthew 5:42 Jesus said, "Give to the one who asks you, and do not turn away from the one who wants to borrow from you."

Jesus wanted His followers to be generous. He did not want us to refuse to help someone who truly needs it. This does not mean giving everything away without thinking or giving to anyone who asks. It means being ready and willing to share what we have.

### Other Bible teachings on helping others
• Acts 20:35 reminds us that Jesus said, "It is more blessed to give than to receive."
• In Luke 10:25-37, Jesus told the story of a Samaritan who stopped to help a wounded man on the road when others passed by. Jesus said, "Go and do likewise."

### What we learn
God cares about the poor, and He wants us to care as well. When we help others, we are serving God.

## PAGE 3: Why we should help
There are many reasons to help those in need.
1. God asks us to. Jesus taught us to give and share.
2. It shows love. A kind action shows that we care.
3. It makes the community stronger. When neighbours help each other, everyone benefits.
4. It makes us thankful for what we have.
5. Tomorrow we may need help ourselves.

### An example of sharing from the Bible
In John 6, a boy shared his five loaves and two fish, and Jesus used them to feed a very large crowd. A small gift, when it is given willingly, can do a great deal.

### Kenyan ways of helping
In Kenya, people help each other in many ways. Neighbours may help a family after a funeral, or contribute in a harambee to pay school fees or hospital bills. Churches and schools often collect food and clothes for people in need. Children can join in by giving what they can.

## PAGE 4: How children can help
Children may not have much money, but there is still much that we can do.

### Ways to help at school
• Share your pencil or rubber with someone who has none.
• Share your lunch with a classmate who has none.
• Help a friend who is struggling with a lesson.
• Welcome a new pupil and show them around.

### Ways to help at home
• Help your parents with chores, especially when someone is sick.
• Give old clothes that still fit to those who need them.
• Look after younger brothers and sisters.

### Ways to help in church or the community
• Take part in collections for the poor.
• Visit a sick neighbour with your parents.
• Take part in clean-up days.

### Help promptly
Do not wait until later. If someone needs help today, help them today. A quick act of kindness is like a cup of water to a thirsty person.

## PAGE 5: Sharing with a good heart
It is good to give, but how we give also matters.

### Good ways of giving
• Give cheerfully. Do not grumble.
• Give quietly. Do not boast or show off.
• Give respectfully. Do not laugh at the person.
• Give what is useful.

### Being safe
When you want to help, make sure you stay safe. Tell your parents or teacher before you go to a place you do not know or before giving away something valuable.

### Role play
In groups, act out a scene where one child has no lunch, and another child shares. Then act out another where a child refuses to share. Discuss how each child feels.

### Summary
• Needy people lack important things such as food, clothing or care.
• Jesus taught, "Give to the one who asks you" (Matthew 5:42).
• We help because God asks us to and because we love others.
• Children can help by sharing, welcoming and caring, and they should do it quickly and cheerfully.

## QUIZ
1. Which verse tells us to give to the one who asks?
   A) Matthew 5:42
   B) Genesis 1:1
   C) Luke 1:37
   D) John 3:16
   Answer: A
   Why: Matthew 5:42 says, "Give to the one who asks you."
2. Who is a needy person?
   A) Someone who lacks something important, like food or clothing
   B) Anyone who is wealthy
   C) Only people who live in towns
   D) Only adults
   Answer: A
   Why: Needy people are those who lack basic things they need.
3. Which is an example of helping a needy classmate?
   A) Laughing at their old uniform
   B) Sharing your pencil with a pupil who has none
   C) Hiding your lunch
   D) Telling everyone about their problem
   Answer: B
   Why: Sharing a pencil meets a real need.
4. What did Jesus say the Samaritan story teaches?
   A) Go and do likewise
   B) Pass by quickly
   C) Wait for others to help
   D) Keep what you have
   Answer: A
   Why: After the story of the Samaritan who helped, Jesus told the man to go and do likewise.
5. What is the best way of giving to someone in need?
   A) Grumbling as you give
   B) Boasting to everyone
   C) Giving cheerfully and respectfully
   D) Laughing at the person
   Answer: C
   Why: Kind giving respects the person and is done with a good heart.
6. Give two examples of needs that a person may have. (2 marks)
   Answer: Food, clothing, shelter, medicine, school items, friendship. Any two.
   Why: 1 mark for each valid need.
7. Describe three ways in which children can help those in need at school. (3 marks)
   Answer: Share a pencil or lunch, help a classmate with a lesson, welcome a new pupil.
   Why: 1 mark for each valid way.
8. Explain why Christians should help the needy promptly. (3 marks)
   Answer: Jesus taught us to give; needs may be urgent, so delay can make suffering worse; helping shows love for God and neighbour.
   Why: 1 mark for Jesus' teaching, 1 mark for urgency of need, 1 mark for love.
