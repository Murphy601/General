## PAGE 1: The problem of King Balak
The story of Balaam and his donkey is in Numbers 22. The people of Israel had travelled through the desert and camped on the plains of Moab. Balak, the king of Moab, saw how many they were, and he was afraid. He decided to ask for help from a man named Balaam, whom people believed could bless or curse.

Balak sent messengers to Balaam with a request. "Come and put a curse on these people," he said, "because they are too powerful for me." Balak promised to pay Balaam well.

### Words to know
• Bless: to speak good things over someone, asking God to help them.
• Curse: to speak evil against someone, wishing them harm.
• Obey: to do what God or our leaders tell us.

### What did God say?
Balaam asked God what to do. God told him not to go with the messengers and not to curse the Israelites, because they were blessed (Numbers 22:12). Balaam sent the messengers home. Balak, however, sent more important officials with an even bigger reward. This time God allowed Balaam to go, but told him to do only what God told him.

## PAGE 2: The journey and the angel
Balaam got up in the morning, saddled his donkey and set out with the officials. But God was not pleased with the way Balaam's heart was leaning towards the reward. The angel of the Lord stood in the road with a drawn sword to block him.

Balaam did not see the angel, but the donkey did. The donkey turned off the road into a field. Balaam struck her to get her back onto the path.

Next the road went between two vineyard walls. The angel stood there again. The donkey pressed close to the wall and crushed Balaam's foot against it. Balaam struck her again.

The angel moved to a narrow place where there was no room to turn left or right. This time the donkey lay down under Balaam. Balaam became angry and beat her with his staff.

### Think about it
The donkey was trying to protect Balaam, yet Balaam punished her. What can we learn about how we should treat animals?

## PAGE 3: The donkey speaks
Then the Lord opened the donkey's mouth, and she said to Balaam, "What have I done to you to make you beat me these three times?" Balaam answered angrily that she had made a fool of him. The donkey reminded him that she had always carried him faithfully and had never behaved like this before.

Then the Lord opened Balaam's eyes, and he saw the angel of the Lord standing in the road with his sword drawn. Balaam bowed low and fell face down.

The angel asked Balaam why he had beaten his donkey three times. The angel said that the donkey had turned away from him three times, and if she had not, he would have killed Balaam but spared her.

### Why did the donkey talk?
The donkey talked because God made it happen. It shows that God is great and can do anything. It also showed Balaam that he needed to listen to God and not follow his own way.

### Balaam's reply
Balaam admitted that he had sinned. He offered to go back home. The angel told him to go with the men but to speak only what God told him to say.

## PAGE 4: Blessing, not cursing
Balaam went on, and God used him to bless Israel instead of cursing them (Numbers 23 and 24). King Balak was not happy, but God's word was more powerful than the king's wish.

### Lessons from the story
• Obey God. God told Balaam what to say, and Balaam had to obey Him even though a king was offering rich rewards.
• Do not be greedy. The reward from Balak was tempting, but God wants us to do right.
• Bless others. We should speak good words about others, not curse them, insult them or gossip. Words can build people up or break them down.
• Be kind to animals. The donkey served Balaam faithfully. He should not have beaten her for doing the right thing.
• God is great. He can do things that people cannot do, and He should be revered.

### Practical ideas
1. Give your domestic animals food and water.
2. Do not beat or overwork them.
3. Speak well of your classmates and neighbours.
4. Obey your parents and teachers, as long as their instruction is good.

## PAGE 5: Living out the lessons
Some children speak badly about others. They call names, spread rumours or wish others bad luck. The story of Balaam shows that God wants His people to bless, not curse. Speaking good things about others builds love and peace.

### Obeying God and those in authority
God speaks to us through the Bible, through our parents and teachers, and through our conscience. If we do not listen, we can get into trouble, like Balaam nearly did. Obeying quickly saves us from harm.

### Caring for animals
Many Kenyan families keep cows, goats, chickens or donkeys. Donkeys carry water, firewood and goods to the market. A donkey is a hardworking animal that deserves food, water, rest and kind treatment. The Bible says that a person who is good takes care of their animals (Proverbs 12:10).

### Summary
• Balak asked Balaam to curse Israel, but God told Balaam not to.
• The donkey saw the angel three times, and God let the donkey speak.
• Balaam learnt to obey and to speak only what God told him.
• We should obey God, bless others and be kind to animals.

## QUIZ
1. Who asked Balaam to curse the Israelites?
   A) Pharaoh
   B) King Balak of Moab
   C) King Herod
   D) Joshua
   Answer: B
   Why: Numbers 22 says that Balak, king of Moab, sent for Balaam.
2. What did the donkey see that Balaam could not see?
   A) A lion
   B) A snake
   C) The angel of the Lord with a drawn sword
   D) A wall
   Answer: C
   Why: The angel stood in the road, and the donkey turned aside to avoid him.
3. How many times did Balaam beat the donkey before she spoke?
   A) Once
   B) Two times
   C) Three times
   D) Five times
   Answer: C
   Why: The donkey said, "You have beaten me these three times."
4. What did the angel tell Balaam to do at the end of the encounter?
   A) Go home and never return
   B) Go with the men, but say only what God tells him to say
   C) Curse Israel
   D) Kill the donkey
   Answer: B
   Why: The angel allowed Balaam to go but ordered him to speak only God's words.
5. Which action shows kindness to animals?
   A) Beating a donkey to make it work faster
   B) Not feeding the goats
   C) Giving animals food, water and rest
   D) Overloading a donkey with heavy loads
   Answer: C
   Why: Kind treatment includes meeting an animal's needs and not overworking it.
6. Give two lessons we learn from the story of Balaam and his donkey. (2 marks)
   Answer: Obey God; do not be greedy; bless others; be kind to animals; God is great. Any two.
   Why: 1 mark for each valid lesson.
7. Why should we bless others and not curse or speak ill about them? (3 marks)
   Answer: Words can build up or break down people. God wants His people to love one another. Speaking well builds peace and good relationships.
   Why: 1 mark for the power of words, 1 mark for God's wish for love, 1 mark for peace or good relationships.
8. Describe two ways in which you can show care for animals at home. (2 marks)
   Answer: Give them food and water, shelter and rest, do not beat them, and treat them gently.
   Why: 1 mark for each valid way.
