## PAGE 1: Why we pray every day
Dainik means daily and Prarthana means prayer. Dainik Prarthana are the prayers that we say each day. A prayer is a way of talking to God. We thank God, we ask for wisdom and we promise to do good.

Many Hindu families pray in the morning after bathing and again in the evening. Some pray before meals or before starting studies. Children can pray too, and prayers do not have to be long.

### Why daily prayer is good for us
• It helps us begin the day calmly.
• It reminds us to be thankful for our food, our family and our health.
• It teaches us discipline, because we do it regularly.
• It brings peace of mind before an exam or a difficult task.
• It reminds us to be kind and honest.

In this lesson we learn three prayers: the Gayatri Mantra, Twameva Mata cha Pita Twameva, and the Saraswati Vandana. We also learn about the Mandir, the Hindu place of worship.

### Think about it
When do you feel most peaceful during your day? What could you say quietly to thank God in the morning?

## PAGE 2: The Gayatri Mantra
The Gayatri Mantra is one of the oldest and best-known Hindu prayers. It comes from the Rig Veda. Many Hindus say it in the morning and at the start of special events. It is a prayer for wisdom.

### The words
Om Bhur Bhuvah Svah
Tat Savitur Varenyam
Bhargo Devasya Dhimahi
Dhiyo Yo Nah Prachodayat

### The meaning
We think about the shining light of the divine Sun, who gives life to the earth, the sky and all the worlds. May that light guide our minds towards what is good and true.

### How to say it
1. Sit quietly with your back straight.
2. Close your eyes and take a slow breath.
3. Say the mantra slowly and clearly, one line at a time.
4. Listen to a teacher or an audio recording first, so you copy the right sounds.

The main message is that wisdom is like light: it clears away darkness and helps us choose well. It is a good prayer to say before studying.

## PAGE 3: Twameva Mata cha Pita Twameva
This prayer is said before meals, at assembly and in the evening. It speaks to God as our closest family and dearest friend.

### The words
Twameva mata cha pita twameva
Twameva bandhushcha sakha twameva
Twameva vidya dravinam twameva
Twameva sarvam mama deva deva

### The meaning
You are my mother and my father.
You are my relative and my friend.
You are my knowledge and my wealth.
You are everything to me, O God of gods.

### What it teaches
• God cares for us like a loving parent.
• God is with us like a true friend, even when we feel lonely.
• Knowledge and wealth are gifts, and we should use them well.
• When we thank God, we also learn to thank the people who care for us: parents, teachers and friends.

### Activity
Learn the four lines by heart with a friend. Say one line each, then swap. Then say what each line means in your own words.

## PAGE 4: Saraswati Vandana
Vandana means an act of respect and prayer. Saraswati is the Hindu goddess of knowledge, learning, music and speech. Students pray to her at the start of school, before exams and on the festival of Vasant Panchami.

Saraswati is shown wearing white clothes and sitting on a white lotus. She holds a veena (a stringed instrument) and a book. White stands for purity and peace, the veena stands for music and art, and the book stands for knowledge.

### The opening of the prayer
The well-known Saraswati Vandana begins with the words: Ya Kundendu Tushara Hara Dhavala. It praises the goddess as being as pure and white as the jasmine flower, the moon and the snow. The prayer asks her to protect us and take away the darkness of ignorance.

### What we ask for
• A mind that is clear and ready to learn
• Good speech that is kind and truthful
• Love for reading, music and the arts

### Practise
Ask your teacher or a resource person to lead the Saraswati Vandana and listen carefully to the sounds before you join in.

### Manners for prayer
• Bathe or wash your hands and feet first.
• Sit calmly on the floor or on a mat.
• Fold your hands in Namaste.
• Say the prayer slowly and clearly, with understanding.
• Do not laugh or talk during prayer.

### Reciting in school
During assembly we can recite a short prayer. Children who find it hard to speak can join by humming or listening. We should encourage our classmates with different needs to join in at their own pace.

### Common mistakes
• Rushing through the words without thinking of the meaning.
• Saying a prayer only because it is a rule. A prayer should come from the heart.

## PAGE 6: The Mandir, a Hindu place of worship
A Mandir is a Hindu temple. It is a holy place where people gather to pray, sing hymns and celebrate festivals. Mandirs are built in many towns in Kenya, such as Nairobi, Mombasa and Kisumu. Ask an adult which Mandirs are near your home.

### Main parts of a Mandir
• The entrance, where visitors remove their shoes.
• The main hall, where people sit for prayers and songs.
• The inner room (garbhagriha) where the murti, the statue of God, is placed.
• The spire (shikhara) or dome above the inner room.
• A bell that worshippers ring gently to announce they have arrived.

### Rites and rituals in a Mandir
• Darshan: looking at the murti with respect.
• Aarti: waving a lamp in front of the murti while singing a prayer.
• Prasad: blessed food shared after prayers.
• Bhajans: devotional songs.

### Manners when visiting
1. Remove your shoes before you enter.
2. Dress modestly.
3. Speak softly and switch off your phone.
4. Do not touch the murti or offerings unless you are asked.
5. Ask permission before taking photographs.
6. Accept prasad with your right hand or both hands, and say thank you.

## PAGE 7: Mapping and drawing a Mandir
The Mandir is not only a place for prayer. It is also a work of art. Builders and artists use carvings, paintings and shapes to make it beautiful. Some Mandirs have tall towers, with carvings of gods and flowers. Others are simpler buildings. In each case, the architecture is made to help people feel calm and respectful.

### Activity 1: Map the Mandirs
Ask your teacher to help you use a safe map on a tablet or phone. Search for the Mandirs in your county or in Nairobi and Mombasa. Mark them on a sketch map of Kenya, with a small symbol for each. Write the name of the town beside it.

### Activity 2: Draw and colour a Mandir
1. Draw a rectangle for the main hall.
2. Draw a triangle or a pointed tower on top for the spire.
3. Draw a door at the front and steps leading up to it.
4. Add flags and small windows.
5. Colour it and write labels for the main parts.

### Activity 3: Make a collage
Cut out pictures of Mandirs from old magazines or print photos with permission. Stick them on a sheet of paper to make a collage.

### Summary
• Dainik Prarthana are our daily prayers.
• The three prayers are the Gayatri Mantra, Twameva Mata cha Pita Twameva and the Saraswati Vandana.
• A Mandir is a place of worship with special customs of respect.

   B) Daily prayers
   C) Holy rivers
   D) Yoga exercises
   Answer: B
   Why: Dainik means daily and Prarthana means prayer.
2. The Gayatri Mantra is mainly a prayer for what?
   A) Wealth
   B) Wisdom
   C) Rain
   D) Sleep
   Answer: B
   Why: It asks that the divine light guide our minds toward what is good and true.
3. In the prayer Twameva Mata cha Pita Twameva, God is called all of these EXCEPT which one?
   A) Mother and father
   B) Friend
   C) Knowledge
   D) Enemy
   Answer: D
   Why: The prayer calls God our mother, father, relative, friend, knowledge and wealth.
4. Which goddess is prayed to for knowledge and learning?
   A) Saraswati
   B) Ganga
   C) Tulsi
   D) Kali
   Answer: A
   Why: Saraswati is the goddess of knowledge, learning, music and speech.
5. What do worshippers do before entering a Mandir?
   A) Wear shoes
   B) Remove their shoes
   C) Shout greetings
   D) Play music
   Answer: B
   Why: Shoes are removed as a sign of respect and to keep the holy place clean.

   Answer: God is my mother and father, my relative and friend, my knowledge and wealth, and my everything.

   Answer: The book stands for knowledge and the veena stands for music and the arts.

   Answer: For example the bell, rung to announce arrival; the murti, looked at during darshan; the aarti lamp, waved during prayer (any two, with uses).
   Why: 1 mark for each item and 1 mark for each correct use.
