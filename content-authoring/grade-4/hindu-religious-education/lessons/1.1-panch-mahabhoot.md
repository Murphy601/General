## PAGE 1: The five great elements
Everything around us is part of creation. Hindu teaching says that the world is made from five great elements. In Sanskrit they are called the Panch Mahabhoot. Panch means five, maha means great, and bhoot means element.

The five elements are:
• Prithvi: earth, the soil and rocks under our feet
• Jal (also called Apas): water
• Agni: fire, heat and light
• Vayu: air, the wind and the breath we take in
• Akash: space, the open sky that holds everything

Think about a maize farm in Kenya. The soil is Prithvi. Rain and river water are Jal. The sun's heat that ripens the maize is Agni. The wind that carries pollen is Vayu. The wide sky above the shamba is Akash. All five work together to help the crop grow.

In this topic we will learn about one of these elements, Jal, and about plants that many people hold sacred.

## PAGE 2: Jal, water in our daily lives
Jal means water. No living thing can live without it. People, animals and plants all need water every day.

### How we use water at home
• Drinking and cooking
• Washing our bodies, clothes and dishes
• Cleaning the house and the toilet
• Watering vegetables and giving drinks to cattle and goats
• Growing crops such as rice, maize and tea

Water is also important in worship. In a Hindu home, a person may wash the hands, feet and mouth before praying. Water is poured over the murti (statue of God) in some prayers. Water is sprinkled to show that a place is clean and ready for prayer. Because water cleans, it has become a sign of a clean and peaceful heart.

Other faiths also use water in their religious life. Sikhs wash their hands and feet before entering a gurdwara, and the Golden Temple in Amritsar stands in the middle of a holy pool called the Sarovar. Many Buddhists pour water as a gentle offering. The four faiths in this course all show respect for water.

### Think about it
A jerrican of water may be carried from a river or a kiosk. Families who fetch water from far away know very well how precious water is. What can we do so that no water is wasted in our homes and school?

## PAGE 3: Sacred rivers
Hindu scriptures speak of many rivers as holy. The most famous is River Ganga (Ganges) in India. It begins high in the Himalaya mountains and flows across northern India to the sea. Many Hindus bathe in the Ganga on special days and keep a little Ganga jal (Ganga water) at home for prayers. In the stories, Ganga is also honoured as a goddess.

The Beas is a river in the Punjab region of northern India. Punjab is the land where Sikhism began, so the Beas is part of the story of the Sikh people.

The river Rijubalika (also spelt Rijuvalika) is important to Jains. Jains believe that Lord Mahavir became fully enlightened while sitting on the bank of this river.

### Sacred waters near us
Kenya also has rivers and lakes that people cherish, such as the Tana, the Nzoia and Lake Victoria. Hindus in Kenya remember the Ganga in their prayers, and they also give thanks for the water of the land where they live.

## PAGE 4: Sacred trees and plants
Many trees and plants are sacred because they give food, shade, medicine and clean air. Each faith honours certain plants.

### Tulsi
Tulsi is the holy basil plant. Hindu families often grow it in a pot or a raised stand called the Tulsi Vrindavan in the courtyard. Family members water it every morning, walk around it and pray. Its leaves are offered to Lord Vishnu and Lord Krishna. Tulsi leaves are also used in home medicine, for example in tea for coughs and colds.

### Beri (ber) tree
Sikhs remember the Ber Sahib at Sultanpur Lodhi in Punjab. This is a ber tree beside a gurdwara. It is linked with Guru Nanak Dev Ji, the first Sikh Guru, who lived in that town for some years.

### Bodhi tree
The Bodhi tree is a kind of fig tree, also called the peepal. Buddhists honour it because Prince Siddhartha sat under a Bodhi tree at Bodh Gaya and became the Buddha, the Awakened One. The peepal is also sacred to Hindus.

### Sal tree
Jains say that Lord Mahavir became enlightened under a sal tree.

## PAGE 5: Roles of sacred plants and how to care for them
Sacred plants teach us to respect all of nature. They have many roles.

• They give oxygen and clean the air.
• They give shade where people can sit, rest and pray.
• Their leaves, fruits and bark are used for food and medicine.
• Their leaves and flowers are used in worship.
• They are a reminder that life needs care.

### Activity: seeds in dry and wet conditions
Plant two lots of bean seeds in two small tins of soil. Give one tin water every day. Give the other no water at all. Look at both tins after one week and after two weeks. Draw what you see. The watered seeds sprout and grow. The dry seeds stay hard and do not grow. This shows how much life depends on Jal.

### Caring for plants and water
1. Plant a tree or a Tulsi plant in the school compound with permission.
2. Water plants in the early morning or evening so less water dries up.
3. Collect rainwater from a roof in a tank or a jerrican.
4. Close the tap fully after use and mend any leak.
5. Never throw plastic or waste into rivers or streams.

Children with different abilities can join in the planting activity by holding the seedlings, filling the watering can, or drawing the plants.

## PAGE 6: Summary and things to remember
### What we have learned
• The Panch Mahabhoot are the five great elements: Prithvi (earth), Jal (water), Agni (fire), Vayu (air) and Akash (space).
• Jal is needed for life, health, farming and cleanliness. It is also used in worship, to show respect and purity.
• Rivers such as the Ganga, the Beas and the Rijubalika are honoured in Hindu, Sikh and Jain traditions.
• Tulsi, the Ber tree, the Bodhi tree and the Sal tree are honoured in different faiths.
• We show respect by caring for water and for trees.

### Common mistakes
• Saying there are four elements. There are five.
• Thinking that only Hindus respect water and trees. People of many faiths show respect for them, each in their own way.
• Thinking a river is holy only in one place. We can care for every river near us.

### Respecting others
When you join a religious activity of another faith, listen carefully, follow the instructions and behave politely. This is how we live in peace.

## QUIZ
1. How many great elements are in the Panch Mahabhoot?
   A) Three
   B) Four
   C) Five
   D) Seven
   Answer: C
   Why: The five elements are Prithvi, Jal, Agni, Vayu and Akash.
2. Which element is Jal?
   A) Fire
   B) Water
   C) Air
   D) Earth
   Answer: B
   Why: Jal is the Sanskrit word for water.
3. Which plant do Hindu families often grow in the courtyard and water every morning?
   A) Tulsi
   B) Maize
   C) Cactus
   D) Sisal
   Answer: A
   Why: Tulsi, the holy basil, is grown in many Hindu homes and used in worship.
4. Under which tree did Prince Siddhartha become the Buddha?
   A) Baobab
   B) Mango
   C) Bodhi tree
   D) Ber tree
   Answer: C
   Why: The Bodhi tree, a kind of fig or peepal, at Bodh Gaya is where he became enlightened.
5. Which river is especially important to Jains because of Lord Mahavir?
   A) Ganga
   B) Rijubalika
   C) Tana
   D) Nzoia
   Answer: B
   Why: Jains believe Lord Mahavir became enlightened on the bank of the Rijubalika.
6. Name the five great elements in the Panch Mahabhoot. (3 marks)
   Answer: Prithvi (earth), Jal (water), Agni (fire), Vayu (air) and Akash (space).
   Why: Award 1 mark for any two correct elements and 3 marks for all five (award 2 marks for four).
7. State three ways in which water is used at home. (3 marks)
   Answer: Drinking, cooking, washing, cleaning and watering plants or animals (any three).
   Why: 1 mark for each correct use.
8. Explain why sacred trees and plants are important to people. (3 marks)
   Answer: They give oxygen, shade, food and medicine, and their leaves are used in worship. They also remind us to care for nature.
   Why: 1 mark for each correct reason, up to 3.
9. Describe an experiment that shows water is important for plants. (3 marks)
   Answer: Plant seeds in two tins. Water one every day and give none to the other. After some days the watered seeds grow and the dry ones do not.
   Why: 1 mark for the two tins, 1 mark for the different watering, 1 mark for the result.
10. List two ways of saving water in your school. (2 marks)
   Answer: Closing taps fully, mending leaks, collecting rainwater, reusing water to water plants (any two).
   Why: 1 mark for each sensible way.
