## PAGE 1: Who are Enlightened Beings?
Some people in history lived lives so full of wisdom, courage and kindness that millions of people still remember and follow them. We call them Enlightened Beings. Enlightened means full of light: the light of true knowledge. Many people believe such beings show us something of Paramatma, the Supreme Soul, in the world.

Different faiths tell about different Enlightened Beings. In this lesson we meet four:
• Lord Narsimha, an Avtar (a coming down of God) in Hinduism
• Lord Parshvanath, a Tirthankar of Jainism
• Lord Buddha, the Awakened One, in his childhood
• Sri Guru Ram Das Ji, the fourth Guru of the Sikhs

### Words you need
• Avtar: a form in which God is believed to come to the earth to protect goodness.
• Tirthankar: a Jain teacher who shows the path across the river of life.
• Guru: a teacher who leads us from darkness to light.
• Teaching: advice or lesson that a wise person gives.

### Think about it
Who is a person that has inspired you? What did that person do? Keep this question in mind as you read.

## PAGE 2: Lord Narsimha
Narsimha is one of the Avtars of Lord Vishnu. His name comes from nar (man) and simha (lion). He appears with the body of a man and the head and claws of a lion.

The story is about a king called Hiranyakashipu and his young son, Prahlad. Hiranyakashipu was very proud and powerful. He believed a special gift he had received made it impossible for anyone to defeat him. He ordered everyone in his kingdom to call him God.

Prahlad loved Lord Vishnu and prayed to Him every day. His father was angry and tried many times to make the boy stop, but Prahlad stayed calm and did not give up his faith.

One evening Hiranyakashipu pointed to a pillar and asked mockingly whether God was inside it. Suddenly the pillar broke open and Lord Narsimha appeared. He was neither fully man nor fully animal. It was neither day nor night, but the time between. He stopped the king at the doorway, neither inside nor outside the palace, and defeated him without an ordinary weapon.

### What we learn
• Pride and cruelty do not last.
• A child's faith and honesty can be stronger than a powerful bully.
• God protects those who do right.

## PAGE 3: Lord Parshvanath
Lord Parshvanath is the twenty-third Tirthankar of Jainism. Jains believe he lived long before Lord Mahavir, the twenty-fourth. He was born as a prince in the city of Varanasi in India. Even as a young man he cared deeply about all living creatures.

A well-known story says that once he saw a man burning a log of wood to make a fire. Parshvanath saw with his wisdom that a snake was inside the log. He asked the man to stop, and the snake was rescued. The man was angry at first, but later understood the lesson.

Later Parshvanath gave up his palace life, became a monk and taught people the path of Ahimsa. Ahimsa means not hurting any living being by thought, word or action. He taught four great rules:
1. Do not harm any living being (Ahimsa).
2. Always speak the truth (Satya).
3. Do not take what is not given (Asteya).
4. Do not be greedy or hold on to too many things (Aparigraha).

The snake is often shown over the head of Parshvanath's statues as a sign that he is remembered.

### Teaching for today
When we pick up a beetle from the road and put it on the grass, or when we do not waste food, we practise Ahimsa.

## PAGE 4: Lord Buddha, the childhood of Prince Siddhartha
The Buddha was born as Prince Siddhartha, more than 2,500 years ago, in a place called Lumbini. This place is now in the country of Nepal. His father was King Suddhodana. His mother, Queen Maya, died a few days after he was born, and his mother's sister, Mahaprajapati Gotami, brought him up with great love.

Siddhartha grew up in comfort in the palace. But he had a gentle heart and felt sorry when he saw others suffer.

### The story of the wounded swan
One day Siddhartha and his cousin Devadatta were walking in the garden. Devadatta shot an arrow and hit a swan. The bird fell wounded. Siddhartha ran to it, gently pulled out the arrow, and nursed it. Devadatta said that the swan belonged to him because he had shot it. They asked the wise men of the court to decide. They said that the one who saves a life has a greater right to it than the one who tries to end it. Siddhartha let the swan go free when it was well.

### What we learn
• Be kind to animals.
• Help those who are hurt.
• Do not use power to harm others.

When he was grown up, Siddhartha left the palace to search for the answer to suffering. He became the Buddha, the Awakened One.

## PAGE 5: Sri Guru Ram Das Ji
Sri Guru Ram Das Ji was the fourth Guru of the Sikhs. He was born in the city of Lahore, in a family that was not rich, and his childhood name was Jetha. He lost his parents while he was very young. A story says that he sold boiled grain in the streets to help his family, which shows that he learnt honest work as a child.

He later served Guru Amar Das Ji, the third Guru, with deep humility and love. Guru Amar Das Ji saw his goodness and chose him as the next Guru.

### His contributions
• He started the city that became Amritsar and had a large pool dug there, called the Sarovar.
• He composed hymns, which are included in the Guru Granth Sahib.
• He wrote the Laavan, the hymns that are sung at a Sikh wedding.
• He taught that all people are equal and that serving others is a way of serving God.

### Teaching for today
When we clean the school compound together or serve tea at a community event, we follow the same teaching of service.

## PAGE 6: Learning from Enlightened Beings
All these great beings lived at different times and in different places. Yet their teachings are alike in many ways. They teach kindness, truth, humility and service.

### Matching the being with the teaching
• Narsimha: God protects the good, and pride falls.
• Parshvanath: Do not harm any living being.
• Buddha: Care for those who are hurt, as Siddhartha cared for the swan.
• Guru Ram Das Ji: Honest work, equality and service.

### Living the teachings
1. Do not tease or bully a classmate. Stand up for a child who is being troubled.
2. Take care of animals, plants and school property.
3. Share what you have with those who have less.
4. Work honestly and be humble when you are praised.

### Activity ideas
Act out a short skit of one story with your group. Make a poster showing your favourite teaching. Say what you can do this week to follow it.

### Common mistakes
• Mixing up names. Remember that Parshvanath is a Jain Tirthankar, Buddha is the founder of Buddhism and Guru Ram Das is a Sikh Guru.
• Thinking these beings were only for people long ago. Their teachings help us today.

## QUIZ
1. Which Enlightened Being had the body of a man and the head of a lion?
   A) Lord Buddha
   B) Lord Parshvanath
   C) Guru Ram Das Ji
   D) Lord Narsimha
   Answer: D
   Why: Narsimha means man-lion, and he is an Avtar of Lord Vishnu.
2. What is the name of the young devotee whom Narsimha protected?
   A) Prahlad
   B) Devadatta
   C) Jetha
   D) Suddhodana
   Answer: A
   Why: Prahlad was the young son of Hiranyakashipu who kept praying to Vishnu.
3. Which teaching is most closely linked with Lord Parshvanath?
   A) Ahimsa
   B) War
   C) Pride
   D) Wealth
   Answer: A
   Why: Parshvanath taught Ahimsa, not harming any living being.
4. Who was the fourth Guru of the Sikhs?
   A) Guru Nanak Dev Ji
   B) Guru Amar Das Ji
   C) Guru Ram Das Ji
   D) Guru Arjan Dev Ji
   Answer: C
   Why: Sri Guru Ram Das Ji followed Guru Amar Das Ji as the fourth Guru.
5. Who looked after Prince Siddhartha after his mother died?
   A) Devadatta
   B) His aunt Mahaprajapati Gotami
   C) Guru Ram Das
   D) Prahlad
   Answer: B
   Why: His mother's sister raised him with love.
6. Tell in your own words the story of Siddhartha and the swan. (3 marks)
   Answer: Devadatta shot a swan with an arrow. Siddhartha pulled out the arrow and nursed the bird. The wise men said the one who saves a life has more right to it, so Siddhartha let the swan go free.
   Why: 1 mark for the wounded swan, 1 mark for Siddhartha helping it, 1 mark for the decision.
7. Give two contributions of Sri Guru Ram Das Ji. (2 marks)
   Answer: He started the city of Amritsar and had the Sarovar dug there; he composed hymns and the Laavan; he taught equality and service (any two).
   Why: 1 mark for each correct contribution.
8. Explain what Ahimsa means and give one way to practise it at school. (3 marks)
   Answer: Ahimsa means not harming any living being in thought, word or deed. At school we can practise it by not bullying classmates or by caring for animals and plants.
   Why: 2 marks for a correct meaning and 1 mark for a sensible example.
9. What lesson does the story of Narsimha and Prahlad teach us? (2 marks)
   Answer: Pride and cruelty do not last, and God protects those who have faith and do right.
   Why: 1 mark for each correct lesson.
10. Choose one Enlightened Being and describe how you will follow his teaching this week. (3 marks)
   Answer: Any sensible plan linked to the chosen teaching, for example being kind to a lonely classmate to follow Siddhartha's kindness.
   Why: 1 mark for the being, 1 mark for the teaching, 1 mark for a clear action.
