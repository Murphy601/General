## PAGE 1: What is Yoga?
Yoga is an ancient practice from India that joins the body, the breath and the mind. The word yoga comes from a Sanskrit word that means to join or to unite. People all over the world practise yoga to stay healthy, calm and strong.

Yoga has many parts. In this topic we learn two of them:
• Pranayam: breathing exercises. Prana means life energy or breath, and ayam means to control or lengthen.
• Asanas: body postures. Asana means a seat or a posture.

### Why we practise yoga
• It makes the body flexible and strong.
• It helps us breathe well and stay calm.
• It helps us focus on our studies.
• It teaches discipline, because we do it regularly.

### International Yoga Day
Every year on 21 June, people around the world celebrate International Yoga Day. The United Nations decided on this day in 2014, and it was first celebrated in 2015. In many places, including Kenya, people gather to do yoga together in parks, halls and schools.

### Staying safe
Yoga must always be done with a teacher or a trusted adult guiding you. Practise on a mat, wear loose clothes and do it when your stomach is not full. Stop at once if you feel dizzy or pain.

## PAGE 2: Breathing well
Breathing is something we do all the time without noticing. Fast, shallow breathing can make us feel tense. Slow, deep breathing can help us feel relaxed. Pranayam teaches us to notice our breath and use it well.

### How to sit for Pranayam
1. Sit on the floor with your legs crossed, or on a chair with your feet flat.
2. Keep your back and neck straight, but relaxed.
3. Rest your hands on your knees.
4. Close your eyes gently.
5. Breathe in and out through the nose.

### Try a simple deep breath
Put your hands on your stomach. Breathe in slowly and count to four. Feel your stomach rise. Breathe out slowly and count to four. Feel your stomach go down. Repeat five times. Notice how you feel.

Pranayam is not a competition. We do not hold our breath for long, and we never force ourselves. Children should practise gentle versions with an expert.

### Think about it
How do you breathe when you are running? How do you breathe when you are asleep? What is different?

## PAGE 3: Bhastrika and Kapaal Bhaati
### Bhastrika
Bhastrika is called the bellows breath. A bellows is a tool that blowers use to pump air into a fire. In Bhastrika, you breathe in and out with equal strength, like a bellows.

1. Sit tall with your eyes closed.
2. Take a deep breath in through your nose, filling the chest.
3. Breathe out through your nose with the same force.
4. Repeat slowly a few times, then go back to normal breathing.
5. Rest. If you feel dizzy, stop.

It warms the body and wakes it up. Children should do only a few rounds at a slow pace, with a teacher.

### Kapaal Bhaati
Kapaal means the forehead or skull, and bhaati means shining. So Kapaal Bhaati means shining forehead. In this exercise, the breath goes out in short, quick pushes, while breathing in happens by itself.

1. Sit tall and rest your hands on your knees.
2. Take a normal breath in.
3. Push the air out through the nose with a quick, gentle pull of the tummy inward.
4. Let the next breath in happen by itself.
5. Do it a few times and rest.

### Safety
Both practices are active. Children must do them gently, only under the eye of a trained resource person, and must stop when they tire. Anyone unwell should not join in.

## PAGE 4: Asanas, postures for the body
Asanas are postures that stretch and strengthen the body. They should be done slowly, with steady breathing. We hold each pose calmly for a few breaths and never force our body.

### Rules for asanas
• Practise on a mat, in a clean and quiet place.
• Wear loose and comfortable clothes.
• Do them when your stomach is not full.
• Move slowly and smoothly.
• Stop if something hurts.
• Practise with a teacher who can show you the correct way.

### Why an expert should guide us
A trained teacher knows how each posture should look and can correct mistakes. Wrong posture can strain the back or knees. That is why the key inquiry of this topic asks: Why is it important to perform asanas in the presence of an expert?

### Working in pairs
In groups or pairs, children can encourage one another. One person does the posture and the other checks that the back is straight and that the person is breathing calmly. Then you swap. This builds teamwork and kindness.

## PAGE 5: Vajra Asana
Vajra Asana is often called the thunderbolt or diamond pose. Vajra means a diamond or thunderbolt, which is very strong. In this posture you sit on your heels. Many people use it for sitting during prayer, study and meditation.

### How to do Vajra Asana
1. Kneel on the mat with your knees together.
2. Bring your big toes close to each other.
3. Sit back so that your bottom rests on your heels.
4. Keep your back and head straight.
5. Place your palms on your thighs.
6. Look straight ahead or close your eyes. Breathe slowly.

### Benefits
It helps the legs stay flexible, keeps the back straight and helps us feel calm. Some people find it comfortable to sit in this posture after a meal, but this should be discussed with a teacher.

Do not stay in this pose if your knees or ankles hurt.

## PAGE 6: Sasankasana
Sasankasana is the hare or moon pose. Sasanka means the moon (or the mark of a hare on the moon, which is how some people picture it). The posture looks like a rabbit resting, or like a crescent moon.

### How to do Sasankasana
1. Start in Vajra Asana, sitting on your heels.
2. Breathe in and raise both arms above your head.
3. Breathe out and bend forward slowly from the hips.
4. Rest your forehead on the mat and stretch your arms forward on the floor.
5. Stay for a few calm breaths.
6. Breathe in and slowly come up. Lower your arms.

## PAGE 7: Yoga in our community
### Activities you can do
• Take part in International Yoga Day on 21 June at school or in the community, with a teacher's permission. Photos or videos can be taken.
• Make a chart with pictures of asanas cut from magazines or printed with an adult's help.
• Make a short yoga guidance video, with a trained adult present, showing how to sit in Vajra Asana.
• Practise in groups and encourage one another.

### Including everyone
Children with special needs can join yoga in their own way. They may breathe slowly, do a seated pose or help by holding the chart. Everyone's effort is respected.

### Summary
• Pranayam is breathing practice. Bhastrika is the bellows breath and Kapaal Bhaati is the shining forehead breath.
• Asanas are body postures. We learnt Vajra Asana and Sasankasana.
• Always practise with a teacher, on a clean mat, gently and safely.
• International Yoga Day is 21 June.

### Common mistakes
• Practising very fast or forcing the body.
• Holding the breath for too long.
• Doing yoga without an adult who knows it well.
• Comparing yourself with others.

   A) Vajra Asana
   B) Sasankasana
   C) Bhastrika
   D) Namaste

   Why: Bhastrika is the bellows breath, a Pranayam.

   B) So that postures are done correctly and safely
   C) To finish quickly
   D) So nobody else can join
   Answer: B
   Why: A trained teacher can correct mistakes and prevent injury.
