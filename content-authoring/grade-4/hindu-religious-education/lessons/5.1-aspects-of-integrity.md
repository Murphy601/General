## PAGE 1: What is integrity?
Sadachaar means good conduct, the way a good person behaves. One of the most important parts of good conduct is integrity. A person with integrity is honest and does what is right, even when nobody is watching.

### Signs of integrity
• Telling the truth, even when it is hard.
• Returning something that is not yours.
• Keeping your promises.
• Not cheating in games or tests.
• Being fair to everyone.

Imagine that you find a Ksh 100 note on the school field. Nobody saw you pick it up. A person with integrity takes it to the teacher, so it can be returned to its owner. That is integrity.

Integrity is like a strong wall. If the wall has one weak brick, it can fall. A person who lies once or twice finds it harder to be trusted. A person who is honest every day is trusted by everyone.

### Why integrity matters in daily life
• People trust us and want to be our friends.
• We feel calm inside because we have nothing to hide.
• Our family, school and community live in peace.
• We become good leaders and good citizens.

### Think about it
Have you ever been tempted to say something that was not true? What did you do? How did you feel afterwards?

## PAGE 2: Parables from the four faiths, part 1
A parable is a short story with a lesson. Each faith has moral stories. Here are two.

### Hinduism: King Harishchandra
Harishchandra was a king known for always speaking the truth. In the story, he had promised a sage that he would give whatever was asked of him. The sage asked for his whole kingdom, and Harishchandra gave it up rather than break his word. He and his family then faced great hardship, and he took very humble work to earn a living. Through all this he did not tell a lie or go back on his promise. In the end, the story says, the gods were pleased with his honesty and everything was restored to him.

Lesson: Truth is worth more than wealth or comfort.

### Sikhism: Guru Nanak and honest earnings
A well-known story tells that Guru Nanak Dev Ji, the first Sikh Guru, once stayed at the humble home of Bhai Lalo, a carpenter who earned his living by honest work. A rich man named Malik Bhago invited him to a feast, but Guru Nanak preferred to eat with Lalo. The story says he explained that food earned by honest labour is pure, while wealth taken unfairly from others is not.

Lesson: Honest work is the best way to earn a living. Kirat Karni means earning by honest labour.

## PAGE 3: Parables from the four faiths, part 2
### Jainism: Asteya, not taking what is not given
Lord Mahavir and other Jain teachers taught Asteya, which means not stealing. This includes not taking small things, like a friend's pencil, or big things. It also means not using someone's time or ideas without asking.

A short example story: A boy found a purse on the road and it had money in it. He thought it would help his family, but he remembered that he was taught not to take what is not his. He asked in the village until he found the owner. The owner was so happy that he thanked the boy and his family. The boy felt light and happy in his heart.

Lesson: What is not ours, we do not keep.

### Buddhism: Right speech and the five precepts
The Buddha taught his followers five simple rules of good living. Two of them are not to steal and not to tell lies. He taught that a person's words should be true, kind and helpful. When a person spoke in a false or unkind way, people stopped trusting him.

Lesson: Be truthful. Speak in a way that helps and does not hurt.

## PAGE 4: Integrity with money and technology
### Integrity in handling money
• If a shopkeeper gives you too much change, give it back.
• Pay for what you take, such as a mandazi at the school kiosk.
• If you are given money to buy something for your family, bring back the correct change and receipt.
• Save honestly and do not take from other people's savings.
• Never borrow without telling the owner.

### Worked example
Amina is sent to the shop with Ksh 200 to buy sugar that costs Ksh 150. The shopkeeper gives her Ksh 100 in change by mistake.
Step 1: Correct change is 200 minus 150 = Ksh 50.
Step 2: She was given Ksh 100, which is Ksh 50 too much.
Step 3: A person with integrity tells the shopkeeper and returns the Ksh 50.

### Integrity when using technology
• Ask permission before using a phone, tablet or computer.
• Do not copy another person's work and call it your own.
• Do not send unkind or untrue messages.
• Do not share personal details with strangers.
• Tell a trusted adult if you see something that makes you uncomfortable.

When you use the internet, behave as though your teacher and parents are watching. That is integrity online.

## PAGE 5: Integrity at school and in the community
We can practise integrity in many places.

### In class
• Do your own work in tests.
• Own up when you break something.
• Give the teacher any note or item that belongs to someone else.

### In games and sports
• Follow the rules even when the referee is not looking.
• Accept the result of the game with dignity.
• Do not push or trip others to win.

### A lost and found corner
Make a box or table in your classroom where found items are placed. Each item is written in a book with the date and the name of the finder. Owners can come and claim their things. Children who return items should be thanked. This teaches the whole class that being honest is respected.

### Sharing experiences
In groups, tell the class about a time you or someone you know did the right thing when it was hard. Write a short essay titled "Why honesty is the best policy".

### Skits
Choose a parable and act it out. One learner tells the story, others play the characters. After the skit, say what lesson it teaches.

## PAGE 6: Ideal practices of a passenger
Integrity and safety go together when we travel. Whether we ride a matatu, a bus, a train or a school bus, passengers must behave well so that everyone is safe.

### Boarding
1. Wait for the vehicle to stop completely.
2. Form a line. Do not push.
3. Let children, elders, and people with disabilities board first.
4. Pay your fare honestly and take your change.
5. Find a seat and wear the seat belt if there is one.

### During the journey
• Sit still. Do not put your head or hands out of the window.
• Keep the aisle clear of bags.
• Speak quietly and do not distract the driver.
• Do not litter. Keep your rubbish until you can put it in a bin.
• Give up your seat to an elderly person or a pregnant woman.

### Alighting (getting off)
1. Wait for the vehicle to stop before standing up.
2. Take all your belongings with you.
3. Get off in an orderly way, one at a time.
4. Look out for traffic before you step onto the road.
5. Thank the driver or conductor.

If you find something left on the vehicle, hand it to the driver or conductor.

## PAGE 7: Summary and common mistakes
### Summary
• Integrity means being honest and doing what is right when no one is watching.
• Stories from the four faiths teach truth, honest work, not stealing and right speech.
• We practise integrity with money, with technology, in school and in games.
• A good passenger is patient, polite and safe when boarding, travelling and alighting.

### Common mistakes
• Thinking that a small lie does not matter. Small lies grow.
• Thinking integrity is only about big things. It is also about small daily choices.
• Keeping a lost item because nobody saw you find it.
• Believing that we only need to be honest with our teachers. We should be honest with everyone.

### Compose your personal pledge
Write three sentences that begin with "I will" and show how you will practise integrity this week. For example: I will return anything that I find. I will tell the truth even when I am afraid. I will use the internet safely.

Put your pledge in your book and read it every morning.

## QUIZ
1. What is integrity?
   A) Winning by any means
   B) Being honest and doing what is right even when nobody is watching
   C) Being rich
   D) Being the best in class
   Answer: B
   Why: Integrity means honesty and doing the right thing at all times.
2. Which king is known for always telling the truth, in a Hindu story?
   A) Harishchandra
   B) Ravan
   C) Hiranyakashipu
   D) Kansa
   Answer: A
   Why: King Harishchandra kept the truth even when it cost him all he had.
3. What does Asteya mean?
   A) Not lying
   B) Not stealing
   C) Not eating
   D) Not sleeping
   Answer: B
   Why: Asteya is the Jain teaching of not taking what is not given.
4. What should a good passenger do before standing to get off a matatu?
   A) Push others
   B) Wait for the vehicle to stop
   C) Jump out at once
   D) Put the head out of the window
   Answer: B
   Why: Standing or moving while the vehicle is moving is unsafe.
5. You are given Ksh 100 change but should get Ksh 60. What should you do?
   A) Keep the extra
   B) Say nothing
   C) Return the extra Ksh 40
   D) Spend it quickly
   Answer: C
   Why: 100 minus 60 is Ksh 40, which belongs to the shopkeeper and must be returned.
