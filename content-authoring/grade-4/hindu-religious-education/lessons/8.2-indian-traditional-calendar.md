## PAGE 1: A calendar with a story
A calendar tells us the days, weeks and months of the year. Every culture has its own way of counting time. In India, many people follow a traditional calendar in addition to the calendar we use daily. It is used to decide the dates of many festivals and holy days.

In the Indian traditional calendar each day of the week is linked with a heavenly body. Long ago, sages watched the sky and saw seven bright things that moved: the Sun, the Moon and five planets. They named the days of the week after them. In this lesson we learn the names of the seven days and their heavenly partners.

### Words you need
• Vaar (or var): day of the week.
• Graha: a heavenly body. In this tradition, the Sun and the Moon are also counted as grahas.
• Panchang: the traditional Indian calendar book that lists days, months and festival dates.
• Solar system: the Sun and everything that travels around it, including the Earth and the planets.

### Think about it
Do you know why our week has seven days? In this lesson you will find that many old calendars in the world linked days with the Sun, the Moon and the planets.

## PAGE 2: Ravivaar, Somvaar and Mangalvaar
Each day's name is made of the name of a heavenly body and the word vaar, meaning day.

### Ravivaar: Sunday
Ravi is another name for the Sun, which is also called Surya. The Sun gives us light and heat, so Sunday is the day of the Sun.

### Somvaar: Monday
Som is another name for the Moon, also called Chandra. So Somvaar is the day of the Moon. Many Hindus fast or pray to Lord Shiva on Mondays.

### Mangalvaar: Tuesday
Mangal is the planet we call Mars. It is the red planet. In the Indian tradition, Mangalvaar is the day of Mangal. Many Hindus pray to Lord Hanuman on Tuesdays.

### Quick chart
Sunday: Ravivaar, the Sun
Monday: Somvaar, the Moon
Tuesday: Mangalvaar, Mars

### Try it
Say the three names aloud, then cover the chart and try to write them from memory.

## PAGE 3: Budhvaar, Guruvaar, Shukravaar and Shanivaar
### Budhvaar: Wednesday
Budh is the planet Mercury. It is the smallest planet and the closest one to the Sun. Budhvaar is the day of Budh.

### Guruvaar: Thursday
Guru here means Jupiter, the largest planet in our solar system. Jupiter is also called Brihaspati, so the day is sometimes named Brihaspativaar. Many Hindus honour their teachers and pray to Lord Vishnu or Sai Baba on Thursdays.

### Shukravaar: Friday
Shukra is the planet Venus. It is the bright planet that we sometimes see in the evening or early morning sky. Shukravaar is the day of Shukra.

### Shanivaar: Saturday
Shani is the planet Saturn, famous for its rings. Shanivaar is the day of Shani. Many Hindus pray to Lord Hanuman or to Shani Dev on Saturdays.

### Quick chart
Wednesday: Budhvaar, Mercury
Thursday: Guruvaar, Jupiter
Friday: Shukravaar, Venus
Saturday: Shanivaar, Saturn

### A memory trick
Make a short rhyme or song with the seven names. Sing it to a tune you know. Songs help us remember.

### Worked example
Today is Thursday. What is the Indian name and which planet is linked with it?
Step 1: Look at the table for Thursday.
Step 2: The name is Guruvaar.
Step 3: The linked body is Jupiter.
Answer: Guruvaar, linked with Jupiter.

### Another example
Which day is linked with Venus? Look for Venus in the third column. It is on the Friday line. The answer is Shukravaar.

## PAGE 5: Days of the week and the solar system
Our key question is: How are the days of the week related to the solar system? The answer is that the names come from the Sun, the Moon and five planets that people could see in the sky without a telescope.

### Some facts about them
• The Sun is a star. It is the centre of our solar system.
• The Moon goes around the Earth.
• Mercury and Venus are closer to the Sun than the Earth is.
• Mars is the red planet.
• Jupiter is the biggest planet.
• Saturn has bright rings around it.

### A point to remember
In the traditional Indian view, the Sun and the Moon are called grahas along with the planets, though in modern science the Sun is a star and the Moon is a natural satellite of the Earth. The Earth is not in the list, because in this old way of counting the days people were watching the sky from the Earth.

### Activity
Make a poster of the solar system. Write the Indian day name beside each of the five planets. Add the Sun and the Moon in their places.

## PAGE 6: The months and festivals
The Indian traditional calendar is based on both the Moon and the Sun. Its months are named. The twelve months, in order, are:
1. Chaitra
2. Vaishakha
3. Jyeshtha
4. Ashadha
5. Shravana
6. Bhadrapada
7. Ashvin
8. Kartika
9. Margashirsha
10. Pausha
11. Magha
12. Phalguna

Maha Shivratri, which we studied, falls in the month of Phalguna. Paryushan Parva falls in Bhadrapada. Because the months follow the Moon, the dates of these festivals move from year to year on the calendar we use every day.

### Uses of the Panchang
• Fixing the dates of festivals
• Choosing good days for prayers and family events
• Keeping the tradition of a culture alive

### Why it is special
It is special because it connects daily life to the sky, the seasons and the traditions of many generations. Learning it helps us to preserve a tradition and to appreciate how different people count time.

## PAGE 7: Making and using our own calendar
### Plan your week
Download or copy a simple Indian traditional calendar with an adult's help. Then write a weekly plan with the English day, the Indian name and one activity for each day. For example: Somvaar: help at home. Guruvaar: thank a teacher.

### Make a decorated calendar
1. Draw a table with seven columns for the seven days.
2. Write the English and Indian names in each column.
3. Draw the Sun, the Moon and the planets above their days.
4. Decorate it with bright colours, or use a computer with adult help.

### Greeting cards and songs
• Make a greeting card for a friend or family member with a message for a special day.
• Compose a short song that names the seven days and their planets.

### Summary
• The seven days are named after the Sun, Moon and five planets.
• Ravivaar: Sun. Somvaar: Moon. Mangalvaar: Mars. Budhvaar: Mercury. Guruvaar: Jupiter. Shukravaar: Venus. Shanivaar: Saturn.
• The Panchang is the traditional calendar book.
• We appreciate the calendar because it keeps a tradition alive.

### Common mistakes
• Mixing up Guruvaar (Jupiter) and Shukravaar (Venus).
• Forgetting that Sunday is Ravivaar, the first day in this list.
• Thinking the traditional calendar is only for adults.

## QUIZ
1. What is the Indian traditional name for Sunday?
   A) Somvaar
   B) Ravivaar
   C) Shanivaar
   D) Budhvaar
   Answer: B
   Why: Ravi means the Sun, so Ravivaar is Sunday.
2. Which day is named after the Moon?
   A) Somvaar
   B) Mangalvaar
   C) Guruvaar
   D) Shukravaar
   Answer: A
   Why: Som is the Moon, so Somvaar is Monday.
3. Which planet is linked with Guruvaar?
   A) Venus
   B) Saturn
   C) Jupiter
   D) Mars
   Answer: C
   Why: Guru or Brihaspati is Jupiter, and Guruvaar is Thursday.
4. Shanivaar is the day of which planet?
   A) Mercury
   B) Saturn
   C) Mars
   D) Venus
   Answer: B
   Why: Shani is Saturn, and Shanivaar is Saturday.
5. Which is the correct pair?
   A) Mangalvaar: Mercury
   B) Budhvaar: Mars
   C) Shukravaar: Venus
   D) Ravivaar: Moon
   Answer: C
   Why: Shukra is Venus. Mangal is Mars, Budh is Mercury and Ravi is the Sun.
6. Write the Indian names of Tuesday, Wednesday and Friday. (3 marks)
   Answer: Tuesday: Mangalvaar. Wednesday: Budhvaar. Friday: Shukravaar.
   Why: 1 mark for each correct name.
7. Name the heavenly body linked with each of these days: Ravivaar, Somvaar and Mangalvaar. (3 marks)
   Answer: Ravivaar: the Sun. Somvaar: the Moon. Mangalvaar: Mars.
   Why: 1 mark for each correct pair.
8. Explain how the days of the week are related to the solar system. (3 marks)
   Answer: The days are named after the Sun, the Moon and five planets that people could see in the sky, so each day has a heavenly partner.
   Why: 1 mark for the Sun and Moon, 1 mark for the planets, 1 mark for the link between names and days.
9. Give two uses of the Panchang. (2 marks)
   Answer: It fixes the dates of festivals and helps choose days for prayers and family events (any two valid uses).
   Why: 1 mark for each use.
10. Suggest two ways of using the Indian traditional calendar in your class. (2 marks)
   Answer: Make a weekly plan with Indian day names, decorate a class calendar, make greeting cards, or compose a song (any two).
   Why: 1 mark for each sensible way.
