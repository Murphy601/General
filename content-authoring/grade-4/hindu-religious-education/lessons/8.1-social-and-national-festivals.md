## PAGE 1: Why do we celebrate festivals?
Utsav means festival. A festival is a special day or set of days when people come together to remember something important. Some festivals are religious. Some belong to a whole nation. In both cases, people dress up, sing, pray, share food and enjoy time with others.

### Why festivals matter
• They help us remember great events, people and teachings.
• They bring families, friends and neighbours together.
• They teach us values such as kindness, forgiveness and love for our country.
• They pass our traditions on to the next generation.

In this lesson we study three festivals:
• Maha Shivratri, a Hindu festival
• Paryushan Parva, a Jain festival
• Madaraka Day, a national festival in Kenya

### Rites and rituals
A rite or ritual is a special action that is done in a set way on a festival, such as lighting a lamp, singing a hymn or raising a flag. Rites and rituals give a festival its meaning and help us remember what it is about.

### Think about it
Which festival do you enjoy most? What special things do you do on that day?

## PAGE 2: Maha Shivratri
Maha Shivratri means the great night of Lord Shiva. It is one of the most important Hindu festivals. It is celebrated every year at night in the month of Phalguna, which usually falls in February or March. Lord Shiva is one of the main gods of Hinduism, honoured as a great yogi, a protector and a giver of peace.

### Why it is celebrated
Different stories are told. Some say it is the night Lord Shiva and Goddess Parvati were married. Others remember it as the night of Shiva's great dance. Most agree that it is a night to remember God, to think about our mistakes and to try to become better.

### How it is celebrated
• Many people fast during the day, eating only fruit or simple food, or nothing at all.
• Devotees stay awake all night singing and praying. This is called a vigil, or jagaran.
• They bathe the Shiva Linga, the symbol of Shiva, with water and milk. This is called abhishek.
• They offer bel (bilva) leaves.
• They chant Om Namah Shivaya, a well-known prayer to Shiva.
• Families visit the Mandir and share prasad.

### What we learn
Maha Shivratri teaches self-control, faith and staying alert to do good.

## PAGE 3: Paryushan Parva
Paryushan Parva is the most important festival of the Jains. It is a time of prayer, self-control and forgiveness. It is held once a year, usually in August or September. It lasts for eight days in one Jain tradition and ten days in another.

### Main practices
• Fasting, for as many days or hours as a person can, or eating simple food.
• Praying and meditating every day.
• Listening to teachings from Jain scriptures, such as the Kalpasutra.
• Practising Ahimsa with extra care, for example taking care not to harm any small creature.
• Giving to those in need (charity).
• Asking forgiveness.

### The day of forgiveness
On the last day, Jains ask forgiveness from everyone. They say Micchami Dukkadam, which means, may any harm I did, in thought, word or deed, be forgiven. They say it to family, friends and even people they had quarrels with.

### What we learn
Paryushan Parva teaches us to say sorry, to forgive others and to live gently. It reminds us that a peaceful heart is more precious than winning an argument.

## PAGE 4: Madaraka Day
Madaraka Day is a national holiday in Kenya, celebrated every year on 1 June. Madaraka is a Kiswahili word that means power or authority to govern. On 1 June 1963, Kenya gained internal self-government, which means Kenyans began to govern their own affairs. Jomo Kenyatta became the first Prime Minister at that time. Kenya became fully independent on 12 December 1963 and a republic a year later.

### How it is celebrated
• A national ceremony is held, with the President giving a speech.
• The national flag is raised and the National Anthem is sung.
• There are parades, songs and dances from different communities.
• Schools and communities hold their own events.
• People remember the heroes who worked for freedom.

### What it teaches
Madaraka Day teaches patriotism, which means love for our country. It teaches unity, because people of all faiths and communities take part together.

### Respect for national symbols
• Stand straight while the National Anthem is sung.
• Do not talk or play while the flag is being raised.
• Never let the flag touch the ground.
• Learn the words of the National Anthem, Ee Mungu Nguvu Yetu, and sing them proudly.

## PAGE 6: Celebrating safely and including everyone
Festivals are joyful, but we must take care of safety and make sure everyone is included.

### Safety measures
• Keep away from open flames and candles. Ask an adult to light lamps.
• Stay with a grown-up in crowded places, and know where the exits are.
• Wash your hands before eating festival food and eat only clean, well-cooked food.
• Do not throw litter. Use bins.
• Walk, do not run, in a place of worship or on a stage.

### Including children with special needs
• Give clear instructions and use simple words or pictures.
• Help a friend who uses a wheelchair or a walking stick to move safely.
• Ask friends with special needs to take part in songs, drawings or decorations in the way they can.

### Charity for inclusive celebrations
Some families cannot afford festival food or new clothes. A class can collect food, used clothes and school supplies to share with those in need. This kind of service makes celebrations happy for everyone.

### Talking about respect
When you visit a friend's festival, listen to what they teach you. Do not laugh at customs you do not understand. Ask polite questions.

## PAGE 7: Activities and summary
### Things to do
• Write a short composition titled My Favourite Festival.
• Draw a Maha Shivratri or Madaraka Day scene.
• Learn a song about your country and sing it at assembly.
• Say Micchami Dukkadam to a friend you have argued with, and mean it.
• Invite a resource person to talk about how a festival is celebrated.

### Summary
• Maha Shivratri is a night of prayer and vigil in honour of Lord Shiva.
• Paryushan Parva is the Jain festival of fasting, prayer and forgiveness.
• Madaraka Day, 1 June, celebrates Kenya's step to self-government.
• Festivals bring people together and pass on our values.
• Rites and rituals help us understand what a festival means.

### Common mistakes
• Thinking that Maha Shivratri and Diwali are the same festival. They are different.
• Thinking that Madaraka Day is Independence Day. Independence Day is Jamhuri Day on 12 December.
• Saying that fasting means being unkind or angry. Fasting should make us calmer.

## QUIZ
1. Maha Shivratri is celebrated in honour of which god?
   A) Lord Ganesh
   B) Lord Shiva
   C) Lord Ram
   D) Lord Hanuman
   Answer: B
   Why: Maha Shivratri means the great night of Shiva.
2. What do Jains say on the last day of Paryushan to ask forgiveness?
   A) Namaste
   B) Micchami Dukkadam
   C) Om Namah Shivaya
   D) Sat Sri Akal
   Answer: B
   Why: Micchami Dukkadam asks for forgiveness for any harm done.
3. On which date is Madaraka Day celebrated in Kenya?
   A) 1 June
   B) 21 June
   C) 12 December
   D) 1 January
   Answer: A
   Why: Madaraka Day marks 1 June 1963, when Kenya gained internal self-government.
4. What is a vigil at Maha Shivratri?
   A) Sleeping all night
   B) Staying awake all night to pray and sing
   C) Running a race
   D) Buying gifts
   Answer: B
   Why: Devotees stay awake during the night in prayer and singing.
5. Which is a safety rule during a festival?
   A) Play with open flames
   B) Wander off from adults in a crowd
   C) Ask an adult to light lamps
   D) Eat food that has been left in the sun
   Answer: C
   Why: Flames can burn, so adults should handle them.
