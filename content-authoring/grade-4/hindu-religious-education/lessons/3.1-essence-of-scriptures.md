## PAGE 1: What are scriptures?
Scriptures are holy books. They hold the teachings of great teachers and the wisdom that people have kept for hundreds or thousands of years. Each faith has its own scriptures, and people read, listen to and respect them with care.

The word essence means the most important message inside something. When we study the essence of a scripture, we ask: What is the main lesson this book gives us for our lives?

In this lesson we meet scriptures from four faiths:
• Ramayan (Hinduism)
• Kalpasutra and Uttaradhyayan Sutra (Jainism)
• Dhammapada and Sigalovada Sutta (Buddhism)
• Sri Guru Granth Sahib Ji (Sikhism)

### How to treat a scripture
• Keep it clean and in a high, respectful place.
• Wash your hands before you touch it.
• Listen quietly when it is read.
• Try to follow what you learn.

### Think about it
Why do you think people keep scriptures for so long? What could a book teach that is still useful to a child in Kenya today?

## PAGE 2: The Ramayan, Bal Kaand
The Ramayan is a great Hindu story. It was written in Sanskrit by the sage Valmiki. It is divided into sections called kaands. The first is the Bal Kaand, which means the book of childhood.

### The story so far
King Dasharatha ruled the city of Ayodhya. He had four sons: Ram, Bharat, Lakshman and Shatrughna. The four brothers grew up loving each other. Ram was known for being truthful, polite and brave. He respected his parents and teachers.

A wise sage named Vishwamitra took Ram and Lakshman to protect his forest prayers from wicked demons. Ram and Lakshman helped him with courage and obedience. Later Ram married Sita, a gentle and wise princess, in the kingdom of Mithila.

### The lessons of Bal Kaand
• Respect for parents and teachers
• Love between brothers and sisters
• Courage used to protect others
• Obedience to wise guidance

## PAGE 3: The Ramayan, Ayodhya Kaand
The next section is the Ayodhya Kaand, the story of Ayodhya. King Dasharatha was ready to hand the kingdom to Ram. The people were very happy.

Then, Queen Kaikeyi reminded the king of two promises he had once made to her. She asked that Bharat be made king and that Ram go to live in the forest for fourteen years. Dasharatha was heartbroken, but a king must keep his word.

Ram did not argue or become angry. He accepted his father's promise calmly and left for the forest. His wife Sita and his brother Lakshman chose to go with him.

When Bharat returned and heard what had happened, he was very sad. He refused to take the throne because he knew it belonged to Ram. He went into the forest to ask Ram to return. Ram said he must keep his father's promise. Bharat took Ram's sandals (paduka), placed them on the throne and ruled in Ram's name as a humble servant.

### The lessons of Ayodhya Kaand
• Keep your promises.
• Give up selfish wishes for the good of the family.
• Be humble and do not be greedy for power.
• Treat elders with respect, even in difficult times.

## PAGE 4: Jain scriptures
### Kalpasutra
The Kalpasutra is a Jain scripture. It tells the life of Lord Mahavir, the twenty-fourth Tirthankar, and also speaks about earlier Tirthankars such as Lord Parshvanath. Many Jains listen to it being read during Paryushan, the special days of prayer and forgiveness. It teaches us to live gently and with self-control.

### Uttaradhyayan Sutra
The Uttaradhyayan Sutra is another respected Jain scripture. It has advice on how to live a disciplined life. Its main messages are:
• Practise Ahimsa (not harming any living being).
• Be honest and truthful.
• Control greed and anger.
• Respect teachers and study with a humble heart.

## PAGE 5: Buddhist scriptures
### Dhammapada
The Dhammapada is a short and much-loved Buddhist book of sayings. It is a collection of verses about how to live wisely. Some of its main ideas, told in simple words, are:
• Our thoughts shape our actions, so we should keep our minds kind and clear.
• Hatred is not ended by hatred but by love.
• A person who controls his or her own anger is truly strong.
• Doing good brings happiness and peace.

### Sigalovada Sutta
This sutta is a talk by the Buddha to a young man named Sigala. It explains how we should behave towards the people around us, which are called the six directions:
1. Parents
2. Teachers
3. Wife or husband and family
4. Friends
5. Workers and helpers
6. Holy teachers

The Buddha said that we should respect them and care for them, and they should care for us in return. He also told Sigala to avoid bad habits that waste wealth and time.

### Think about it
Which of these six groups of people do you meet every day? How do you show them respect?

## PAGE 6: Sri Guru Granth Sahib Ji
Sri Guru Granth Sahib Ji is the holy scripture of the Sikhs and is treated as their eternal Guru. It was first compiled by Guru Arjan Dev Ji, the fifth Guru, and Guru Gobind Singh Ji, the tenth Guru, declared that the Sikhs should follow the Granth as their Guru. It is written in Gurmukhi script.

It holds hymns from the Sikh Gurus and also from holy men of other traditions, such as Bhagat Kabir Ji and Bhagat Ravidas Ji. This shows that truth can be found in many places.

### Main teachings
• There is one God for all people (Ik Onkar).
• All human beings are equal.
• Earn your living honestly, remember God, and share with others.
• Serve people without expecting anything.

At a gurdwara, Guru Granth Sahib Ji is placed on a raised seat. People cover their heads, remove their shoes and bow before it. Everyone eats together in the langar (community kitchen), sitting side by side.

## PAGE 7: Applying the teachings
Scriptures are not only to be read. They are to be lived. Look at how one lesson from each faith can guide a child at school and at home.

### From the Ramayan
Keep your promise. If you promise a friend that you will return a book, do it on time.

### From Jain scriptures
Be gentle to living things. Do not step on ants or hurt animals for fun.

### From Buddhist scriptures
Answer anger with calm. If a classmate shouts at you, take a deep breath and speak kindly.

### From Sri Guru Granth Sahib Ji
Share with others. Give some of your lunch to a child who has none.

### Ways to learn more
• Listen to stories from an elder or a resource person.
• Visit a temple, community centre or library, with permission.
• Discuss with classmates what each story teaches.
• Use a safe search engine with an adult's help.

### Common mistakes
• Thinking all scriptures are the same book. Each faith has its own.
• Only memorising stories without doing what they teach.
• Making fun of another faith's holy book. We must respect them all.

## QUIZ
1. Who wrote the Ramayan in Sanskrit?
   A) Valmiki
   B) Guru Nanak
   C) Sigala
   D) Mahavir
   Answer: A
   Why: The sage Valmiki is honoured as the author of the Ramayan.
2. What does Bal Kaand mean?
   A) Book of war
   B) Book of childhood
   C) Book of the forest
   D) Book of prayers
   Answer: B
   Why: Bal means child, and the Bal Kaand tells about the childhood of Ram and his brothers.
3. Which lesson is taught by Bharat placing Ram's sandals on the throne?
   A) Take what you want
   B) Be humble and do not be greedy for power
   C) Never obey elders
   D) Run away from problems
   Answer: B
   Why: Bharat ruled as a humble servant of Ram and did not claim the throne for himself.
4. The Sigalovada Sutta is a talk given by whom?
   A) Guru Arjan Dev Ji
   B) Valmiki
   C) The Buddha
   D) Kaikeyi
   Answer: C
   Why: The Buddha gave this teaching to a young man named Sigala.
5. Which scripture is the eternal Guru of the Sikhs?
   A) Kalpasutra
   B) Dhammapada
   C) Ramayan
   D) Sri Guru Granth Sahib Ji
   Answer: D
   Why: Sikhs follow Sri Guru Granth Sahib Ji as their eternal Guru.
6. Match each scripture to its faith: Kalpasutra, Dhammapada, Sri Guru Granth Sahib Ji. (3 marks)
   Answer: Kalpasutra: Jainism. Dhammapada: Buddhism. Sri Guru Granth Sahib Ji: Sikhism.
   Why: 1 mark for each correct pair.
7. Give two lessons from the Ayodhya Kaand of the Ramayan. (2 marks)
   Answer: Keep your promises; be humble and unselfish; respect elders (any two).
   Why: 1 mark for each lesson.
8. Name the six directions (groups of people) in the Sigalovada Sutta. (3 marks)
   Answer: Parents, teachers, wife or husband and family, friends, workers and helpers, holy teachers.
   Why: 1 mark for two correct groups, 2 marks for four, 3 marks for all or five.
9. Explain how a child can show respect to a scripture. (3 marks)
   Answer: Keep it clean and in a high place, wash hands before touching it, listen quietly when it is read, and follow its teachings.
   Why: 1 mark for each correct way, up to 3.
10. Choose one teaching from a scripture you have studied and say how you will apply it at school. (3 marks)
   Answer: Any sensible answer, for example: Keep my promise to return a friend's book on time, as Ram kept his father's promise.
   Why: 1 mark for the teaching, 1 mark for the scripture, 1 mark for a clear action.
