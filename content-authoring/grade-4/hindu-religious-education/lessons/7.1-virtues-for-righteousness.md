## PAGE 1: What is Dharma?
Dharma is a Sanskrit word that means the right way of living. It includes doing our duty, being honest, being kind and taking care of others and of nature. When people follow Dharma, families and communities live in peace.

The Sanatan or Vedic tradition, which is the ancient Hindu way, teaches many principles of Dharma. In this lesson we study five of them. They all begin with the letter P:
1. Paramatma
2. Purushartha
3. Prani Daya (called Pranidaya in the syllabus)
4. Punarjanam
5. Prarthana

## PAGE 2: Paramatma, the Supreme Soul
Paramatma means the Supreme Soul. Param means highest, and atma means soul. Hindus believe there is one Supreme Being who created the world and is present everywhere. People pray to Paramatma using different names and forms. In the Vedas it is said that the truth is one, but the wise call it by many names.

Every living being also has an atma, a soul, which is a small part of the divine. This is why Hindus respect all life.

### What this principle teaches us
• God is present in everyone, so we should treat everyone with respect.
• We can pray to God anywhere: at home, at school and in the Mandir.
• Because God sees all we do, we should be honest even when alone.

### A story: Prahlad
Prahlad, the boy we met in the lesson on Enlightened Beings, believed that God was everywhere, even in a pillar. He kept his faith in Paramatma when his father was angry. His faith was strong, and he was protected.

### Living this principle
When you greet a person with Namaste, you show respect for the divine within them. Try to greet elders and friends politely every day.

• Dharma: living rightly and doing our duty.
• Artha: earning wealth and things we need, in an honest way.
• Kama: having good and healthy wishes, such as joy, family and friendship.
• Moksha: freedom of the soul, the highest goal.

### How a child can follow these
• Dharma: do homework, respect elders and speak the truth.
• Artha: save pocket money and never take what is not yours.
• Kama: enjoy play, music and friendship in a good way.
• Moksha: pray and try to grow into a kind person.

The important idea is balance. Wealth and wishes are good, but they must follow Dharma.

## PAGE 4: Prani Daya, kindness to all living beings
Prani means a living being, and daya means kindness or compassion. Prani Daya means being kind to all living creatures. Hindus, Jains, Buddhists and Sikhs all teach compassion.

### What Prani Daya looks like
• Giving water to a thirsty animal or bird.
• Feeding a hungry person.
• Not throwing stones at animals or harming insects for fun.
• Helping a friend who is sad or hurt.
• Caring for plants and not wasting food.

### A story: Shibi and the dove
An old story tells of King Shibi. A frightened dove flew into his lap for safety. A hawk followed and demanded the dove as its food. The king did not want to give up the dove that had come to him for protection, and he did not want the hawk to go hungry either. He offered his own flesh, weight for weight, to save the dove. The story says that he was being tested, and that his great kindness was honoured.

Lesson: A kind person protects the weak, even at a cost to himself.

### Activity
Make a plan for a week of Prani Daya. Each day, choose one kind act toward a person, an animal or a plant.

## PAGE 5: Punarjanam, rebirth
Punarjanam means being born again. Hindus believe that the atma, the soul, does not die when the body dies. After death, it takes a new body, and this is called rebirth. The cycle continues until the soul reaches Moksha, freedom.

### Karma
Karma means action. Hindus believe that our actions (karma) have results. Good actions bring good results and unkind actions bring trouble. So, we try to do good every day.

### A simple picture
The Bhagavad Gita, a holy Hindu book, compares the soul to a person who takes off old clothes and puts on new ones. The old body is like worn clothes, and the soul is like the person who wears them and stays the same.

### Points to remember
• The soul is believed to be eternal.
• Our actions matter.
• We should live so that we leave a good name and a good example.

### A note about respect
Other faiths hold different beliefs about death. We listen to others with respect and do not argue about who is right.

### Think about it
If good actions bring good results, what good action can you do today?

## PAGE 6: Prarthana, prayer
Prarthana means prayer. It is our way of talking to God with love. We can thank God, ask for help and promise to become better.

### Kinds of prayer
• Prayers we say aloud, such as the Gayatri Mantra.
• Silent prayers, in the heart.
• Prayers in song, called bhajans.
• Prayers of action, such as helping someone.

### Why prayer is a principle of Dharma
• It reminds us that we are not alone.
• It helps us to feel calm and thankful.
• It gives courage when we are afraid.
• It makes us humble, because we know we still need help.

### How to pray well
1. Sit quietly and fold your hands.
2. Think of God with love.
3. Say thank you for what you have.
4. Ask for wisdom and kindness, not just for gifts.
5. End with a promise to do good.

### Activity
Write a short prayer of your own: three lines to thank God and to ask for a good heart. Read it to your friend or keep it in your book.

## PAGE 7: Living the five principles
The five principles work together. Paramatma tells us that God is in everyone. Purushartha guides our goals. Prani Daya shows us how to treat others. Punarjanam reminds us that our actions matter. Prarthana keeps us close to God.

### A day in the life of a child who follows Dharma
• Morning: says a prayer (Prarthana) and greets parents with Namaste (respect for Paramatma in everyone).
• School: does honest work (Dharma and Artha) and helps a friend (Prani Daya).
• Evening: thanks God and thinks about the good things done during the day (Punarjanam and Prarthana).

### Ways to learn more
• Ask a grandparent or an elder to tell a story from the scriptures.
• Visit a Mandir with permission and ask about these principles.
• Read short stories about Prahlad, Shibi and Harishchandra.
• Act out a story in a small skit.

### Common mistakes
• Mixing up Purushartha (goals) and Prarthana (prayer).
• Thinking Dharma is only for grown-ups. Children can practise it too.
• Thinking kindness is only for people. It also includes animals and nature.

## QUIZ
1. What does Paramatma mean?
   A) Supreme Soul
   B) Holy river
   C) Daily prayer
   D) Festival
   Answer: A
   Why: Param means highest and atma means soul.
2. Which of these is one of the four Purusharthas?
   A) Prarthana
   B) Dharma
   C) Bhajan
   D) Aarti
   Answer: B
   Why: The four goals are Dharma, Artha, Kama and Moksha.
3. Prani Daya means what?
   A) Kindness to all living beings
   B) Study of maths
   C) Fasting
   D) Ringing bells
   Answer: A
   Why: Prani means living being and daya means kindness.
4. What does Punarjanam mean?
   A) Being born again
   B) Being rich
   C) Being quiet
   D) Being tall
   Answer: A
   Why: Punarjanam means rebirth of the soul.
5. What is Prarthana?
   A) Prayer
   B) Rebirth
   C) Wealth
   D) Duty
   Answer: A
   Why: Prarthana is the Sanskrit word for prayer.

   Answer: Dharma, Artha, Kama and Moksha.
   Why: 1 mark for each correct goal.
7. Explain how a child can practise Prani Daya. (3 marks)
   Answer: By giving water to thirsty animals, sharing food, helping a hurt friend, and not harming insects or plants.
   Why: 1 mark for each correct example, up to 3.
8. What lesson do we learn from the story of King Shibi and the dove? (2 marks)
   Answer: A kind person protects the weak, even at a cost to himself.
   Why: 1 mark for protecting the weak, 1 mark for sacrifice or kindness.

   Answer: Karma means action. Good actions bring good results; for example, helping a classmate builds friendship.
   Why: 1 mark for the meaning, 1 mark for the idea of results, 1 mark for the example.
