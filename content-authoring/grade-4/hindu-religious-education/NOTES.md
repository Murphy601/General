# Notes for human review: Grade 4 Hindu Religious Education

Lessons cover all 9 real sub-strands (1.1, 2.1, 3.1, 4.1, 5.1, 6.1, 7.1, 8.1, 8.2). All manifest numbers (1.1, 4.1, 5.1, 6.1, 7.1, 8.1, 8.2) have lessons, so no retire.json is needed. 2.1 and 3.1 were missing from the manifest and are new.

Please have a Hindu/Jain/Buddhist/Sikh teacher check:

1. Prayer texts (4.1): Gayatri Mantra and Twameva Mata cha Pita Twameva are given in Roman transliteration; check spelling and diacritics. Saraswati Vandana: only the opening line (Ya Kundendu Tushara Hara Dhavala) is quoted, with a summary of meaning. The full verse was deliberately not reproduced.
2. Sacred plants (1.1): Beri = the Ber Sahib at Sultanpur Lodhi (Guru Nanak); Sal tree linked to Mahavir's enlightenment; Bodhi tree = peepal. Beas linked to Sikh history only in general terms (Punjab). Rijubalika spelling varies (Rijuvalika). Confirm the syllabus intends these links.
3. 2.1: Narsimha story details (boon conditions), Parshvanath snake story, Buddha and the swan (Devadatta), Guru Ram Das (born Lahore, childhood name Jetha, selling boiled grain, Amritsar, Laavan). These are traditional accounts; wording says "story says" where appropriate.
4. 3.1: Dhammapada teachings are paraphrased, not quoted. Sigalovada six directions listed as in the sutta. Kalpasutra and Uttaradhyayan described in general terms only.
5. 5.1: Harishchandra and Guru Nanak / Bhai Lalo are told in short traditional form. The Jain "purse" story is labelled an example story (not a scripture story). Passenger practices reflect Kenyan matatu/bus context and are not from a source text.
6. 6.1: Bhastrika and Kapaal Bhaati are vigorous; lesson stresses gentle practice, teacher supervision and stopping if dizzy. Vajra Asana is described as sitting on heels (syllabus says "ankle pose"). Stick-figure SVGs are simple; a yoga teacher should confirm posture drawings.
7. 7.1: The syllabus term "Pranidaya" is interpreted as Prani Daya (kindness to living beings). If the syllabus meant something else (for example Ishvara Pranidhana), that page must change. Bhagavad Gita clothes simile is paraphrased without a verse number. King Shibi story told briefly.
8. 8.1: Maha Shivratri month (Phalguna, Feb or Mar) and Paryushan (Bhadrapada, Aug or Sep; 8 days in one tradition, 10 in another) are approximate. Madaraka Day: 1 June 1963 internal self-government; Kenyatta first Prime Minister; independence 12 Dec 1963; republic 1964. Timeline figure marks Yoga Day (21 June) too.
9. 8.2: Day names and planets (Ravi/Sun, Som/Moon, Mangal/Mars, Budh/Mercury, Guru/Jupiter, Shukra/Venus, Shani/Saturn). Deity-by-weekday remarks (Shiva on Monday, Hanuman on Tuesday, Vishnu or Sai Baba on Thursday, Hanuman or Shani on Saturday) are common practice but vary by community; consider trimming. Month list is the standard twelve Hindu months.
10. 4.1: Mandirs said to exist in Nairobi, Mombasa and Kisumu; no specific temple names or addresses given. Teachers should add local ones.

Bank: 402 MCQ, 169 short items across all topics. Figures: 12 (five elements, rivers, beings summary, scriptures table, day-of-prayers timeline, integrity values, two asanas, principles, purusharthas, festival timeline, weekday table).
