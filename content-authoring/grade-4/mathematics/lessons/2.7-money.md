## PAGE 1: Kenyan coins and notes
In Kenya we use shillings to buy and sell things. We write the shilling as Ksh. For example, Ksh 50 means fifty shillings. Money comes as coins and notes.

The coins we use most are the 1 shilling, 5 shilling, 10 shilling and 20 shilling coins. There is also a 40 shilling coin. The notes we use are 50, 100, 200, 500 and 1,000 shillings.

### Making amounts
We can make the same amount in different ways. To make Ksh 50 you can use one 50 shilling note, or five 10 shilling coins, or two 20 shilling coins and one 10 shilling coin.

### Adding money
A pupil has one 100 shilling note, one 50 shilling note and two 20 shilling coins. Total: 100 + 50 + 20 + 20 = Ksh 190.

### Caring for money
Keep money safe. Count it carefully and give honest change. Money can be used to buy what we need and to save for the future.

## PAGE 2: Shillings and cents
One shilling is made of 100 cents. We write cents as c. So:

Ksh 1 = 100 cents

Cent coins are rarely seen now, but cents are still used in prices, in banks and on receipts. A price such as Ksh 12.50 means 12 shillings and 50 cents. The decimal point separates the shillings from the cents.

### Shillings to cents: multiply by 100
Ksh 3 = 3 × 100 = 300 cents.
Ksh 12 = 12 × 100 = 1,200 cents.

### Cents to shillings: divide by 100
400 cents = 400 ÷ 100 = Ksh 4.
450 cents = 450 ÷ 100 = 4 remainder 50, so Ksh 4 and 50 cents, written Ksh 4.50.

### Half and quarter of a shilling
50 cents is half of a shilling. 25 cents is a quarter of a shilling. 75 cents is three quarters of a shilling.

### Example
A sweet costs 50 cents. How many sweets can you buy with Ksh 10? Ksh 10 = 1,000 cents. 1,000 ÷ 50 = 20 sweets.

## PAGE 3: Preparing a shopping list
A shopping list is a list of the things we plan to buy. Preparing a list helps us to remember everything, to buy only what we need and not to spend more money than we have. Families use shopping lists for food, school things and household goods.

### How to prepare a shopping list
Step 1: Think about what is needed. Discuss with others at home.
Step 2: Write the names of the items. Start with the most important.
Step 3: Write how many or how much of each item you need.
Step 4: Find out the price of each item.
Step 5: Work out the cost of each item and the total cost.

The cost of each item is the number multiplied by the price of one. For maize flour: 2 × 120 = Ksh 240.

### Why a list?
If you do not write a list you may forget something or buy things you do not need. Planning helps you save money.

### Try it
Write a shopping list of three food items your family needs. Ask for the prices in a shop or from your parents.

## PAGE 4: Working out the total cost
To find the total cost of the shopping list, first find the cost of each item and then add the costs.

### Worked example
Look at the shopping list on the last page.
Step 1: Maize flour: 2 × 120 = Ksh 240.
Step 2: Sugar: 1 × 160 = Ksh 160.
Step 3: Cooking fat: 1 × 350 = Ksh 350.
Step 4: Total = 240 + 160 + 350. First 240 + 160 = 400, then 400 + 350 = 750.
The total cost is Ksh 750.

### A larger shopping list
A school uniform costs a shirt Ksh 650, shorts Ksh 800 and shoes Ksh 1,900.
Total = 650 + 800 + 1,900 = 1,450 + 1,900 = Ksh 3,350.

### Bigger amounts, up to Ksh 10,000
A bicycle costs Ksh 6,500, a helmet Ksh 1,750 and a lock Ksh 850.
Total = 6,500 + 1,750 + 850. First 6,500 + 1,750 = 8,250. Then 8,250 + 850 = 9,100.
The total is Ksh 9,100, which is within Ksh 10,000.

### Estimating the total
Round each price to the nearest hundred and add. 6,500 + 1,800 + 900 = 9,200. The estimate is close to the exact total of Ksh 9,100.

### Common mistakes
• Adding the number of items to the price instead of multiplying.
• Forgetting the unit Ksh.
• Making an error when adding: always check by adding in a different order.

## PAGE 5: Change and budgeting
When we pay for goods with an amount larger than the cost, the shopkeeper gives us back the difference. This is called change.

Change = amount given − total cost

### Worked example 1
Wanjiru buys goods that cost Ksh 750 and pays with a Ksh 1,000 note.
Change = 1,000 − 750 = Ksh 250.
Check: 750 + 250 = 1,000.

### Worked example 2: A budget
Mr Otieno has Ksh 2,000 to spend. He buys items costing Ksh 1,340. Does he have enough money? 2,000 is larger than 1,340, so yes. Money left = 2,000 − 1,340 = Ksh 660.

### Counting change up
A shopkeeper counts up from the cost to the amount paid. For a Ksh 750 bill paid with Ksh 1,000: 750 to 800 is 50, then 800 to 1,000 is 200. So the change is 50 + 200 = Ksh 250.

### What if there is not enough?
If the total is more than the money you have, you must choose what to buy first. Put the most important items on the list first and leave out the less important ones.

### Saving
If you have Ksh 500 and spend Ksh 350, you save Ksh 150. Saving helps you to buy bigger things later.

## PAGE 6: Shopping in a model shop
You can practise money skills in a class shop. One group becomes shopkeepers and another group becomes buyers. Change roles after a while so that everyone gets a turn.

### Setting up the shop
• Use empty packets and tins from home as goods. This reuses materials instead of throwing them away.
• Write a price on each item.
• Make paper money or coins for buying and selling. Do not use real money.

### Role play
The buyer chooses three items from the shop and adds the costs. The shopkeeper checks the total and gives change.

### Example
A buyer chooses a packet of biscuits for Ksh 50, a bottle of juice for Ksh 80 and a book for Ksh 120.
Step 1: Total = 50 + 80 + 120 = Ksh 250.
Step 2: The buyer pays with a Ksh 500 note.
Step 3: Change = 500 − 250 = Ksh 250.

### Being honest
Check the total twice, give back the correct change and never take more than the cost. Honest traders are trusted by their customers.

### Summary
• Ksh 1 = 100 cents.
• Shillings to cents: multiply by 100. Cents to shillings: divide by 100.
• A shopping list helps us plan and control spending.
• Total cost = the sum of the cost of each item.
• Change = amount paid − total cost.

## QUIZ
1. How many cents are in Ksh 3?
   A) 30 cents
   B) 103 cents
   C) 300 cents
   D) 3,000 cents
   Answer: C
   Why: Ksh 1 = 100 cents, so Ksh 3 = 3 × 100 = 300 cents.
2. 450 cents is the same as
   A) Ksh 4.05
   B) Ksh 4.50
   C) Ksh 45
   D) Ksh 450
   Answer: B
   Why: 450 ÷ 100 = 4 remainder 50, so it is Ksh 4 and 50 cents, written Ksh 4.50.
3. Maize flour costs Ksh 120 a packet. What is the cost of 2 packets?
   A) Ksh 122
   B) Ksh 240
   C) Ksh 360
   D) Ksh 60
   Answer: B
   Why: 2 × 120 = Ksh 240.
4. A bicycle costs Ksh 6,500, a helmet Ksh 1,750 and a lock Ksh 850. What is the total cost?
   A) Ksh 8,100
   B) Ksh 9,000
   C) Ksh 9,100
   D) Ksh 10,100
   Answer: C
   Why: 6,500 + 1,750 = 8,250 and 8,250 + 850 = 9,100.
5. Wanjiru buys goods worth Ksh 750 and pays with a Ksh 1,000 note. What change should she get?
   A) Ksh 150
   B) Ksh 250
   C) Ksh 350
   D) Ksh 1,750
   Answer: B
   Why: Change = 1,000 − 750 = Ksh 250.
6. Change Ksh 12 to cents and 1,250 cents to shillings. (2 marks)
   Answer: Ksh 12 = 1,200 cents; 1,250 cents = Ksh 12.50
   Why: 12 × 100 = 1,200 cents (1 mark). 1,250 ÷ 100 = 12 remainder 50, so Ksh 12 and 50 cents (1 mark).
7. Write three reasons why we prepare a shopping list. (3 marks)
   Answer: To remember everything we need; to buy only what is needed; to avoid spending more than we have.
   Why: 1 mark for each sensible reason, up to 3 marks.
8. A shopping list has 3 packets of rice at Ksh 130 each, 2 bottles of oil at Ksh 250 each and 1 tin of tomatoes at Ksh 60. Find the total cost. (4 marks)
   Answer: Ksh 950
   Why: Rice 3 × 130 = 390 (1 mark). Oil 2 × 250 = 500 (1 mark). Tomatoes Ksh 60. Total 390 + 500 + 60 = 950 (1 mark for correct addition, 1 mark for the answer with Ksh).
9. A school uniform costs a shirt Ksh 650, shorts Ksh 800 and shoes Ksh 1,900. Find the total cost and the change from Ksh 5,000. (4 marks)
   Answer: Total Ksh 3,350; change Ksh 1,650
   Why: 650 + 800 = 1,450 and 1,450 + 1,900 = 3,350 (2 marks). Change = 5,000 − 3,350 = 1,650 (2 marks).
10. Mr Otieno has Ksh 2,000. He buys items costing Ksh 1,340. Does he have enough money? How much is left? (3 marks)
   Answer: Yes, he has Ksh 660 left.
   Why: 2,000 is greater than 1,340, so he has enough (1 mark). 2,000 − 1,340 (1 mark) = Ksh 660 (1 mark).
