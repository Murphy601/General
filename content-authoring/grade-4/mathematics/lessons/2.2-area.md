## PAGE 1: What is area?
Area is the amount of flat surface that a shape covers. The top of your desk has an area. So does the floor of your classroom, a football pitch, a page of your book and the shamba where a farmer plants maize. Area is not the same as length. Length measures along a line. Area measures how much surface is covered.

To decide which of two surfaces is bigger, we compare their areas. Sometimes we can see the answer. If a page of an exercise book is put on top of a desk, we can see the desk is bigger. But when two surfaces look about the same size, we must do more.

### Comparing by placing one on the other
Cut two pieces of paper. Put one on top of the other with the corners lined up. If one piece sticks out, that piece has the bigger area. If they fit exactly, their areas are equal.

## PAGE 2: Measuring area by counting unit squares
Placing one surface on another only tells us which is bigger. It does not tell us by how much. To measure area we need a unit. A good unit for area is a square, called a unit square. All unit squares are the same size.

To find the area of a shape, cover it with unit squares with no gaps and no overlaps, then count the squares. The number of squares is the area.

### Square centimetres
If each small square has sides of 1 cm, the unit is called a square centimetre. We write it as cm². A rectangle covered by 15 such squares has an area of 15 square centimetres.

### Using local materials
Cut squares from old newspaper or cardboard. Use them to cover a book cover or a small table. Count how many squares you used. Your answer is the area in those squares. Remember that if you use larger squares, you will need fewer of them, so always say which unit you used.

### Careful counting
Count each square once. Put a small dot in each square as you count so you do not miss any or count twice.

This rectangle has 4 rows. Each row has 6 squares. So the total is 6 + 6 + 6 + 6 = 24 squares. Repeated addition is multiplication, so we can multiply:

Area = number of rows × number of columns
Area = 4 × 6 = 24 square units.

### Why it works
Each row holds the same number of squares as there are columns. So the number of rows tells us how many equal rows there are. Multiplying gives the total without counting one by one.

### Worked example
A rectangle has 5 rows and 8 columns of unit squares.
Step 1: Rows = 5 and columns = 8.
Step 2: Area = 5 × 8.
Step 3: 5 × 8 = 40.
Answer: 40 square units.

### The order does not matter
4 × 6 and 6 × 4 both give 24. You can count the rows first or the columns first.

### Square numbers
The numbers 1, 4, 9, 16, 25, 36 and so on are the areas of squares with 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6 unit rows. They are worth learning by heart.

### Example with lengths
A square tile has sides of 10 cm. Cover it with 1 cm squares: there are 10 rows with 10 squares each. Area = 10 × 10 = 100 square centimetres.

### Rectangle or square?
Find the area the same way for both. Multiply the number of rows by the number of columns. If the number of rows equals the number of columns, the shape is a square.

## PAGE 5: Area in real life
People who build, farm and decorate need to work out area. Tiles, paving stones and planting spaces are often laid in rows and columns.

### Worked example 1: The tiled floor
The floor of a small shop is covered with square tiles in 8 rows. Each row has 12 tiles. How many tiles cover the floor?
Step 1: Rows = 8 and columns = 12.
Step 2: Area = 8 × 12.
Step 3: 8 × 12 = 8 × 10 + 8 × 2 = 80 + 16 = 96.
Answer: 96 tiles, so the area is 96 square units, where one tile is one unit.

### Worked example 2: The vegetable garden
A garden bed is marked into 1 m squares. It has 9 rows and 4 columns. Area = 9 × 4 = 36 square metres. That is 36 squares each with sides of 1 m.

### Finding a missing number
A rectangle has an area of 36 square units and 4 rows. How many columns are there?
Since rows × columns = 36, we need 4 × ? = 36. Dividing, 36 ÷ 4 = 9. There are 9 columns.

### Comparing areas
Rectangle P has 5 rows and 7 columns, so its area is 35 square units. Rectangle Q has 6 rows and 6 columns, so its area is 36 square units. Q has the bigger area, by 1 square unit.

## PAGE 6: Summary and common mistakes
Let us pull together what we know about area.

### Key ideas
• Area is the amount of flat surface a shape covers.
• We compare surfaces by placing one on the other, or by counting unit squares.
• To find the area of a rectangle, multiply the number of rows by the number of columns.
• A square has the same number of rows as columns.
• Area is measured in square units, such as square centimetres (cm²) and square metres (m²).

### Worked example: which is larger?
A book cover is covered with 12 rows of 9 squares. A notebook cover is covered with 10 rows of 10 squares.
Step 1: Book cover: 12 × 9 = 108 squares.
Step 2: Notebook cover: 10 × 10 = 100 squares.
Step 3: 108 is more than 100, so the book cover is larger by 8 squares.

### Common mistakes
• Adding the rows and columns instead of multiplying. For 4 rows and 6 columns, 4 + 6 = 10 is wrong. The correct area is 4 × 6 = 24.
• Mixing up area and perimeter. Perimeter is the distance round the edge. Area is the surface inside.
• Forgetting the units. Write square units, cm² or m².
• Counting squares twice or missing them. Mark each one as you count.

### Practice at home
Find a floor with square tiles at home or in school. Count the tiles in one row and the number of rows. Multiply to find how many tiles cover the floor.

## QUIZ
1. What does area measure?
   A) How long a line is
   B) How heavy a thing is
   C) How much flat surface a shape covers
   D) How much water a container holds
   Answer: C
   Why: Area is the amount of flat surface covered by a shape. Length, mass and capacity measure other things.
2. A rectangle is made of 3 rows of 5 unit squares. What is its area?
   A) 8 square units
   B) 15 square units
   C) 16 square units
   D) 30 square units
   Answer: B
   Why: Area = rows × columns = 3 × 5 = 15 square units.
3. What is the area of a square with 6 rows and 6 columns of unit squares?
   A) 12 square units
   B) 24 square units
   C) 36 square units
   D) 66 square units
   Answer: C
   Why: 6 × 6 = 36 square units.
4. A rectangle has an area of 36 square units and 4 rows. How many columns does it have?
   A) 8
   B) 9
   C) 10
   D) 32
   Answer: B
   Why: Rows × columns = 36. 36 ÷ 4 = 9 columns.
5. Which is a correct way to find the area of a rectangle with 4 rows and 6 columns?
   A) 4 + 6
   B) 4 − 6
   C) 4 × 6
   D) 6 ÷ 4
   Answer: C
   Why: Area is the number of rows multiplied by the number of columns: 4 × 6 = 24 square units.
6. The floor of a shop has 8 rows of 12 square tiles. How many tiles are on the floor? (3 marks)
   Answer: 96 tiles
   Why: Area = 8 × 12 (1 mark). 8 × 10 = 80 and 8 × 2 = 16 (1 mark). 80 + 16 = 96 tiles (1 mark).
7. Rectangle P has 5 rows and 7 columns. Rectangle Q has 6 rows and 6 columns. Which has the bigger area and by how much? (4 marks)
   Answer: Q is bigger by 1 square unit.
   Why: P: 5 × 7 = 35 square units (1 mark). Q: 6 × 6 = 36 square units (1 mark). Q is bigger (1 mark). 36 − 35 = 1 square unit (1 mark).
8. Explain how you can compare the areas of two sheets of paper without using a ruler. (2 marks)
   Answer: Put one sheet on top of the other with corners lined up. The sheet that sticks out has the bigger area.
   Why: 1 mark for placing one sheet on the other and 1 mark for saying the sheet that sticks out is bigger.
9. A garden bed has 9 rows and 4 columns of 1 m squares. Find its area in square metres. (2 marks)
   Answer: 36 square metres
   Why: 9 × 4 = 36. 1 mark for the multiplication, 1 mark for the unit square metres.
10. Amina says the area of a rectangle with 4 rows and 6 columns is 10 because 4 + 6 = 10. Explain her mistake and give the correct area. (3 marks)
   Answer: She added the rows and columns. She should multiply. Area = 4 × 6 = 24 square units.
   Why: 1 mark for saying she added, 1 mark for saying we multiply rows by columns, 1 mark for 24 square units.
