## PAGE 1: Numerator and denominator
A fraction shows a part of a whole. If a chapati is cut into 4 equal pieces and you eat 1 piece, you have eaten one quarter of the chapati. We write one quarter as 1/4.

Every fraction has two numbers. The bottom number is the denominator. It tells us how many equal parts the whole is cut into. The top number is the numerator. It tells us how many of those parts we are talking about.

In the fraction 3/4:
• The denominator is 4: the whole is cut into 4 equal parts.
• The numerator is 3: we take 3 of those parts.

### The parts must be equal
A fraction only works when the parts are equal in size. If a cake is cut into one big piece and two small pieces, we cannot say each piece is one third.

### Reading fractions
• 1/2 is read one half.
• 1/4 is read one quarter.
• 3/4 is read three quarters.
• 5/8 is read five eighths.
• 7/10 is read seven tenths.

### Try it
In 5/8, the numerator is 5 and the denominator is 8. It means 5 out of 8 equal parts.

### Fractions on a clock face
A clock face is a circle with 12 equal spaces for the hours. Half an hour is 30 minutes, which is 1/2 of an hour. A quarter of an hour is 15 minutes, which is 1/4 of an hour. Each hour mark is 1/12 of the clock face.

### Cutting paper
Take a strip of paper. Fold it in half, then in half again and again. You get halves, quarters and eighths. Each time you fold, the parts get smaller, but they are still equal.

### Example
A rope is cut into 10 equal pieces and Amina takes 7 of them. She has 7/10 of the rope.

### Check yourself
Draw a rectangle and cut it into 12 equal parts. Shade 5 parts. The fraction shaded is 5/12. The fraction not shaded is 7/12. Together they make 12/12, which is one whole.

## PAGE 3: Fractions as part of a group

### Another example
Wafula has 20 goats. 1/5 of them are brown. How many are brown?
Step 1: 20 ÷ 5 = 4.
Step 2: 1 × 4 = 4.

### Part of a group in a class
A class has 30 pupils. 2/5 of them walk to school. Divide 30 by 5 to get 6. Multiply by 2 to get 12. So 12 pupils walk.

### Remember
Fractions of a group are always whole numbers here. Use your division facts to find them quickly.

## PAGE 4: Proper, improper and mixed fractions
There are three kinds of fractions.

### Proper fractions
In a proper fraction, the numerator is smaller than the denominator. It is less than one whole. Examples: 1/2, 3/4, 5/8.

### Improper fractions
In an improper fraction, the numerator is equal to or bigger than the denominator. It is one whole or more. Examples: 5/4, 7/3, 9/9.

### Mixed fractions
A mixed fraction has a whole number and a proper fraction together. Example: 1 3/4, read one and three quarters.

### Sorting fractions
• 2/9 is proper (2 is less than 9).
• 11/5 is improper (11 is more than 5).
• 3 1/2 is mixed (a whole number 3 and a fraction 1/2).
• 8/8 is improper, and equals 1 whole.

### Example
A jug holds 1/4 of a litre when full. Three full jugs of juice make 3/4 of a litre.

### More examples
• 2 1/3: 2 × 3 = 6, 6 + 1 = 7, so 7/3.
• 4 3/8: 4 × 8 = 32, 32 + 3 = 35, so 35/8.
• 1 5/6: 1 × 6 = 6, 6 + 5 = 11, so 11/6.

### Going both ways
Changing improper to mixed uses division. Changing mixed to improper uses multiplication and addition. They undo each other. Check: 17 ÷ 5 = 3 remainder 2, which gives 3 2/5 again.

### Common mistakes
• Changing the denominator. It never changes in these conversions.
• Forgetting to add the numerator after multiplying.
• Writing the remainder as the whole number instead of the quotient.

## QUIZ
1. In the fraction 5/8, which number is the denominator?
   A) 5
   B) 8
   C) 13
   D) 3
   Answer: B
   Why: The denominator is the bottom number. It shows the whole is cut into 8 equal parts.
2. Which of these is an improper fraction?
   A) 3/8
   B) 7/4
   C) 2 1/2
   D) 1/10
   Answer: B
   Why: In 7/4 the numerator 7 is bigger than the denominator 4. 3/8 and 1/10 are proper, and 2 1/2 is mixed.
3. What is 3/4 of 12?
   A) 3
   B) 4
   C) 9
   D) 16
   Answer: C
   Why: 12 ÷ 4 = 3 and 3 × 3 = 9.
4. Change 11/4 to a mixed fraction.
   A) 2 1/4
   B) 2 3/4
   C) 3 1/4
   D) 3 3/4
   Answer: B
   Why: 11 ÷ 4 = 2 remainder 3, so 11/4 = 2 3/4.
5. Change 3 2/5 to an improper fraction.
   A) 5/17
   B) 15/5
   C) 17/5
   D) 32/5
   Answer: C
   Why: 3 × 5 = 15 and 15 + 2 = 17. The denominator stays 5, so the answer is 17/5.
6. A rectangle is cut into 12 equal parts and 5 parts are shaded. What fraction is shaded and what fraction is not shaded? (2 marks)
   Answer: 5/12 is shaded and 7/12 is not shaded.
   Why: 1 mark for 5/12. The unshaded parts are 12 − 5 = 7, so 1 mark for 7/12.
7. Wafula has 20 goats and 1/5 of them are brown. How many goats are brown? Show your working. (3 marks)
   Answer: 4 goats
   Why: 20 ÷ 5 = 4 (2 marks for correct working) and 1 × 4 = 4 goats (1 mark).
8. Change 23/6 to a mixed fraction. (3 marks)
   Answer: 3 5/6
   Why: 23 ÷ 6 = 3 remainder 5 (2 marks). So 23/6 = 3 5/6 with the denominator unchanged (1 mark).
9. Write 4 3/8 as an improper fraction. (3 marks)
   Answer: 35/8
   Why: 4 × 8 = 32 (1 mark); 32 + 3 = 35 (1 mark); keep the denominator 8 to give 35/8 (1 mark).
10. Explain why the parts must be equal when we write 1/3. (2 marks)
   Answer: A fraction shows equal parts of a whole. If the parts are different sizes, one piece is not one third.
   Why: 1 mark for saying the parts must be equal in size and 1 mark for the reasoning that the denominator counts equal parts.
