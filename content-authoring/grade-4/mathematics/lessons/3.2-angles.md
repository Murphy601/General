## PAGE 1: What is an angle?
An angle is made when two straight lines meet at a point. The point where they meet is called the vertex. The lines are called the arms of the angle. The amount that one arm is turned away from the other arm is the size of the angle.

Open a book and stand it on a table. The two covers meet at the spine. They make an angle. If you open the book wider, the angle gets bigger. If you close it a little, the angle gets smaller.

### Where do you see angles?
• A pair of scissors opening and closing.
• The hands of a clock.
• A gate that is partly open.
• The corner of a page, a table or a window.

### Try it
Open your arms wide, then close them a little. Say when your arms make a small angle and when they make a big angle.

## PAGE 2: Angles in objects around us
Angles are all around us. We can find them at the corners of objects. A corner is where two edges meet. Each corner makes an angle.

### Square corners and right angles
The corner of a page in your exercise book is a right angle. So are the corners of a window, a door, a table top, a blackboard and a brick. Because they look like the corner of a square, we call them square corners.

### A test for a right angle
Fold a piece of paper twice, so that the folds make a neat corner. This is a right angle checker. Place its corner on the corner of an object. If the checker fits exactly, the corner is a right angle.

### Angles in a cube and a cuboid
A cube and a cuboid both have 8 corners. Each face is a square or a rectangle, so every corner of each face is a right angle. Look at a box of tea leaves or a matchbox. You will find right angles at every corner.

### Other angles
Not every angle is a right angle. The point of a pencil, the corner of a triangular sandwich or the roof of a house has angles that are not right angles. Some are smaller than a right angle and some are bigger.

• If the arms are closer together than the arms of the right angle, the angle is smaller than a right angle.
• If the arms fit the checker exactly, it is a right angle.
• If the arms are further apart than the arms of the checker, the angle is bigger than a right angle.

### Which is bigger?
The size of an angle does not depend on how long the arms are. It depends on how far apart the arms are. Two angles with arms of different lengths can be the same size. Long arms just make an angle look bigger.

### Sorting corners
In a rectangle all four corners are right angles. In a triangle whose corners are not square, some angles are smaller than a right angle. A triangle can have one angle bigger than a right angle, but it never has two.

### Right angles in a shape
Count the right angles in these shapes: a square has 4, a rectangle has 4, a circle has none and a triangle may have 0 or 1.

## PAGE 4: Relating turns to angles
When you make a turn, you sweep out an angle. The bigger the turn, the bigger the angle.

• A quarter turn makes a right angle.
• A half turn makes a straight line. Two right angles make a straight line.
• A full turn goes all the way round. Four right angles make a full turn.

### Degrees
Later you will learn to measure angles with numbers called degrees. A right angle is 90 degrees. A straight line is 180 degrees. A full turn is 360 degrees. You do not need to measure with degrees yet.

## PAGE 5: Making and drawing angles
You can make angles with things you have at home. Two straight sticks joined at one end with a pin or a bottle top make an angle that you can open and close. Two strips of cardboard joined by a paper fastener work the same way.

### Making a right angle
Fold a page once, then fold it again so that the first fold lines up with itself. The corner you make is a right angle.

### Drawing an angle
Step 1: Draw a straight line with a ruler. This is one arm.
Step 2: Mark a point at one end of the line. This is the vertex.
Step 3: From the vertex, draw a second line in another direction.
Step 4: Draw a small curve between the two arms to show the angle.

### Drawing a right angle
Place the corner of a piece of paper at the vertex, with one edge along the first arm. Draw along the other edge of the paper. The angle you have drawn is a right angle. Mark it with a small square.

### Using the environment
Walk around your school compound. Find a right angle at a door frame, a smaller angle at a roof and a bigger angle at an open gate. Sketch each one in your book and say whether it is smaller than, equal to or bigger than a right angle.

## PAGE 6: Summary and common mistakes
### What we have learned
• An angle is made when two lines meet at a point, the vertex.
• The size of an angle tells us how much one arm is turned from the other.
• A right angle is like the corner of a page. It is marked with a small square.
• A quarter turn makes a right angle. A half turn makes a straight line. A full turn is four right angles.
• We can compare any angle with a right angle: it is smaller, equal or bigger.

### Worked example
A gate opens from closed to a quarter turn. What kind of angle does it make with the closed position? It makes a right angle. If it opens to a half turn, the gate and the wall make a straight line.

### More practice
How many right angles are in a rectangle? A rectangle has 4 corners and each is a right angle, so it has 4 right angles. How many right angles make a full turn? 4 right angles, because 4 quarter turns make a full turn.

### Common mistakes
• Thinking that longer arms make a bigger angle. The size depends on how far apart the arms are.
• Forgetting the square mark for right angles.
• Thinking the corner of every object is a right angle. A triangular roof has angles that are not right angles.
• Confusing a half turn (a straight line) with a right angle.

### Challenge
Use your right angle checker to find five right angles in your classroom. Then find two angles that are smaller than a right angle. Write down where you found them.

## QUIZ
1. What do we call the point where the two arms of an angle meet?
   A) Arm
   B) Vertex
   C) Side
   D) Edge
   Answer: B
   Why: The arms of an angle meet at a point called the vertex.
2. Which of these is a right angle?
   A) The corner of a page in an exercise book
   B) The point of a sharp pencil
   C) A half-open pair of scissors
   D) A wide open gate
   Answer: A
   Why: A page corner is a square corner, which is a right angle.
3. A quarter turn makes
   A) a straight line
   B) a right angle
   C) a full turn
   D) no angle
   Answer: B
   Why: A quarter turn sweeps out a right angle.
4. How many right angles make a full turn?
   A) 1
   B) 2
   C) 3
   D) 4
   Answer: D
   Why: A full turn is four quarter turns, and each quarter turn is a right angle.
5. At 6 o'clock the hands of a clock make
   A) a right angle
   B) a small angle
   C) a straight line
   D) no angle
   Answer: C
   Why: At 6 o'clock the hands point in opposite directions, forming a straight line, which is a half turn.
6. How many right angles does a rectangle have? Explain. (2 marks)
   Answer: 4. A rectangle has four corners and each corner is a right angle.
   Why: 1 mark for the number 4 and 1 mark for the reason.
7. Describe how you can use a folded piece of paper to test whether a corner is a right angle. (3 marks)
   Answer: Fold a piece of paper twice to make a neat square corner. Place its corner on the corner of the object. If it fits exactly, the corner is a right angle.
   Why: 1 mark for folding paper to make a square corner, 1 mark for placing it on the corner of the object, 1 mark for saying it fits exactly means a right angle.
8. Name three objects in your classroom that have right angles. (3 marks)
   Answer: Any three correct, for example a window, a door and a book.
   Why: 1 mark for each correct object.
9. A gate opens through a half turn from its closed position. Describe the angle between the gate and the wall in its closed position. (2 marks)
   Answer: The gate and the closed position make a straight line, which is two right angles.
   Why: 1 mark for saying a straight line and 1 mark for two right angles.
10. Nanjala says an angle with longer arms is always bigger. Is she right? Explain. (3 marks)
   Answer: No. The size of an angle depends on how far apart the arms are, not on how long they are.
   Why: 1 mark for saying no, 1 mark for saying size depends on the opening between the arms, 1 mark for saying length of arms does not matter.
