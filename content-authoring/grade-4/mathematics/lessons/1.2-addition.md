## PAGE 1: Adding 4-digit numbers without regrouping
Addition means putting amounts together to find the total. The answer to an addition is called the sum. When we add big numbers we write them in columns, with ones under ones, tens under tens, hundreds under hundreds and thousands under thousands. Then we add column by column, starting from the ones on the right.

Let us add 2,345 and 1,432.
Step 1: Ones: 5 + 2 = 7.
Step 2: Tens: 4 + 3 = 7.
Step 3: Hundreds: 3 + 4 = 7.
Step 4: Thousands: 2 + 1 = 3.
The sum is 3,777.

### Place value apparatus
You can also add with bottle tops. Use one colour for thousands, another for hundreds, another for tens and another for ones. Put the two numbers together and count each colour separately.

### Example
A school had 1,204 girls and 1,315 boys. Total pupils: 1,204 + 1,315 = 2,519 pupils.

## PAGE 2: Adding with single regrouping
Sometimes the digits in a column add up to 10 or more. We cannot write a two-digit number in one column, so we regroup. We keep the ones digit of the total in that column and carry the tens to the next column.

Add 3,425 and 2,318.
Step 1: Ones: 5 + 8 = 13. Write 3 in the ones place and carry 1 ten to the tens column.
Step 2: Tens: 2 + 1 = 3, and add the carried 1, which gives 4.
Step 3: Hundreds: 4 + 3 = 7.
Step 4: Thousands: 3 + 2 = 5.
The sum is 5,743.

### Remember the carried number
The small carried digit must be added in the next column. Forgetting it is the most common mistake. If you had forgotten it, you would have got 5,733, which is wrong. A quick check: 3,425 is about 3,400 and 2,318 is about 2,300. About 5,700 is close to 5,743, so the answer looks sensible.

### Try it
Add 4,236 and 1,528. The ones make 14, so write 4 and carry 1. The answer is 5,764.

## PAGE 3: Adding with double regrouping
Double regrouping means two columns give totals of 10 or more, one after the other. Take your time: the carry from the first column changes the next column.

Add 3,568 and 2,276.
Step 1: Ones: 8 + 6 = 14. Write 4 and carry 1 ten.
Step 2: Tens: 6 + 7 = 13, plus the carried 1 makes 14. Write 4 and carry 1 hundred.
Step 3: Hundreds: 5 + 2 = 7, plus the carried 1 makes 8. No new carry.
Step 4: Thousands: 3 + 2 = 5.
The sum is 5,844.

### When the sum goes over 9,999
In this grade the biggest sum we add is 10,000. If the thousands column gives 10 or more, we write a fifth digit. For example, 6,500 + 3,500 = 10,000.

### Example
A farmer sold 4,750 kg of tea leaves in May and 3,895 kg in June. How much did she sell in all?
4,750 + 3,895: ones 0 + 5 = 5; tens 5 + 9 = 14, write 4 and carry 1; hundreds 7 + 8 + 1 = 16, write 6 and carry 1; thousands 4 + 3 + 1 = 8. Total: 8,645 kg.

## PAGE 4: Addition problems in real life
Addition is used whenever we combine things. In the market, in school and on the shamba, we add to find totals. Read the problem, decide what must be added, write the sum in columns, and give the answer with a unit.

### Word problem 1: The bus
A bus company carried 5,684 passengers on Saturday and 3,749 on Sunday. How many passengers did it carry over the two days?
Step 1: Add 5,684 + 3,749.
Step 2: Ones: 4 + 9 = 13. Write 3, carry 1.
Step 3: Tens: 8 + 4 + 1 = 13. Write 3, carry 1.
Step 4: Hundreds: 6 + 7 + 1 = 14. Write 4, carry 1.
Step 5: Thousands: 5 + 3 + 1 = 9.
Answer: 9,433 passengers.

### Word problem 2: The shop
Mama Achieng sold 2,450 eggs one week and 3,875 eggs the next week. Total eggs: 2,450 + 3,875 = 6,325 eggs.

### Checking your answer
Add the numbers again in the opposite order. 3,875 + 2,450 should give the same answer. Addition can be done in any order, and the sum stays the same.

### Words that tell us to add
Look for words like total, altogether, sum, in all, plus and more than. When you see them, think of addition.

## PAGE 5: Estimating a sum by rounding to the nearest ten
Sometimes we only need an answer that is close, not exact. We estimate. To estimate a sum, round each number to the nearest ten and add the rounded numbers. Rounding makes the numbers easier to add in your head.

To round to the nearest ten, look at the ones digit. If it is 0 to 4, keep the tens the same. If it is 5 to 9, go up to the next ten.

Estimate 4,368 + 2,547.

Step 2: 2,547 rounds to 2,550 because the ones digit 7 is 5 or more.
Step 3: Add the rounded numbers: 4,370 + 2,550 = 6,920.
The estimate is 6,920.

The exact sum is 4,368 + 2,547 = 6,915. The estimate 6,920 is very close, so the exact answer is sensible.

### Why estimate?
Estimating helps you catch big mistakes. If you found 5,915 as the exact answer, the estimate 6,920 would show that something is wrong.

## PAGE 6: Number patterns using addition
A pattern is a set of numbers that follows a rule. In an addition pattern, we add the same number each time. The number we add is the step or difference.

Look at 125, 250, 375, 500, 625. Each number is 125 more than the one before it. The rule is add 125.

### Finding the rule
Subtract any number from the one after it. In 2,000, 2,300, 2,600, 2,900: 2,300 − 2,000 = 300, so the rule is add 300. The next numbers are 3,200 and 3,500.

### Making your own pattern
Choose a start and a rule. Start at 1,500 and add 1,000: 1,500, 2,500, 3,500, 4,500, 5,500. Keep the numbers below 10,000.

### Real-life pattern
Wanjiru saves Ksh 250 every week. After 1 week she has 250, then 500, 750, 1,000, and so on. This is an addition pattern with the rule add 250.

### Common mistakes
• Adding to the wrong column when a number has fewer digits.
• Forgetting to add the carried digit.
• Rounding wrongly. Check the ones digit only.

## QUIZ
1. What is 2,345 + 1,432?
   A) 3,677
   B) 3,777
   C) 3,787
   D) 4,777
   Answer: B
   Why: Ones 5 + 2 = 7; tens 4 + 3 = 7; hundreds 3 + 4 = 7; thousands 2 + 1 = 3. The sum is 3,777.
2. Work out 3,425 + 2,318.
   A) 5,633
   B) 5,743
   C) 5,733
   D) 6,743
   Answer: B
   Why: Ones 5 + 8 = 13, write 3 carry 1; tens 2 + 1 + 1 = 4; hundreds 4 + 3 = 7; thousands 3 + 2 = 5. The sum is 5,743.
3. Estimate 4,368 + 2,547 by rounding each number to the nearest ten.
   A) 6,900
   B) 6,910
   C) 6,920
   D) 7,000
   Answer: C
   Why: 4,368 rounds to 4,370 and 2,547 rounds to 2,550. 4,370 + 2,550 = 6,920.
4. The rule of the pattern 1,200, 1,500, 1,800, 2,100 is
   A) add 200
   B) add 300
   C) add 500
   D) add 1,200
   Answer: B
   Why: 1,500 − 1,200 = 300, and each step is the same, so the rule is add 300.
5. In 6,478 + 2,355, how many times do we need to regroup?
   A) 0 times
   B) 1 time
   C) 2 times
   D) 3 times
   Answer: C
   Why: Ones 8 + 5 = 13 (regroup); tens 7 + 5 + 1 = 13 (regroup); hundreds 4 + 3 + 1 = 8 (no regroup); thousands 6 + 2 = 8. Two regroupings are needed.
6. Add 4,236 and 1,528. Show your working. (3 marks)
   Answer: 5,764
   Why: Ones 6 + 8 = 14, write 4 carry 1 (1 mark); tens 3 + 2 + 1 = 6 (1 mark); hundreds 2 + 5 = 7 and thousands 4 + 1 = 5, giving 5,764 (1 mark).
7. A shop earned Ksh 4,750 on Monday and Ksh 3,895 on Tuesday. How much did it earn on the two days? (3 marks)
   Answer: Ksh 8,645
   Why: 4,750 + 3,895. Ones 0 + 5 = 5; tens 5 + 9 = 14, write 4 carry 1; hundreds 7 + 8 + 1 = 16, write 6 carry 1; thousands 4 + 3 + 1 = 8. 1 mark for setting up the addition, 1 mark for correct regrouping, 1 mark for the answer with units.
8. Write the next three numbers in the pattern 2,000, 2,300, 2,600, 2,900. (3 marks)
   Answer: 3,200, 3,500, 3,800
   Why: The rule is add 300 (1 mark). 2,900 + 300 = 3,200; 3,200 + 300 = 3,500; 3,500 + 300 = 3,800 (1 mark for each correct pair).
9. Estimate 5,382 + 3,617 by rounding to the nearest ten. Then work out the exact sum and say how close the estimate is. (4 marks)
   Answer: Estimate 5,380 + 3,620 = 9,000. Exact sum 8,999. The estimate is only 1 more than the exact sum.
   Why: 1 mark for each rounded number (5,380 and 3,620); 1 mark for the estimate 9,000 and exact sum 5,382 + 3,617 = 8,999; 1 mark for comparing them.
