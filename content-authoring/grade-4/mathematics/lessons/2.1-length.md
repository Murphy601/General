## PAGE 1: The centimetre as a unit of length
Length tells us how long something is or how far apart two points are. To measure length we need a unit, which is a fixed amount that everyone agrees on. A very useful small unit is the centimetre. We write it as cm.

A centimetre is a small length. The width of your little fingernail is about one centimetre. The thickness of a new pencil is a little less than one centimetre. You can use a ruler to see exactly how long one centimetre is. On a ruler, the numbers 0, 1, 2, 3 and so on mark the centimetres. The space from one number to the next is 1 cm.

### How to read the pencil
The pencil starts at 2 and ends at 9. To find its length, count the centimetre spaces from 2 to 9, or subtract: 9 − 2 = 7. The pencil is 7 cm long.

### Why we start from a number
It is best to line up one end of the object with 0. Then the number at the other end is the length. If the object starts at another number, subtract the smaller number from the larger number.

### Try it
Use a ruler to mark a line of exactly 1 cm in your exercise book. Then mark lines of 5 cm and 10 cm.

## PAGE 2: Measuring and estimating length in centimetres
To measure a length, place the zero mark of the ruler at one end of the object. Keep the ruler straight along the edge. Read the number at the other end. Always write the unit, cm, after the number.

### Measuring a leaf
A leaf lies against a ruler with its stalk at 0 and its tip at 6. It is 6 cm long. If the tip is halfway between 6 and 7, it is 6 and a half centimetres, which we write as 6 1/2 cm or 6.5 cm.

### Estimating first
An estimate is a sensible guess. Before you measure, guess the length. Then measure and see how close you were. To estimate, compare with something you know. If your exercise book is 21 cm long, an object about the same length is about 21 cm.

### A useful habit
• Estimate the length of your pencil, your eraser and your desk.
• Write your estimates in a table.
• Measure each one with a ruler. Compare your estimate with the real length.
The more you practise, the better your estimates will be.

### Longer lengths
For longer objects, such as a classroom wall or a table, we use a tape measure or a metre ruler. A metre ruler is 100 cm long.

## PAGE 3: Metres and centimetres
A ruler of 30 cm is short. To measure a room or a piece of cloth, we use a bigger unit called the metre. We write it as m. The metre is 100 times as long as the centimetre.

1 metre = 100 centimetres

A metre ruler is marked with 100 centimetres. Half of it is 50 cm. A quarter of it is 25 cm.

### How lengths are written
A length can have metres and centimetres together. 2 m 35 cm means 2 metres and 35 centimetres. The centimetres part is always less than 100. If it reaches 100, we make it another metre.

### Which unit to use?
• Use centimetres for small things: a pencil, a book, a cup.
• Use metres for bigger things: a room, a football pitch, a length of cloth.
• A door is about 2 m high. A desk is about 75 cm high.

### Activity
Use a metre stick or tape to measure your desk. Write the length in metres and centimetres, for example 1 m 20 cm.

## PAGE 4: Converting metres and centimetres
Since 1 m = 100 cm, we can change from one unit to the other.

### Metres to centimetres: multiply by 100
3 m = 3 × 100 = 300 cm.
2 m 45 cm = 200 cm + 45 cm = 245 cm.

### Centimetres to metres: divide by 100
400 cm = 400 ÷ 100 = 4 m.
250 cm: 250 ÷ 100 = 2 remainder 50, so 250 cm = 2 m 50 cm.

### Using decimals and fractions
• 250 cm = 2 m 50 cm = 2.5 m, because 50 cm is half a metre.
• 75 cm = 0.75 m, because 75 cm is 75 hundredths of a metre.
• 1/2 m = 50 cm and 1/4 m = 25 cm.
• 1.2 m = 1 m 20 cm = 120 cm.

### Worked example
A tailor has 3.4 m of cloth. How many centimetres is that?
Step 1: 3 m = 300 cm.
Step 2: 0.4 m = 40 cm.
Step 3: Add: 300 + 40 = 340 cm.

## PAGE 5: Perimeter of plane figures
The perimeter is the distance all the way round the edge of a shape. Imagine walking round a school field. The total distance you walk is the perimeter. To find the perimeter, add the lengths of all the sides. Perimeter is measured in units of length, such as cm or m.

For any shape with straight sides, add every side. An L-shaped garden with sides 6 m, 2 m, 3 m, 2 m, 3 m and 4 m has perimeter 6 + 2 + 3 + 2 + 3 + 4 = 20 m.

## PAGE 6: Adding and subtracting length
We can add and subtract lengths given in metres and centimetres. The easiest way is to change everything to centimetres, work out the answer, and change back. Or we can add the metres and the centimetres separately.

### Addition
A rope 3 m 45 cm long is joined to a rope 2 m 80 cm long. What is the total length?
Method 1: centimetres only. 3 m 45 cm = 345 cm. 2 m 80 cm = 280 cm. 345 + 280 = 625 cm = 6 m 25 cm.
Method 2: add separately. Centimetres: 45 + 80 = 125 cm = 1 m 25 cm. Metres: 3 + 2 = 5 m. Add: 5 m + 1 m 25 cm = 6 m 25 cm.

### Subtraction
A ribbon is 5 m 20 cm long. A piece of 2 m 75 cm is cut off. How much is left?
520 cm − 275 cm = 245 cm = 2 m 45 cm.
Check: 245 + 275 = 520.

### Tip
When the centimetres in the top number are fewer than the centimetres below, change 1 m into 100 cm. For 5 m 20 cm − 2 m 75 cm: 5 m 20 cm is 4 m 120 cm. 120 − 75 = 45 cm and 4 − 2 = 2 m. The answer is 2 m 45 cm.

## PAGE 7: Multiplying and dividing length
Length can also be multiplied and divided. Again, change to centimetres first to keep things simple.

### Multiplication
A fence has 4 pieces of wire, each 2 m 30 cm long. What is the total length?
Step 1: 2 m 30 cm = 230 cm.
Step 2: 230 × 4 = 920 cm.
Step 3: 920 cm = 9 m 20 cm.
The total length is 9 m 20 cm.

### Division
A rope 7 m 20 cm long is cut into 6 equal pieces. How long is each piece?
Step 1: 7 m 20 cm = 720 cm.
Step 2: 720 ÷ 6 = 120 cm.
Step 3: 120 cm = 1 m 20 cm.
Each piece is 1 m 20 cm long.

### Real-life use
A farmer plants seedlings 50 cm apart along a row. For 9 gaps, the row is 9 × 50 = 450 cm = 4 m 50 cm long.

### Summary
• 1 m = 100 cm.
• Metres to centimetres: multiply by 100. Centimetres to metres: divide by 100.
• Perimeter is the total length of all sides.
• Change to centimetres before adding, subtracting, multiplying or dividing.

## QUIZ
1. A pencil starts at the 2 cm mark on a ruler and ends at the 9 cm mark. How long is the pencil?
   A) 2 cm
   B) 7 cm
   C) 9 cm
   D) 11 cm
   Answer: B
   Why: Subtract the start from the end: 9 − 2 = 7 cm.
2. How many centimetres are in 3 m?
   A) 30 cm
   B) 300 cm
   C) 3,000 cm
   D) 103 cm
   Answer: B
   Why: 1 m = 100 cm, so 3 m = 3 × 100 = 300 cm.
3. What is 250 cm written in metres?
   A) 0.25 m
   B) 2.05 m
   C) 2.5 m
   D) 25 m
   Answer: C
   Why: 250 cm = 200 cm + 50 cm = 2 m and half a metre = 2.5 m.
4. A rectangle is 8 cm long and 5 cm wide. What is its perimeter?
   A) 13 cm
   B) 26 cm
   C) 40 cm
   D) 18 cm
   Answer: B
   Why: Perimeter = 8 + 5 + 8 + 5 = 26 cm.
5. What is 3 m 45 cm + 2 m 80 cm?
   A) 5 m 125 cm
   B) 5 m 25 cm
   C) 6 m 25 cm
   D) 6 m 125 cm
   Answer: C
   Why: 345 cm + 280 cm = 625 cm = 6 m 25 cm.
6. Change 3.4 m to centimetres. (2 marks)
   Answer: 340 cm
   Why: 3 m = 300 cm and 0.4 m = 40 cm (1 mark). 300 + 40 = 340 cm (1 mark).
7. A square garden has sides of 15 m. Find the perimeter of the garden. (2 marks)
   Answer: 60 m
   Why: A square has four equal sides. 15 + 15 + 15 + 15 = 60 m. 1 mark for the method and 1 mark for the answer with unit.
8. A ribbon is 5 m 20 cm long. A piece 2 m 75 cm long is cut off. How much ribbon is left? (3 marks)
   Answer: 2 m 45 cm
   Why: 5 m 20 cm = 520 cm and 2 m 75 cm = 275 cm (1 mark). 520 − 275 = 245 cm (1 mark). 245 cm = 2 m 45 cm (1 mark).
9. A fence uses 4 pieces of wire, each 2 m 30 cm long. What is the total length of wire? (3 marks)
   Answer: 9 m 20 cm
   Why: 2 m 30 cm = 230 cm (1 mark). 230 × 4 = 920 cm (1 mark). 920 cm = 9 m 20 cm (1 mark).
10. A rope 7 m 20 cm long is cut into 6 equal pieces. How long is each piece? (3 marks)
   Answer: 1 m 20 cm
   Why: 7 m 20 cm = 720 cm (1 mark). 720 ÷ 6 = 120 cm (1 mark). 120 cm = 1 m 20 cm (1 mark).
