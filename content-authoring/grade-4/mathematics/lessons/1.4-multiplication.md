## PAGE 1: Multiplying by multiples of 10
Multiplication is repeated addition. 3 × 4 means 4 + 4 + 4, or 3 groups of 4. The answer is called the product. Multiples of 10 are the numbers 10, 20, 30, 40 and so on. Multiplying by a multiple of 10 is quick if you know your times tables.

First, multiply by 10. When you multiply by 10, every digit moves one place to the left and a 0 takes the ones place. So 24 × 10 = 240 and 57 × 10 = 570.

To multiply by 20, 30, 40 and so on, use two steps:
Step 1: Multiply by the tens digit (2, 3, 4 ...).
Step 2: Multiply the result by 10 by writing a 0 at the end.

Work out 24 × 30.
Step 1: 24 × 3 = 72.
Step 2: 72 × 10 = 720.
So 24 × 30 = 720.

## PAGE 2: Multiplying by a two-digit number without regrouping
To multiply by a two-digit number, we split it into tens and ones. We multiply by the ones first, then by the tens, and add the two answers. These answers are called partial products.

Work out 12 × 21.
Step 1: Multiply 12 by the ones digit 1: 12 × 1 = 12.
Step 2: Multiply 12 by the tens digit, which is really 20: 12 × 20 = 240.
Step 3: Add the partial products: 12 + 240 = 252.

The partial product for the tens digit always ends in 0 because we are multiplying by tens. Do not forget to write that 0.

### Why this works
21 is 20 + 1. So 12 × 21 = 12 × 20 + 12 × 1 = 240 + 12 = 252. Each part is easy, and adding them gives the whole answer.

### Example
A classroom has 13 rows of 12 desks. Total desks: 13 × 12. 13 × 2 = 26 and 13 × 10 = 130. Add: 26 + 130 = 156 desks.

### Try it
Work out 32 × 21. 32 × 1 = 32; 32 × 20 = 640; 32 + 640 = 672.

## PAGE 3: Multiplying with regrouping
Sometimes the partial products need regrouping, just as in addition. Take your time and write neatly.

Work out 36 × 24.
Step 1: Multiply by the ones digit 4: 36 × 4 = 144. (6 × 4 = 24, write 4, carry 2; 3 × 4 = 12, plus 2 = 14.)
Step 2: Multiply by the tens digit, 20: 36 × 20 = 720. (36 × 2 = 72, then add a 0.)
Step 3: Add the partial products: 144 + 720 = 864.

## PAGE 4: Multiplying by 100
When we multiply by 100, every digit moves two places to the left and we write two zeros in the empty places. So the number becomes 100 times bigger.

Look at 34 × 100.
The digits 3 and 4 move two places to the left. The 3 moves from tens to thousands. The 4 moves from ones to hundreds. Zeros fill the tens and ones places. The answer is 3,400.

### The zeros rule
Count the zeros in 100. There are two. Put two zeros on the end of the number. This works for whole numbers.

### Example
A jerrycan holds 20 litres. A water tanker fills 100 jerrycans. Total: 20 × 100 = 2,000 litres.

### Common mistake
Some learners write only one zero because they think of multiplying by 10. Multiplying by 100 needs two zeros.

## PAGE 5: Estimating products
To estimate a product, round each number to the nearest ten, then multiply. Estimating helps you check whether your exact answer makes sense. In this grade we estimate products that do not go beyond 1,000.

Estimate 38 × 22.
Step 1: Round 38 to the nearest ten: 40.
Step 2: Round 22 to the nearest ten: 20.
Step 3: Multiply: 40 × 20. First 4 × 2 = 8, then two zeros make 800.
The estimate is 800.

The exact answer is 38 × 22: 38 × 2 = 76 and 38 × 20 = 760. Then 76 + 760 = 836. The estimate 800 is close to 836, so our exact answer is sensible.

### More examples
• 19 × 31: round to 20 × 30 = 600. Exact: 589.
• 27 × 13: round to 30 × 10 = 300. Exact: 351.

### Why estimates are not exact
Rounding changes the numbers a little, so the estimate is a little bigger or smaller than the exact product. It gives us the size of the answer.

### Shopping example
Wangari buys 24 exercise books at Ksh 18 each. Estimate the total: 24 rounds to 20 and 18 rounds to 20. 20 × 20 = 400. The exact cost is 24 × 18 = 432 shillings. The estimate 400 is close.

### Patterns to try
• Multiples of 5 always end in 0 or 5: 5, 10, 15, 20, 25 ...
• The 9 times table: digits add up to 9 (18 → 1 + 8 = 9; 36 → 3 + 6 = 9; 72 → 7 + 2 = 9).
• The 10 times table: every product ends in 0.
• Doubling: 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64. Each number is the one before multiplied by 2. The next would be 128, which is above 100, so we stop.

### Making your own pattern
Start at 2 and multiply by 3 each time: 2, 6, 18, 54. The next would be 162, which is above 100. This pattern has the rule multiply by 3.

### Preparing a chart
Draw a chart with the numbers 1 to 10 across the top and down the side. Fill each square with the product of its row and column number. Use recycled cardboard to make your chart. You will spot patterns along the diagonals.

## QUIZ
1. What is 24 × 30?
   A) 72
   B) 270
   C) 720
   D) 7,200
   Answer: C
   Why: 24 × 3 = 72, then multiplying by 10 gives 720.
2. Work out 36 × 24.
   A) 720
   B) 864
   C) 844
   D) 964
   Answer: B
   Why: 36 × 4 = 144; 36 × 20 = 720; 144 + 720 = 864.
3. What is 34 × 100?
   A) 340
   B) 3,004
   C) 3,400
   D) 34,000
   Answer: C
   Why: Multiplying by 100 moves each digit two places to the left, so 34 becomes 3,400.
4. Estimate 38 × 22 by rounding to the nearest ten.
   A) 600
   B) 800
   C) 880
   D) 1,000
   Answer: B
   Why: 38 rounds to 40 and 22 rounds to 20. 40 × 20 = 800.
5. Which pattern has the rule multiply by 2?
   A) 3, 5, 7, 9
   B) 2, 4, 8, 16
   C) 2, 4, 6, 8
   D) 5, 10, 15, 20
   Answer: B
   Why: 2 × 2 = 4, 4 × 2 = 8, 8 × 2 = 16. In the others the rule is add 2, add 2 or add 5.
6. A packet holds 40 sweets. How many sweets are in 15 packets? (2 marks)
   Answer: 600 sweets
   Why: 15 × 4 = 60 (1 mark), then 60 × 10 = 600 sweets (1 mark).
7. A trader buys 18 crates with 35 bananas in each crate. How many bananas are there altogether? (3 marks)
   Answer: 630 bananas
   Why: 18 × 5 = 90 (1 mark); 18 × 30 = 540 (1 mark); 90 + 540 = 630 bananas (1 mark).
8. A tanker fills 100 jerrycans of 20 litres each. How many litres is that? (2 marks)
   Answer: 2,000 litres
   Why: 20 × 100 = 2,000. 1 mark for the multiplication, 1 mark for the unit.
