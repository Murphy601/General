## PAGE 1: What is mass?
Mass tells us how heavy an object is. A bag of maize has more mass than an egg. A mango has more mass than a feather. In daily life, people often say weight when they mean mass. In mathematics we say mass.

We cannot tell the mass of an object just by its size. A big sponge is large but light. A small stone can be heavy. To find the mass exactly, we must measure it.

### The kilogram
The unit we use to measure mass is the kilogram. We write it as kg. A packet of sugar in a shop often has a mass of 1 kg. A packet of flour may be 2 kg. A bag of maize flour may be 2 kg, 5 kg or more. A school child may have a mass of about 25 kg.

### Instruments for measuring mass
• A beam balance compares the mass of an object with known masses.
• A bathroom scale shows the mass of a person.
• A shop scale shows the mass of goods.
• An electronic balance shows the mass as numbers on a screen.

### Compare by feeling
Hold a book in one hand and a pencil in the other. Which do you feel is heavier? The book has more mass. Feeling is good for comparing, but measuring is better for exact answers.

### Steps for using a beam balance
Step 1: Check that the beam is level when both pans are empty.
Step 2: Place the object in the left pan.
Step 3: Add 1 kg masses one at a time to the right pan until the beam becomes level.
Step 4: Count the masses and write the total mass with the unit kg.

### If the beam does not become level
If the beam is level with 3 masses, the object is 3 kg. If 3 masses are too few and 4 masses are too many, the object is between 3 kg and 4 kg. In the next lesson we use smaller masses to measure between whole kilograms.

### Share the equipment
Take turns and handle the balance carefully. Share the masses fairly so that every learner has a chance to measure.

## PAGE 3: Half a kilogram and a quarter of a kilogram
Sometimes objects are lighter than 1 kg, or are between whole kilograms. We use a half-kilogram mass, written 1/2 kg, and a quarter-kilogram mass, written 1/4 kg.

Two 1/2 kg masses balance one 1 kg mass. Four 1/4 kg masses balance one 1 kg mass. Also, two 1/4 kg masses balance one 1/2 kg mass.

### Making your own masses
You can make masses from sand. Put sand in a small bag on one pan and a 1 kg mass in the other. Add or remove sand until the beam is level. Now the sand has a mass of 1 kg. Share this sand equally between two bags, checking with the balance, to make two bags of 1/2 kg each. Share one of those between two bags again to get 1/4 kg bags.

### Combining masses
• 1/2 kg + 1/2 kg = 1 kg.
• 1 kg + 1/2 kg = 1 1/2 kg.
• 1 kg + 1/4 kg = 1 1/4 kg.

### Reading the small marks
Between two whole numbers there are three small marks. They divide each kilogram into four equal parts of 1/4 kg. The first small mark after 3 is 3 1/4 kg. The next is 3 1/2 kg and the next is 3 3/4 kg.

### Electronic balances
An electronic balance shows a number such as 2.5 kg. That means 2 1/2 kg. The number 0.25 kg means 1/4 kg. The number 0.75 kg means 3/4 kg.

### Estimating first
Before you measure, guess. A large watermelon may have a mass of about 4 kg. A single egg has a small mass, much less than 1 kg. Compare your guess to the measured value.

## PAGE 5: Adding mass
To add masses, add the whole kilograms first and then add the parts of a kilogram.

### Whole kilograms
A trader has 12 kg of beans and 8 kg of maize. The total mass is 12 + 8 = 20 kg.

### Kilograms and parts of a kilogram
Add 2 1/2 kg and 1 3/4 kg.
Step 1: Add the whole kilograms: 2 + 1 = 3 kg.
Step 2: Add the parts. 1/2 = 2/4, so 2/4 + 3/4 = 5/4 kg. That is 1 1/4 kg.
Step 3: Add the results: 3 kg + 1 1/4 kg = 4 1/4 kg.

### Another example
A mother buys 3/4 kg of tomatoes and 1/2 kg of onions. Total = 3/4 + 2/4 = 5/4 = 1 1/4 kg.

### Check
Estimate first: 2 1/2 is about 2 and 1 3/4 is about 2. The answer should be about 4 kg. 4 1/4 is close to 4, so it is sensible.

## PAGE 6: Subtracting mass
Subtraction of mass tells us how much is left or how much heavier one thing is than another.

### Whole kilograms
A sack of flour of 50 kg is used to fill packets. 18 kg is used. Left: 50 − 18 = 32 kg.

### Kilograms and parts
A bag has a mass of 5 kg. Musa takes out 1 1/2 kg of beans. What mass is left?
Step 1: Take away 1 kg: 5 − 1 = 4 kg.
Step 2: Take away 1/2 kg: 4 − 1/2 = 3 1/2 kg.
The mass left is 3 1/2 kg.

### A harder example
Work out 6 1/4 kg − 2 3/4 kg.
Step 1: 1/4 is smaller than 3/4, so change one kilogram into quarters. 6 1/4 = 5 5/4.
Step 2: Subtract the wholes: 5 − 2 = 3.
Step 3: Subtract the quarters: 5/4 − 3/4 = 2/4 = 1/2.
The answer is 3 1/2 kg.

### Check by adding
Add the answer to the mass taken away: 3 1/2 + 2 3/4. Wholes: 3 + 2 = 5. Parts: 1/2 + 3/4 = 2/4 + 3/4 = 5/4 = 1 1/4. Total: 5 + 1 1/4 = 6 1/4 kg. It matches the starting mass.

### Difference of two masses
A goat has a mass of 32 kg and a sheep has a mass of 27 kg. The goat is 32 − 27 = 5 kg heavier.

## PAGE 7: Problems about mass and summary
### Problem 1
A 50 kg sack of rice is packed into bags of 5 kg each. How many bags? 50 ÷ 5 = 10 bags.

### Problem 2
Achieng buys 3 packets of flour of 1/2 kg each and 2 packets of sugar of 1 kg each. What is the total mass? 3 × 1/2 kg = 1 1/2 kg. 2 × 1 kg = 2 kg. Total = 3 1/2 kg.

### Problem 3
A basket of mangoes has a mass of 8 kg. The basket alone has a mass of 1 1/2 kg. What is the mass of the mangoes? 8 − 1 1/2 = 6 1/2 kg.

### Summary
• Mass tells us how heavy an object is.
• The kilogram (kg) is the unit of mass.
• 1/2 kg and 1/4 kg are useful parts of a kilogram.
• We use a beam balance, dial scale or electronic balance to measure mass.
• To add or subtract mass, work with whole kilograms first, then the parts.

### Common mistakes
• Thinking a large object must be heavy. Size and mass are different.
• Forgetting the unit kg.
• Not changing a kilogram into quarters when subtracting.
• Confusing 1/4 kg with 1/2 kg. Two quarters make a half.

## QUIZ
1. Which unit do we use for measuring the mass of a bag of maize flour?
   A) Litre
   B) Metre
   C) Kilogram
   D) Centimetre
   Answer: C
   Why: Mass is measured in kilograms. Litres measure capacity and metres and centimetres measure length.
2. Two 1/4 kg masses balance
   A) 1/4 kg
   B) 1/2 kg
   C) 3/4 kg
   D) 1 kg
   Answer: B
   Why: 1/4 + 1/4 = 2/4 = 1/2, so two quarters make one half.
3. A beam balance is level with a bag of rice in one pan and a 1/2 kg mass and a 1/4 kg mass in the other pan. What is the mass of the rice?
   A) 1/4 kg
   B) 1/2 kg
   C) 3/4 kg
   D) 1 kg
   Answer: C
   Why: 1/2 kg + 1/4 kg = 2/4 kg + 1/4 kg = 3/4 kg.
4. What is 2 1/2 kg + 1 3/4 kg?
   A) 3 1/4 kg
   B) 3 3/4 kg
   C) 4 kg
   D) 4 1/4 kg
   Answer: D
   Why: Wholes 2 + 1 = 3. Parts 2/4 + 3/4 = 5/4 = 1 1/4. Total 3 + 1 1/4 = 4 1/4 kg.
5. A bag has a mass of 5 kg. Musa takes out 1 1/2 kg. What is left?
   A) 3 kg
   B) 3 1/2 kg
   C) 4 1/2 kg
   D) 6 1/2 kg
   Answer: B
   Why: 5 − 1 1/2 = 3 1/2 kg.
6. A 50 kg sack of rice is packed into bags of 5 kg. How many bags are filled? (2 marks)
   Answer: 10 bags
   Why: 50 ÷ 5 = 10. 1 mark for the division and 1 mark for the answer.
7. Achieng buys 3 packets of flour of 1/2 kg each and 2 packets of sugar of 1 kg each. Find the total mass. (3 marks)
   Answer: 3 1/2 kg
   Why: 3 × 1/2 kg = 1 1/2 kg (1 mark). 2 × 1 kg = 2 kg (1 mark). 1 1/2 + 2 = 3 1/2 kg (1 mark).
8. A basket of mangoes has a mass of 8 kg. The basket alone has a mass of 1 1/2 kg. What is the mass of the mangoes? (2 marks)
   Answer: 6 1/2 kg
   Why: 8 − 1 1/2 = 6 1/2. 1 mark for the subtraction and 1 mark for the unit.
9. Work out 6 1/4 kg − 2 3/4 kg. (3 marks)
   Answer: 3 1/2 kg
   Why: Change 6 1/4 to 5 5/4 (1 mark). Wholes 5 − 2 = 3 and quarters 5/4 − 3/4 = 2/4 = 1/2 (1 mark). Answer 3 1/2 kg (1 mark).
10. Explain how you can use a beam balance to find the mass of a bag of sugar. (3 marks)
   Answer: Put the sugar in one pan. Add known masses to the other pan until the beam is level. Add up the masses to find the mass of the sugar.
   Why: 1 mark for putting the sugar in one pan, 1 mark for adding known masses until level, 1 mark for adding the masses.
