## PAGE 1: Tenths and the decimal point
Sometimes a whole is cut into 10 equal parts. Each part is one tenth. We can write one tenth as a fraction, 1/10, or as a decimal, 0.1. The dot in a decimal is called the decimal point. It separates the whole-number part from the part that is smaller than one.

Think of a metre ruler split into 10 equal parts, or a litre of milk poured into 10 equal cups. Each cup holds 1/10 of a litre, or 0.1 of a litre. Three cups hold 3/10 = 0.3 of a litre.

### Reading tenths
• 0.1 is read zero point one, or one tenth.
• 0.7 is read zero point seven, or seven tenths.
• 1.0 is one whole, which is ten tenths.

### Writing tenths as decimals
Write the whole-number digit before the point and the tenths digit after the point. Two and four tenths is 2.4. Five tenths is 0.5. The zero before the point shows there are no whole ones.

## PAGE 2: Hundredths
When a whole is cut into 100 equal parts, each part is one hundredth. We write one hundredth as 1/100 or 0.01. A hundredth is ten times smaller than a tenth. It takes 10 hundredths to make 1 tenth, and 100 hundredths to make one whole.

### Reading hundredths
• 0.05 is five hundredths.
• 0.25 is twenty-five hundredths.
• 0.99 is ninety-nine hundredths.
• 1.00 is one whole, or one hundred hundredths.

### Money helps
One shilling is 100 cents. So 1 cent is one hundredth of a shilling. Ksh 0.25 means 25 cents. Ksh 3.50 means 3 shillings and 50 cents.

### Example
A ribbon is 1 metre long, or 100 centimetres. 1 centimetre is 1/100 of a metre, which is 0.01 m. So 27 cm is 27/100 = 0.27 m.

### Check yourself
If 7 squares of a hundred square are shaded, the shaded part is 7/100 = 0.07.

## PAGE 3: Decimal notation and place value
The decimal point splits a number in two. Digits to the left are whole numbers: ones, tens, hundreds. Digits to the right are parts of one: tenths, then hundredths. Each place is ten times smaller than the place on its left.

### Place value and total value
In 5.68, the digit 6 is in the tenths place, so its total value is 6 tenths, or 0.6. The digit 8 is in the hundredths place, so its total value is 8 hundredths, or 0.08.

### The zero in decimals
In 4.05 there are no tenths, so we write 0 in the tenths place. 4.05 is very different from 4.5. Also, 0.5 and 0.50 mean the same amount, because 5 tenths equals 50 hundredths.

### Try it
What is the place value of 7 in 9.27? It is in the hundredths place, so its value is 7/100 = 0.07.

## PAGE 4: Changing fractions to decimals and back
A fraction with 10 or 100 as the denominator can be written directly as a decimal.

### Tenths
Write the numerator after the decimal point. 7/10 = 0.7. 3/10 = 0.3. 9/10 = 0.9.

### Hundredths
Two digits must come after the point. 45/100 = 0.45. 6/100 = 0.06 (write a zero in the tenths place). 90/100 = 0.90 = 0.9.

### Mixed numbers
The whole number goes before the point. 3 1/10 = 3.1. 2 25/100 = 2.25. 5 7/100 = 5.07.

### Changing a decimal back to a fraction
Look at the last digit's place. 0.8 has its last digit in the tenths place, so it is 8/10. 0.37 has its last digit in the hundredths place, so it is 37/100.

### Handy facts
• 1/2 is the same as 5/10, so 1/2 = 0.5.
• 1/4 is the same as 25/100, so 1/4 = 0.25.
• 3/4 is the same as 75/100, so 3/4 = 0.75.

### Example
A jug holds 0.5 litre. That is half a litre. A bottle holds 0.25 litre, which is a quarter of a litre.

## PAGE 5: Ordering decimals
To compare decimals, start at the left. First compare the whole numbers. If they are the same, compare the tenths. If those are the same, compare the hundredths. It helps to give each number the same number of decimal places by adding zeros at the end.

Which is greater, 0.5 or 0.45?
Write 0.5 as 0.50. Compare 0.50 and 0.45 by tenths: 5 tenths is more than 4 tenths. So 0.5 is greater than 0.45.

Notice that 0.45 has more digits, but that does not make it bigger.

### Ordering a list
Arrange 0.7, 0.07, 0.75 and 0.57 from the largest to the smallest.
Step 1: Write every number with two decimal places: 0.70, 0.07, 0.75, 0.57.
Step 2: Compare tenths: 0.75 and 0.70 have 7 tenths, 0.57 has 5 tenths, 0.07 has 0 tenths.
Step 3: Of 0.75 and 0.70, the hundredths 5 is more than 0, so 0.75 is larger.
Descending order: 0.75, 0.7, 0.57, 0.07.

## PAGE 6: Decimals in everyday life
We use decimals every day, often without noticing. Shops use them for prices, tailors and builders use them for lengths, and traders use them for the mass of goods.

### Money
A price of Ksh 12.50 has 12 whole shillings and 0.50, or 50 cents. Ksh 12.05 is 12 shillings and 5 cents, which is less than Ksh 12.50. Order these prices from cheapest to most expensive: Ksh 8.25, Ksh 8.05, Ksh 8.50, Ksh 7.99. The order is Ksh 7.99, Ksh 8.05, Ksh 8.25, Ksh 8.50.

### Length
A stick measures 1.5 metres. That is 1 metre and 5 tenths of a metre, which is 1 m and 50 cm. A rope of 0.75 m is 75 cm long.

### Mass
A packet of sugar may weigh 0.5 kg. That is half a kilogram.

### Comparing
Two runners finish a race in 12.4 and 12.35 seconds. Write 12.4 as 12.40. Compare 12.40 and 12.35: the tenths are 4 and 3, so 12.35 is smaller. The runner who took 12.35 seconds was faster.

### Remember
• The digit in the first place after the point is tenths.
• The digit in the second place is hundredths.
• Adding zeros at the end of a decimal does not change its value: 0.7 = 0.70.
• A longer decimal is not always a bigger decimal.

## QUIZ
1. What is 3/10 written as a decimal?
   A) 0.03
   B) 0.3
   C) 3.0
   D) 30
   Answer: B
   Why: Three tenths is written with the 3 in the tenths place, giving 0.3.
2. In 5.68, what is the place value of the digit 6?
   A) Ones
   B) Tens
   C) Tenths
   D) Hundredths
   Answer: C
   Why: The first digit after the decimal point is in the tenths place, so the 6 means 6 tenths.
3. Which decimal is the same as 45/100?
   A) 4.5
   B) 0.45
   C) 0.045
   D) 45.100
   Answer: B
   Why: Hundredths need two digits after the point, so 45/100 = 0.45.
4. Which list shows 0.7, 0.07, 0.75 and 0.57 in order from smallest to largest?
   A) 0.07, 0.57, 0.7, 0.75
   B) 0.07, 0.7, 0.57, 0.75
   C) 0.75, 0.7, 0.57, 0.07
   D) 0.57, 0.07, 0.7, 0.75
   Answer: A
   Why: As hundredths the numbers are 0.70, 0.07, 0.75 and 0.57. In order they are 0.07, 0.57, 0.70 and 0.75.
5. Which is the biggest number?
   A) 0.9
   B) 0.09
   C) 0.85
   D) 0.099
   Answer: A
   Why: Write them with the same places: 0.90, 0.09, 0.85 and 0.099. The tenths digit decides first, and 9 tenths is the most.
6. Write 2 25/100 and 5 7/100 as decimals. (2 marks)
   Answer: 2.25 and 5.07
   Why: 1 mark for 2.25. In 5 7/100 the hundredths digit is 7 and the tenths digit is 0, so 5.07 earns 1 mark.
7. A hundred square has 60 of its small squares shaded. Write the shaded part as a fraction and as a decimal. (2 marks)
   Answer: 60/100 and 0.60 (or 0.6)
   Why: 1 mark for the fraction 60/100 and 1 mark for the decimal 0.6 or 0.60.
8. Arrange these prices from the cheapest to the most expensive: Ksh 8.25, Ksh 8.05, Ksh 8.50, Ksh 7.99. (3 marks)
   Answer: Ksh 7.99, Ksh 8.05, Ksh 8.25, Ksh 8.50
   Why: Compare the whole shillings first, so 7.99 is smallest (1 mark). Then compare the tenths: 8.05 has 0, 8.25 has 2, 8.50 has 5 (2 marks for the correct order of the last three).
9. Write the decimal 13.42 in expanded form and give the total value of the digit 4. (3 marks)
   Answer: 10 + 3 + 0.4 + 0.02. The total value of 4 is 0.4 (4 tenths).
   Why: 1 mark for 10 + 3, 1 mark for 0.4 + 0.02, 1 mark for the total value of 4 as 0.4.
10. Explain why 0.5 is greater than 0.45 even though 0.45 has more digits. (3 marks)
   Answer: 0.5 can be written as 0.50. Comparing tenths, 5 tenths is more than 4 tenths, so 0.50 is bigger than 0.45. The number of digits does not decide the size.
   Why: 1 mark for writing 0.5 as 0.50, 1 mark for comparing the tenths digits 5 and 4, 1 mark for the conclusion that 0.5 is greater.
