## PAGE 1: Space taken up by objects
Everything around us takes up space. A stone takes up space on the ground. A box of soap takes up space on a shop shelf. A brick takes up space in a wall. The amount of space that a solid object takes up is called its volume.

Volume is different from length and area. Length measures along a line. Area measures a flat surface. Volume measures how much room a solid object fills, so it depends on length, width and height all together.

### Comparing volume by piling
The easiest way to compare the volume of two objects is to build them from the same small blocks. If a wooden block is used as a unit, the object that needs more blocks has the greater volume.

A cube is a block with 6 equal square faces. Every side of a cube is the same length. A cuboid is a block with 6 rectangular faces. A matchbox, a brick and a book are all cuboids.

### Using local materials
Use small identical objects, like wooden blocks, bottle tops, dice or small boxes. Stack them neatly to form cubes and cuboids. Always stack carefully, so that they do not fall on your feet.

## PAGE 2: Piling blocks into a cuboid
To make a cuboid, we stack blocks in layers. First we make one flat layer. Each layer has rows and columns of blocks, like tiles on a floor. Then we put more layers on top.

Take a layer of blocks that is 4 blocks long and 3 blocks wide. The number of blocks in the layer is 4 × 3 = 12, just as when we found area by rows and columns.

Now put a second layer on top of the first layer. The second layer also has 12 blocks. The total number of blocks is 12 + 12 = 24.

So the volume of this cuboid is 24 blocks, or 24 cubic units.

### Counting layers
1 layer has 12 blocks.
2 layers have 12 × 2 = 24 blocks.
3 layers would have 12 × 3 = 36 blocks.

### Key idea
The number of blocks in a cuboid is the number of blocks in one layer multiplied by the number of layers.

## PAGE 3: Working out the volume of a cuboid
Follow these steps to find the volume of a cuboid built from unit blocks.

Step 1: Count the blocks in the length of one layer.
Step 2: Count the blocks in the width of one layer.
Step 3: Multiply length by width to get the blocks in one layer.
Step 4: Count how many layers there are (the height).
Step 5: Multiply the blocks in one layer by the number of layers.

### Worked example
A cuboid is 6 blocks long, 4 blocks wide and 3 blocks high.
Step 1: Blocks in one layer = 6 × 4 = 24.
Step 2: Number of layers = 3.
Step 3: Volume = 24 × 3 = 72 blocks.
The volume is 72 cubic units.

### Cubic centimetres
If each little block is a cube with sides of 1 cm, its volume is one cubic centimetre, written 1 cm³. A cuboid that uses 72 such blocks has a volume of 72 cubic centimetres.

### Try it
A cuboid is 5 blocks long, 2 blocks wide and 3 blocks high. One layer has 5 × 2 = 10 blocks. Three layers give 10 × 3 = 30 blocks.

## PAGE 4: Volume of a cube
A cube is a special cuboid where the length, width and height are all the same. If a cube is made of blocks, it has the same number of blocks in each direction.

A cube that is 3 blocks long, 3 blocks wide and 3 blocks high:
Step 1: One layer has 3 × 3 = 9 blocks.
Step 2: There are 3 layers.
Step 3: Volume = 9 × 3 = 27 blocks.

### More cubes
• 2 blocks each way: 2 × 2 × 2 = 8 blocks.
• 4 blocks each way: one layer has 4 × 4 = 16 blocks and there are 4 layers, so 16 × 4 = 64 blocks.
• 5 blocks each way: 5 × 5 = 25, and 25 × 5 = 125 blocks.

### Cube or cuboid?
If the length, width and height are all equal, the shape is a cube. If they are not all equal, it is a cuboid. A cube is always a cuboid, but a cuboid is not always a cube.

### A real cube
A dice is a cube. A box that is 10 cm long, 10 cm wide and 10 cm high has a volume of 1,000 cubic centimetres.

## PAGE 5: Volume problems from real life
Shopkeepers, farmers and builders stack goods in cuboids. Working out the volume tells them how much will fit.

### Problem 1: Stacking crates
A store keeps crates of soda in a stack. Each layer has 5 crates along the wall and 4 crates deep. The stack is 4 layers high. How many crates are in the stack?
Step 1: One layer = 5 × 4 = 20 crates.
Step 2: Four layers = 20 × 4 = 80 crates.
Answer: 80 crates.

### Problem 2: Finding the number of layers
A cuboid has a volume of 60 blocks. One layer has 12 blocks. How many layers are there?
Since layers × 12 = 60, layers = 60 ÷ 12 = 5. There are 5 layers.

### Problem 3: Which is bigger?
Cuboid A is 4 blocks long, 3 wide and 2 high. Cuboid B is 3 long, 3 wide and 3 high.
Volume of A = 4 × 3 × 2 = 24 blocks. Volume of B = 3 × 3 × 3 = 27 blocks.
B has the greater volume, by 3 blocks.

### Packing safely
When you stack real boxes, put the heavy boxes at the bottom. Do not stack them so high that they may fall.

## PAGE 6: Summary and common mistakes
### What we have learned
• Volume is the amount of space that a solid object takes up.
• We can measure volume by piling blocks of equal size.
• The blocks in a cuboid = blocks in one layer × number of layers.
• Blocks in one layer = length × width.
• A cube is a cuboid whose length, width and height are equal.
• Volume is measured in cubic units, for example cubic centimetres (cm³).

### Worked example: comparing two boxes
Box P is 5 blocks long, 2 wide and 4 high. Box Q is 4 long, 4 wide and 2 high.
Volume of P = 5 × 2 × 4. First 5 × 2 = 10, and 10 × 4 = 40 blocks.
Volume of Q = 4 × 4 × 2. First 4 × 4 = 16, and 16 × 2 = 32 blocks.
P has the greater volume, by 40 − 32 = 8 blocks.

### Common mistakes
• Adding the length, width and height instead of multiplying. For 4, 3 and 2 the sum is 9 but the volume is 24.
• Confusing volume with area. Area covers a flat surface, so it has two directions. Volume fills space, so it has three.
• Forgetting the unit. Write cubic units, blocks or cm³.
• Counting only the blocks you can see in a picture. Some blocks are hidden inside or behind.

### Project idea
Use small cubes or dice to build three different cuboids. Draw each cuboid, count the number of blocks in a layer, count the layers and work out the volume. Then check by counting all the blocks one by one.

## QUIZ
1. What does volume measure?
   A) The space a solid object takes up
   B) The distance round a shape
   C) The flat surface a shape covers
   D) The time an event takes
   Answer: A
   Why: Volume is the amount of space that a solid object fills.
2. One layer of a cuboid has 12 blocks and there are 2 layers. What is the volume?
   A) 14 blocks
   B) 24 blocks
   C) 6 blocks
   D) 48 blocks
   Answer: B
   Why: Volume = blocks in one layer × layers = 12 × 2 = 24 blocks.
3. A cuboid is 6 blocks long, 4 blocks wide and 3 blocks high. What is its volume?
   A) 13 blocks
   B) 24 blocks
   C) 72 blocks
   D) 144 blocks
   Answer: C
   Why: One layer = 6 × 4 = 24 blocks. Three layers = 24 × 3 = 72 blocks.
4. What is the volume of a cube that is 3 blocks long, 3 wide and 3 high?
   A) 9 blocks
   B) 12 blocks
   C) 27 blocks
   D) 81 blocks
   Answer: C
   Why: One layer = 3 × 3 = 9 blocks. Three layers = 9 × 3 = 27 blocks.
5. Which statement about a cube is true?
   A) Its length, width and height are equal
   B) It has only 4 faces
   C) It has no corners
   D) Its length is always double its width
   Answer: A
   Why: A cube has 6 equal square faces, so its length, width and height are the same.
6. A stack of soda crates has 5 crates along the wall, 4 crates deep and 4 layers high. How many crates are in the stack? (3 marks)
   Answer: 80 crates
   Why: One layer = 5 × 4 = 20 crates (1 mark). Four layers = 20 × 4 (1 mark) = 80 crates (1 mark).
7. A cuboid has a volume of 60 blocks. One layer has 12 blocks. How many layers does it have? (2 marks)
   Answer: 5 layers
   Why: 60 ÷ 12 = 5. 1 mark for the division and 1 mark for the answer.
8. Box P is 5 blocks long, 2 wide and 4 high. Box Q is 4 blocks long, 4 wide and 2 high. Which has the greater volume and by how many blocks? (4 marks)
   Answer: Box P, by 8 blocks
   Why: P = 5 × 2 × 4 = 40 blocks (1 mark). Q = 4 × 4 × 2 = 32 blocks (1 mark). P is greater (1 mark). 40 − 32 = 8 blocks (1 mark).
9. Explain the difference between area and volume. (2 marks)
   Answer: Area is the amount of flat surface a shape covers, measured in square units. Volume is the amount of space a solid takes up, measured in cubic units.
   Why: 1 mark for area as a flat surface and 1 mark for volume as space filled by a solid.
10. A learner says the volume of a cuboid 4 blocks long, 3 wide and 2 high is 9 blocks because 4 + 3 + 2 = 9. What mistake did the learner make and what is the correct volume? (3 marks)
   Answer: The learner added the lengths. The lengths must be multiplied. Volume = 4 × 3 × 2 = 24 blocks.
   Why: 1 mark for saying the learner added, 1 mark for saying to multiply (layer of 12 blocks × 2 layers), 1 mark for 24 blocks.
