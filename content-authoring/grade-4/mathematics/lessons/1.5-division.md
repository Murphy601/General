## PAGE 1: What division means
Division is sharing equally or making equal groups. Suppose 12 oranges are shared equally among 3 children. Each child gets 4 oranges. We write this as 12 ÷ 3 = 4. The number being divided (12) is called the dividend. The number we divide by (3) is the divisor. The answer (4) is the quotient.

There are two ways to think about division.
• Sharing: 12 shared among 3 people gives 4 each.
• Grouping: 12 put into groups of 3 makes 4 groups. So 12 ÷ 3 = 4 either way.

### Division by repeated subtraction
Start with 12 and keep taking away 3 until nothing is left: 12 − 3 = 9, 9 − 3 = 6, 6 − 3 = 3, 3 − 3 = 0. We took away 3 four times, so 12 ÷ 3 = 4.

### Using times tables
Division uses your times tables backwards. To find 42 ÷ 6, ask: 6 times what number makes 42? Since 6 × 7 = 42, the answer is 7.

### Try it
20 ÷ 5 = 4 because 5 × 4 = 20. 36 ÷ 9 = 4 because 9 × 4 = 36.

## PAGE 2: Dividing a two-digit number by a one-digit number
To divide a two-digit number, split it into tens and ones. Divide the tens first, then the ones.

Work out 96 ÷ 3.
Step 1: Divide the tens. 9 tens ÷ 3 = 3 tens. Write 3 in the tens place of the answer.
Step 2: Divide the ones. 6 ones ÷ 3 = 2 ones. Write 2 in the ones place.
The answer is 32.

### When a tens digit is too small
Work out 72 ÷ 6. The tens digit 7 can share into 6 once, so write 1 in the tens place. That uses 6 tens, leaving 1 ten. Change the 1 ten into 10 ones and add the 2 ones to make 12 ones. 12 ÷ 6 = 2. Answer: 12.

### Checking with multiplication
Multiply the answer by the divisor. 32 × 3 = 96, and 12 × 6 = 72. Both give the number we started with, so the answers are right.

### Example
A farmer packs 84 mangoes equally into 4 baskets. 80 ÷ 4 = 20 and 4 ÷ 4 = 1. So each basket holds 21 mangoes.

Three full groups of 5 use 15 counters. That leaves 2. We write 17 ÷ 5 = 3 remainder 2, or 3 R 2.

### Two-digit example
Work out 58 ÷ 4.
Step 1: Tens: 5 tens ÷ 4 = 1 ten, with 1 ten left over. Write 1 in the tens place.
Step 2: Change the left-over ten into 10 ones and add the 8 ones. That makes 18 ones.
Step 3: 18 ÷ 4 = 4, with 2 left over. Write 4 in the ones place.
Answer: 14 remainder 2 (14 R 2).

### Check a division with a remainder
Multiply the quotient by the divisor and add the remainder. 14 × 4 = 56, and 56 + 2 = 58. This is the number we started with.

### Example
47 ÷ 5: 5 × 9 = 45 and 47 − 45 = 2. So 47 ÷ 5 = 9 R 2.

## PAGE 4: Dividing by 100
Dividing by 100 is the opposite of multiplying by 100. When we divide a number that ends in two zeros by 100, we simply remove the two zeros. Each digit moves two places to the right.

• 300 ÷ 100 = 3
• 700 ÷ 100 = 7
• 900 ÷ 100 = 9
• 100 ÷ 100 = 1

### Numbers that are not exact hundreds
If the number is 450, we ask how many whole hundreds are in it. There are 4 hundreds, and 50 is left over. So 450 ÷ 100 = 4 remainder 50. Check: 4 × 100 = 400 and 400 + 50 = 450.

### Why does it work?
100 is a hundred ones. Sharing 300 shillings into groups of 100 shillings gives 3 groups. Think of three Ksh 100 notes.

### Real-life example
A trader has 800 eggs. Each crate holds 100 eggs. Number of crates needed: 800 ÷ 100 = 8 crates.

### Common mistake
Do not remove only one zero. Dividing by 10 removes one zero. Dividing by 100 removes two zeros.

From this one picture we get four facts, called a fact family:
• 4 × 6 = 24
• 6 × 4 = 24
• 24 ÷ 4 = 6
• 24 ÷ 6 = 4

### Using the partner fact
To find 56 ÷ 8, think of 8 × ? = 56. Since 8 × 7 = 56, we know 56 ÷ 8 = 7. To check any division, multiply the answer by the divisor.

### Missing numbers
If ? × 9 = 63, then ? = 63 ÷ 9 = 7. If 45 ÷ ? = 5, then ? = 45 ÷ 5 = 9.

### Example
Wekesa knows that 12 × 5 = 60. So 60 ÷ 5 = 12 and 60 ÷ 12 = 5. He shares 60 shillings equally among 5 friends. Each friend gets 12 shillings.

### Practise the fact families
Choose three numbers from the 6 times table and write all four facts. For example: 6 × 8 = 48, 8 × 6 = 48, 48 ÷ 6 = 8, 48 ÷ 8 = 6.

## PAGE 6: Solving division problems
In word problems, read carefully to see whether the remainder matters. The answer depends on the story.

### Problem 1: Sharing
38 mangoes are shared equally among 5 children. How many does each child get and how many are left?
38 ÷ 5: 5 × 7 = 35 and 38 − 35 = 3. Each child gets 7 mangoes and 3 mangoes are left.

### Problem 2: Packing
45 eggs are packed in trays that each hold 6 eggs. How many trays are needed to hold all the eggs?
45 ÷ 6 = 7 remainder 3. Seven trays are full, and 3 eggs remain. The 3 eggs still need a tray, so we need 8 trays.

### Problem 3: Exact answer
A rope 96 metres long is cut into pieces of 8 metres. 96 ÷ 8 = 12 pieces. There is no remainder.

### What does the remainder mean?
• If the leftover items are just left out (like the 3 mangoes), the remainder is the answer to "how many are left?"
• If every item must be stored or carried (like the eggs), you may need one more container.

### Checklist for a division problem
1. Find what is being shared or grouped.
2. Divide. Note any remainder.
3. Check by multiplying and adding the remainder.
4. Decide what the remainder means, then write the answer with units.

## QUIZ
1. What is 96 ÷ 3?
   A) 23
   B) 32
   C) 33
   D) 92
   Answer: B
   Why: 9 tens ÷ 3 = 3 tens and 6 ones ÷ 3 = 2 ones, so the answer is 32. Check: 32 × 3 = 96.
2. What is 47 ÷ 5?
   A) 8 remainder 7
   B) 9 remainder 2
   C) 9 remainder 5
   D) 10 remainder 2
   Answer: B
   Why: 5 × 9 = 45 and 47 − 45 = 2, so 47 ÷ 5 = 9 R 2.
3. What is 700 ÷ 100?
   A) 7
   B) 70
   C) 700
   D) 7,000
   Answer: A
   Why: Dividing by 100 removes two zeros, so 700 ÷ 100 = 7.
4. Which fact belongs to the same family as 6 × 7 = 42?
   A) 42 ÷ 6 = 7
   B) 42 ÷ 7 = 7
   C) 42 ÷ 42 = 6
   D) 7 ÷ 6 = 42
   Answer: A
   Why: Division undoes multiplication. Since 6 × 7 = 42, both 42 ÷ 6 = 7 and 42 ÷ 7 = 6 are true.
5. A remainder must always be
   A) bigger than the divisor
   B) equal to the divisor
   C) smaller than the divisor
   D) equal to the quotient
   Answer: C
   Why: If the remainder were as big as the divisor, another whole group could still be made.
6. Divide 84 by 4 and check your answer. (3 marks)
   Answer: 21
   Why: 80 ÷ 4 = 20 (1 mark); 4 ÷ 4 = 1 so 20 + 1 = 21 (1 mark); check 21 × 4 = 84 (1 mark).
7. Divide 58 by 4 and give the remainder. (3 marks)
   Answer: 14 remainder 2
   Why: 5 tens ÷ 4 = 1 ten remainder 1 (1 mark); 18 ones ÷ 4 = 4 remainder 2 (1 mark); answer 14 R 2 (1 mark). Check: 14 × 4 + 2 = 58.
8. 45 eggs are packed in trays that hold 6 eggs each. How many trays are needed to pack all the eggs? Explain. (3 marks)
   Answer: 8 trays
   Why: 45 ÷ 6 = 7 remainder 3 (1 mark). Seven trays are full and 3 eggs are left (1 mark). The 3 left-over eggs need another tray, so 8 trays are needed (1 mark).
9. Write the four facts that belong to the array of 3 rows with 8 dots in each row. (4 marks)
   Answer: 3 × 8 = 24, 8 × 3 = 24, 24 ÷ 3 = 8, 24 ÷ 8 = 3
   Why: 1 mark for each correct fact.
10. A trader has 800 eggs. Each crate holds 100 eggs. How many crates are needed? (2 marks)
   Answer: 8 crates
   Why: 800 ÷ 100 = 8. 1 mark for the division, 1 mark for the answer with units.
