## PAGE 1: Recognising plane figures
A plane figure is a flat shape. It can be drawn on paper, and it has length and width but no thickness. The shapes we learn in this grade are the rectangle, the square, the triangle, the circle and the oval.

### Finding shapes in the environment
Look around you and you will find these shapes everywhere. Think of the face or top of an object, not the whole solid.
• Rectangle: the door, the cover of an exercise book, a blackboard, a matatu window.
• Square: a floor tile, a handkerchief, a window pane in some houses.
• Triangle: the roof end of a house, a slice of a triangular sandwich, a road sign, a samosa seen from the top.
• Circle: a wheel, a coin, a plate, a cup seen from the top, the sun.
• Oval: an egg seen from the side, a rugby ball seen from the side, a leaf of some plants.

### How to tell them apart
Ask two questions. Are the edges straight or curved? If straight, how many sides are there? Three straight sides make a triangle. Four straight sides make a rectangle or a square. A curved edge with no corners makes a circle or an oval.

### Try it
Find one example of each shape in your classroom. Say the shape and where you saw it.

## PAGE 2: Drawing plane figures
We can draw each shape from objects around us. To draw a shape, trace round the object, or use a ruler for straight sides.

### Tracing
Put a coin, a plate or the bottom of a cup on a page. Hold it still and trace all the way round its edge with a pencil. This gives a circle. To draw a triangle, trace a set square or a cardboard triangle. To draw a rectangle, trace round the cover of a small book.

### Drawing with a ruler
Let us draw a rectangle 6 cm long and 3 cm wide.
Step 1: Draw a straight line 6 cm long with a ruler.
Step 2: At each end, use the corner of a paper to draw a line 3 cm long straight up at a right angle.
Step 3: Join the top ends with a straight line. It should also be 6 cm long.
Step 4: Label the sides 6 cm and 3 cm.

### Drawing a square
A square has four equal sides. To draw a square of side 4 cm, draw a line 4 cm, make right angles at both ends, mark 4 cm on each new line, and join the ends.

### Drawing a circle and an oval
Trace a round object to draw a circle. For an oval, draw a curved shape like an egg, longer in one direction than the other, with no corners.

## PAGE 3: Lines of symmetry
Some shapes can be folded so that one half fits exactly on top of the other half. The fold line is called a line of symmetry. The two halves are mirror images of each other.

Cut a rectangle from paper. Fold it down the middle from top to bottom. The two halves match exactly. The fold is a line of symmetry. Now unfold and fold it from side to side. The halves match again, so this is a second line of symmetry.

### How many lines of symmetry?
• A rectangle (not a square) has 2 lines of symmetry.
• A square has 4 lines of symmetry.
• A triangle with three equal sides has 3 lines of symmetry.
• A triangle with two equal sides has 1 line of symmetry.
• A triangle with no equal sides has no line of symmetry.
• A circle has many lines of symmetry. Any fold through the centre works.
• An oval has 2 lines of symmetry.

### A fold test
Fold a shape. If the two halves match exactly, the fold is a line of symmetry. If one part sticks out, it is not.

The triangle with two equal sides has one line of symmetry, from the top corner to the middle of the base. The triangle with three different sides has none, because no fold makes the halves match. The oval has two: one along its length and one across its width.

### Common mistake: the diagonal of a rectangle
The diagonal of a rectangle that is not a square is not a line of symmetry. If you fold a paper rectangle along its diagonal, the corners will not match. Try it and see. In a square, both diagonals are lines of symmetry.

### Symmetry in nature
A butterfly's wings, a leaf and a face are almost symmetrical. Fold a picture of a leaf and see whether the two halves match.

### Letters and symmetry
Some letters have a line of symmetry: A, M, T and H. The letter F has none. Test each with a fold or a mirror.

## PAGE 5: Properties of squares, rectangles and triangles
The properties of a shape tell us what is true about its sides and corners. Knowing properties helps us to describe shapes and to tell them apart.

### Square
• 4 straight sides, all equal in length.
• 4 corners, all right angles.
• 4 lines of symmetry.

### Rectangle
• 4 straight sides. Opposite sides are equal in length.
• 4 corners, all right angles.
• 2 lines of symmetry.

### Square or rectangle?
A square is a special rectangle with all four sides equal. If a shape has four right angles and its sides are equal in pairs but not all equal, it is a rectangle that is not a square.

### Making your own
Choose one or two shapes. Decide how they repeat: one after the other, two of one and one of the other, or one in each colour. Cut shapes from old newspapers or magazines and paste them in a row on a strip of paper. Use recycled materials whenever you can.

### Summary
• A plane figure is a flat shape.
• A line of symmetry divides a shape into two matching halves.
• A square has 4 equal sides and 4 right angles. A rectangle has opposite sides equal and 4 right angles. A triangle has 3 sides and 3 corners.
• A pattern is made by repeating a shape or a group of shapes in a regular way.

## QUIZ
1. Which shape has three straight sides?
   A) Rectangle
   B) Triangle
   C) Circle
   D) Square
   Answer: B
   Why: A triangle has three straight sides and three corners.
2. How many lines of symmetry does a square have?
   A) 1
   B) 2
   C) 3
   D) 4
   Answer: D
   Why: A square can be folded down the middle both ways and along its two diagonals, giving 4 lines of symmetry.
3. How many lines of symmetry does a rectangle that is not a square have?
   A) 0
   B) 2
   C) 4
   D) 6
   Answer: B
   Why: It can be folded along the line joining the middles of the long sides and along the line joining the middles of the short sides. The diagonals do not work.
4. Which shape has no corners?
   A) Triangle
   B) Rectangle
   C) Square
   D) Circle
   Answer: D
   Why: A circle has one curved edge and no corners.
5. Which statement about a rectangle is true?
   A) It has three sides
   B) Opposite sides are equal and all corners are right angles
   C) All sides are always equal
   D) It has a curved edge
   Answer: B
   Why: A rectangle has four sides, opposite sides equal in length and four right angles. Only a square has all sides equal.
6. Draw a rectangle 6 cm long and 3 cm wide. Label its sides. (3 marks)
   Answer: A rectangle with two sides 6 cm and two sides 3 cm, with square corners.
   Why: 1 mark for four straight sides with square corners, 1 mark for the sides drawn 6 cm and 3 cm correctly, 1 mark for the labels.
7. Name the plane figure of each object: a plate, a floor tile, a door. (3 marks)
   Answer: Plate: circle. Floor tile: square. Door: rectangle.
   Why: 1 mark for each correct shape.
8. A triangle has two equal sides. How many lines of symmetry does it have? Explain how to check. (3 marks)
   Answer: One line of symmetry. Fold it from the top corner to the middle of the base; the two halves match exactly.
   Why: 1 mark for one, 1 mark for the fold from the top corner to the base, 1 mark for saying the halves match.
9. List three properties of a square. (3 marks)
   Answer: It has four equal sides, four right angles and four lines of symmetry.
   Why: 1 mark for each correct property.
10. Describe a pattern of shapes that you can make with squares and triangles, and write down the first six shapes. (3 marks)
   Answer: For example: square, triangle, square, triangle, square, triangle. The pattern repeats a square and a triangle in turn.
   Why: 1 mark for using both shapes, 1 mark for a clear repeating rule, 1 mark for six correct shapes following the rule.
