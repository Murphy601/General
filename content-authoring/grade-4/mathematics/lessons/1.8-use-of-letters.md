## PAGE 1: Letters can stand for things
Mathematicians often use letters instead of long words or unknown numbers. A letter is a short way to write something down. When you write a shopping list quickly, you may write m for maize flour or s for sugar. In mathematics, we do the same.

Kenya has many kinds of produce from different places. Farmers in Kericho grow tea, farmers in Nyeri grow coffee, fishermen at Lake Victoria catch fish, and farmers in many counties grow maize. We can use a letter for each item:
• t for a kilogram of tea
• c for a kilogram of coffee
• f for a fish
• m for a bag of maize

Suppose a trader has 3 bags of maize and 2 fish. We can write this as 3m + 2f. The letter tells us the item. The number in front tells us how many.

### Rules of writing
• 3m means 3 × m, which is 3 bags of maize. We do not write the multiplication sign.
• If there is only one item, we write the letter alone: m, not 1m.
• m + m is 2m: two bags of maize.

### Try it
Choose letters for these items: mango, orange and banana. Then write 4 mangoes, 3 oranges and 1 banana using letters. If you chose a for mango, o for orange and b for banana, you would write 4a + 3o + b.

## PAGE 2: Letters stand for unknown numbers
Sometimes we do not know a number yet. We can use a letter to hold its place until we find it. The letter is called an unknown or a variable.

Mercy has some sweets in a bag, but she does not know how many. Let the number of sweets in the bag be n. Then she buys 5 more sweets.
The total number of sweets is n + 5.

n + 5 is called an expression. It has an unknown and a number joined by an operation. It does not have an equals sign.

### Working out an expression when the letter is known
If we find that n is 4, we replace n by 4. So n + 5 = 4 + 5 = 9. If n is 10, then n + 5 = 15.

### Another example
A packet of biscuits costs Ksh p. Two packets cost 2 × p = 2p shillings. If p = 30, then 2p = 2 × 30 = 60 shillings.

### Letters can be any letter
It does not matter which letter you use. The expression x + 5 works the same as n + 5. What matters is that the same letter stands for the same thing in one problem.

## PAGE 3: Forming expressions from words
To form an expression, look for the key words. They tell us which operation to use.

### Key words
• more than, plus, added to, sum, total: use +
• less than, minus, taken from, fewer than, remains: use −
• times, groups of, multiplied by: use × (write 3a for 3 × a)
• shared equally, divided by: use ÷

### Examples
• 5 more than x is x + 5.
• 6 less than k is k − 6.
• 3 times a number a is 3a.
• A number y shared equally among 4 people is y ÷ 4.
• The cost of 6 books at b shillings each is 6b.

### Worked example
A matatu carries m passengers. At the next stop 8 passengers get in and 3 get out. Form an expression for the number of passengers now.
Step 1: Start with m.
Step 2: 8 get in: m + 8.
Step 3: 3 get out: m + 8 − 3.
Step 4: Combine the numbers: 8 − 3 = 5. The expression is m + 5.

### Try it
A farmer has g goats. She buys 7 more and then sells 2. The expression is g + 7 − 2 = g + 5. If she started with 10 goats, she now has 10 + 5 = 15 goats.

## PAGE 4: Simplifying expressions with one letter
To simplify means to write an expression in a shorter, neater way. We can add or subtract things that are the same kind. These are called like terms. For example, 3a and 2a are like terms because both are about the letter a.

3a means 3 boxes each holding a. 2a means 2 more boxes each holding a. Together there are 5 boxes each holding a.

### Subtracting like terms
7x − 4x = 3x. Seven boxes of x take away four boxes of x leaves three boxes of x.

### Adding numbers and letters
In 2a + 5 + 3a + 1 we group the like terms. The a terms: 2a + 3a = 5a. The numbers: 5 + 1 = 6. The simplified expression is 5a + 6.

### Unlike terms cannot be joined
We cannot add a and 5 to make 5a or 6, because one is a letter and one is a plain number. So a + 5 stays as a + 5.

### Example
A trader has 4b + 3b bags of beans. Simplify: 4b + 3b = 7b. If each bag b holds 5 kg, the total is 7 × 5 = 35 kg.

## PAGE 5: Simplifying expressions with two unknowns
Real-life problems often have more than one unknown. Only like terms are combined. Terms with different letters stay separate.

Mama Kioko sells mangoes in bags (m) and oranges in bags (o). On Monday she sells 3 bags of mangoes and 2 bags of oranges. On Tuesday she sells 1 bag of mangoes and 4 bags of oranges. How many bags of each does she sell altogether?

Step 1: Write the expression: 3m + 2o + m + 4o.
Step 2: Group the m terms: 3m + m = 4m.
Step 3: Group the o terms: 2o + 4o = 6o.
Step 4: Write the simplified expression: 4m + 6o.
She sold 4 bags of mangoes and 6 bags of oranges.

### Another example
Simplify 5x + 3y − 2x + y.
The x terms: 5x − 2x = 3x. The y terms: 3y + y = 4y. The answer is 3x + 4y.

### Do not join different letters
4m + 6o is not 10mo and not 10m. It stays as 4m + 6o, because a bag of mangoes is not the same as a bag of oranges.

## PAGE 6: Using expressions in real life
Expressions help us describe a situation once and use it many times. We only need to replace the letter with a number to get an answer.

### Example 1: School fees for a trip
A trip costs Ksh t for each pupil and there are 20 pupils. The total cost is 20t. If t = 150, then 20t = 20 × 150 = Ksh 3,000.

### Example 2: Age
Otieno is a years old. His sister is 3 years older. Her age is a + 3. If Otieno is 9, his sister is 9 + 3 = 12 years old.

### Example 3: Change
Wairimu had d shillings. She spent 45 shillings. She has d − 45 left. If d = 100, she has 100 − 45 = 55 shillings left.

### Checklist for forming and simplifying
1. Choose a letter for each unknown.
2. Use the key words to pick + , −, × or ÷.
3. Group like terms.
4. Add or subtract only like terms.
5. Replace the letters with numbers if you are told their values.

### Common mistakes
• Writing 3a as 3 + a. Remember that 3a means 3 × a.
• Joining unlike terms: 2a + 3b is not 5ab.
• Forgetting that a on its own means 1a.

## QUIZ
1. What does 4m mean?
   A) 4 + m
   B) 4 − m
   C) 4 × m
   D) 4 ÷ m
   Answer: C
   Why: A number written next to a letter means multiply. 4m is 4 times m.
2. A number x is increased by 8. Which expression shows this?
   A) x − 8
   B) 8x
   C) x ÷ 8
   D) x + 8
   Answer: D
   Why: Increased by means added to, so the expression is x + 8.
3. Simplify 3a + 2a.
   A) 5
   B) 5a
   C) 6a
   D) a5
   Answer: B
   Why: 3a and 2a are like terms. 3 + 2 = 5, so the answer is 5a.
4. Simplify 3m + 2o + m + 4o.
   A) 10mo
   B) 4m + 6o
   C) 3m + 6o
   D) 6m + 4o
   Answer: B
   Why: The m terms: 3m + m = 4m. The o terms: 2o + 4o = 6o. The answer is 4m + 6o.
5. If n = 6, what is the value of n + 5?
   A) 30
   B) 11
   C) 65
   D) 1
   Answer: B
   Why: Replace n by 6. 6 + 5 = 11.
6. Simplify 5x + 3y − 2x + y. (3 marks)
   Answer: 3x + 4y
   Why: x terms: 5x − 2x = 3x (1 mark). y terms: 3y + y = 4y (1 mark). Write the answer 3x + 4y (1 mark).
7. Otieno is a years old and his sister is 3 years older. Write an expression for his sister's age and find her age if Otieno is 9. (3 marks)
   Answer: a + 3. When a = 9, her age is 12 years.
   Why: 1 mark for a + 3, 1 mark for replacing a by 9, 1 mark for 12 years.
8. A matatu carries m passengers. At a stop, 8 get in and 3 get out. Write and simplify an expression for the number of passengers now. (3 marks)
   Answer: m + 8 − 3 = m + 5
   Why: 1 mark for m + 8, 1 mark for taking away 3, 1 mark for simplifying 8 − 3 to 5 to give m + 5.
9. A trip costs Ksh t for each pupil. Write an expression for the cost for 20 pupils. Find the cost if t = 150. (3 marks)
   Answer: 20t. When t = 150, cost = 20 × 150 = Ksh 3,000.
   Why: 1 mark for 20t, 1 mark for replacing t by 150, 1 mark for Ksh 3,000.
10. Explain why 2a + 3b cannot be written as 5ab. (2 marks)
   Answer: a and b are different kinds of things, so they are not like terms and cannot be added together.
   Why: 1 mark for saying they are unlike terms, 1 mark for the reason that different letters stand for different things.
