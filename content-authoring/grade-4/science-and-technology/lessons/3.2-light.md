## PAGE 1: What is light?
Close your eyes tightly. What do you see? Now open them in a bright room. Light lets us see. Without light, we cannot see anything, even if our eyes are open. When there is a power cut at night, you cannot see the objects in your house until someone lights a lamp or a torch.

Light is a form of energy that lets us see. It comes from things that give out light. We can see an object when light from a source hits the object and bounces off it into our eyes. This is how you see this page.

### A place with no light
Go into a cupboard or a dark room with a closed door. Cover all the cracks. Can you see your hand? Now switch on a torch. Now you can see. This shows that we need light to see.

### Light travels in straight lines
Take three cards with a hole in the middle. Line the holes up in a straight line and shine a torch through them. The light passes through. Now move the middle card slightly. The light is blocked. This shows that light travels in straight lines.

### Safety
Never look straight at the sun. It can harm your eyes. Do not shine a torch or laser pointer into anyone's eyes.

## PAGE 2: Sources of light
A source of light is something that gives out its own light. There are two kinds of sources: natural and artificial.

### Natural sources
Natural sources are found in nature. They were not made by people.
• The sun is the biggest source of light for us. It lights the Earth in the day.
• Stars are like distant suns. We see them at night.
• Lightning is a flash of light in the sky during a storm.
• Fireflies and glow-worms make their own light in the dark.

### Artificial sources
Artificial sources are made by people.
• Electric bulbs, tube lights and LED lamps.
• Torches, candles and paraffin lamps (lanterns).
• Solar lamps, which store energy from the sun during the day and give light at night.
• Fire from a burning log or charcoal. People make a fire, so we count it as artificial.
• Car and motorbike headlights, street lights and phone screens.

### The moon is not a source
The moon shines at night but it does not make its own light. It reflects light from the sun, like a mirror. So the moon is not a source of light. A mirror, a shiny cup and a white wall also do not make light.

## PAGE 4: Ways of lighting a house
People want light in their homes during the day and at night. There are many ways to bring light into a house.

### Daylight from windows
Windows are openings in the wall. They let sunlight enter. Windows should be big enough and face the light. Windows with clear glass are transparent. Curtains can be opened to let more light in. Painting walls with light colours helps, because light colours reflect more light and make the room look brighter.

### Translucent roofs
Translucent means that some light passes through but you cannot see clearly through it. Some roofs have a few sheets of clear or translucent plastic or glass among the iron sheets. They let daylight in through the roof. These are sometimes called skylights. This saves the cost of using electric light during the day.

### Artificial lighting
At night or on dark days we use artificial light.
• Electric bulbs and LED lamps if the house has electricity.
• Solar lamps, which are cheap to run because the sun's energy is free.
• Paraffin lamps and candles. They make smoke and can cause fires, so keep them away from curtains and paper.

### Which is best?
Using daylight saves money. LED and solar lamps use less energy than old bulbs.

• Transparent materials let almost all light through, so you can see clearly. Examples: clear glass, clear plastic, clean water.
• Translucent materials let some light through but you cannot see clearly. Examples: frosted glass, tissue paper, thin cloth, some plastic sheets.
• Opaque materials do not let light through. Examples: wood, metal, stone, thick cardboard.

An opaque object blocks the light and makes a shadow behind it.

### Try it
Hold a sheet of clear plastic, tissue paper and a book in front of a torch in a dark room. Say what you see. Which is transparent, which is translucent, and which is opaque?

## PAGE 6: Uses of light
Light is useful in many ways.

### To see
Light lets us read, write, cook, work and move around safely. Farmers need daylight to see their crops. Students need good light to read so their eyes do not become tired.

### For safety and security
• Street lights help drivers and people who walk at night.
• Traffic lights tell drivers when to stop, get ready and go. Red means stop, and green means go.
• Torches and lamps help us to see dangers on the road.
• Security lights help to keep thieves away because they do not like to be seen.
• Reflectors on bicycles and on the road shine when car lights fall on them.
• Lighthouses warn ships of dangerous rocks.

### To discourage pests
Some farmers use bright or flashing lights at night around their fields or animal pens to scare away wild animals that come to eat crops or attack livestock. Lamps near the animal shed also help the farmer to see a thief or a pest coming.

### Other uses
• Plants need light to make their food.
• Sunlight dries clothes, grain and fish.
• Solar panels change sunlight into electricity.

### Common mistakes
• Thinking that the moon gives out its own light. It only reflects light from the sun.
• Thinking that all fires are natural. A cooking fire made by people is artificial.
• Mixing up translucent and transparent. You can see clearly through a transparent material but not a translucent one.

## QUIZ
1. Which of these is a natural source of light?
   A) Candle
   B) Torch
   C) The sun
   D) Electric bulb
   Answer: C
   Why: The sun is found in nature. Candles, torches and bulbs are made by people.
2. Which of these is an artificial source of light?
   A) Stars
   B) Lightning
   C) Firefly
   D) Solar lamp
   Answer: D
   Why: A solar lamp is made by people. The others are natural.
3. Which material lets no light pass through?
   A) Clear glass
   B) Wood
   C) Tissue paper
   D) Clean water
   Answer: B
   Why: Wood is opaque so light cannot pass through it.
4. Why is the moon not a source of light?
   A) It is too far
   B) It is too cold
   C) It only reflects light from the sun
   D) It is too big
   Answer: C
   Why: The moon does not make its own light, it reflects sunlight.
5. What does a translucent roof sheet do?
   A) It blocks all light
   B) It lets some light through so the room is lit by day
   C) It makes the room dark
   D) It makes electricity
   Answer: B
   Why: Translucent materials let some light through, so they light rooms in the day.
6. Give two examples each of natural and artificial sources of light. (2 marks)
   Answer: Natural: sun, stars, lightning, firefly. Artificial: bulb, torch, candle, lantern (any two of each).
   Why: 1 mark for two correct natural sources and 1 mark for two correct artificial sources.
7. State three ways of lighting a house. (3 marks)
   Answer: Windows, translucent roofs or skylights, electric lights, solar lamps, paraffin lamps or candles (any three).
   Why: 1 mark for each correct way.
8. Why is it cheaper to use windows and translucent roof sheets than electric bulbs during the day? (2 marks)
   Answer: They let sunlight in, which is free, so no electricity is used.
   Why: 1 mark for saying sunlight is free and 1 mark for saving electricity or money.
9. Explain how light helps to keep us safe. Give three examples. (3 marks)
   Answer: Street lights help people see at night, traffic lights direct drivers, security lights keep thieves away, torches show dangers, reflectors on roads.
   Why: 1 mark for each correct example, up to 3.
10. Describe a simple test to show that a book is opaque and tissue paper is translucent. (3 marks)
   Answer: In a dark room, shine a torch through each. Light does not pass through the book, so it is opaque. Some light passes through the tissue paper but you cannot see clearly, so it is translucent.
   Why: 1 mark for the test, 1 mark for the book result, 1 mark for the tissue result.
