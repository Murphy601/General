## PAGE 1: Why do we need to digest food?
When you eat a piece of ugali or a boiled egg, your body cannot use it straight away. The food is in big pieces. Your body can only use food when it has been broken down into tiny parts that can pass into the blood. The blood then carries these parts to every part of your body. The body uses them for energy, growth and repair.

Digestion is the breaking down of food into tiny parts that the body can use. The parts of the body that do this together are called the digestive system. Food travels through a long tube that starts at the mouth and ends at the anus. This tube is many metres long when stretched out. It is folded up neatly inside your tummy.

### Feel it happen
Chew a small piece of dry bread or a cracker slowly and do not swallow for a while. After some time it starts to taste slightly sweeter and becomes soft. Your teeth broke the bread into small pieces and saliva in your mouth started to change the starch in it. This is the beginning of digestion.

### Think about it
Does food you swallow really stay outside your body until it is digested? Yes, in a way. The tube through your body is open at both ends. Food is only really taken into the body when tiny digested parts pass through the wall of the small intestine into the blood.

### Words to remember
• Gullet: the tube that carries swallowed food to the stomach. It is also called the oesophagus.
• Small intestine: the long coiled tube after the stomach.
• Large intestine: the wider tube that goes around the small intestine.
• Rectum: the last part of the large intestine.
• Anus: the opening where waste leaves the body.

## PAGE 3: What each part does
### Mouth
Teeth cut and grind the food into small pieces. The tongue moves the food around and helps you swallow. Saliva makes food wet and starts to digest starchy food.

### Gullet
When you swallow, the food goes into the gullet. The gullet squeezes and pushes the food down to the stomach. It works even if you stand on your head or lie down, because its walls push the food along.

### Stomach
The stomach is like a stretchy bag. It churns the food and mixes it with strong juices. The juices kill many germs and break the food down more. The food becomes a thick soup.

### Small intestine
Here digestion is finished. More juices join the food. The tiny digested parts then pass through the wall of the small intestine into the blood. This is called absorption. The blood then carries them all over the body.

### Large intestine
What is left is mostly water and parts of food that cannot be digested, such as tough plant fibre. The large intestine takes back most of the water. The rest becomes solid waste, called faeces.

### Rectum and anus
The rectum stores faeces for a short time. When you visit the toilet, the faeces leave the body through the anus.

### Worked example
Baraka swallowed a whole peanut without chewing. It reached his stomach but it was not broken into small pieces. Why is chewing important? Chewing makes food smaller and wetter, so digestion is easier and quicker in the rest of the system.

## PAGE 5: Keeping the digestive system healthy
A healthy digestive system lets you get all the goodness from your food. We keep it healthy by good habits.

### Dental hygiene
Teeth are the first part of the system. Brush your teeth at least twice a day, in the morning and before bed. Use a soft brush and toothpaste, or a chewing stick if that is what you have. Do not eat too many sweets, sugary drinks and biscuits. See a dentist if a tooth hurts.

### Deworming
Worms can live in the intestines and take food from us. Take deworming tablets when a health worker gives them, for example at school, and always follow their advice. Wear shoes, keep your nails short and wash your hands, because worm eggs are found in dirty soil and dirty hands.

### Healthy eating
• Eat a balanced diet with energy foods, body-building foods and protective foods.
• Eat fruits, vegetables and whole grains. They contain fibre, which helps food move through the intestines.
• Drink enough clean, safe water.
• Eat slowly and chew well.
• Wash fruits and vegetables. Cover your food. Do not eat rotten or old food.
• Wash your hands with soap and water before eating and after using the toilet.

## PAGE 6: When the digestive system is not healthy
Sometimes the digestive system does not work well. The body tells us in these ways. These are called symptoms.

### Common symptoms
• Stomach ache or pain. It may be from hunger, eating too fast, germs or worms.
• Bloating. The tummy feels full and swollen, often with too much gas.
• Worms. You may see worms in the faeces, have an itchy anus or feel weak.
• Diarrhoea. Loose, watery faeces passed many times. It makes the body lose water quickly.
• Vomiting. Food comes out through the mouth.
• Constipation. Faeces are hard and dry and it is difficult to pass them.

### What to do
Tell an adult at once when you feel unwell. Diarrhoea is dangerous for young children because they lose water fast. Drink plenty of clean water and follow the advice of a health worker, who may give oral rehydration solution (ORS). Do not take medicine unless a health worker or a parent gives it. To prevent constipation, eat fibre foods and drink water.

### Make a plan
Write a plan for one week for a healthy digestive system. Include when you will brush your teeth, what fruits or vegetables you will eat, how much water you will drink and when you will wash your hands.

## QUIZ
1. Which part of the digestive system carries swallowed food to the stomach?
   A) Gullet
   B) Rectum
   C) Small intestine
   D) Anus
   Answer: A
   Why: The gullet (oesophagus) is the tube that pushes swallowed food down to the stomach.
2. In which part do the digested food parts pass into the blood?
   A) Mouth
   B) Stomach
   C) Small intestine
   D) Large intestine
   Answer: C
   Why: The small intestine absorbs digested food into the blood.
3. What does the large intestine mainly take back from the leftover food?
   A) Salt
   B) Water
   C) Teeth
   D) Bones
   Answer: B
   Why: The large intestine takes back most of the water from what is left.
4. Which of these helps to prevent constipation?
   A) Eating fibre such as vegetables
   B) Eating only sweets
   C) Drinking no water
   D) Skipping meals
   Answer: A
   Why: Fibre helps food and waste move through the intestines, and water keeps faeces soft.
5. What is the last part of the digestive tube called?
   A) Rectum
   B) Anus
   C) Gullet
   D) Stomach
   Answer: B
   Why: The anus is the opening at the end of the tube where faeces leave the body.
6. Arrange in the correct order: stomach, mouth, small intestine, gullet. (2 marks)
   Answer: Mouth, gullet, stomach, small intestine.
   Why: 2 marks for the whole order. 1 mark if only two neighbouring parts are correctly placed.
7. State two things the stomach does to the food. (2 marks)
   Answer: It churns the food and mixes it with juices that break it down and kill many germs.
   Why: 1 mark for churning or mixing, 1 mark for juices breaking down food or killing germs.
8. Write three ways of keeping the digestive system healthy. (3 marks)
   Answer: Brush teeth twice a day, take deworming tablets, eat fruits and vegetables, drink clean water, wash hands with soap.
   Why: 1 mark for each correct way, up to 3.
9. Name three signs (symptoms) of an unhealthy digestive system. (3 marks)
   Answer: Stomach ache, bloating, worms, diarrhoea, vomiting, constipation (any three).
   Why: 1 mark for each correct symptom, up to 3.
10. Explain why a child with diarrhoea should drink clean water and see a health worker. (3 marks)
   Answer: Diarrhoea makes the body lose a lot of water quickly, so clean water replaces it. A health worker can give ORS and the right advice.
   Why: 1 mark for water being lost, 1 mark for drinking clean water, 1 mark for seeing a health worker.
