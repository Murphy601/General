## PAGE 1: What is a force?
When you push a door open, kick a football, pull a rope in a tug of war or lift a bucket of water, you are using a force. A force is a push or a pull. We cannot see a force itself, but we can see what it does.

Forces are all around us. The wind pushes a kite. A tractor pulls a plough. A goat pulls on the rope tied to it. Your hand pushes a pencil across a page. Even when you sit still on a chair, forces are acting on you.

### Push or pull?
Decide whether each action is a push or a pull.
• Opening a drawer by its handle: a pull.
• Closing a door: a push.
• Squeezing a tube of toothpaste: a push.
• Drawing water from a well with a rope: a pull.
• Kicking a ball: a push.

### Try it
Place a book on your desk. Push it gently with one finger. Now pull it back by its edge. Say which of these was a push and which was a pull. Then push harder. What changed?

A stronger force makes a bigger effect. A small push moves the book slowly. A big push moves it faster and further.

## PAGE 2: Force of gravity
Throw a ball into the air. It rises, slows down and then falls back to the ground. Drop a pencil and it falls. Rain falls from clouds. Ripe mangoes drop from the tree. What pulls all of these things down?

The force that pulls objects towards the Earth is called gravity. Gravity pulls everything down towards the centre of the Earth, wherever you are on the planet. That is why people in Kenya and people on the other side of the world both stand on the ground and do not fall off.

### What gravity does for us
• It keeps us and everything else on the ground.
• It makes water flow downhill in rivers and makes rain fall.
• It makes a bucket of water heavy to lift. The weight of an object is the pull of gravity on it.
• It brings a thrown ball back to the ground.

### Try it: do heavy things fall faster?
Drop a flat sheet of paper and a stone from the same height at the same time. The stone lands first. Now crumple the paper into a tight ball and drop it with the stone. They land almost together. Air pushes up on the flat paper more than on the crumpled paper. Gravity pulls both down.

## PAGE 3: Force of friction
Rub your palms together quickly. They become warm. Slide a book across a smooth floor, and it stops after a while. Slide it across a rough carpet, and it stops sooner. The force that stops or slows things that rub against each other is called friction.

Friction acts when two surfaces touch and one tries to slide over the other. It always acts in the opposite direction to the movement. Rough surfaces make a lot of friction. Smooth surfaces make less. Friction also makes heat. That is why your hands feel warm when you rub them.

## PAGE 4: Effects of force on objects
A force can do seven things to an object. Watch for them when you play, work or use tools.
1. Change shape. Squeezing clay, a sponge or a lump of dough changes its shape. Pressing a spring makes it shorter.
2. Start motion. A push moves a still football. A pull moves a resting cart.
3. Stop a moving object. A goalkeeper stops a ball. Brakes stop a bicycle.
4. Increase speed. Pedalling harder makes the bicycle go faster.
5. Decrease speed. Pressing the brakes slows the bicycle.
6. Change direction. A hockey stick sends a rolling ball in another direction.
7. Hold objects together. Glue, nails, ropes, screws and stitches use force to keep things together.

### Try it
Use a lump of play dough or wet clay. Press it, roll it, stretch it and squash it. Which effect of force did you show? Now roll a marble on the floor and stop it with your hand. Then flick it to make it go a different way.

### Worked example
Otieno kicks a stationary ball. The ball moves. The kick started motion. The ball rolls towards Mercy, who stops it with her foot. Her foot stopped the motion. Then she kicks it to the right. That force changed the direction.

## PAGE 5: Friction in daily life
Friction can be helpful or a problem.

### Where friction helps
• Shoes with rough soles grip the ground so we do not slip.
• Brakes on a bicycle use friction to slow the wheels.
• A match lights when we rub the head on the box.
• Rough tyres grip the road, especially on wet roads.
• Rubbing a stick against a stone makes a knife sharp.
• Rubbing hands together makes them warm on a cold morning.

### Where friction is a problem
• It wears away shoes, tyres and machine parts.
• It slows down moving parts and wastes energy.
• It makes machines hot.

### Ways of reducing friction
• Make surfaces smooth. Polish or sand them.
• Use oil or grease. A thin layer of oil between metal parts helps them slide.
• Use rollers. Logs can roll a heavy load along the ground.
• Use ball bearings. These are small steel balls inside wheels, such as those of a bicycle. They roll and cut down friction.
• Use wheels instead of dragging things.

### Friction on the farm
Farmers oil the joints of tools such as a wheelbarrow to keep them running well. A wheelbarrow works well because of its wheel.

## PAGE 6: Using forces every day
Forces help us to do many jobs.
• Pushing a wheelbarrow, pulling a cart or a plough.
• Lifting water from a well with a bucket and rope.
• Using a hammer to push a nail into wood.
• Kicking, throwing and catching in sports.
• Sewing and gluing use forces to hold things together.
• A pump pushes water up a pipe or air into a tyre.
• Pulling the string of a bow or a slingshot.

### Investigation: friction on ramps
Lean a board against a pile of books to make a ramp. Slide a block down. Cover the ramp with a cloth, then with paper, then sandpaper, if you have it, or with a cardboard. On which does the block slide the shortest distance? That is where friction is the biggest. Record your results in a table.

### Safety
Do not push or pull other children. Do not throw heavy things. Keep fingers away from moving parts when you do experiments.

### Common mistakes
• Saying gravity works only on heavy things. It pulls on everything.
• Thinking friction is always bad. It helps us walk and stop.
• Thinking that only big pushes are forces. A tiny touch is a force too.
• Saying that force is measured in kilograms. Do not worry about the unit for now. Just remember it is a push or a pull.

## QUIZ
1. What is a force?
   A) A push or a pull
   B) A kind of matter
   C) A type of energy in food
   D) A form of light
   Answer: A
   Why: In science a force is a push or a pull acting on an object.
2. Which force pulls objects towards the Earth?
   A) Friction
   B) Gravity
   C) Wind
   D) Magnetism
   Answer: B
   Why: Gravity is the force that pulls everything towards the Earth.
3. Which surface gives the biggest friction?
   A) A wet tile
   B) A polished table
   C) A rough carpet
   D) An oiled metal plate
   Answer: C
   Why: Rough surfaces have more friction than smooth or oiled ones.
4. Which of these is an effect of force?
   A) Changing the colour of an object
   B) Changing the shape of a lump of clay
   C) Making an object hotter without touching it
   D) Making an object lighter
   Answer: B
   Why: Pressing or squeezing changes shape. The force effects are change of shape, start or stop motion, speed, direction and holding together.
5. Why do bicycles have ball bearings in their wheels?
   A) To make the bicycle heavy
   B) To increase friction
   C) To reduce friction
   D) To make noise
   Answer: C
   Why: Ball bearings roll and cut down friction so the wheels turn easily.
6. State two ways of reducing friction between moving parts. (2 marks)
   Answer: Any two of: use oil or grease, make surfaces smooth, use rollers, use ball bearings.
   Why: 1 mark for each correct way.
7. Name any three effects of a force on an object. (3 marks)
   Answer: It can change shape, start motion, stop motion, increase speed, decrease speed, change direction and hold things together (any three).
   Why: 1 mark for each correct effect.
8. Kelvin drops a stone and a flat sheet of paper. The stone lands first. Later he crumples the paper and both land almost together. Explain why the crumpled paper falls faster than the flat paper. (3 marks)
   Answer: Gravity pulls both down. The flat paper is pushed up by the air more, so it falls slowly. Crumpled paper has less surface, so air pushes it less and it falls faster.
   Why: 1 mark for gravity pulling both, 1 mark for air pushing up on the flat paper, 1 mark for crumpled paper having less air push.
9. Give two places where friction is useful and one place where it is a problem. (3 marks)
   Answer: Useful: brakes and shoes gripping the ground. Problem: wearing away tyres or machine parts.
   Why: 1 mark for each correct example.
10. Explain what happens when you push a ball that is already rolling in the same direction and then hold it with your hands. Name the effects of the force each time. (3 marks)
   Answer: When you push it in the same direction it speeds up (increase speed). When you hold it with your hands it stops (stops motion).
   Why: 1 mark for speeds up, 1 mark for stops, 1 mark for naming the effects.
