## PAGE 1: Plants are living things
Look at the maize in a shamba, the grass by the road and the mango tree at school. All of them are plants, and all of them are alive. A stone is not alive and a plastic flower is not alive. How can we tell the difference? Scientists look for signs of life. Living things show these signs and non-living things do not.

Plants show five signs of life that we can see and check for ourselves:
• They grow. A seedling becomes taller and bigger.
• They reproduce. They make new plants, usually from seeds.
• They remove waste. They get rid of things their bodies do not need.
• They respond to changes around them, for example to light.
• They die. Every plant lives for a limited time.

Plants also need water, air, light and nutrients from the soil to stay alive. A plant that does not get them becomes weak and may die.

### Try it
Walk around the school compound with a partner. Look for at least five different plants. Choose one and write down one sign of life you can see, such as a new leaf, a flower or a dry leaf on the ground. Handle plants gently. Do not taste any plant or fruit unless a teacher says it is safe, and do not touch plants with thorns or sap that itches.

## PAGE 2: How plants grow and reproduce
Growth means getting bigger and more developed over time. Plant a bean seed in a tin with soil, give it a little water each day and watch. First a root comes out of the seed and goes down into the soil. Then a shoot pushes up and opens into leaves. Measure the height of the shoot with a ruler every two days and record it. You will see the plant grow.

### Reproduction
Reproduce means to make new living things of the same kind. Most plants you know have flowers. After a flower is pollinated, it makes fruits that hold seeds. When a seed falls on good soil and gets water, it grows into a new plant. Some plants reproduce in other ways. A piece of sweet potato vine or a cassava stem cutting can grow roots and become a new plant.

### Remember
Growing and reproducing are two different signs. Growing is one plant getting bigger. Reproducing is making new plants.

## PAGE 3: Removing waste, responding and dying
### Removing waste
All living things make waste inside their bodies. Plants get rid of waste too. Some waste gases leave through tiny holes in the leaves. Some waste is stored in old leaves, bark or fruits. When a leaf turns yellow and falls off, it can carry waste away with it. Some plants make sticky sap or gum that they push out.

### Responding to changes
A response is the way a living thing reacts when something around it changes. Plants respond slowly, but they do respond.
• A plant on a windowsill bends and grows towards the light.
• Roots grow downwards, towards water and soil.
• The leaves of the touch-me-not plant fold when you touch them lightly. This plant is common in Kenya, and it grows in many compounds.
• Many flowers open in the daytime and some close at night.

### Dying
Every plant dies one day. Some plants, like maize and beans, live for only one season. Others, like mango and baobab trees, live for many years. A dead plant dries up, stops growing and does not respond to anything. Its body rots and returns nutrients to the soil.

### Activity
Put one young plant in a box with one small hole for light and put another plant outside the box in the light. After a week, look at how the plants have grown. Write what you see in your book.

### Roots
Roots are the parts that grow in the soil. Some plants have one thick main root with thin side roots, like a bean plant. Others, like maize and grass, have many thin roots of similar size.

### Stem
The stem is the part that holds the plant up. The leaves, flowers and fruits are attached to it.

### Leaves
Leaves are usually flat and green. They grow from the stem and spread out to catch light.

### Flowers and fruits
Flowers are often colourful. Fruits grow from flowers and contain the seeds.

## PAGE 5: What each part does
Every part of a plant has a job. If one part is damaged, the whole plant can suffer.

### Roots
• Roots hold the plant firmly in the soil so wind does not blow it over.
• Roots take in water and mineral salts from the soil.
• In some plants roots also store food. Carrots, cassava and sweet potatoes are storage roots that we eat.

### Stem
• The stem holds the leaves up to the light.
• It carries water and mineral salts from the roots up to the leaves, and it carries food made in the leaves to other parts.
• Some stems store food, for example sugarcane.

### Leaves
• Leaves make food for the plant. They use sunlight, water and air to do this. The process is called photosynthesis, but you only need to remember that leaves are the food factories.
• Air goes in and out through tiny holes in the leaves.

### Flowers, fruits and seeds
• Flowers make fruits and seeds so that new plants can grow.
• Fruits protect the seeds. Many fruits are also food for animals and people.

### Worked example
Kamau pulls the leaves off a young bean plant in his tin. After some days the plant looks weak and stops growing. Why? Without leaves it cannot make enough food. Its roots can still take in water, but the plant has no food factory.

## PAGE 6: Observing and caring for plants
Plants give us food, shade, medicine, wood and clean air. Animals need plants for food and shelter. That is why we must care for them.

### Draw and label
Take a young plant or a seedling from a pot or from the school garden. Draw it in your book with a pencil. Draw the roots, the stem and the leaves. Use a ruler to draw label lines and write the name of each part. If you have a tablet or computer at school, you can draw the plant with a drawing app and type the labels.

### Ways to care for plants
• Water plants when the soil is dry, using water carefully.
• Do not pick flowers or pull leaves without a reason.
• Do not step on young plants or trample on flower beds.
• Plant a tree and look after it until it is strong.
• Put waste in a bin, not on the garden.
• Remove weeds so that crops have enough room, water and light.

### Common mistakes
• Saying that a plant does not move so it is not alive. Plants show life by growing and responding, even if they do not walk.
• Mixing up roots and stems. Roots are usually underground and take in water. Stems are usually above ground and carry things up and down.
• Saying that roots make food. Leaves make food.

### Safety
Wash your hands after touching soil and plants. Some plants can hurt you, so ask an adult before touching a plant you do not know.

## QUIZ
1. Which of these is a sign that a plant is alive?
   A) It changes colour in the sun
   B) It grows taller over time
   C) It stays the same size
   D) It is green
   Answer: B
   Why: Growth is one of the signs of life. Being green or changing colour does not prove something is alive.
2. Which part of a plant takes in water from the soil?
   A) Leaf
   B) Flower
   C) Root
   D) Fruit
   Answer: C
   Why: Roots grow in the soil and take in water and mineral salts.
3. What is the main job of the leaves?
   A) To hold the plant in the soil
   B) To make food for the plant
   C) To carry seeds away
   D) To take in water from soil
   Answer: B
   Why: Leaves make food using sunlight, water and air.
4. A plant on a windowsill bends towards the window. Which sign of life is this?
   A) Reproducing
   B) Removing waste
   C) Dying
   D) Responding to changes
   Answer: D
   Why: The plant reacts to light coming from one side, so it is responding to a change in its environment.
5. Which of these is a root that people eat?
   A) Maize grain
   B) Cassava
   C) Mango
   D) Cabbage leaf
   Answer: B
   Why: Cassava is a storage root. Maize grain is a seed, a mango is a fruit and cabbage is leaves.
6. List the five signs of life in plants that you learnt. (3 marks)
   Answer: Grow, reproduce, remove waste, respond to changes and die.
   Why: Award 1 mark for any two, 2 marks for any four, 3 marks for all five.
7. State two jobs of the stem. (2 marks)
   Answer: It holds the plant up and carries water and food between the roots and the leaves.
   Why: 1 mark for each correct job. Storing food in some plants (like sugarcane) also earns a mark.
8. Wanjiru pulled up a bean seedling. Describe how she can tell which part is the root and which part is the stem. (2 marks)
   Answer: The root is the part that was underground, thin and often has side roots. The stem is above the soil and holds the leaves.
   Why: 1 mark for describing the root and 1 mark for describing the stem.
9. Explain why a plant that has lost all its leaves will become weak. (3 marks)
   Answer: The leaves make the food for the plant. Without leaves the plant cannot make enough food, even if its roots take in water, so it becomes weak.
   Why: 1 mark for saying leaves make food, 1 mark for saying no food is made, 1 mark for the result (weak or dies).
10. Write three ways you can care for plants at school. (3 marks)
   Answer: Water them when dry, do not trample or pick them, plant trees, remove weeds, put litter in bins.
   Why: 1 mark for each sensible way, up to 3 marks.
