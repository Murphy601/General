## PAGE 1: Water is precious
Turn on the tap and clean water comes out. In some homes, a mother or a child must walk a long way to the river or borehole and carry the water home in jerrycans. If you have ever carried a 20 litre jerrycan of water, you know that water is heavy and hard work to fetch. It is also expensive when we pay for it.

We use water to drink, cook, wash, clean, grow food and give to animals. Factories and hospitals also need water. There is a lot of water on Earth, but most of it is salty sea water. Only a small part is fresh water that people can drink. This is why we must use it with great care.

Water conservation means using water carefully and protecting our water sources so that there is enough for everyone, now and in the future. To conserve is to keep safe and not waste.

### Think about it
Where does the water in your home or school come from? It may come from rain, a river, a dam, a well, a borehole or a tap from the county water company. Talk to a partner and write down your answers.

### Brainstorm
How is water wasted at home and at school? Make a list. You may write: a tap left running while brushing teeth, a leaking pipe, water spilt when filling a bucket, and using too much water to wash a small item.

## PAGE 2: How water is wasted
Look at the places where water is wasted every day.

• A dripping tap. A drop at a time does not look like much, but drops keep coming all day and all night. Imagine a tap that leaks 2 litres every hour. In one day (24 hours) it wastes 2 x 24 = 48 litres. That is more than two full 20 litre jerrycans of water.
• Leaving the tap open while washing hands, brushing teeth or washing dishes.
• Leaking or broken pipes that nobody reports.
• Using a hose pipe to wash a car or water the compound when a bucket would be enough.
• Using much more water than needed for washing or cooking.
• Letting animals play in water troughs or leaving containers uncovered so water evaporates.

### Who pays for wasted water?
Water bills are paid by families, schools and businesses. Water wasted is money wasted. When water is wasted, less is left for other people, animals, plants and industries. Where water is short, people may go without.

### Activity
Put a bucket under a slowly dripping tap for one hour. Measure the water you collected using a jug marked in millilitres. Multiply to find out how much would be wasted in a day.

## PAGE 3: Ways of conserving water
Water can be conserved in three easy ways: reduce, reuse and recycle.

### Reduce wastage
• Close taps tightly after use. Fix leaking taps and pipes or report them to an adult.
• Use a cup of water for brushing your teeth rather than leaving the tap running.
• Use a bucket instead of a hose pipe.
• Take short showers or bathe using a basin.
• Wash vegetables in a basin, not under a running tap.
• Do not drink and then throw away half a bottle of clean water.

### Reuse water
Some water can be used a second time for something else.
• Water used to wash vegetables or rice can be used to water plants.
• Water used to wash clothes can be used to mop floors or clean the toilet.
• Rainwater can be caught from the roof and stored in a tank or drum. This is called rainwater harvesting.
Water that has been used for washing is not safe to drink.

### Recycle water
Recycling means cleaning used water so it can be used again. In big places, used water is taken to a treatment plant where it is cleaned. Some car wash businesses clean and reuse the same water several times. In a school, a simple filter using sand and charcoal can help remove dirt from water for watering the garden or washing, but the water is still not for drinking.

### How it works
1. Rain falls on the clean roof.
2. The roof slopes, so the water runs to the gutter.
3. The gutter carries water to a pipe.
4. The pipe leads to a covered tank or drum.
Keep the tank covered so mosquitoes cannot breed in it and so leaves and dirt stay out. Do not drink the stored water unless it has been boiled or treated. Wash the roof and gutters from time to time.

## PAGE 5: Why we conserve water
### Reasons to conserve water
• There is enough for everyone. Saving water leaves some for people who live in dry places.
• It saves money because we pay for less water.
• Plants and animals need water to live. Farmers need water for crops and livestock.
• It reduces the work of fetching water.
• It protects the environment, as sources such as rivers and springs do not dry up so quickly.
• It helps in dry seasons and times of drought.

### Conserving water on the farm
The farmer can use drip irrigation, which gives water to the roots of each plant a drop at a time, so very little is lost. Mulching means covering the soil around the crops with dry grass or leaves. It keeps the soil moist because less water evaporates in the sun.

### Protect water sources
• Do not throw waste or wash clothes directly in rivers and streams.
• Plant trees near rivers, because tree roots hold the soil and protect the bank.
• Keep animals away from water sources meant for drinking.

### Worked example
A school of 200 pupils lets one tap drip. The tap leaks 1 litre every hour. How much water is lost in a school day of 8 hours?
Step 1: 1 litre x 8 hours = 8 litres.
Answer: 8 litres are wasted in 8 hours. In 5 school days, it would be 8 x 5 = 40 litres.

## PAGE 6: Our water conservation plan
Now it is your turn to be a water hero. You can do the following.

### At home
• Close every tap fully. If you see a drip, tell an adult.
• Use a basin or a cup instead of leaving the tap running.
• Collect used washing water for the garden.
• Keep drinking water covered in a clean container.

### At school
• Make water monitors who check that taps are closed.
• Use a bucket when watering the school garden and water in the evening or early morning, when there is less evaporation.
• Put up posters that remind everyone to save water.

### Project: make a poster
Make a poster to teach the school and the community about water conservation. Follow these steps.
1. Choose one clear message, for example "Close the tap!"
2. Draw a large picture with clear colours.
3. Write a short slogan that is easy to read from far away.
4. Add one or two ways to save water.
5. Put the poster near a tap, a borehole or the school gate.

### Common mistakes
• Thinking that there is always plenty of clean water. It is limited.
• Using water that has washed clothes for drinking or cooking. It is not safe.
• Believing that saving water is only for dry places. Every place should save water.

## QUIZ
1. What does water conservation mean?
   A) Using water carefully and not wasting it
   B) Selling water to others
   C) Storing water only for washing cars
   D) Drinking more water
   Answer: A
   Why: Conservation means saving something and using it carefully so that it does not run out.
2. Which action wastes water?
   A) Closing the tap after use
   B) Using a bucket to wash a bicycle
   C) Leaving a tap running while brushing teeth
   D) Watering plants with used washing water
   Answer: C
   Why: A running tap lets clean water flow away when it is not being used.
3. What is rainwater harvesting?
   A) Catching and storing rainwater
   B) Drinking rainwater straight from the roof
   C) Digging up rain
   D) Pouring water on crops during rain
   Answer: A
   Why: Rainwater harvesting means collecting rainwater from roofs in a tank or drum.
4. Which of these is an example of reusing water?
   A) Throwing away washing water in the road
   B) Leaving a tap open
   C) Using water that washed vegetables to water plants
   D) Buying bottled water
   Answer: C
   Why: Using the same water a second time for a different job is reusing.
5. Why is a rainwater tank kept covered?
   A) To make the water hot
   B) To stop mosquitoes and dirt getting in
   C) To make it rain more
   D) To make water heavier
   Answer: B
   Why: A cover stops mosquitoes from breeding and keeps leaves and dirt out.
6. Give two reasons why we should conserve water. (2 marks)
   Answer: So there is enough water for everyone, to save money, for crops and animals, or to protect water sources.
   Why: 1 mark for each correct reason.
7. A leaking tap wastes 3 litres each hour. How much water is wasted in 10 hours? (2 marks)
   Answer: 30 litres.
   Why: 3 litres x 10 hours = 30 litres. 1 mark for the working and 1 mark for the answer with units.
8. Explain the difference between reducing and reusing water. Give one example of each. (3 marks)
   Answer: Reducing means using less water, for example closing a tap. Reusing means using the same water again, for example using washing water to mop.
   Why: 1 mark for each meaning and 1 mark for correct examples.
9. Describe how rainwater is harvested from a roof. (3 marks)
   Answer: Rain falls on the roof, runs into the gutter, goes down a pipe and is stored in a covered tank.
   Why: 1 mark for roof and gutter, 1 mark for the pipe, 1 mark for the tank.
10. Explain how mulching and drip irrigation help a farmer to save water. (4 marks)
   Answer: Mulching covers the soil, so less water evaporates and the soil stays moist. Drip irrigation gives a little water directly to the roots, so little water is wasted.
   Why: 2 marks for mulching (what it is and its effect) and 2 marks for drip irrigation (what it is and its effect).
