## PAGE 1: Animals are living things
A cow, a chicken, a housefly, a fish in Lake Victoria and a child are all animals. A toy car and a wooden doll are not animals, even though they may move or look like animals. Scientists say animals are living things because they show all the signs of life.

Try this: sit quietly outside for five minutes and watch an ant, a bird or a lizard. Write down everything it does. You will probably see it move, look for food and react when you come close.

### Signs of life in animals
Animals show eight signs of life:
• They feed. They take in food to get energy and to grow.
• They grow. Young animals become bigger.
• They breathe. They take in air, because they need oxygen.
• They reproduce. They make young ones of their own kind.
• They remove waste. They get rid of waste from their bodies, such as urine and droppings.
• They move. They can move their whole body from place to place, or move parts of it.
• They respond to changes around them, for example by running from danger.
• They die.

Plants also grow, reproduce and die. The big difference is that animals take in ready-made food and can move from place to place. Plants make their own food.

## PAGE 2: Studying the signs of life in animals
Let us look at each sign of life in animals one at a time, with examples from home and school.

### Feeding
A goat eats grass, a hen pecks grains, a lion hunts, and a mosquito sucks blood. Some animals eat plants only. Some eat other animals. Some eat both.

### Growing and breathing
A chick grows into a hen. A tadpole grows into a frog. A caterpillar grows and later turns into a butterfly. Land animals breathe air through lungs or through tiny holes in the body. Fish take oxygen from the water using gills.

### Reproducing
Some animals give birth to live young, such as cows, goats and people. Others lay eggs, such as hens, snakes and insects. Parents often look after their young ones while they are small.

### Removing waste, moving and responding
Animals pass out waste as urine, faeces and stale air breathed out. Animals move by walking, running, flying, swimming or crawling. When a snail feels touch it pulls its body into its shell. A goat lifts its head when it hears a noise.

### Dying
When an animal dies, it stops breathing, moving and feeding. Its body starts to decay.

### Activity
Watch a school hen or a bird. Tick which of the signs of life you can actually see during ten minutes of watching.

## PAGE 3: Vertebrates and invertebrates
Scientists put animals into two big groups. The first group has a backbone. The second group has no backbone.

The backbone is a line of small bones joined together down the middle of the back. You can feel yours by running a finger down the middle of your back. Animals with a backbone are called vertebrates. Animals without a backbone are called invertebrates.

### Vertebrates in Kenya
• Mammals have hair or fur and feed their babies with milk: cow, goat, lion, human, bat.
• Birds have feathers and wings and lay eggs: hen, ostrich, flamingo.
• Reptiles have dry, scaly skin: snake, lizard, crocodile, tortoise.
• Amphibians have smooth, wet skin and spend part of life in water: frog, toad.
• Fish live in water, have fins and breathe with gills: tilapia, omena.

## PAGE 4: Invertebrates around us
Invertebrates are the biggest group of animals. Most of the animals on Earth have no backbone. Because they have no bones inside, many have a hard covering outside or a soft body.

### Common invertebrates
• Insects have six legs and three body parts. Examples are ants, bees, houseflies, butterflies and grasshoppers. Most insects have wings.
• Spiders have eight legs. A spider is not an insect.
• Earthworms and other worms have soft, long bodies with no legs.
• Snails and slugs have soft bodies. A snail has a hard shell.
• Millipedes have many legs and a long body.
• Crabs have a hard outer shell and ten legs.

## PAGE 5: Finding and grouping animals in our surroundings
A science walk is a good way to find animals near your school. Go with your teacher and a partner. Look under stones, on leaves, on tree trunks, in the grass and near a puddle. Look gently and put every stone or log back the way you found it.

### How to decide the group
Ask one question first: does this animal have a backbone?
• If it has a skeleton inside its body with a backbone, it is a vertebrate.
• If you see a soft body, a shell or a hard outer covering, and no backbone, it is an invertebrate.
Then look for clues. Feathers mean a bird. Fur or hair usually means a mammal. Scales suggest a reptile or a fish.

### Worked example
Njeri finds a small animal under a stone. It has a long soft body with no legs and no shell. It pulls back when she touches it. She decides that it is an earthworm. It is an invertebrate because it has no backbone. It responds to touch, so it shows one sign of life.

### Safety and animal welfare
• Do not pick up animals that you do not know. Some can bite or sting.
• Never harm or kill animals for fun.
• Wash your hands after touching soil or animals.
• Return every animal to where you found it.

### Project: make a portfolio
Make a portfolio of animals in your environment. Take a sheet of paper for each animal and draw it, write its name, and write whether it is a vertebrate or an invertebrate. Add one sign of life you saw. Put the sheets together in a folder with a cover.

## PAGE 6: Caring for animals and common mistakes
Animals give us milk, meat, eggs and honey. Some help us to carry goods or plough. Others clean the environment or pollinate flowers. Wild animals also bring visitors to Kenya and help our country earn money. We should care for all animals.

### How to care for animals
• Give pets and farm animals clean water and food every day.
• Give them shelter from rain and hot sun.
• Keep their houses clean.
• Take sick animals to the animal health worker.
• Do not beat, tease or throw stones at animals.
• Protect wild animals and their homes. Do not hunt them.

### Common mistakes
• Thinking that all small animals are insects. An insect has six legs. A spider has eight, so it is not an insect.
• Thinking that a bat is a bird. A bat has fur and feeds its babies milk, so it is a mammal.
• Thinking that a fish is an invertebrate because it is small. Every fish has a backbone.
• Thinking that only big animals are animals. Ants and worms are animals too.

### Quick summary
Animals feed, grow, breathe, reproduce, remove waste, move, respond and die. Vertebrates have a backbone. Invertebrates do not.

## QUIZ
1. Which of these animals is a vertebrate?
   A) Ant
   B) Snail
   C) Tilapia
   D) Earthworm
   Answer: C
   Why: A tilapia is a fish and every fish has a backbone. The others have no backbone.
2. How many legs does an insect have?
   A) Four
   B) Six
   C) Eight
   D) Ten
   Answer: B
   Why: All insects have six legs. Spiders have eight.
3. Which sign of life do animals show that plants do not usually show?
   A) Growing
   B) Dying
   C) Moving from place to place
   D) Reproducing
   Answer: C
   Why: Animals usually move their whole body from one place to another. Plants grow, reproduce and die too.
4. A frog has smooth, wet skin and lives in water and on land. Which group of vertebrates does it belong to?
   A) Reptiles
   B) Birds
   C) Mammals
   D) Amphibians
   Answer: D
   Why: Animals with wet skin that spend part of their life in water are amphibians.
5. A bat has fur and feeds its babies with milk. What is it?
   A) A bird
   B) A mammal
   C) An insect
   D) A reptile
   Answer: B
   Why: Feeding young with milk is a mammal feature, even though a bat can fly.
6. Name four signs of life that animals show. (2 marks)
   Answer: Any four of feeding, growing, breathing, reproducing, removing waste, moving, responding, dying.
   Why: 1 mark for any two correct signs and 2 marks for four correct signs.
7. What is the difference between a vertebrate and an invertebrate? (2 marks)
   Answer: A vertebrate has a backbone and an invertebrate has no backbone.
   Why: 1 mark for each correct statement.
8. Put these animals in two groups: goat, ant, snake, snail. (3 marks)
   Answer: Vertebrates: goat and snake. Invertebrates: ant and snail.
   Why: Award marks for the two groups and for putting all four animals in the correct group.
9. A spider has eight legs. Explain why it is not an insect but is still an invertebrate. (3 marks)
   Answer: Insects have six legs and a spider has eight, so it is not an insect. It has no backbone, so it is an invertebrate.
   Why: 1 mark for six legs in insects, 1 mark for eight legs in a spider, 1 mark for no backbone.
10. Write three ways of caring for farm animals. (3 marks)
   Answer: Give clean water and food, give shelter, keep their houses clean, take sick animals to a health worker, do not beat them.
   Why: 1 mark for each sensible way, up to 3 marks.
