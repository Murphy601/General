## Arabic and transliteration
- No Arabic script is used anywhere. All surahs, duas and phrases are transliterated. Please verify transliteration of the seven surahs (1.1), Al-Alaq 96:1-5 (7.1), and the duas for leaving/entering the home (5.3.1) and for parents (6.2).
- The Tashahhud and Salat Ibrahimiyya wording is deliberately not written out (learn from a teacher).
- Al-Fatiha is counted as 7 verses with Bismillah as verse 1 (Hafs count).

## Hadith and references to verify (wording paraphrased or grading uncertain)
- 2.1: "I have been sent to perfect good morals" - syllabus gives Al-Hakim; also reported elsewhere.
- 2.3: "Brushing teeth purifies the mouth and pleases Allah" - syllabus says Ibn Majah; other scholars attribute a similar wording to An-Nasa'i/Ahmad (and Al-Bukhari as a mu'allaq narration). Kept as in the syllabus.
- 2.3: siwak "before reading Qur'an" and "on entering the home" are reported practices; check.
- 2.4: "Teach your children swimming, archery and horse riding" - syllabus says Muslim; the well-known wording is not in Sahih Muslim as such and chains are debated. Presented "as in the syllabus". The Prophet racing Aisha (Abu Dawud, Ahmad) included as "reported".
- 2.2: "bismillahi awwalahu wa akhirahu" (Abu Dawud, At-Tirmidhi) and not filling the stomach (moderation, At-Tirmidhi, not quoted).
- 5.1.1: advice to fear Allah regarding animals and not overload them (Abu Dawud) paraphrased.
- 5.2.1: firewood hadith (Al-Bukhari) and the three categories (hadith of Qabisa, Muslim) paraphrased.
- 6.1: "seeking knowledge is a duty" paraphrased (Ibn Majah).
- 4.3: "Pray as you have seen me praying" (Al-Bukhari), the man who prayed hurriedly (Al-Bukhari, Muslim).

## Fiqh (madhhab) points
- Najasaat categories (light/medium/heavy), sprinkling for a baby boy who feeds on milk only, seven washes for dog/pig - Shafi'i teaching, which fits the syllabus wording and most Kenyan Muslims. Other schools differ.
- Wudhu nullifiers: the first three are agreed in general; "touching the private part directly" is Shafi'i/Hanbali (Hanafi differ). Touching between men and women is deliberately omitted.
- Pillars of swalah: the 14-item list follows the syllabus wording (books group them as 13 or 17). Please check against the school textbook used.
- Awrah in prayer (boys navel to knees; girls all but face and hands) is the common minimum.
- Qibla from Kenya taught as "roughly north" (Nairobi about 7 degrees east of north, Kisumu about 12 degrees; Mombasa nearly north). Teachers should confirm with local mosques or an app.

## History (sira) points that come from traditional accounts, not Qur'an/Sahih hadith alone
- Dates are approximate: birth about 570, marriage about 595, first revelation about 610, Taif about 619, Hijrah 622.
- Ali about ten years at conversion, raised in the Prophet's home because of Abu Talib's hardship (Ibn Ishaq).
- First converts: "first woman Khadija, first boy Ali, first free man Abu Bakr" is the common textbook order; scholars differ.
- Taif: grapes offered by a servant, the garden of Utba and Shayba, Mut'im's protection, Thaqif delegation in year 9 AH are from sira sources. The angel of the mountains story is from Al-Bukhari/Muslim.
- Mikail's duty (rain and provision) and Raqib/Atid "right records good, left records bad" are common teaching; Qur'an 50:17-18 and 82:10-12 are the base texts.
- Al-Masad: the meaning of "carrier of firewood" varies among commentators; the text says so.

## Design decisions
- 22 figures (wudhu, prayer stick figures, Qibla compass, prayer-time arc, timeline, family trees, tables). Stick figures for prayer postures are simplified.
- Sensitive topics (Abu Lahab, Taif) are written gently for age 9-10.
