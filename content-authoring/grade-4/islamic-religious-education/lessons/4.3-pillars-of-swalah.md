## PAGE 1: What are the pillars of swalah?
The pillars (arkan, singular rukn) of swalah are the parts that make up the prayer. If a pillar is left out, the prayer is not valid, just as a house cannot stand if one of its main pillars is removed. If we forget one by mistake, we must go back and do it.

Remember the difference:
• Conditions come before the prayer: wudhu, clean clothes, covering the awrah, facing the Qibla and the right time.
• Pillars are inside the prayer: the actions and words that we must do.
• Sunnah acts are extra parts, such as raising the hands, that bring more reward. Leaving one does not spoil the prayer.

### The pillars listed in your syllabus
1. Niyyah (intention)
2. Qiyam (standing, if able)
3. Takbiratul ihram (saying "Allahu Akbar" to begin)
4. Reciting Al-Fatiha
5. Ruku (bowing)
6. Rising from ruku (i'tidal)
7. Sujud (prostration)
8. Sitting between the two sujud
9. Sitting for the last tashahhud
10. Reciting the tashahhud
11. Sending blessings on the Prophet (the prayer that mentions Ibrahim)
12. Taslim (the ending greeting)
13. Tuma'ninah (calmness in each part)
14. Tartib (doing them in order)

Some books group these in slightly different ways, so listen to your teacher.

## PAGE 2: Starting the prayer
### 1. Niyyah: the intention
The intention is a decision in the heart to pray a particular prayer, such as "I am praying Dhuhr for Allah". It is made as we begin. The Prophet (S.A.W.) taught that deeds are judged by intentions (Al-Bukhari and Muslim).

### 2. Qiyam: standing
We stand straight facing the Qibla for the obligatory prayers if we are able. A person who is sick and cannot stand may pray sitting, and if unable, lying down. Islam brings ease.

### 3. Takbiratul ihram
We say "Allahu Akbar", meaning "Allah is the Greatest", to begin the prayer. After this we are in the state of prayer and must not talk or eat until we finish. It is a Sunnah to raise the hands to the shoulders when saying it.

### 4. Reciting Al-Fatiha
We recite Surah Al-Fatiha in every rak'ah of every prayer. The Prophet (S.A.W.) taught that the prayer is not complete without it. Recite it slowly and clearly, with each word in its proper place. After the Fatiha, we recite another short surah in the first two rak'ahs as Sunnah.

## PAGE 3: Bowing and rising
### 5. Ruku: bowing
We say "Allahu Akbar" and bow. Our back is flat and straight, and our hands rest on our knees. In ruku we say "Subhana Rabbiyal Azim", which means "Glory to my Lord, the Most Great". We commonly say it three times.

### 6. Rising from ruku (i'tidal)
We rise until we are standing straight again. While rising we say "Sami'allahu liman hamidah", meaning "Allah hears the one who praises Him". When standing, we say "Rabbana lakal hamd", meaning "Our Lord, to You is all praise".

### Common mistakes
• Bending only a little, so the hands do not reach the knees.
• Rising quickly and not standing straight before going down.
• Bending the head down with the back curved.

### Try it
Stand as if to pray. Bow until your back is straight like a table. Ask a partner to see if a glass could stay on your back. Then rise slowly and stand straight, and count to three inside your head before going down.

## PAGE 4: Prostration and sitting
### 7. Sujud: prostration
We say "Allahu Akbar" and go down until seven parts of the body touch the ground: the forehead (with the nose), the two palms, the two knees and the toes of both feet. The Prophet (S.A.W.) was ordered to prostrate on seven parts (Al-Bukhari and Muslim). In sujud we say "Subhana Rabbiyal A'la", meaning "Glory to my Lord, the Most High". The closest a servant is to Allah is in sujud, so it is a good time for dua.

### 8. Sitting between the two sujud
We say "Allahu Akbar", rise from the first sujud and sit calmly. A dua we can say is "Rabbighfir li", meaning "My Lord, forgive me". Then we prostrate again. Each rak'ah has two sujud.

## PAGE 5: Sitting, tashahhud and ending
### 9 and 10. Last sitting and tashahhud
In the last rak'ah, after the second sujud, we sit and recite the tashahhud. The tashahhud is a greeting to Allah and a salutation to the Prophet, and it includes the testimony of faith: "Ash-hadu an la ilaha illallah, wa ash-hadu anna Muhammadan abduhu wa rasuluh". Learn the complete words from your teacher or a book, and recite them carefully.

### 11. Sending blessings on the Prophet
After the tashahhud we send blessings (salawat) on Prophet Muhammad (S.A.W.), and on his family, in words that mention Prophet Ibrahim and his family. It is the prayer we call "Salat Ibrahimiyya". Ask your teacher to teach you the full words.

### 12. Taslim: the ending greeting
We finish the prayer by turning our face to the right and saying "As-salamu alaykum wa rahmatullah", and then to the left, saying the same words. Now we are out of the prayer and may speak or leave.

### A prayer of two rak'ahs
• Fajr has two rak'ahs. The tashahhud and the taslim come after the second sujud of the second rak'ah.
• In prayers with three or four rak'ahs there is also a sitting after the second rak'ah, with the first tashahhud, and then we stand for the next rak'ah.

## PAGE 6: Tuma'ninah and tartib
### 13. Tuma'ninah: calmness
Tuma'ninah means to stay still for a moment in every position, until each bone settles. It is a pillar. One man prayed in a hurry in the mosque, and the Prophet (S.A.W.) told him three times to go back and pray again, because he had not prayed properly. Then the Prophet taught him to pray calmly and to stay in each position (Al-Bukhari and Muslim).

### 14. Tartib: order
We must do the pillars in the correct order: intention, standing and takbir, Fatiha, ruku, rising, sujud, sitting, second sujud, and so on. It would be wrong, for example, to prostrate before bowing.

### Sequence of one rak'ah
1. Stand and recite Al-Fatiha and another surah.
2. Bow (ruku).
3. Rise (i'tidal).
4. Prostrate (first sujud).
5. Sit between the two sujud.
6. Prostrate (second sujud).
7. Stand for the next rak'ah, or sit for tashahhud if it is the last.

### Practice
Put the flash cards for the pillars on a table in mixed order. Work in groups to arrange them in the correct order. Then practise performing them, one child reading the steps aloud and another demonstrating.

## PAGE 7: Benefits and summary
### Prayer and health
The movements of swalah, such as standing, bowing and prostrating, stretch the body and keep joints flexible. Praying five times a day teaches a person to stop and rest. Regular movement helps the body, and it is also one way in which prayer helps to prevent lifestyle diseases.

### Following the Sunnah
The Prophet (S.A.W.) said: "Pray as you have seen me praying" (Al-Bukhari). So we learn how to pray by copying his way, and we learn it from teachers and books.

### Summary
• Pillars are the parts that must be done for the prayer to be valid.
• Beginning: intention, standing, takbir, Al-Fatiha.
• Middle: ruku, rising, sujud, sitting between the two sujud.
• End: last sitting, tashahhud, blessings on the Prophet, taslim.
• Throughout: tuma'ninah (calm) and tartib (order).

### Common mistakes
• Rushing and not staying still in each position.
• Forgetting Al-Fatiha or reciting it too fast.
• Confusing conditions with pillars.
• Doing the movements in the wrong order.

## QUIZ
1. What is a pillar (rukn) of swalah?
   A) A part of the prayer which must be done for it to be valid
   B) A type of clothing
   C) A place of prayer
   D) A fast
   Answer: A
   Why: Pillars are the essential parts of the prayer; if one is left out, the prayer is not valid.
2. Which of these is said to begin the prayer?
   A) Taslim
   B) Takbiratul ihram
   C) Tashahhud
   D) Sujud
   Answer: B
   Why: We begin the prayer by saying "Allahu Akbar", which is called takbiratul ihram.
3. Which surah is recited in every rak'ah?
   A) Al-Masad
   B) An-Nasr
   C) Al-Fatiha
   D) Al-Kafirun
   Answer: C
   Why: Reciting Al-Fatiha is a pillar in every rak'ah.
4. Which parts of the body touch the ground in sujud?
   A) Only the forehead
   B) Forehead, two palms, two knees and toes of both feet
   C) Elbows and stomach
   D) Only the hands and knees
   Answer: B
   Why: The seven parts of sujud are the forehead (with the nose), two palms, two knees and the toes of both feet.
5. What does "tuma'ninah" mean?
   A) Speaking loudly
   B) Praying quickly
   C) Calmness and stillness in each position
   D) Wearing new clothes
   Answer: C
   Why: Tuma'ninah is staying still for a moment in each position of the prayer.
6. Write the six pillars of swalah from ruku up to taslim in order. (5 marks)
   Answer: Ruku, rising from ruku, sujud, sitting between the two sujud, last sitting with the tashahhud and blessings on the Prophet, taslim.
   Why: 1 mark for each of ruku, rising, sujud, sitting between sujud, tashahhud with last sitting, and taslim, up to 5 marks.
7. State the difference between a condition and a pillar of swalah. (2 marks)
   Answer: A condition is met before the prayer starts, such as wudhu; a pillar is a part done inside the prayer, such as ruku.
   Why: 1 mark for each correct meaning.
8. What did the Prophet (S.A.W.) teach a man who prayed in a hurry? (3 marks)
   Answer: He told him to go back and pray again, because he did not pray properly, and taught him to pray calmly and stay still in each position.
   Why: 1 mark for telling him to repeat, 1 mark for the reason, 1 mark for teaching calmness.
9. Explain how the postures of swalah are good for health. (2 marks)
   Answer: Standing, bowing and prostrating stretch and move the body, keeping joints flexible and helping prevent lifestyle diseases.
   Why: 1 mark for movement or stretching and 1 mark for a health benefit.
10. What do we say in ruku and in sujud? Give the words and meaning of one. (2 marks)
   Answer: In ruku: "Subhana Rabbiyal Azim", Glory to my Lord, the Most Great. In sujud: "Subhana Rabbiyal A'la", Glory to my Lord, the Most High.
   Why: 1 mark for the correct words and 1 mark for the correct meaning.
