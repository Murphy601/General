## PAGE 1: Getting ready to read the Qur'an
The Qur'an is the Book of Allah. Muslims believe it was revealed to Prophet Muhammad (S.A.W.) through the angel Jibril. It is divided into chapters called surahs. A surah is made of verses called ayat (one verse is an ayah).

In this lesson you will meet seven surahs: Al-Fatiha, Al-Kafirun, An-Nasr, Al-Masad, Al-Ikhlas, Al-Falaq and An-Naas. Muslims recite many of them every day, so they are worth learning well.

### Manners of reading the Qur'an
• Be clean. Make wudhu if you can before you touch the Mushaf (the written copy of the Qur'an).
• Sit calmly and keep the Qur'an in a clean, high place, not on the floor.
• Begin by asking Allah's protection from Shaytan and then say Bismillah.
• Read slowly and clearly, following your teacher's rules of tajweed. Tajweed means giving each letter and sound its proper pronunciation.
• Listen carefully when someone else is reciting.

### Two ways of learning
Listening to a good reciter helps you copy the sounds correctly. Your teacher can also correct your mistakes. In this lesson the words are written in English letters (transliteration) to help you remember them. Always learn the real Arabic recitation from a teacher or a good recording.

## PAGE 2: Al-Fatiha, the Opening
Al-Fatiha means "The Opening". It is the first surah in the Qur'an and has seven verses. Muslims recite it in every rak'ah (unit) of every prayer, so you recite it many times a day. The Prophet (S.A.W.) taught that the prayer is not complete without it.

### The verses in transliteration
1. Bismillahir-Rahmanir-Rahim
2. Alhamdu lillahi Rabbil-'alamin
3. Ar-Rahmanir-Rahim
4. Maliki yawmid-din
5. Iyyaka na'budu wa iyyaka nasta'in
6. Ihdinas-siratal-mustaqim
7. Siratal-ladhina an'amta 'alayhim, ghayril-maghdubi 'alayhim wa lad-dallin

### The meaning
• In the name of Allah, the Most Kind, the Most Merciful.
• All praise belongs to Allah, Lord of all the worlds.
• The Most Kind, the Most Merciful.
• Master of the Day of Judgement.
• You alone we worship and You alone we ask for help.
• Guide us to the straight path.
• The path of those You have blessed, not of those who earned Your anger, nor of those who went astray.

Al-Fatiha is a prayer. We praise Allah first and then ask Him for guidance. That is why we recite it in every swalah.

## PAGE 3: Al-Ikhlas, sincerity and the oneness of Allah
Al-Ikhlas is surah number 112 and has four verses. Its name means sincerity, because it teaches us to be sincere in believing that Allah is One. The Prophet (S.A.W.) said that reciting it is equal to a third of the Qur'an.

### The verses
1. Qul huwallahu ahad
2. Allahus-samad
3. Lam yalid wa lam yulad
4. Wa lam yakun lahu kufuwan ahad

### The meaning
• Say: He is Allah, the One.
• Allah is the Eternal Refuge. Everyone needs Him, and He needs nobody.
• He has no child and He was not born.
• And there is nobody equal to Him.

### What we learn
Allah is One. He has no partner, no parents and no children. We worship Him alone and we ask Him alone for what we need. When a child in Kenya says "Allah is the One who made me", that child is already learning the message of Al-Ikhlas.

Activity: Say the four verses slowly with a friend. Then take turns to explain each verse in your own sentence.

## PAGE 4: Al-Falaq and An-Naas, asking Allah for protection
These two surahs are called the Mu'awwidhatayn, the two surahs of seeking refuge. Both begin with "Qul a'udhu", which means "Say: I seek refuge". Al-Falaq is number 113 with five verses. An-Naas is number 114 with six verses, the last surah of the Qur'an.

### Al-Falaq
1. Qul a'udhu bi Rabbil-falaq
2. Min sharri ma khalaq
3. Wa min sharri ghasiqin idha waqab
4. Wa min sharrin-naffathati fil-'uqad
5. Wa min sharri hasidin idha hasad
Meaning: I seek refuge with the Lord of the daybreak, from the evil of what He has created, from the evil of the darkness when it spreads, from the evil of those who blow on knots, and from the evil of the envier when he envies.

### An-Naas
1. Qul a'udhu bi Rabbin-nas
2. Malikin-nas
3. Ilahin-nas
4. Min sharril-waswasil-khannas
5. Alladhi yuwaswisu fi sudurin-nas
6. Minal-jinnati wan-nas
Meaning: I seek refuge with the Lord of people, the King of people, the God of people, from the evil of the whisperer who hides away, who whispers in the hearts of people, from among jinn and people.

## PAGE 5: Using the protection surahs in daily life
Aisha (R.A.) reported that when the Prophet (S.A.W.) went to bed each night, he held his hands together, blew into them and recited Al-Ikhlas, Al-Falaq and An-Naas. Then he wiped his hands over as much of his body as he could reach. Muslims follow this Sunnah before sleeping.

### How we apply these surahs
• We remember that only Allah can protect us, so we ask Him, not other things.
• When a bad thought whispers "cheat", "lie" or "be unkind", we seek refuge in Allah and choose the good.
• Envy is a harmful feeling. Al-Falaq teaches us not to envy others and to ask Allah for protection from the envy of others.
• We recite them in the morning and evening and before sleeping.

### A daily habit
Try this bedtime routine: brush your teeth, make wudhu if you can, say Bismillah, recite Al-Ikhlas, Al-Falaq and An-Naas, and then sleep. It is a peaceful way to end the day.

## PAGE 6: Al-Masad, a warning against pride and harm
Al-Masad is surah number 111 and has five verses. "Masad" means twisted rope of fibre. The surah speaks about Abu Lahab, an uncle of the Prophet (S.A.W.). Abu Lahab was rich, but he insulted the Prophet and tried to stop people from listening to him.

### The verses
1. Tabbat yada Abi Lahabin wa tabb
2. Ma aghna 'anhu maluhu wa ma kasab
3. Sayasla naran dhata lahab
4. Wamra'atuhu hammalatal-hatab
5. Fi jidiha hablum-mim masad

### The meaning
• May the hands of Abu Lahab be ruined, and ruined he is.
• His wealth and what he earned did not help him.
• He will enter a blazing fire.
• And his wife, the carrier of firewood (scholars explain this in different ways, for example that she spread trouble).
• Around her neck will be a rope of twisted fibre.

### Lessons
• Money and family name cannot save a person who refuses the truth.
• Harming and mocking good people is wrong.
• Being related to a Prophet did not help Abu Lahab, because what counts is faith and good deeds.

## PAGE 7: An-Nasr and Al-Kafirun
An-Nasr is surah number 110 with three verses. Nasr means "help". It speaks of the help of Allah and the victory, and of people entering Islam in large groups. Scholars explain that this refers to the time after the opening of Makkah.

### An-Nasr
1. Idha ja'a nasrullahi wal-fath
2. Wa ra'ayta-nnasa yadkhuluna fi dinillahi afwaja
3. Fasabbih bihamdi Rabbika wastaghfirh, innahu kana tawwaba
Meaning: When the help of Allah and the victory come, and you see people entering the religion of Allah in crowds, then praise your Lord and glorify Him, and ask His forgiveness. He always accepts repentance.
Lesson: When we succeed, we thank Allah and stay humble instead of boasting.

### Al-Kafirun
Al-Kafirun is surah number 109 with six verses.
1. Qul ya ayyuhal-kafirun
2. La a'budu ma ta'budun
3. Wa la antum 'abiduna ma a'bud
4. Wa la ana 'abidum ma 'abattum
5. Wa la antum 'abiduna ma a'bud
6. Lakum dinukum wa liya din
Meaning: Say: O you who reject faith, I do not worship what you worship, and you do not worship what I worship. I will never worship what you worship, and you will not worship what I worship. You have your religion and I have mine.
Lesson: A Muslim worships Allah alone, stays firm in faith, and lives peacefully with people of other beliefs.

## PAGE 8: Living by the surahs
Reciting is the first step. The next step is to live what the surahs teach. Match each surah with its lesson.

### Lessons at a glance
• Al-Fatiha: praise Allah and ask Him for guidance every day.
• Al-Kafirun: stay firm in your faith and respect others.
• An-Nasr: thank Allah when you succeed and ask forgiveness.
• Al-Masad: do not be proud or harm others.
• Al-Ikhlas: Allah is One and we worship Him sincerely.
• Al-Falaq and An-Naas: seek Allah's protection from harm, envy and evil whispers.

### Ways to practise
1. Recite the surahs after each swalah and before sleep.
2. Listen to a good reciter on a phone, radio or recording and copy the rhythm.
3. Test yourself with flash cards: write a verse on one card and its meaning on another, then match them.
4. Choose one lesson each week and practise it, for example "be humble when I win a game".

### Common mistakes
• Rushing through the words. Slow, clear reading earns more understanding.
• Learning only from written English letters. Always learn the Arabic sounds from a teacher.
• Reciting without thinking about the meaning.

## QUIZ
1. How many verses are in Surah Al-Fatiha?
   A) 5
   B) 6
   C) 7
   D) 4
   Answer: C
   Why: Al-Fatiha has seven verses.
2. Which surah tells us that Allah is One and has no equal?
   A) Al-Masad
   B) Al-Ikhlas
   C) An-Nasr
   D) Al-Kafirun
   Answer: B
   Why: Al-Ikhlas says "Say: He is Allah, the One" and that there is none equal to Him.
3. Which two surahs are recited to seek Allah's protection?
   A) Al-Fatiha and An-Nasr
   B) Al-Masad and Al-Kafirun
   C) Al-Falaq and An-Naas
   D) Al-Ikhlas and Al-Masad
   Answer: C
   Why: Al-Falaq and An-Naas both begin with "I seek refuge" and are called the two surahs of refuge.
4. Which surah teaches "You have your religion and I have mine"?
   A) Al-Kafirun
   B) An-Nasr
   C) Al-Fatiha
   D) An-Naas
   Answer: A
   Why: This is the last verse of Al-Kafirun.
5. Abu Lahab is mentioned in which surah?
   A) An-Nasr
   B) Al-Falaq
   C) Al-Masad
   D) Al-Ikhlas
   Answer: C
   Why: Al-Masad speaks about Abu Lahab, whose wealth did not save him.
6. Write the meaning of the verse "Iyyaka na'budu wa iyyaka nasta'in". (2 marks)
   Answer: You alone we worship and You alone we ask for help.
   Why: 1 mark for worship of Allah alone and 1 mark for asking help from Him alone.
7. Explain why a Muslim recites Al-Fatiha in every swalah. (3 marks)
   Answer: It is a prayer in which we praise Allah and ask Him for guidance to the straight path. The Prophet (S.A.W.) taught that the prayer is not complete without it. We need guidance every day.
   Why: 1 mark for praise and asking guidance, 1 mark for it being required in prayer, 1 mark for the reason.
8. Describe what the Prophet (S.A.W.) did before sleeping according to Aisha (R.A.). (3 marks)
   Answer: He held his hands together, blew into them, recited Al-Ikhlas, Al-Falaq and An-Naas, and wiped his hands over his body.
   Why: 1 mark for each correct action: blowing and reciting, the three surahs, wiping the body.
9. What lesson from An-Nasr can you apply when you win a football match? (2 marks)
   Answer: Thank Allah, stay humble and do not boast.
   Why: 1 mark for thanking Allah and 1 mark for humility.
10. Choose one of the seven surahs and give its number of verses and one lesson from it. (3 marks)
   Answer: For example, Al-Ikhlas has four verses and teaches that Allah is One. Other correct examples are accepted.
   Why: 1 mark for the surah name, 1 mark for the correct number of verses, 1 mark for a correct lesson.
