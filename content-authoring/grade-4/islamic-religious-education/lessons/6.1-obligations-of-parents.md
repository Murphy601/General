## PAGE 1: Muamalat and the family
Muamalat means the way people deal with each other. It includes how we treat our family, neighbours and community. The family is the first place where we learn good manners. Allah made the family a place of love, safety and learning.

In every family, people have rights and duties. A right is something a person should receive, and a duty (or obligation) is something a person should do for others. Parents have duties to their children, and children have duties to their parents. In this lesson we study what parents owe their children.

### Why do parents have obligations?
Children are a trust (amanah) from Allah. A trust is something valuable that is given to us to look after. Allah will ask parents how they cared for it. The Prophet (S.A.W.) said: "Each of you is a shepherd, and each of you is responsible for his flock." He then mentioned that a man is a guardian over his family and will be asked about them (Al-Bukhari and Muslim).

### Discussion
Think about what your parents or guardians do for you in a day. List five things and share with a partner.

## PAGE 2: Providing basic needs
### 1. Food, clothing and shelter
Parents must give their children food, clean water, clothing and a safe place to live. Even when money is short, parents try their best. The Qur'an tells fathers that they must provide for children according to what is reasonable for them (2:233).

### 2. Health care
Parents take their children to the clinic when they are sick, and give them medicine and vaccinations. They also teach them to keep clean.

### 3. Safety and protection
Parents keep children safe from danger, bad company and harm. In Kenya, this could mean holding a child's hand on a busy road, or making sure a child is not left alone near a river.

### Example
Mama Fatuma sells vegetables in the market. Each morning, she prepares porridge for her children, irons their school uniforms and checks that they wear shoes. In the evening she asks about their day. She is fulfilling her obligations.

### Think
Why is it good to say "thank you" to your parents for these things?

## PAGE 3: Teaching and guiding
### 4. A good name
The Prophet (S.A.W.) taught us to give children good names. A name with a good meaning, like Abdullah, Fatima, Yusuf or Aisha, is a gift. Parents should choose names that show faith and love.

### 5. Education
Parents should send their children to school and make sure they learn to read and write. The Prophet (S.A.W.) said that seeking knowledge is a duty on every Muslim. Parents pay school fees, buy books and encourage homework.

### 6. Teaching the faith
Parents teach children to say Bismillah, to pray, to fast when they are old enough, to read the Qur'an, and to have good manners. The Qur'an (66:6) tells believers to protect themselves and their families. Teaching faith helps children to grow up upright.

### 7. Teaching good character
Parents show children how to be truthful, kind, honest and respectful, by words and by their own example. Children learn a lot by watching.

### Story
A boy, Hamisi, asks his father, "Why do we wash our hands before eating?" His father explains the Sunnah and the health benefits. Hamisi understands and remembers.

## PAGE 4: Love, fairness and care
### 8. Love and affection
The Prophet (S.A.W.) loved children and kissed them. Once a man saw him kissing his grandson Al-Hasan and said that he had ten children and had never kissed any of them. The Prophet said that whoever shows no mercy will not be shown mercy (Al-Bukhari and Muslim). Parents show love by hugging, listening and praising good behaviour.

### 9. Fairness
The Prophet (S.A.W.) told parents to fear Allah and be fair among their children (Al-Bukhari and Muslim). A parent should not favour one child over the others, whether a boy or a girl, older or younger.

### 10. Kind discipline
Parents correct their children when they do wrong. This should be done with wisdom and gentleness, never with cruelty. Hurting a child is not the teaching of Islam.

### 11. Listening and forgiving
Good parents listen to their children's problems and forgive their small mistakes.

### Think
How do you feel when your parents praise you for doing well? Why is fairness important in a home?

## PAGE 5: Why is it important for parents to fulfil their obligations?
When parents fulfil their duties, children and the whole community benefit.

### Benefits for children
• They grow healthy, confident and safe.
• They learn faith and good manners.
• They do well at school.
• They feel loved and know that they belong.

### Benefits for the family and community
• Strong families make a peaceful society.
• Well-raised children become responsible adults who help others.
• Parents earn Allah's reward, since caring for the family is worship. The Prophet (S.A.W.) said that what a person spends on his family, seeking Allah's pleasure, is charity (Al-Bukhari and Muslim).

### What if parents cannot do everything?
Some parents are poor, sick or far away, or a child may live with a grandmother or guardian. The important thing is that the child is cared for. Children should be patient, understand what their parents are able to do, and make dua for them.

### Role-play
Act a scene in which a parent helps a child with homework and another in which a parent listens to a worried child. Then discuss how the children felt.

## PAGE 6: Showing thanks and summary
Children can cherish (value and appreciate) the care of their parents.

### Ways to show appreciation
1. Say "thank you" and make dua for them.
2. Obey them in what is good, and help with chores.
3. Do your homework and be well behaved.
4. Show love by hugging and speaking politely.
5. Take care of the things that they buy for you.

### Anashid
Listen to a nasheed about parents. Discuss the message and how it makes you feel.

### Summary
• Children are a trust from Allah.
• Parents provide needs, teach faith and manners, show love and fairness, and protect their children.
• Children show appreciation with gratitude, obedience and good behaviour.

### Common mistakes
• Taking parents' efforts for granted.
• Comparing your parents to others.
• Thinking that only fathers, or only mothers, have obligations. Both share them.

## QUIZ
1. Who does the hadith say is a "shepherd" responsible for his flock?
   A) Only teachers
   B) Only shopkeepers
   C) Every person, including a parent for his family
   D) Only kings
   Answer: C
   Why: The hadith says each of us is a shepherd and will be asked about those in our care; parents are responsible for their family.
2. Which of these is an obligation of parents to children?
   A) Giving them food and shelter
   B) Making them work all day
   C) Ignoring them
   D) Leaving them alone on the road
   Answer: A
   Why: Providing food, clothing and shelter is a basic duty of parents.
3. What did the Prophet (S.A.W.) advise about treating children?
   A) Favour the boys
   B) Favour the eldest
   C) Be fair among them
   D) Favour the youngest
   Answer: C
   Why: He told parents to fear Allah and be fair among their children.
4. Which of these is a good name for a child, according to the Prophet's advice?
   A) A name with a good meaning
   B) A name that is rude
   C) A name that means nothing
   D) A name that insults
   Answer: A
   Why: Parents should choose names with good meanings.
5. Why do we say children are an "amanah"?
   A) They are a trust from Allah to be looked after
   B) They are property
   C) They are servants
   D) They are strangers
   Answer: A
   Why: Amanah means a trust; parents will be asked how they cared for it.
6. List four obligations of parents to their children. (4 marks)
   Answer: Any four: food, clothing and shelter; health care; education; a good name; teaching faith and manners; love and fairness; protection.
   Why: 1 mark for each obligation.
7. Explain why it is important for parents to fulfil their obligations. (3 marks)
   Answer: Children grow healthy, safe and well-behaved; families and society are strong; and parents earn reward from Allah.
   Why: 1 mark for each valid point.
8. Describe how Prophet Muhammad (S.A.W.) showed love to children. (2 marks)
   Answer: He kissed and hugged them, such as his grandson Al-Hasan, and told a man that he who shows no mercy will not be shown mercy.
   Why: 1 mark for the action and 1 mark for the teaching.
9. Write three ways a child can show thanks to his or her parents. (3 marks)
   Answer: Say thank you and make dua for them; obey them in what is good; help at home and behave well.
   Why: 1 mark for each way.
