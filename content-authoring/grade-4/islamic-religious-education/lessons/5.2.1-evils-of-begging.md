## PAGE 1: What is begging?
Begging is asking other people for money or food without doing any work in return. In towns and along roads, you may have seen people who sit and ask passers-by for help. Some are truly in need. Some beg because they have got used to it, and some children are made to beg by others.

Islam teaches us to care for those who are in need. But Islam also teaches that a healthy person should work, earn an honest living and not depend on others. The Prophet (S.A.W.) said: "The upper hand is better than the lower hand." This means that the person who gives is better than the person who takes (Al-Bukhari and Muslim).

### Key words
• Begging: asking people for money or things without a real need.
• Sadaqah: charity given to please Allah.
• Destitute: a person who has nothing to live on.
• Income: money earned from work.

### Why do people beg?
• Poverty, hunger and unemployment.
• Disability or illness, and old age with no one to care for them.
• Disasters such as floods, fire and drought.
• Laziness or bad habits, for some people.
• Being forced by adults to beg.

## PAGE 2: Who deserves help?
Islam does not tell us to turn away from the poor. The Qur'an (93:10) says: "As for the one who asks, do not drive him away." We should treat a person who asks with kindness and respect, even if we cannot give.

The Prophet (S.A.W.) explained that asking for help is only allowed in real need. Your syllabus names three groups of people who deserve assistance.

### 1. People in debt
A person who has taken a debt for a good reason and cannot pay it may be helped. For example, a farmer who borrowed to buy seeds and lost his crop.

### 2. People who lose their property through disaster
Those whose homes, shops or farms are destroyed by floods, fire, landslides or drought deserve help until they recover.

### 3. The destitute
People who are so poor that they cannot feed themselves or their family, and cannot work, such as the very old, the sick and people with severe disabilities.

### Others in Islam
The Qur'an also names the needy, orphans and travellers who run out of resources as those who should receive help. Muslims give zakah and sadaqah to help them. We should give quietly and with kindness, without insulting or shaming them.

## PAGE 3: Why is begging discouraged?
When someone begs without need, it harms them and the community.

### The evils of begging
1. It lowers a person's dignity. The person loses self-respect.
2. It makes a person lazy. They depend on others instead of using their strength and talents.
3. It wastes resources. Help meant for the truly needy goes to those who could work.
4. It can lead to lies. Some people pretend to be sick or disabled.
5. It harms children. When children beg, they miss school and may be exposed to danger.
6. It weakens the community. A community with many beggars is poor and unproductive.

### The Prophet's teaching
The Prophet (S.A.W.) said that it is better for a person to take a rope, go to the mountain, cut firewood, carry it on his back and sell it, so that Allah protects his dignity, than to ask people who may give him or refuse (Al-Bukhari). Honest work, even hard or humble work, is better than begging.

### Discuss
Why do you think the Prophet chose the example of firewood? Because it is simple, honest work that almost anyone can do.

## PAGE 4: The value of work in Islam
Islam values hard work. Allah says in the Qur'an (62:10) that after the prayer is finished, we should spread through the land and seek Allah's bounty. So Muslims pray and also work.

### Prophets who worked
The prophets worked with their hands. Many were shepherds. The Prophet Muhammad (S.A.W.) took care of sheep as a young man and later worked as a trader. Prophet Dawud (A.S.) ate from what he earned by the work of his hands, as the Prophet (S.A.W.) told us (Al-Bukhari).

### Benefits of working
• Provides food, shelter, clothes and school fees.
• Builds self-respect and independence.
• Helps us to give to others through sadaqah.
• Reduces poverty in the family and the community.
• Develops skills like discipline and patience.

### Honest ways of earning
• Farming, keeping animals and selling farm produce.
• Trading in a shop, kiosk or market.
• Skills such as tailoring, carpentry, mechanics, weaving and jua kali work.
• Teaching, nursing, driving and other professions.

## PAGE 5: How can we earn and generate income?
A Grade 4 child cannot go to a full-time job, but we can still learn to work and save. We can do it safely, with our parents' permission.

### Ideas for children
• Do chores at home, such as fetching water, washing dishes, or feeding chickens, and be thankful for what parents provide.
• Keep a little pocket money in a savings box or a school savings scheme.
• Plant vegetables in a small plot or a sack garden, and share or sell the harvest with a parent's help.
• Make crafts such as beaded items or baskets under adult guidance.
• Learn a skill that will be useful later.

### Ideas for a community
• Start a small group project such as a poultry or vegetable venture.
• Learn skills at a village polytechnic or youth centre.
• Support small businesses in your area.

### Helping those who cannot work
Islam asks us to give zakah and sadaqah, to visit the sick, and to help neighbours in real need. If you see a person who is hungry, share food or tell an adult. Do not laugh at or shout at poor people.

### Example
Shaban's family lost their maize crop in a drought. The neighbours brought food and helped to plant again. Shaban's family worked hard the next season and was soon giving to others. Help in time of need, followed by hard work, is the Islamic way.

## PAGE 6: Summary and things to remember
### Summary
• Begging means asking for help without real need. Islam discourages it.
• People who deserve help include those in debt, those who lose property through disaster, and the destitute.
• Evils of begging: loss of dignity, laziness, wasted resources, lies, harm to children.
• The Prophet taught that the giving hand is better than the receiving hand.
• Muslims should work, earn honestly and give charity.

### How to treat people who ask
1. Be polite and never insult them.
2. Help those who are truly in need.
3. Give quietly, to protect their dignity.
4. If you cannot help, say a kind word or make dua for them.
5. Tell an adult if a child is being forced to beg.

### Common mistakes
• Thinking that all beggars are lazy. Some are truly in need.
• Being rude to a person who asks for help.
• Depending on others when we can work.
• Giving with a proud attitude.

## QUIZ
1. What does the hadith "The upper hand is better than the lower hand" mean?
   A) The one who gives is better than the one who takes
   B) Tall people are better
   C) The right hand is stronger
   D) Beggars are better than workers
   Answer: A
   Why: The upper hand is the giving hand; the lower hand is the receiving hand.
2. Which of these groups deserves assistance?
   A) A healthy person who does not want to work
   B) A person who lost his farm in a flood
   C) A person who wants to buy sweets
   D) A person who wants an easy life
   Answer: B
   Why: Those who lose property through disaster are one of the groups who deserve help.
3. Which of these is an evil of begging?
   A) It builds self-respect
   B) It makes a person lazy and lowers dignity
   C) It creates jobs for others
   D) It teaches people to work
   Answer: B
   Why: Begging without need makes a person depend on others and lowers dignity.
4. What does the Qur'an (93:10) tell us about a person who asks?
   A) Shout at him
   B) Do not drive him away
   C) Ignore him
   D) Ask him to leave
   Answer: B
   Why: We should be kind to a person who asks, even if we cannot give.
5. Which of these is an honest way of earning a living?
   A) Stealing
   B) Cheating in a shop
   C) Farming
   D) Gambling
   Answer: C
   Why: Farming is honest work. Stealing, cheating and gambling are not allowed.
6. Name the three groups of people who deserve help according to the syllabus. (3 marks)
   Answer: People in debt, people who lost property through disaster, and the destitute.
   Why: 1 mark for each group.
7. State three evils of begging. (3 marks)
   Answer: Any three: lowers dignity, makes a person lazy, wastes resources, leads to lies, harms children, weakens the community.
   Why: 1 mark for each evil.
8. Explain why Islam encourages work instead of begging. (3 marks)
   Answer: Work brings honest income and self-respect. The Prophet taught that even cutting and selling firewood is better than asking others. Work also lets us give charity.
   Why: 1 mark for honest income or dignity, 1 mark for the teaching, 1 mark for giving to others.
9. Suggest two ways a Grade 4 child can earn or save money safely. (2 marks)
   Answer: For example: doing chores for a small reward with parents' permission, and saving in a box or a school savings scheme.
   Why: 1 mark for each suitable way.
