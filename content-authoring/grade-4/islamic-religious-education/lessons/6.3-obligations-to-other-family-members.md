## PAGE 1: Who are our family members?
Your family is bigger than the people who live in your house. Your family includes your parents, brothers and sisters, and also your grandparents, aunts, uncles and cousins. We call this the extended family. Islam calls the relationship of blood "silat ar-rahm" or ties of kinship.

### Family members and their Kiswahili names
• Grandmother: bibi
• Grandfather: babu
• Father's sister: shangazi
• Father's brother: baba mdogo or baba mkubwa (younger or older than father)
• Mother's brother: mjomba
• Mother's sister: mama mdogo or mama mkubwa (younger or older than mother)
• Cousin: binamu
• Brother: kaka or ndugu, and sister: dada

Names may be different in your mother tongue. Ask your grandparents what they are in your community's language.

### Allah's command
The Qur'an (4:1) tells us to fear Allah, in whose name we ask one another, and to respect the ties of the womb (family relations). Family bonds are so important that Allah mentions them together with fearing Him.

### Activity
Draw or prepare a chart of your extended family. Write the names of your relatives and how they are related to you. Hang it where the family can see it.

## PAGE 2: The rights of relatives
The Prophet (S.A.W.) taught us to keep good relations with relatives. He said: "Whoever would like his provision to be increased and his life to be extended should keep good relations with his relatives" (Al-Bukhari and Muslim).

He also said that the person who cuts off family ties will not enter Paradise (Al-Bukhari and Muslim). This warns us to take our family bonds seriously.

### What does it mean to keep family ties?
• Visit relatives and greet them.
• Call or send messages, especially to those who live far.
• Help them when they are in need.
• Forgive them when they make mistakes.
• Share gifts and food.

### Why is it important?
• It is a command of Allah.
• It brings love, unity and support in hard times.
• It brings blessing (barakah) in life.
• It is a form of ibadah, when we do it for the sake of Allah.

### Think
What happens in a family that does not visit or greet each other for many years? How can it be repaired?

## PAGE 3: Obligations to grandparents and elders
### Respect
The Prophet (S.A.W.) said that the one who does not show mercy to our young ones and does not respect our elders is not one of us (At-Tirmidhi). Grandparents have experience and wisdom. Greet them first, listen to their stories and do not interrupt.

### Care and help
• Help them to walk, carry bags and fetch water.
• Bring them food and tea.
• Read for them if their eyesight is weak.
• Visit them regularly, especially in school holidays.
• Make dua for their health and long life.

### Example
During the school holiday, Wambui travels to her grandmother's home in the village. She helps to draw water, sweeps the compound and listens to her grandmother's stories about old times. Her grandmother is very happy and prays for her.

### Respect for all elders
Our aunts and uncles are also like parents. They may advise us, and we should listen politely, help them and respect their homes.

## PAGE 4: Obligations to brothers, sisters, aunts, uncles and cousins
### Brothers and sisters
• Share toys, books and food.
• Help each other with homework and chores.
• Do not fight, tease or insult each other. Settle disagreements kindly.
• Older siblings protect and guide younger ones. Younger siblings respect older ones.

### Aunts, uncles and cousins
• Respect them, greet them and accept their advice.
• Visit them, especially on Eid and other special days.
• Play kindly with cousins and include everyone.
• Attend family ceremonies such as weddings, graduations and naming ceremonies, and support them in funerals with prayers and help.
• Care for them when they are sick, by visiting or sending greetings.

### Other duties
• Be honest and trustworthy when visiting a relative's home.
• Do not spread stories about relatives or gossip about them.
• Do not envy a cousin who has more than you. Be happy for them.

## PAGE 5: Ways of keeping good relations
### Simple habits
1. Greet relatives with "As-salamu alaykum" and a smile.
2. Visit or call at least on special days like Eid.
3. Send a small gift or a card.
4. Remember birthdays and achievements.
5. Give help without being asked.
6. Say sorry and forgive quickly.
7. Speak well about them.

### Solving problems
Even in a good family, people sometimes disagree. Islam teaches us to solve problems politely, to forgive and to ask elders for help. The Prophet (S.A.W.) said that the person who keeps family ties is not the one who only returns visits, but the one who joins relations when they have been cut off (Al-Bukhari). So if a relative does not visit, we still visit them.

### Family gathering
In a family gathering, help to serve food, wash dishes and take care of the younger cousins. Keep the place clean.

### Role-play
Act out these scenes:
• Welcoming your grandmother who arrives for a visit.
• Helping an uncle who is sick.
• Greeting and settling a quarrel between two cousins.

## PAGE 6: Summary and benefits
### Benefits of fulfilling obligations to family
• Allah is pleased with us and rewards us.
• We receive love, support and protection from our relatives.
• Our family stays united.
• We gain blessings and a good name.
• We learn to be responsible and thoughtful.

### Summary
• The extended family includes grandparents, aunts, uncles and cousins.
• Islam commands us to keep family ties and warns against cutting them.
• Our duties are to respect, visit, help, care for and forgive relatives.
• We should try to keep good relations even when a relative does not.

### Common mistakes
• Visiting relatives only when we need something.
• Ignoring relatives because they are poor.
• Gossiping about family members.
• Refusing to forgive a small wrong.

### Reminder
Each time you greet a grandparent, share with a cousin or attend a family event, you are worshipping Allah by keeping the bond of kinship.

## QUIZ
1. What is the meaning of "silat ar-rahm"?
   A) Prayer times
   B) Keeping family ties
   C) Giving zakat
   D) Fasting
   Answer: B
   Why: Silat ar-rahm means keeping good relations with relatives.
2. Who is a "shangazi"?
   A) Father's sister
   B) Mother's brother
   C) Grandfather
   D) Cousin
   Answer: A
   Why: Shangazi is the Kiswahili word for the father's sister.
3. According to the hadith, what is the reward of keeping family ties?
   A) Longer life and increase in provision
   B) Loss of money
   C) Nothing at all
   D) Less time to play
   Answer: A
   Why: The Prophet said that whoever wants his provision to increase and his life to be extended should keep good relations with relatives.
4. Which action helps to strengthen family ties?
   A) Gossiping about relatives
   B) Visiting and greeting relatives
   C) Refusing to speak
   D) Ignoring family events
   Answer: B
   Why: Visiting, greeting and helping relatives strengthen the bond.
5. What should you do if your cousin quarrels with you?
   A) Never talk to them again
   B) Forgive and make peace
   C) Insult them
   D) Tell everyone
   Answer: B
   Why: Islam teaches forgiveness and settling disagreements kindly.
6. List four duties of a child to relatives such as grandparents, aunts and uncles. (4 marks)
   Answer: Any four: respect and greet them, visit them, help and care for them, attend family ceremonies, forgive them, make dua for them.
   Why: 1 mark for each duty.
7. Explain why keeping family ties is important in Islam. (3 marks)
   Answer: It is a command of Allah, it brings love and unity, and it brings blessing and reward. The Prophet warned against cutting off family ties.
   Why: 1 mark for the command, 1 mark for unity or support, 1 mark for blessing or reward.
8. Name three family members in the extended family and how they are related to you. (3 marks)
   Answer: For example: bibi is my grandmother; mjomba is my mother's brother; binamu is my cousin.
   Why: 1 mark for each correct relative with its relationship.
9. Write two ways to keep in touch with a relative who lives far away. (2 marks)
   Answer: Call or send a message; send a letter or small gift; visit during holidays.
   Why: 1 mark for each way.
