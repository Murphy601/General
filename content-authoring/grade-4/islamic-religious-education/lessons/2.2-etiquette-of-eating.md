## PAGE 1: Food is a gift from Allah
Every meal we eat is a gift from Allah. The ugali on the plate, the sukuma wiki, the rice and the fruit all come from the earth that Allah made, through the work of farmers, traders and cooks. Islam teaches us to eat in a way that shows thanks.

The Prophet (S.A.W.) gave us simple rules for eating. These rules are called the etiquette of eating, or "adab of eating" in Arabic. Following them is a Sunnah, which means we copy the way of the Prophet.

### Our hadith for this lesson
Umar ibn Abi Salamah (R.A.) was a boy who lived in the Prophet's home. His hand used to move all over the dish while eating. The Prophet (S.A.W.) told him: "Mention the name of Allah, eat with your right hand, and eat from what is directly in front of you." This hadith is narrated by Al-Bukhari and Muslim.

### The three teachings in this hadith
1. Mention the name of Allah before you eat.
2. Eat with your right hand.
3. Eat from the part of the dish that is in front of you.

## PAGE 2: Saying Bismillah
The first teaching is to say "Bismillah", which means "In the name of Allah", before you start eating or drinking. We do this to ask Allah to bless our food and to remember that it came from Him.

### If I forget
If someone forgets to say Bismillah at the start, the Prophet (S.A.W.) taught that they can say "Bismillahi awwalahu wa akhirahu" when they remember. It means "In the name of Allah at its beginning and its end."

### After eating
When we finish, we say "Alhamdulillah", which means "All praise belongs to Allah". We thank Allah for the food and for the strength it gives us.

### Why is saying Bismillah a benefit?
• It reminds us that Allah is the Provider.
• It brings blessing (barakah) to our food.
• It makes eating an act of worship, because we do it as Allah likes.
• It teaches us to be grateful and not greedy.

### Try it
Before your next meal at home or at school, quietly say "Bismillah". Then after you finish, say "Alhamdulillah". Do this for one week and see if it becomes a habit.

## PAGE 3: Eating with the right hand and from what is near you
The second teaching is to eat with the right hand. Muslims use the right hand for clean and honourable actions, like eating, giving and greeting. We do not use it for cleaning after using the toilet. This is one way of keeping our food clean.

The third teaching is to eat from the part of the dish that is in front of you. When many people eat from one dish, as families often do at a wedding or on Eid, it is rude to reach across the plate and take food from the middle or from another person's side. Take from what is near you, and let each person take from their own area.

### Other good manners the Prophet taught
• Wash your hands before eating.
• Sit down calmly. Do not run or play while you eat.
• Never criticise food. If you do not like a food, leave it quietly and politely. The Prophet never found fault with food.
• Do not speak with a full mouth and do not chew loudly.
• Ask an elder to start first. Then pass food to people near you.
• Eat slowly and take small bites.

## PAGE 4: Do not waste and do not overeat
Allah says in the Qur'an (7:31) that we should eat and drink but not be wasteful. Allah does not love those who waste. Islam teaches moderation in eating.

### Moderation
The Prophet (S.A.W.) taught that we should not fill our stomach completely. Overeating can make us lazy and sick. Leave some room, and eat healthy food so that you have strength to worship, study and play.

### Avoid waste
• Take only as much as you can finish.
• Share leftovers with family or neighbours while still fresh, instead of throwing them away.
• Feed leftovers to animals if it is safe. Do not throw food on the ground.
• Remember that many people in the world go to bed hungry.

### Example
Baraka serves himself a huge pile of rice at school and cannot finish it. He throws half in the bin. What should he have done? He should have taken a smaller portion, and gone back for more if he was still hungry. Then nothing would be wasted.

## PAGE 5: Demonstrating the etiquette
Learning manners is easier when we practise them. Try these activities in your class or with your family.

### Activity 1: Role-play
Work in a group of four. Sit around a dish or a table, and act a meal from start to finish. One child should play the person who forgets a manner, for example reaching across the plate. The others kindly remind them what the Prophet taught.

### Activity 2: Good or not good?
Say whether each action follows the Sunnah.
• Saying Bismillah and taking from the part of the plate near you. (Yes)
• Saying "I hate this food!" (No, we do not criticise food.)
• Eating with the left hand while playing on the phone. (No)
• Washing hands and then thanking Allah after the meal. (Yes)

### Eating together
Islam encourages eating together. Sharing meals builds love in the family. Invite a friend or neighbour to eat with you, and pass the dish politely. A guest should be served first and treated with respect.

### Summary
Our food is a blessing. We mention Allah's name, use the right hand, eat from in front of us, do not waste and thank Allah at the end.

## QUIZ
1. In the hadith about eating, who was the boy the Prophet (S.A.W.) advised?
   A) Umar ibn Abi Salamah (R.A.)
   B) Abu Lahab
   C) Zayd ibn Haritha (R.A.)
   D) Waraqa
   Answer: A
   Why: Umar ibn Abi Salamah lived in the Prophet's home and was taught these manners; the hadith is recorded by Al-Bukhari and Muslim.
2. What should a Muslim say before eating?
   A) Alhamdulillah
   B) Bismillah
   C) Subhanallah
   D) Astaghfirullah
   Answer: B
   Why: Bismillah means "In the name of Allah" and is said at the start of eating.
3. Which hand should we use for eating?
   A) The left
   B) Either, it does not matter
   C) The right
   D) Both at the same time
   Answer: C
   Why: The Prophet told us to eat with the right hand.
4. What does Islam teach about wasting food?
   A) It is fine if you are rich
   B) It is disliked and we should avoid it
   C) It is a good habit
   D) It is only wrong at school
   Answer: B
   Why: Allah says in the Qur'an (7:31) that He does not love the wasteful.
5. Which action is correct when eating from a shared dish?
   A) Eating from the part in front of you
   B) Reaching across for the biggest piece
   C) Taking food from another person's side
   D) Playing with the food
   Answer: A
   Why: The hadith says to eat from what is directly in front of you.
6. State the three teachings in the hadith about eating. (3 marks)
   Answer: Mention the name of Allah, eat with the right hand, and eat from what is in front of you.
   Why: 1 mark for each teaching.
7. Give two benefits of saying Bismillah before eating. (2 marks)
   Answer: It reminds us that food comes from Allah and it brings blessing to our food. Other correct answers may be accepted, for example it makes eating an act of worship.
   Why: 1 mark for each correct benefit.
8. What can a person say if they forget to say Bismillah at the start? (2 marks)
   Answer: "Bismillahi awwalahu wa akhirahu", meaning "In the name of Allah at its beginning and its end."
   Why: 1 mark for the words or the correct meaning and 1 mark for saying it when they remember during the meal.
9. Kamau does not like the vegetables on his plate and says loudly, "This food is terrible!" Explain what he should do instead. (3 marks)
   Answer: The Prophet did not criticise food. Kamau should leave it quietly and politely, and he can thank the cook for the other food.
   Why: 1 mark for not criticising, 1 mark for leaving it politely, 1 mark for showing thanks.
