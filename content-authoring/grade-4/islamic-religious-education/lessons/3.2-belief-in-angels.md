## PAGE 1: Who are the angels?
Angels are creatures that Allah made from light. We cannot see them, but Muslims believe in them because Allah and His Messenger told us about them. Belief in angels is one of the six pillars of Iman, which are belief in Allah, His angels, His books, His messengers, the Last Day, and destiny.

The Prophet (S.A.W.) said that angels were created from light and jinn from smokeless fire, and that Adam (A.S.) was created from the material Allah described to us in the Qur'an, which is clay. This is narrated by Muslim.

### What angels are like
• They never disobey Allah. The Qur'an (66:6) says that they do not disobey what Allah commands them and they do what they are ordered.
• They do not eat or sleep, and they do not get tired of worshipping Allah.
• They are not male or female, and they are not Allah's children. They are His honoured servants.
• They can take different forms when Allah orders them.

### Why did Allah create angels?
Allah does not need them. He made them to worship Him and to carry out His orders in the universe. They also help us in ways that we may not see.

### In this lesson
We will study five duties of angels and the names Jibril, Mikail, Raqib and Atid, Malakul Maut (also called Izrail) and Israfil.

## PAGE 2: Jibril, the angel of revelation
Jibril (A.S.) is the greatest of the angels in rank. His duty is to bring Allah's revelation to the Prophets. He brought the Qur'an to Prophet Muhammad (S.A.W.) over twenty-three years.

### Jibril in the Prophet's life
• He met the Prophet at the cave of Hira and told him to read. This was the first revelation.
• He taught the Prophet how to pray.
• In the month of Ramadan, Jibril used to review the Qur'an with the Prophet.
• He came in the form of a man to teach the Muslims the meaning of Islam, Iman and Ihsan, in a famous hadith.

### Lessons
• Allah cares about people, so He sent guidance through Jibril.
• We must respect the Qur'an that Jibril brought.
• We should read and follow the Qur'an every day.

### Key point
Jibril is sometimes called "Ar-Ruh al-Amin", the Trustworthy Spirit. The Qur'an says he brought down the revelation to the heart of the Prophet.

## PAGE 3: Mikail, Israfil and Malakul Maut
### Mikail
Mikail (A.S.) is the angel in charge of rain and provision, as scholars explain. When it rains and crops grow on the farm, it is by Allah's command, and Mikail helps in carrying it out. This teaches us to thank Allah for rain, food and life.

### Israfil
Israfil (A.S.) is the angel who will blow the Trumpet (the Sur). The Qur'an (39:68) tells us that when the Trumpet is blown, all creatures will fall unconscious except those whom Allah wills. It will be blown again and all people will be resurrected for judgement. Israfil is waiting for the command of Allah. This teaches us that the Day of Judgement is true, and we must prepare.

### Malakul Maut (Izrail)
Malakul Maut means the Angel of Death. Some people call him Izrail. The Qur'an (32:11) says: "The Angel of Death who has been put in charge of you will take your souls, then to your Lord you will be returned." He takes the souls only by Allah's permission and at the time that Allah has decided. This teaches us that every person will die, so we should do good deeds now.

## PAGE 4: Raqib and Atid, the recording angels
The Qur'an (50:17-18) says that two angels sit beside every person, and that no word is spoken except that a watcher, "Raqib", who is ready, "Atid", is there. They are known as Raqib and Atid. Another surah (82:10-12) says there are noble angels recording what we do.

### Their duty
• Raqib and Atid write down our words and actions. Muslims commonly teach that one angel writes the good deeds and one writes the bad deeds.
• They are with us all the time until death.
• Their record will be shown on the Day of Judgement.

### What we learn
1. Be careful with what we say. Words are recorded.
2. Do good deeds, even small ones, because they are written.
3. When we do wrong, we should say sorry to Allah and repent. Allah forgives those who turn back to Him.
4. We should not hurt others or tell lies, even in secret.

### Example
Neema is tempted to tell a lie so that she can avoid being punished at school. She remembers that the angels write down her words. She says the truth and says sorry instead.

### Memory game
Write the names on one set of cards and the duties on another. Mix them up. Take turns matching them with a partner. You can also make a crossword or a word search with the angel names.

### Remember
When we mention the name of an angel, we say "alayhis-salam" (peace be upon him), written A.S.

## PAGE 6: How belief in angels changes our lives
Believing in angels is not only about knowing names. It changes how we live.

### Effects of belief in angels
• We feel that Allah is close to us and takes care of the world.
• We become more honest, because angels record our deeds.
• We are more thankful when it rains or when we receive a gift.
• We respect the Qur'an, which came through Jibril.
• We remember death and the Day of Judgement, which keeps us humble.
• We love angels because they obey Allah, and we try to obey Him too.

### Manners
• We do not worship angels or ask them for help. We worship Allah only.
• We do not imagine angels as girls or as children of Allah. That is not what Islam teaches.

### Brainstorm
In pairs, list ways angels' duties are important in your life. For example, the rain that fills the tanks and waters the shamba, the guidance in the Qur'an, and the record of your good deeds. Write your points in your exercise book.

### Common mistakes
• Saying angels are Allah's daughters.
• Thinking that angels can act on their own, without Allah's permission.
• Forgetting that belief in angels is a pillar of Iman.

## QUIZ
1. What were angels created from?
   A) Clay
   B) Light
   C) Fire
   D) Water
   Answer: B
   Why: The Prophet (S.A.W.) taught, in Muslim, that angels were created from light.
2. Which angel brought the Qur'an to Prophet Muhammad (S.A.W.)?
   A) Mikail
   B) Israfil
   C) Jibril
   D) Malakul Maut
   Answer: C
   Why: Jibril's duty is to bring revelation to the Prophets.
3. Which angel will blow the Trumpet on the Day of Judgement?
   A) Israfil
   B) Jibril
   C) Raqib
   D) Mikail
   Answer: A
   Why: Israfil is the angel who blows the Trumpet by Allah's command.
4. What is the duty of Raqib and Atid?
   A) To bring rain
   B) To take souls
   C) To record a person's good and bad deeds
   D) To blow the Trumpet
   Answer: C
   Why: They are the two angels who record what each person says and does.
5. Which of these is true about angels?
   A) They sometimes disobey Allah
   B) They are Allah's daughters
   C) They never disobey Allah
   D) They eat and sleep like people
   Answer: C
   Why: The Qur'an (66:6) says they do not disobey what Allah commands them.
6. Give the duty of each angel: Mikail and Malakul Maut. (2 marks)
   Answer: Mikail is in charge of rain and provision; Malakul Maut takes the souls at death.
   Why: 1 mark for each correct duty.
7. Name the six pillars of Iman. (3 marks)
   Answer: Belief in Allah, His angels, His books, His messengers, the Last Day, and destiny (qadar).
   Why: 1 mark for every two correct pillars.
8. Explain how belief in Raqib and Atid can help a child to behave well. (3 marks)
   Answer: The child knows that words and actions are recorded, so he or she will try to tell the truth, avoid wrong actions and do good deeds. If they make a mistake, they will say sorry to Allah.
   Why: 1 mark for knowing deeds are recorded, 1 mark for the good behaviour, 1 mark for repentance.
9. State one reason why Allah created angels. (2 marks)
   Answer: To worship Him and to carry out His orders, such as bringing revelation or recording deeds.
   Why: 1 mark for worship or obeying Allah, 1 mark for an example of duty.
