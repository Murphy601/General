## PAGE 1: A clean mouth pleases Allah
Have you ever met someone with a bright, clean smile and fresh breath? Clean teeth make us look good and feel good. Islam gives great importance to cleanliness, and that includes our teeth.

The Prophet (S.A.W.) said: "Brushing of teeth is a means of purification of the mouth and pleases Allah." This is the hadith given in your syllabus, and scholars record it in the books of hadith such as those of Ibn Majah and An-Nasa'i.

### The siwak
In the time of the Prophet, people cleaned their teeth with a siwak. A siwak is a small stick from the branch or root of the arak tree. People chewed the end into soft fibres and rubbed it on the teeth. Today many Muslims use a toothbrush and toothpaste, and some also use a siwak or mswaki. The idea is the same: clean the mouth.

### Words to know
• Oral hygiene: keeping the mouth and teeth clean.
• Ibadah: worship, any act done to please Allah.
• Purification: making something clean.

## PAGE 2: How often did the Prophet clean his teeth?
The Prophet (S.A.W.) loved the siwak so much that he said: "If it were not hard for my followers, I would have ordered them to use the siwak before every prayer." This hadith is reported by Al-Bukhari and Muslim. He did not make it compulsory, but he showed how important it is.

### Times when the siwak or brush is recommended
• Before making wudhu and praying.
• On waking up from sleep. The Prophet used the siwak when he got up at night.
• Before reading or reciting the Qur'an.
• When the mouth changes smell, for example after eating onions or garlic.
• When entering the home and before meeting people.

### Think about this
Because we pray five times a day, the hadith reminds us to keep our mouths fresh every time we speak to Allah and to other people. A clean mouth is a mark of respect.

## PAGE 3: Why brushing teeth matters
Our teeth are a gift from Allah. They help us to chew food, speak clearly and smile. If we do not clean them, tiny pieces of food stay between the teeth. Germs feed on them, especially on sugar from sweets, soda and juice. This produces acid, which makes holes in the teeth called cavities. This causes pain, bad breath and sometimes lost teeth.

### Health benefits of brushing
• Prevents toothache and cavities.
• Keeps the gums healthy.
• Gives fresh breath, so we do not disturb others when we speak or pray in a row.
• Helps us eat well, because healthy teeth chew food properly.

### Spiritual benefits
• We follow the Sunnah, so brushing can earn reward from Allah when we intend it as worship.
• We keep the body pure, which prepares us to meet Allah in prayer.

### Discussion
Would you rather visit the dentist for a painful tooth or brush every day? Talk in pairs and share your answer.

## PAGE 4: How to brush your teeth correctly
Brushing takes about two minutes. Follow these steps.

### Steps
1. Say Bismillah.
2. Wet the brush and put a small amount (about the size of a pea) of toothpaste on it.
3. Brush the outer surfaces of your upper and lower teeth in small circles.
4. Brush the inner surfaces, behind your teeth.
5. Brush the flat chewing surfaces on top of the teeth.
6. Gently brush your tongue.
7. Spit out the toothpaste and rinse your mouth with clean water.
8. Rinse your brush and store it in a clean, dry place.

### When to brush
• In the morning after waking up.
• At night before sleeping. This is the most important time because saliva reduces while we sleep.
• After meals, if you can, especially after sweet foods.

### Good habits
• Do not share your toothbrush.
• Change your toothbrush when the bristles are bent, usually every three months.
• Eat fewer sweets, and drink water.
• Visit a dentist or a health worker for a check-up.

## PAGE 5: Using the siwak
A siwak (mswaki) is still used in many homes and mosques in Kenya, especially along the coast. It is cheap, natural and follows the Sunnah.

### Rules for using the siwak
1. Use a clean stick from a tree that is not poisonous. Ask an adult which tree is suitable.
2. Cut a fresh end and chew it gently until the fibres are soft.
3. Hold the siwak in your right hand and rub the teeth from side to side and from the gum towards the tip of the tooth.
4. Rinse the stick with water after use.
5. Cut off the used end when it becomes dry or dirty.

### Toothbrush or siwak?
Both are good for cleaning teeth. A brush with toothpaste helps remove food and protects against cavities. The siwak is the way of the Prophet. Many people use both.

### A short poem to remember
Morning and night, I brush my teeth,
Bismillah first, with a smile beneath.
A clean mouth pleases my Lord above,
And my family too will feel my love.

## PAGE 6: Putting it into practice
Make brushing teeth part of your daily worship. When you brush with the intention of following the Prophet (S.A.W.) and keeping clean for prayer, it becomes ibadah.

### Ideas for a class activity
• Demonstrate the steps of brushing using a model or your own brush.
• Make a chart of times to brush teeth and put it on your bathroom wall.
• Compose a song, poem or qasida about brushing teeth and perform it for your class.

### Summary
• The Prophet (S.A.W.) taught that cleaning the teeth purifies the mouth and pleases Allah.
• He encouraged the siwak at prayer times and after waking.
• Clean teeth prevent cavities, toothache and bad breath.
• Brush twice a day, use a small amount of toothpaste, and rinse well.

### Common mistakes
• Brushing only the front teeth.
• Brushing very hard. Gentle brushing is better.
• Forgetting the bedtime brush.
• Using a toothbrush that is old or shared.

## QUIZ
1. What is a siwak?
   A) A type of prayer mat
   B) A small stick used to clean the teeth
   C) A kind of water pot
   D) A prayer time
   Answer: B
   Why: A siwak is a natural stick, often from the arak tree, that is chewed at the end and used to clean the teeth.
2. What did the Prophet (S.A.W.) say about the siwak before prayer?
   A) He said it is forbidden
   B) He said it is only for older people
   C) He said he would have ordered it before every prayer if it were not hard for his followers
   D) He said it is only for Ramadan
   Answer: C
   Why: The hadith in Al-Bukhari and Muslim shows how much he encouraged it, though he did not make it compulsory.
3. Which is the most important time for a child to brush teeth?
   A) Only on Fridays
   B) Only when there is pain
   C) Before sleeping at night
   D) Only after eating sweets
   Answer: C
   Why: Less saliva flows during sleep, so food and germs are more likely to damage the teeth if we do not brush.
4. Which food habit most damages teeth?
   A) Drinking water
   B) Eating a lot of sweets and soda without brushing
   C) Eating vegetables
   D) Eating fruit like a guava
   Answer: B
   Why: Sugar is used by germs to make acid, which causes cavities.
5. When brushing, how much toothpaste should a child use?
   A) A whole tube
   B) None at all
   C) About the size of a pea
   D) A full handful
   Answer: C
   Why: A small amount is enough and it is safer, because swallowing toothpaste is not good.
6. Write the hadith on brushing teeth in your own sentence and say where it is recorded. (3 marks)
   Answer: Brushing the teeth purifies the mouth and pleases Allah. It is recorded in the books of hadith such as that of Ibn Majah, as stated in the syllabus.
   Why: 1 mark for purification, 1 mark for pleasing Allah, 1 mark for the source.
7. State three times when a Muslim can use the siwak or toothbrush. (3 marks)
   Answer: Any three: before wudhu or prayer, on waking up, before reading the Qur'an, when the mouth smells, in the morning and at night.
   Why: 1 mark for each correct time.
8. Give two health benefits of brushing teeth. (2 marks)
   Answer: It prevents cavities and toothache, and it gives fresh breath. Healthy gums are also accepted.
   Why: 1 mark for each benefit.
9. Explain how brushing teeth can become an act of worship. (3 marks)
   Answer: When we brush with the intention of following the Sunnah of the Prophet and keeping clean for prayer, Allah rewards us. We keep the body pure, which pleases Allah.
   Why: 1 mark for intention, 1 mark for following the Sunnah, 1 mark for reward or pleasing Allah.
