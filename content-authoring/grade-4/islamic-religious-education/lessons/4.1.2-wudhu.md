## PAGE 1: What is wudhu?
Wudhu is the washing of certain parts of the body with clean water, with the intention of getting ready for prayer and other acts of worship. It is also called ablution. It is a way of becoming ritually pure.

Allah tells us in the Qur'an (5:6) that when we stand for prayer, we should wash our faces and our hands up to the elbows, wipe our heads, and wash our feet up to the ankles. So wudhu is a command from Allah.

The Prophet (S.A.W.) said that Allah does not accept the prayer of a person who has broken his wudhu until he makes wudhu again (Al-Bukhari and Muslim). This shows why we must keep our wudhu and repeat it when it is broken.

### Key words
• Wudhu: washing the parts of the body for prayer.
• Niyyah: the intention in the heart.
• Nullifier: something that breaks or cancels wudhu.
• Ritual purity: being clean in the way Islam requires for worship.

### Wudhu and health
Washing the hands, mouth, nose, face, arms and feet several times a day keeps germs away. It also refreshes us.

## PAGE 2: A quick reminder of the steps
The steps of wudhu are done in order. Each part is washed clean with water.

1. Make the intention in your heart to make wudhu, and say Bismillah.
2. Wash both hands up to the wrists, three times.
3. Rinse the mouth with water, three times.
4. Sniff water into the nose and blow it out, three times.
5. Wash the whole face, three times.
6. Wash the right arm and then the left arm up to and including the elbows, three times each.
7. Wipe the head with wet hands.
8. Wipe the ears.
9. Wash the right foot and then the left foot up to and including the ankles, three times each.
10. Then recite the words of testimony: "Ash-hadu an la ilaha illallah, wahdahu la sharika lah, wa ash-hadu anna Muhammadan abduhu wa rasuluh." It means "I bear witness that there is no god but Allah alone, with no partner, and I bear witness that Muhammad is His servant and messenger."

## PAGE 3: What nullifies wudhu?
A nullifier of wudhu is something that breaks it. After it happens, we are no longer in a state of wudhu and cannot pray until we make wudhu again.

### The main nullifiers
1. Anything that comes out of the front or back passage, such as urine, stool or passing wind.
2. Deep sleep, when a person loses awareness, especially sleeping lying down. Scholars explain that a person who just dozes off while sitting firmly does not break wudhu.
3. Losing one's senses, for example by fainting, becoming unconscious or being unable to think clearly because of illness or medicine.
4. Touching the private part directly with the palm of the hand. This is the view of the Shafi'i school, followed by many Muslims in Kenya, so ask your teacher about your family's school.

### Things that do not break wudhu
• Eating and drinking.
• Talking, laughing quietly or crying.
• Sweating.
• Cutting hair or nails.

### Example
Zuhura made wudhu in the morning for Fajr. Later she went to the toilet. Her wudhu is now broken. She must make wudhu again before Dhuhr. Her friend Mwajuma only ate lunch and talked. Her wudhu is still valid.

## PAGE 4: If in doubt
Sometimes we are not sure whether our wudhu has broken. Islam teaches ease. The rule is to stay on what we were sure of. If you were sure that you had made wudhu and you are only doubtful whether it broke, then you are still in wudhu. This stops us from worrying too much.

The Prophet (S.A.W.) told a person who felt something during prayer not to leave unless he heard a sound or smelt something (Al-Bukhari and Muslim). This means do not act on doubt alone.

### Other cases
• If you are sure it broke, make wudhu again.
• If your wudhu breaks while praying, stop the prayer, make wudhu and begin the prayer again.
• You do not need to renew wudhu for every prayer if it is not broken.

### Activity
Read the situations and decide if wudhu is still valid.
• Halima has a small nap while sitting in class, and does not fall over. (Wudhu is still valid.)
• Omar passes wind after wudhu. (Broken.)
• Aisha drinks a cup of milk. (Still valid.)
• Baraka is sure that he made wudhu but he is not sure if anything broke it. (Still valid.)

## PAGE 5: Why wudhu is important
### It is required for prayer
Prayer is not valid without wudhu. It is like a key that opens the door to prayer.

### It cleans and refreshes
Wudhu washes the parts of the body that are often exposed to dirt. It protects health and makes us feel fresh and calm.

### It has spiritual reward
The Prophet (S.A.W.) told us that on the Day of Judgement his followers will be recognised by the shining marks of wudhu on their faces, hands and feet (Muslim). He also taught that wudhu, done well, wipes away small sins.

### It prepares the heart
When we wash, we remember that we are about to speak to Allah. This helps our hearts to be calm and respectful.

### It builds discipline
Making wudhu carefully teaches us to do things in order, to be patient and to save water. Islam teaches us not to waste water, even when we make wudhu.

## PAGE 6: When is it recommended to be in wudhu?
Wudhu is required before prayer. It is also good to be in a state of wudhu at other times.

### Times when wudhu is needed or encouraged
• Before every prayer if the wudhu is broken (required).
• Before touching or carrying the Mushaf, the written Qur'an (required, according to the majority of scholars).
• Before reciting the Qur'an, it is recommended, though it can be recited from memory without wudhu.
• Before going to sleep. The Prophet (S.A.W.) told Al-Bara ibn Azib to make wudhu as for prayer when going to bed (Al-Bukhari and Muslim).
• Before the adhkar and dua, and when entering the mosque.
• Before attending Islamic lessons, as a mark of respect.

### Practical tip
If it is convenient, try to remain in wudhu during the day, so that when the adhan is called you are ready and do not delay prayer. Keep water and a clean towel at hand.

### Common mistakes
• Wasting water while making wudhu.
• Leaving spots on the body unwashed, especially heels, elbows and behind the ears.
• Thinking that eating or laughing breaks wudhu.
• Praying without renewing wudhu after passing wind.

## QUIZ
1. What is wudhu?
   A) Washing with water certain parts of the body for prayer
   B) Fasting in the month of Ramadan
   C) Reciting the Qur'an loudly
   D) Giving money to the poor
   Answer: A
   Why: Wudhu is ablution: washing the parts of the body with the intention of preparing for prayer.
2. Which of these breaks wudhu?
   A) Drinking water
   B) Talking
   C) Passing wind
   D) Eating rice
   Answer: C
   Why: Anything that comes out of the front or back passage breaks wudhu.
3. What is the meaning of "nullifier of wudhu"?
   A) A step in wudhu
   B) Something that breaks wudhu
   C) A drink for after wudhu
   D) A type of prayer
   Answer: B
   Why: A nullifier cancels a person's state of wudhu, so wudhu must be made again.
4. Before which of these is a Muslim required to have wudhu?
   A) Eating lunch
   B) Playing football
   C) Praying swalah
   D) Walking to school
   Answer: C
   Why: Prayer is not valid without wudhu.
5. Where does the Qur'an command us to wash our faces, hands and feet before prayer?
   A) Surah 5, verse 6
   B) Surah 112
   C) Surah 1
   D) Surah 114
   Answer: A
   Why: Qur'an 5:6 gives the command for wudhu.
6. List three things that nullify wudhu. (3 marks)
   Answer: Any three: urine, stool or passing wind; deep sleep; fainting or loss of senses; touching the private part directly (according to the Shafi'i school).
   Why: 1 mark for each correct nullifier.
7. State three times when it is recommended to be in wudhu. (3 marks)
   Answer: Any three: before prayer, before touching the Qur'an, before sleeping, before reciting Qur'an, before attending lessons.
   Why: 1 mark for each correct time.
8. Explain why wudhu is important in the life of a Muslim. (3 marks)
   Answer: It is needed for the prayer to be accepted; it cleans and refreshes the body; and it brings reward and prepares the heart to stand before Allah.
   Why: 1 mark for each valid point.
9. Kamal made wudhu at 6 a.m. At 10 a.m. he ate lunch and talked to friends. Is his wudhu still valid? Give a reason. (2 marks)
   Answer: Yes, because eating and talking do not break wudhu.
   Why: 1 mark for yes and 1 mark for the reason.
