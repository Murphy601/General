## PAGE 1: Getting to know Allah through His names
Iman means faith. The first pillar of Iman is to believe in Allah, the One who created us and everything around us. But how do we get to know Him? One way is by learning His names and attributes. An attribute is a quality that describes someone. When we say a teacher is kind, "kind" is an attribute of the teacher.

Allah has many beautiful names. The Qur'an says (7:180) that to Allah belong the best names, so we should call upon Him by them. The Prophet (S.A.W.) told us that Allah has ninety-nine names, and whoever learns them and understands them will be rewarded (Al-Bukhari and Muslim). These names are called Asma'ul Husna, the Most Beautiful Names.

### Allah is unlike anything
The Qur'an (42:11) teaches that there is nothing like Allah, and He is the All-Hearing, the All-Seeing. So we learn His names and meanings, but we never compare Him to a human being or picture Him.

### In this lesson we learn six names
Ar-Rahman, Ar-Rahim, Al-Malik, As-Salam, As-Sami' and Al-Basir. Each name is a lesson about who Allah is.

## PAGE 2: Ar-Rahman and Ar-Rahim
These two names come from the word "rahmah", which means mercy.

### Ar-Rahman: The Most Kind
Ar-Rahman means the One whose mercy is for everyone. Allah's kindness covers all creatures, believers and non-believers, people and animals. The rain, the sun, the air, food and water are signs of Ar-Rahman. He gives them to all.

### Ar-Rahim: The Most Merciful
Ar-Rahim means the One who shows special mercy. Scholars explain that this includes His special mercy to the believers, especially on the Day of Judgement. Allah forgives sins and rewards good deeds.

### You already know them
We say both names in every Bismillah: "Bismillah ir-Rahman ir-Rahim". They also come in Al-Fatiha. So each time we start a task with Bismillah, we remember Allah's mercy.

### Example
A farmer plants maize, and the rains fall on his farm. He did not make the rain. He thanks Ar-Rahman, who sends rain to all. When a child makes a mistake and says sorry, Allah forgives. This shows Ar-Rahim.

### Our response
Since Allah is merciful to us, we should be merciful to people, animals and the environment.

## PAGE 3: Al-Malik and As-Salam
### Al-Malik: The King, the Owner
Al-Malik means the One who owns and rules everything. He is the true King. Human kings and presidents rule for a short time and they can be wrong. Allah's rule is perfect and it never ends. Everything in the heavens and the earth belongs to Him.

What we learn: Our money, our homes and even our bodies are gifts from Allah. We should use them well, and we do not need to fear any human being more than Allah. We should also obey Him, because He knows what is best.

### As-Salam: The Source of Peace
As-Salam means the One who is free from all faults and gives peace and safety. He is perfect. He gives peace to hearts. Paradise is also called the home of peace.

What we learn: When Muslims greet each other, we say "As-salamu alaykum", which means "peace be upon you". This greeting is a prayer for safety and peace. We ask Allah, As-Salam, to give peace to that person. We should also be peacemakers in our homes, schools and villages.

### Compare
• Al-Malik teaches us that Allah is the Owner and Ruler.
• As-Salam teaches us that Allah gives peace and is perfect.

## PAGE 4: As-Sami' and Al-Basir
### As-Sami': The All-Hearing
As-Sami' means the One who hears everything. He hears loud words and whispers, spoken prayers and silent thoughts. Allah never gets tired of listening. When Prophet Musa (A.S.) and his brother Harun (A.S.) were afraid of the tyrant Pharaoh, Allah told them (Qur'an 20:46) that He was with them, hearing and seeing.

### Al-Basir: The All-Seeing
Al-Basir means the One who sees everything. He sees what is in the light and in the dark, near and far, big and small. Nothing is hidden from Him.

### What we learn
• Allah hears our dua, so we can ask Him for anything, at any time.
• Allah sees us even when we are alone, so we should be honest when nobody is watching.
• We should not say bad words or do bad things secretly, since Allah hears and sees.

### Example
Kevin finds a phone charger left on a desk in an empty classroom. No one sees him. He remembers that Allah sees him, and he takes it to the teacher. That is the fruit of believing in Al-Basir.

## PAGE 5: Using Allah's names in dua
Dua is asking Allah for something. Allah told us to call Him by His beautiful names, so a good dua uses the name that matches what we need. We start with praise, then ask.

### How to match a name to a need
• Ya Rahman, Ya Rahim, have mercy on me and forgive me. (when asking for mercy or forgiveness)
• Ya Salam, grant us peace in our home and our country. (when asking for peace)
• Ya Malik, You own all things; give me good things. (when we need help)
• Ya Sami', hear my dua. (when praying)
• Ya Basir, You see my situation; guide and help me. (when in trouble)

### Notes on manners
1. Begin with Bismillah and praise Allah.
2. Use a humble voice, not shouting.
3. Believe that Allah will answer, in the way that is best for you.
4. Say Amin at the end.

### Pair activity
With a partner, take turns saying a dua that uses one of the six names. For example: "Ya Sami', hear my dua and help me pass my exams."

## PAGE 6: Summary and how to live by these names
Knowing Allah's names makes our love and respect for Him grow. It also changes how we behave.

### Summary
• Ar-Rahman: Allah's mercy is for all creation.
• Ar-Rahim: Allah has special mercy, especially for believers.
• Al-Malik: Allah is the King and Owner of everything.
• As-Salam: Allah is perfect and gives peace.
• As-Sami': Allah hears everything.
• Al-Basir: Allah sees everything.

### Living by these names
1. Be merciful to people, animals and plants.
2. Use what Allah owns responsibly and share.
3. Bring peace by greeting and settling quarrels kindly.
4. Talk to Allah in dua because He hears.
5. Behave well even in secret because He sees.

### Common mistakes
• Thinking Allah is like a human being. Nothing is like Him.
• Thinking that a name only applies to one group of people. Ar-Rahman is for all creation.
• Calling on other things for help instead of Allah, who hears and sees us.

## QUIZ
1. What does "Ar-Rahman" mean?
   A) The King
   B) The All-Seeing
   C) The Most Kind (whose mercy is for all)
   D) The All-Hearing
   Answer: C
   Why: Ar-Rahman is the name that shows Allah's mercy to all of His creation.
2. Which name means "The All-Hearing"?
   A) Al-Basir
   B) As-Sami'
   C) As-Salam
   D) Al-Malik
   Answer: B
   Why: As-Sami' means the One who hears everything.
3. Which name of Allah teaches that He owns and rules everything?
   A) Al-Malik
   B) Ar-Rahim
   C) As-Salam
   D) Al-Basir
   Answer: A
   Why: Al-Malik means the King and Owner of all things.
4. Why should a Muslim do good even when nobody is watching?
   A) Because parents always check
   B) Because Allah is Al-Basir and sees everything
   C) Because there might be a camera
   D) Because it is fashionable
   Answer: B
   Why: Believing that Allah sees everything helps us to stay honest in private.
5. When we say "As-salamu alaykum" we pray for
   A) food
   B) money
   C) peace
   D) rain
   Answer: C
   Why: The greeting means "peace be upon you", and As-Salam is one of Allah's names.
6. Match each name with its meaning: Ar-Rahim, As-Salam, Al-Basir. (3 marks)
   Answer: Ar-Rahim: the Most Merciful; As-Salam: the Source of Peace; Al-Basir: the All-Seeing.
   Why: 1 mark for each correct match.
7. Write a short dua that uses one of the names of Allah. (2 marks)
   Answer: For example: "Ya Rahman, Ya Rahim, have mercy on me and my family."
   Why: 1 mark for using a correct name and 1 mark for a suitable request.
8. Explain what we learn about Allah from the two names Ar-Rahman and Ar-Rahim. (3 marks)
   Answer: Allah is full of mercy. His mercy in this world is for everyone, such as rain and food. He also has special mercy for the believers.
   Why: 1 mark for mercy, 1 mark for mercy to all, 1 mark for special mercy for believers.
9. Kamau is alone at home and is tempted to take sweets that are not his. Using a name of Allah, explain what he should remember. (3 marks)
   Answer: He should remember that Allah is Al-Basir, the All-Seeing. Allah sees him even when he is alone, so he should not take them.
   Why: 1 mark for the name, 1 mark for its meaning, 1 mark for the correct behaviour.
