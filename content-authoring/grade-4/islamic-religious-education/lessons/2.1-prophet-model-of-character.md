## PAGE 1: What is a hadith?
A hadith is a saying, action or approval of Prophet Muhammad (S.A.W.) that was remembered and passed on by his companions and then written down by scholars. The hadith help us to understand how the Prophet lived, and how we can follow his example. His way of life is called the Sunnah.

Every hadith is quoted with the name of the book or scholar who recorded it. For example, "narrated by Al-Bukhari" means that the scholar Imam Al-Bukhari collected it in his book. When we say the Prophet's name, Muslims add "Salla Allahu alayhi wa sallam", which means "may Allah's peace and blessings be upon him". We write this as S.A.W.

### Why we study hadith
• The Qur'an tells us what Allah wants. The Prophet showed us how to do it in real life.
• The hadith teach us good manners, kindness and worship.
• When we follow the Prophet, we show our love for him.

### Think about it
Who is a person you look up to at home or school? What do they do that makes you respect them? In this lesson we will study the best example for all people, the Prophet (S.A.W.).

## PAGE 2: The mission to perfect good manners
The Prophet (S.A.W.) said: "I have been sent for the purpose of perfecting good morals." This hadith is narrated by Al-Hakim.

The word "morals" means akhlaq, which are our habits of behaviour: how we speak, how we treat others and how we act when nobody is watching. The Prophet said that a big part of his mission was to complete and perfect good character.

### What the hadith teaches us
• Good character is not a small thing in Islam. It is part of our faith.
• A Muslim is not only someone who prays. A Muslim is also honest, kind and polite.
• We must work at good manners every day, just as we practise reading and Mathematics.

The Qur'an also praises the Prophet. In Surah Al-Qalam (68:4) Allah says, in meaning, that the Prophet is upon a great standard of character.

### Example
Zawadi always prays on time but she shouts at her little brother and takes his mandazi without asking. Does this show the good character taught by the Prophet? No. Prayer and good behaviour should go together.

## PAGE 3: The trustworthy one
Before he became a Prophet, the people of Makkah called Muhammad (S.A.W.) "Al-Amin", which means the trustworthy. Even people who later disagreed with his message left their valuable things with him for safekeeping, because they knew he would return them.

### Trustworthiness means
• keeping a promise
• returning what you borrow, such as a pencil or a book
• keeping a secret that was given to you in trust
• doing a job properly even when nobody checks

### A well-known story
When the Kaaba in Makkah was being rebuilt, the leaders of the tribes argued about who should place the Black Stone in its corner. They agreed to accept the judgement of the first person to enter the courtyard, and that person was Muhammad (S.A.W.). He spread out his cloak, placed the stone on it, and asked a leader from each tribe to hold an edge of the cloak and lift it together. Then he set the stone in its place with his own hands. Everyone was satisfied and there was no fighting.

This shows wisdom, fairness and peace-making.

## PAGE 4: Truthful, kind and generous
The Prophet (S.A.W.) was truthful. He did not lie, even in jokes. A truthful person says what is real and does not cheat in games, shops or exams.

### Kindness
The Prophet was kind to children, elders, neighbours and even animals. He greeted people first and listened to them. He taught that a smile is a kind of charity. This means that when you smile at someone and cheer them up, Allah rewards you as He rewards a person who gives.

### Generosity
The Prophet (S.A.W.) gave to the poor and rarely refused a person who asked him for something. He was also patient. When people insulted him, he did not answer with insults.

## PAGE 5: The Prophet as a youth
Muhammad (S.A.W.) was born in Makkah. His father died before he was born, and his mother died when he was still a small child. He was then cared for by his grandfather and later by his uncle Abu Talib.

As a young man he worked as a shepherd and later as a trader. He was known for honesty in business, and he never cheated on weights or measures. He did not join in the wrong habits of some people around him. He was hardworking and respectful to elders.

### Lessons for children today
• You can be a good example even when you are young.
• Being an orphan or being poor is not a reason to give up. The Prophet grew up without both parents and still became the best example of character.
• Honest work is honourable. Shepherding is a job that teaches patience, care and responsibility.

### Discussion
A shopkeeper in the market gives you extra change by mistake. What would a person of the Prophet's character do? He would return it and say the truth. This is being honest even when nobody notices.

## PAGE 6: Following the Prophet in our daily lives
It is not enough to know the character of the Prophet (S.A.W.). We should copy it. This is called emulating him.

### Ways to practise each trait
• Trustworthy: return borrowed items quickly and keep promises to friends.
• Truthful: tell the truth about a broken cup or a missed homework, and say sorry.
• Kind: share your lunch, help a classmate who is struggling and speak politely to the watchman and the cook.
• Generous: give something to a needy person, even if it is small.
• Patient: take a deep breath when a friend annoys you, instead of fighting.

### Role-play idea
In a group, act a short scene. One child finds a lost Ksh 50 note in the school field. Show how a person of good character handles it. Then act the wrong way and discuss the difference.

### Common mistakes
• Thinking that good character is for adults only.
• Being polite to the teacher but rude to friends or workers.
• Being truthful only when it is easy.

## QUIZ
1. Which of these is a hadith about the Prophet's mission?
   A) "Cleanliness is a game."
   B) "I have been sent for the purpose of perfecting good morals."
   C) "Speak loudly to be heard."
   D) "Money is the best friend."
   Answer: B
   Why: This hadith, narrated by Al-Hakim, is given in the syllabus and says the Prophet was sent to perfect good morals.
2. What does "Al-Amin" mean?
   A) The strong
   B) The rich
   C) The trustworthy
   D) The fast
   Answer: C
   Why: The people of Makkah called him Al-Amin, the trustworthy, because he was honest.
3. Which one is an example of trustworthiness?
   A) Keeping a secret told to you in trust
   B) Taking a friend's pen and not returning it
   C) Telling a lie to avoid trouble
   D) Copying answers in a test
   Answer: A
   Why: Trustworthy people keep secrets and promises and return what they borrow.
4. How did the Prophet (S.A.W.) settle the argument about the Black Stone?
   A) He asked the tribes to fight
   B) He placed it on a cloak and let each tribe's leader hold an edge to lift it
   C) He kept it for himself
   D) He left it on the ground
   Answer: B
   Why: This solution made every tribe share in the honour and stopped the quarrel.
5. What does the word "akhlaq" mean?
   A) Prayers
   B) Fasting
   C) Journeys
   D) Character and manners
   Answer: D
   Why: Akhlaq means moral character: the habits by which we treat others.
6. Name three character traits of the Prophet (S.A.W.). (3 marks)
   Answer: Any three of: trustworthy, truthful, kind, generous, patient.
   Why: 1 mark for each correct trait.
7. Explain why prayer alone is not enough without good manners. (3 marks)
   Answer: The Prophet was sent to perfect good morals, so faith includes behaviour. A person who prays but lies, shouts or cheats does not follow his example. Prayer and good character should go together.
   Why: 1 mark for the hadith idea, 1 mark for the example of wrong behaviour, 1 mark for the conclusion.
8. Describe one way a Grade 4 child can be trustworthy at school. (2 marks)
   Answer: For example, return a borrowed book on time, or hand in a lost item to the teacher.
   Why: 1 mark for an action and 1 mark for linking it to trustworthiness.
9. Write one lesson we learn from the Prophet's childhood and youth. (2 marks)
   Answer: Even a child who loses parents or is poor can become honest and hardworking.
   Why: 1 mark for a fact from his life and 1 mark for a suitable lesson.
