## PAGE 1: What are conditions for swalah?
A condition (shart) of swalah is something that must be in place before we begin the prayer, for the prayer to be valid. If a condition is missing, the prayer is not accepted and must be repeated.

Think of a football match. Before the game starts, the pitch must be marked and the players must be dressed. Those are conditions for starting. Swalah also has conditions we must meet before we say "Allahu Akbar".

Allah says in the Qur'an (4:103) that prayer is a duty upon the believers at fixed times. So swalah has time rules as well.

### The conditions we will study
1. Being a Muslim who is sane and old enough to understand.
2. Being pure: wudhu, and a clean body, clothes and place.
3. Covering the awrah, meaning dressing properly.
4. Facing the Qibla, the direction of the Kaaba in Makkah.
5. The time of the prayer has begun.

### Do not confuse
Conditions come before the prayer starts. Pillars (arkan) are the parts inside the prayer, which you will study next.

## PAGE 2: Purity and clean clothes
### Purity
The prayer is not accepted without wudhu. If a person needs ghusl (a full bath), he or she must do it before praying. The body, the clothes and the place where we pray must also be free of najasa such as urine, blood or dirt from animals.

### Example
Ali is about to pray and notices a wet spot of urine on his trousers. He should change or wash the trousers before he prays. A clean prayer mat can be used to make sure the ground is clean.

### Checking before prayer
• Is my wudhu still valid?
• Are my clothes clean?
• Is the place where I will stand and prostrate clean?

### Why purity?
We stand before Allah, so we must be clean. It also teaches us discipline and cleanliness that help our health. The Prophet (S.A.W.) told us that purity is half of faith.

### Sick people
If a person is ill and cannot use water, Islam gives ease. In some cases a person may use tayammum (clean earth) instead. Ask your teacher when this applies.

## PAGE 3: Dressing for prayer
The part of the body that must be covered in prayer is called the awrah. Covering it is a condition of swalah.

### What must be covered
• For boys and men: at least from the navel to the knees.
• For girls and women: the whole body except the face and the hands.

In practice, we go beyond the minimum. We wear clean, neat, modest clothes, because we stand before Allah. The Qur'an (7:31) tells us to take our proper dress when we go to every mosque.

### Examples of suitable prayer clothes
• A boy wearing a shirt and long trousers, or a kanzu, with a kofia if he wishes.
• A girl wearing a long dress and a scarf over the head, or a swalah dress (hijab).

### Rules for the clothes
1. They should be clean and free from najasa.
2. They should cover properly, not be see-through or tight.
3. They should not carry pictures or writing that disrespects Allah.
4. They can be simple. Expensive clothes are not needed.

### Class activity
Model suitable prayer clothes in class. Explain why each item is suitable.

## PAGE 4: Facing the Qibla
The Qibla is the direction of the Kaaba in Makkah. Allah commanded Muslims in the Qur'an (2:144) to turn their faces towards Al-Masjid al-Haram, in Makkah, wherever they are. Muslims all over the world face the same direction, which unites us.

### Where is the Qibla from Kenya?
Makkah lies to the north and a little to the east of Kenya. In most parts of Kenya the Qibla is roughly north. In Mombasa and Nairobi the direction is only slightly east of north. Exact directions depend on the town, so check with a compass, a Qibla app or the mosque's mihrab (the niche that marks the direction).

## PAGE 5: Finding the Qibla
### Using the sun
The sun rises in the east and sets in the west. Stand with your right arm stretched out towards the place where the sun rises in the morning. Your left arm points west. If you face forward, you are facing north, and this is roughly the Qibla in Kenya. This method is a good estimate. When you have a mosque or a compass nearby, check.

### Using a compass or a phone
1. Hold the compass flat and wait until the needle stops.
2. Turn until the needle points to N (north).
3. Face that direction and adjust to the Qibla, which is roughly north.
Many phones have a Qibla finder. Use it carefully: keep the phone flat and away from metal, and ask an adult to help you.

### Using the mosque
Every mosque is built so that the mihrab faces the Qibla. In an unknown place, ask a local Muslim.

### If you cannot find it
When a person is travelling or truly cannot know, he or she tries their best, and Allah accepts it. In fear or in a moving vehicle, the person faces the Qibla as far as possible.

### Social Studies link
Finding directions on a compass in Social Studies helps you to find the Qibla.

## PAGE 6: The times of prayer
There are five daily prayers. Each has a time window.

• Fajr: from the first light of dawn until the sun rises. It has 2 rak'ahs.
• Dhuhr: from when the sun passes its highest point at midday until the afternoon prayer time. It has 4 rak'ahs.
• Asr: in the afternoon, until the sun sets. It has 4 rak'ahs.
• Maghrib: right after the sun sets. It has 3 rak'ahs.
• Isha: when the red glow has left the sky and it is night. It has 4 rak'ahs.

### How do we know the times?
• Listen to the adhan from the mosque.
• Use a printed timetable from your local mosque.
• Use a prayer app on a phone.
• Watch the sun: the length of shadows and the light in the sky tell us the time.

### Reminders
• Try to pray at the beginning of the time.
• Do not delay a prayer past its time without a good excuse.

## QUIZ
1. What is a condition of swalah?
   A) A part done inside the prayer
   B) Something needed before the prayer, for it to be valid
   C) A voluntary dua
   D) A type of fasting
   Answer: B
   Why: Conditions are met before starting the prayer, for the prayer to be valid.
2. What is the Qibla?
   A) The direction of the Kaaba in Makkah
   B) The call to prayer
   C) A type of clothing
   D) A prayer mat
   Answer: A
   Why: Muslims face the Kaaba when they pray.
3. Which body part must a boy cover in prayer, at the very least?
   A) From the navel to the knees
   B) Only the head
   C) Only the feet
   D) Only the hands
   Answer: A
   Why: For boys and men the minimum awrah in prayer is from the navel to the knees.
4. Which direction is the Qibla roughly in most parts of Kenya?
   A) South
   B) West
   C) North
   D) Down
   Answer: C
   Why: Makkah lies to the north and slightly east of Kenya.
5. Which prayer has three rak'ahs?
   A) Fajr
   B) Maghrib
   C) Dhuhr
   D) Isha
   Answer: B
   Why: Maghrib has three rak'ahs; Fajr has two; Dhuhr, Asr and Isha have four each.
6. State four conditions of swalah. (4 marks)
   Answer: Any four: being Muslim and sane, purity (wudhu and clean body, clothes and place), covering the awrah, facing the Qibla, the time has come.
   Why: 1 mark for each correct condition.
7. Describe how you can use the sun to find the direction of the Qibla in Kenya. (3 marks)
   Answer: Stretch the right arm towards where the sun rises (east). Facing forward then means facing north, which is roughly the Qibla. Confirm with a mosque or compass when possible.
   Why: 1 mark for east with the right arm, 1 mark for facing north, 1 mark for checking.
8. Explain why a Muslim must check that clothes and the prayer place are clean before praying. (2 marks)
   Answer: Purity is a condition for a valid prayer, and we stand before Allah, so we come clean.
   Why: 1 mark for condition and 1 mark for the reason.
9. Name the five daily prayers in the order they come from dawn. (3 marks)
   Answer: Fajr, Dhuhr, Asr, Maghrib and Isha.
   Why: 3 marks for all five in order; 2 marks for four correct; 1 mark for two or three correct.
