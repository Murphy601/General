## PAGE 1: Who are the prophets?
A prophet (nabi) is a man chosen by Allah to receive revelation and guide people to worship Allah alone. A messenger (rasul) is a prophet who was given a message or a book to deliver. Belief in the prophets is one of the pillars of Iman. Muslims love and respect all of them and make no difference between them in belief.

Allah sent prophets to every nation. The Qur'an says (4:163-165) that Allah revealed to Muhammad as He revealed to Nuh and the prophets after him, and that He sent messengers to give good news and warnings so that people would have no excuse. Some prophets are named in the Qur'an and some are not.

### Why did Allah send prophets?
• To teach people to worship Allah alone.
• To show people how to live in a good and honest way.
• To be examples of good character.
• To warn about punishment for wrongdoing and give good news of reward.

### Do you know a prophet's name?
Many children are named after prophets, for example Ibrahim, Yusuf, Musa, Isa, Daudi, Suleiman, Yahya and Muhammad. Do you know someone at school named after a prophet? Their name is a beautiful reminder.

## PAGE 2: The twenty-five prophets named in the Qur'an
Muslims learn the names of twenty-five prophets who are mentioned by name in the Qur'an. Some are named in Surah 4 (verses 163-165), Surah 6 (verses 84-86) and Surah 21 (verses 85-88), which your syllabus suggests as places to read.

### The list

2. Idris
3. Nuh
4. Hud
5. Salih
6. Ibrahim
7. Lut
8. Ismail
9. Ishaq
10. Yaqub
11. Yusuf
12. Ayyub
13. Shuayb
14. Musa
15. Harun
16. Dhul-Kifl
17. Dawud
18. Sulaiman
19. Ilyas
20. Al-Yasa
21. Yunus
22. Zakariyya
23. Yahya
24. Isa
25. Muhammad (S.A.W.)

### Note
Adam (A.S.) was the first human and the first prophet. Muhammad (S.A.W.) is the last prophet. The Qur'an (33:40) calls him the Seal of the Prophets. After his name we say "Salla Allahu alayhi wa sallam". After other prophets we say "alayhis-salam".

### Memory tip
Learn five names each day. Chant them in a group, like a song, and repeat until you can say all twenty-five in order.

### Why family links help
When you know that Yusuf was the son of Yaqub, and Yaqub was the son of Ishaq, and Ishaq was the son of Ibrahim, you can remember four prophets in one chain. Muslim scholars also tell us that Prophet Muhammad (S.A.W.) came from the descendants of Ismail, the other son of Ibrahim. Try to say the chain aloud three times.

### Other links
• Musa and Harun were brothers. Harun helped Musa in his mission.
• Yahya was the son of Zakariyya.
• Sulaiman was the son of Dawud.
• Ismail helped his father Ibrahim to build the Kaaba.

## PAGE 4: Patience and obedience
### Patience: Prophet Ayyub (A.S.)
Ayyub was a good and rich man. Allah tested him with a serious illness and other losses. Ayyub did not complain against Allah. He called upon his Lord, saying that hardship had touched him and Allah is the Most Merciful of those who show mercy (Qur'an 21:83). Allah healed him. The Qur'an (38:44) calls him "an excellent servant", one who always turned back to Allah, and says Allah found him patient.
Lesson: Patience means staying calm and trusting Allah when something hard happens, like sickness, failing a test or losing a game.

### Patience: Prophet Nuh (A.S.)
Nuh called his people to Allah for 950 years (Qur'an 29:14). Most of them refused, but he did not give up.

### Obedience: Prophet Ibrahim and Prophet Ismail (A.S.)
Allah tested Ibrahim by showing him in a dream that he should sacrifice his son. Ibrahim told Ismail, who answered that his father should do what he was commanded, and that he would find him among the patient (Qur'an 37:102). Both were ready to obey Allah. Then Allah replaced the sacrifice with a ram. Muslims remember this on Eid al-Adha.
Lesson: Obeying Allah, and our parents in what is good, brings blessing.

## PAGE 5: Honesty, truthfulness and generosity
### Honesty and truthfulness: Prophet Yusuf (A.S.)
Yusuf was a son of Yaqub. His brothers were jealous of him and threw him in a well, but Allah saved him. Later, in Egypt, a woman tried to make him do wrong. Yusuf refused, saying that he feared Allah. Even when he was put in prison unfairly, he remained honest. Because he was truthful and wise, he was later given an important post. In the Qur'an (12:46), he is called "Siddiq", a man of great truthfulness.
Lesson: Honesty may cost us something now, but it is rewarded in the end.

### Truthfulness: Prophet Ibrahim (A.S.)
The Qur'an (19:41) describes Ibrahim as very truthful and a prophet.

### Generosity: Prophet Ibrahim (A.S.)
When guests arrived at Ibrahim's home, he quickly went to his family and brought a fattened calf to serve them (Qur'an 11:69 and 51:26). He welcomed strangers before asking anything. Muslims still remember Ibrahim as an example of hospitality.
Lesson: We should welcome visitors, share food and give to those in need. In many Kenyan homes, visitors are given tea and food. That is a good habit.

### Honesty: the Prophet Muhammad (S.A.W.)
People called him Al-Amin, the trustworthy, before he became a prophet.

## PAGE 6: Virtues at a glance
Each prophet showed many virtues. In this lesson we learn one story for each of five virtues, and match them.

### Summary table
• Patience: Ayyub, and also Nuh in his long preaching.
• Honesty: Yusuf, who refused wrongdoing.
• Truthfulness: Ibrahim and Yusuf, who are called truthful in the Qur'an.
• Generosity: Ibrahim, who served his guests quickly.
• Obedience: Ibrahim and Ismail, who were ready to follow Allah's command.

### Definition check
• Patience: staying calm and hopeful in hardship, without complaining against Allah.
• Honesty: not cheating or stealing, and keeping trust.
• Truthfulness: speaking what is true.
• Generosity: giving freely, without expecting anything back.
• Obedience: doing what Allah and those in rightful authority command, in what is good.

### Class activity
In groups, choose one virtue. Prepare a short drama that shows how a Grade 4 child can practise it at school or at home. Examples: sharing your lunch (generosity), returning extra change (honesty), waiting calmly for your turn (patience).

## PAGE 7: Applying the virtues today
The virtues of the prophets are not only stories. We can use them in daily life.

### Ideas
• Patience: You lose a race. Instead of getting angry, you say "Congratulations" and try again. When you are ill, you follow the doctor's advice and keep calling on Allah.
• Honesty: You find money at school. You give it to the teacher.
• Truthfulness: You broke a plate. You tell your mother the truth right away.
• Generosity: A classmate has no lunch. You share yours.
• Obedience: Your parents call you to come home before dark, and you come at once.

### Role-play scenes
1. A friend asks you to copy your test answers. What do you do?
2. Visitors come to your home. How do you welcome them?
3. Your younger sister makes a mess with your books. How do you show patience?

### Qur'an reading
The Qur'an tells us that in the stories of the prophets there are lessons for people of understanding (Surah Yusuf, 12:111, in meaning). So when we hear a story, we should ask, "What can I learn and do?"

## PAGE 8: Summary and beliefs
Belief in the prophets means that we accept that they were true messengers of Allah, we love them, and we follow the final prophet, Muhammad (S.A.W.), whose teaching is the last guidance for all people.

### Key points
• There were many prophets. Twenty-five are named in the Qur'an.
• Adam was the first prophet and Muhammad (S.A.W.) is the last.
• Some prophets were relatives. Ibrahim was the father of Ismail and Ishaq.
• Their virtues include patience, honesty, truthfulness, generosity and obedience.
• We follow their example by practising these virtues.

### Words to remember
• Nabi: prophet
• Rasul: messenger
• Wahy: revelation

### Common mistakes
• Saying that some prophets are more important, so we believe in some but not others. A Muslim believes in all prophets.
• Thinking the prophets were not human. They were humans, chosen by Allah.
• Worshipping a prophet. We respect and follow prophets, but we worship Allah alone.

## QUIZ
1. How many prophets are named in the Qur'an, as taught in Grade 4?
   A) 12
   B) 25
   C) 99
   D) 40
   Answer: B
   Why: Muslims learn the names of twenty-five prophets who are mentioned by name in the Qur'an.
2. Who was the first prophet?
   A) Nuh
   B) Musa
   C) Adam
   D) Isa
   Answer: C
   Why: Adam (A.S.) was the first human being and the first prophet.
3. Which prophet is remembered especially for patience in illness?
   A) Ayyub
   B) Yusuf
   C) Dawud
   D) Ibrahim
   Answer: A
   Why: Allah tested Ayyub with illness and he remained patient; the Qur'an praises him.
4. Which two prophets showed obedience when Ibrahim was told to sacrifice his son?
   A) Musa and Harun
   B) Ibrahim and Ismail
   C) Nuh and Hud
   D) Yusuf and Yaqub
   Answer: B
   Why: Ibrahim was ready to obey the command and Ismail was ready to submit; Allah then replaced the sacrifice with a ram.
5. Who is the last prophet?
   A) Isa
   B) Yahya
   C) Sulaiman
   D) Muhammad (S.A.W.)
   Answer: D
   Why: The Qur'an (33:40) calls Muhammad (S.A.W.) the Seal of the Prophets.
6. Write down any ten prophets mentioned in the Qur'an. (5 marks)
   Answer: Any ten of the twenty-five: Adam, Idris, Nuh, Hud, Salih, Ibrahim, Lut, Ismail, Ishaq, Yaqub, Yusuf, Ayyub, Shuayb, Musa, Harun, Dhul-Kifl, Dawud, Sulaiman, Ilyas, Al-Yasa, Yunus, Zakariyya, Yahya, Isa, Muhammad.
   Why: Half a mark for each correct name, up to 5 marks.
7. Name the prophet linked with each virtue: (a) generosity to guests (b) honesty in hard times. (2 marks)
   Answer: (a) Ibrahim (b) Yusuf.
   Why: 1 mark for each.
8. Explain how Prophet Ayyub (A.S.) shows patience. (3 marks)
   Answer: He was tested with illness and losses, but he did not complain against Allah. He called upon his Lord for mercy and stayed patient until Allah healed him.
   Why: 1 mark for the test, 1 mark for not complaining, 1 mark for calling upon Allah and being healed.
9. Describe one way a Grade 4 child can practise honesty at school. (2 marks)
   Answer: Hand in lost money to the teacher, or refuse to copy in a test.
   Why: 1 mark for a suitable action and 1 mark for linking it to honesty or the prophets.
10. Why do we say that Muslims believe in all the prophets? (3 marks)
   Answer: The Qur'an teaches that all prophets were sent by Allah with the same basic message, to worship Him alone. So Muslims respect all of them and do not make a difference between them in belief.
   Why: 1 mark for all sent by Allah, 1 mark for the same message, 1 mark for no difference in belief.
