## PAGE 1: Cleanliness in Islam
Islam loves cleanliness. The Prophet (S.A.W.) said that purity is half of faith (narrated by Muslim). The Qur'an (2:222) tells us that Allah loves those who turn to Him in repentance and those who keep themselves clean.

The Arabic word for purity is "twahara". It means being clean from dirt and impurity so that we can perform acts of worship such as prayer. Before we pray, our body, clothes and place of prayer must be clean.

### Two kinds of purity
• Physical purity: cleaning the body, clothes and surroundings from dirt and impurities (najasaat). Also making wudhu and ghusl.
• Spiritual purity: cleaning the heart from bad feelings such as envy, pride, hatred and dishonesty, by repenting and doing good deeds.

### Key words
• Najasa (plural najasaat): an impure thing, such as urine or blood, that must be washed off before prayer.
• Taahir: something pure and clean.
• Ibadah: acts of worship.

### Why is purity a condition for ibadah?
Prayer is a meeting with Allah, so we come clean, as we would dress well and wash before meeting an important person.

## PAGE 2: What are najasaat?
Najasaat are impure things. If they touch our body, clothes or place of prayer, we must clean them before praying. This is a condition for a valid prayer.

### Examples of najasaat
• Urine and stool of humans.
• Blood that flows out of the body.
• Vomit and pus.
• The dead body of an animal that died without being slaughtered properly.
• Saliva of a dog and a pig.
• Alcohol (in the common view).

### Things that are not najasa
• Human sweat, tears, and clean saliva.
• Water and soil.
• Milk and other foods.
Being unclean is not an insult to a person. A baby who passes urine is not "dirty" as a person. It just means that the place must be washed.

### Remember
Najasaat are not the same as normal dirt like mud. Mud can make our clothes dirty, but it is not najasa. We still wash muddy clothes for neatness, and a mosque will be cleaned.

## PAGE 3: Three types of najasaat
Scholars divide najasaat into three types, depending on how strong they are and how they are cleaned.

### 1. Mukhaffafah (light)
Example: the urine of a baby boy who is still fed only on milk and has not started eating solid food.
How to clean: sprinkle water over the spot until it is fully wet. You do not need to rub it.

### 2. Mutawassitah (medium)
Examples: urine, stool, blood, vomit and pus of a person, and the urine of a baby girl.
How to clean: remove the substance, then wash the place with water until the colour, smell and taste of the najasa have gone.

### 3. Mughalladha (heavy)
Example: the saliva or other wet contact with a dog or a pig.
How to clean: wash the place seven times with water, and one of these washings should be mixed with clean soil.

## PAGE 4: What we use to clean najasaat
### Water
Water is the main tool for cleaning. The Qur'an (25:48) says that Allah sends down pure water from the sky. Clean water from rain, wells, rivers, springs and taps can be used. Water that has changed in colour, smell or taste because of the najasa cannot be used to purify.

### Other items
• Soil: used once in the washing for heavy najasa.
• Soap or a cleaning powder: helps to remove the stain, but the place still must be rinsed with water.
• A clean cloth: to wipe and dry.
• Tissue paper or clean stones: an allowed way to clean after using the toilet when water is not available, but water is best.

### Gloves and care
When cleaning bigger najasaat, such as blood, we can use gloves or a clean cloth and then wash our hands well with soap and water.

## PAGE 5: Cleaning najasaat step by step
### Example 1: Urine of a baby girl on a mat
1. Take the mat outside or to a place where water can drain.
2. Remove any solid remains, if there are any.
3. Pour water over the spot until the smell and colour have gone.
4. Dry it in the sun or with a clean cloth.
This is medium najasa.

### Example 2: A little drop of urine on your trousers
1. Change into clean clothes if possible.
2. Wash the wet part with water and rub until no smell or colour remains.
3. Hang the trousers to dry.

### Example 3: A dog licks a plate
1. Wash the plate with water seven times.
2. Mix one washing with clean soil.
3. Rinse, and dry. Muslims are not asked to hate or hurt dogs. We are only careful about their saliva.

### After cleaning
• Wash your hands.
• Make wudhu if you are going to pray.
• Check your clothes and prayer place for wet spots.

## PAGE 6: Purity in daily life and summary
Twahara is not only for prayer time. A person who is always clean stays healthy and keeps people around them comfortable.

### Good habits
• After using the toilet, clean yourself with water and wash your hands with soap.
• Wash your hands before and after meals.
• Keep your prayer mat or place of prayer clean.
• Throw rubbish into bins and keep the home compound clean.
• Wear clean clothes, especially when going to the mosque or madrasa.

### Health link
Washing away najasaat prevents diseases like diarrhoea and cholera. So Islam's rules of cleanliness protect our health.

### Summary
• Twahara means purity. It is a condition for prayer and other acts of worship.
• Najasaat are impure things such as urine, stool, blood, vomit, pus and dog or pig saliva.
• There are three types: light, medium and heavy.
• Water is the main means of purification. Heavy najasa also needs soil.
• We keep our bodies, clothes and places clean, and we keep our hearts clean.

### Common mistakes
• Praying with a wet spot of najasa on the clothes.
• Confusing ordinary mud with najasa.
• Using dirty water to clean.
• Treating dogs cruelly. Islam teaches kindness to animals.

## QUIZ
1. What is the meaning of "twahara"?
   A) Fasting
   B) Purity
   C) Charity
   D) Journey
   Answer: B
   Why: Twahara is the Arabic word for purity, being clean for worship.
2. Which of these is a najasa?
   A) Water from a clean tap
   B) Human urine
   C) Human sweat
   D) Fresh milk
   Answer: B
   Why: Urine is one of the najasaat that must be washed off before prayer.
3. How is a light najasa, such as the urine of a baby boy on milk only, cleaned?
   A) By burning
   B) By sprinkling water on it
   C) By seven washings with soil
   D) It does not need cleaning
   Answer: B
   Why: Light najasa is purified by sprinkling water over the spot until it is fully wet.
4. How many times is a place washed for a heavy najasa, such as dog saliva?
   A) One time
   B) Three times
   C) Five times
   D) Seven times, once with soil
   Answer: D
   Why: The heavy najasa needs seven washings, and one of them is mixed with clean soil.
5. Which of these is the main means of cleaning najasaat?
   A) Perfume
   B) Water
   C) Paint
   D) Salt only
   Answer: B
   Why: Pure water is the main means of purification, as the Qur'an says that Allah sends down pure water.
6. Name the three types of najasaat and give one example of each. (3 marks)
   Answer: Mukhaffafah (light): urine of a baby boy on milk only. Mutawassitah (medium): urine, stool, blood or vomit. Mughalladha (heavy): dog or pig saliva.
   Why: 1 mark for each type with a correct example.
9. Describe how to clean urine that has fallen on a mat (medium najasa). (3 marks)
   Answer: Remove any solid parts, pour water over the spot until the colour and smell are gone, then dry it.
   Why: 1 mark for removing the substance, 1 mark for washing with water until traces go, 1 mark for drying.
7. Explain the difference between physical and spiritual purity. (2 marks)
   Answer: Physical purity is cleaning the body, clothes and place from najasaat; spiritual purity is cleaning the heart from bad feelings like envy and pride.
   Why: 1 mark for each meaning.
8. Why is it important to be clean before prayer? (2 marks)
   Answer: Purity is a condition for the prayer to be valid, and prayer is a meeting with Allah, so we come clean.
   Why: 1 mark for condition and 1 mark for the reason of respect or meeting Allah.
