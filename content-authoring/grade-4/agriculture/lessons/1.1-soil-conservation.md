## PAGE 1: Why we must look after our soil
Soil is where plants grow. Maize, beans, sukuma wiki and mangoes all take water and food from the soil through their roots. If the soil becomes poor, the crops become small and yellow, and the family harvests less food.

Soil can be spoiled in two main ways. When rain runs fast over bare ground it washes the top soil away. This is called soil erosion. Crops also use up the plant food in the soil, season after season, and nothing puts it back. To conserve soil means to look after it so that it stays healthy and stays where it is.

### Ways of conserving soil
• Cover the ground with grass, leaves or crop remains. This is called mulching.
• Plant trees, grass and cover crops so roots hold the soil.
• Dig terraces or plant along the slope in lines that follow the height of the land.
• Add organic manure such as compost to give the soil back its food.

In this lesson we learn about compost manure, a cheap way to feed the soil that every school and home can make.

### Think about it
Have you seen brown water running down a path after heavy rain? What do you think it is carrying away?

## PAGE 2: What is compost manure?
Compost manure is a dark, crumbly, sweet-smelling material made when plant and animal wastes rot down. Tiny living things in the soil, such as bacteria and fungi, and small creatures such as earthworms, feed on the wastes and break them into small pieces. What is left is compost. Plants can use it as food.

Compost is organic manure. Organic means it comes from things that were once alive. Fertiliser bought in a shop is made in a factory. Compost is made at home from waste that would otherwise be thrown away.

### Why compost is good for farming
• It feeds crops with the plant food they need.
• It makes hard soil softer, so roots can grow easily.
• It helps sandy soil to hold water for longer.
• It is cheap because we make it from waste.
• It cleans up the environment by using kitchen and shamba waste.

Compost also helps to conserve soil. Soil rich in compost sticks together better and is not washed away as easily.

### Two kinds of material
Compost heaps work best with a mix of two kinds of waste.
• Green materials are fresh and wet: fresh cut grass, weeds without seeds, vegetable peelings, fresh leaves and fresh animal dung from cattle, goats or chickens.
• Brown materials are dry: dry grass, dry leaves, straw, maize stalks cut small, and a little sawdust.

Green materials give plant food. Brown materials let in air and stop the heap from becoming a wet, smelly mass.

### Why keep some things out
• Plastic, tins and glass never rot and can cut people who handle the compost.
• Meat, fish bones and fatty food attract rats and flies and smell badly.
• Diseased plants can pass the disease to your crops.
• Dog, cat and human waste can carry germs that make people sick.
• Weeds that already have seeds may grow again when you spread the compost.

### Try it
Look at the kitchen waste at home. Sort it into two piles: waste that can go into compost and waste that cannot.

## PAGE 4: Making compost by the heap method
The heap method needs no special equipment. Ask a teacher or an adult to help with the digging and use gloves when you handle the wastes.

### Steps
1. Choose a shady, flat place that does not flood, and is near water and near the garden.
2. Mark a square about 1.5 metres by 1.5 metres and loosen the soil.
3. Lay a base of thick dry sticks or maize stalks. Air can move through the gaps.
4. Add a layer of dry brown material about as thick as your hand.
5. Add a layer of green material and a layer of animal dung.
6. Sprinkle a thin layer of soil or old compost. This adds the tiny living things that do the rotting.
7. Sprinkle water so that the heap is damp like a squeezed sponge, not dripping.
8. Repeat the layers until the heap is about as high as your chest. A heap that is too low loses heat, and one that is too high is hard to turn.
9. Cover the heap with banana leaves, grass or old sacks to keep the rain and sun off.

## PAGE 5: Looking after the heap and using the compost
A heap needs care while it rots.

### Care of the heap
• Check the wetness each week. If it is dry, sprinkle water. If it is too wet and smells sour, mix in dry material.
• After two to three weeks, turn the heap with a fork or spade. Move the outside to the middle and the inside to the outside. This gives air and helps everything rot evenly.
• Keep the cover on. Keep children and animals away from the heap. Put a warning stick or sign nearby.
• Wash your hands with soap after touching the heap.

### When is compost ready?
Compost is usually ready in about two to three months. It depends on the weather and the materials. Ready compost is dark brown, crumbly, and smells like fresh earth. You can no longer see the leaves and peelings. If you can still see whole pieces, it needs more time.

### Using the compost
• Mix a handful or two into each planting hole before you plant.
• Spread it around growing crops and cover it lightly with soil or mulch.
• Dig it into a vegetable bed before sowing.
Use a small amount at a time and compare. A bed with compost usually grows greener, stronger plants than a bed without it.

### Working together
Making a heap is easier when a group shares tasks: one collects grass, one brings kitchen waste, one waters, one turns. Unity makes the work light.

## PAGE 6: Summary and common mistakes
Compost turns waste into food for the soil. It conserves the environment because the wastes are reused, and it conserves soil because it makes the soil rich and firm.

### Remember
• Use a mix of green and brown materials.
• Build in layers and keep the heap damp, not soaking.
• Turn after two to three weeks. Cover the heap.
• Ready compost is dark, crumbly and smells like earth.

### Common mistakes
• Putting in plastic, glass or tins. These do not rot.
• Adding meat or oily food, which attracts rats and flies.
• Making the heap too dry or too wet.
• Using compost before it is ready, when it can still be hot inside and can harm young roots.
• Forgetting to wash hands after handling wastes.

### Small project
Make a small heap at school with grass cuttings, dry leaves and vegetable peelings from the kitchen. Write the date on a stick. Check it every week and record how it changes. After two months, use it in your school garden.

## QUIZ
1. What is soil erosion?
   A) Soil washed or blown away from the land
   B) Soil made richer by compost
   C) Water soaking into the soil
   D) Seeds growing in the soil
   Answer: A
   Why: Erosion happens when rain or wind carries away the top soil, especially from bare ground.
2. Which of these should NOT be put in a compost heap?
   A) Vegetable peelings
   B) Fresh cut grass
   C) Polythene bags
   D) Dry leaves
   Answer: C
   Why: Polythene never rots and it spoils the compost. The other three rot down well.
3. Which is a brown material for compost?
   A) Fresh kale leaves
   B) Dry maize stalks
   C) Fresh cow dung
   D) Banana peelings
   Answer: B
   Why: Brown materials are dry, like dry grass, straw and stalks. The others are fresh, so they are green materials.
4. Why do we sprinkle a little soil or old compost between the layers?
   A) To make the heap look neat
   B) To stop the heap from rotting
   C) To add the tiny living things that rot the wastes
   D) To make the heap heavier
   Answer: C
   Why: Soil and old compost carry bacteria and fungi that break the waste down.
5. How can you tell that compost is ready to use? (2 marks)
   Answer: It is dark brown and crumbly, it smells like fresh earth, and the original materials can no longer be recognised.
   Why: 1 mark for any two correct signs of ready compost (colour, crumbly feel, earthy smell, no whole pieces), 1 mark for a third or for correct explanation.
6. Explain two reasons why we should not put meat or fatty food in a compost heap. (2 marks)
   Answer: It attracts rats and flies, and it gives a bad smell.
   Why: 1 mark for each reason.
7. List the steps for starting a compost heap, in the correct order, using at least five steps. (4 marks)
   Answer: Choose a shady, well-drained place; mark the area and loosen the soil; lay sticks at the bottom; add layers of dry material, green material and dung; sprinkle soil and water; cover the heap.
   Why: 1 mark for each correct step in the right order, up to 4 marks.
8. Give three ways in which compost helps a farmer. (3 marks)
   Answer: It feeds the crops, it makes the soil soft and better at holding water, and it is cheap because it is made from waste.
   Why: 1 mark for each correct benefit.
9. A heap is smelly and very wet after heavy rain. What should you do? (2 marks)
   Answer: Cover the heap and turn it, mixing in dry material such as dry grass or leaves.
   Why: 1 mark for covering or turning, 1 mark for adding dry material to soak up the extra water.
