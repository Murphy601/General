## PAGE 1: Domestic animals in our community
Domestic animals are animals that people keep and look after at home or on the farm. They live near people, depend on people for food, water and shelter, and give us useful things in return. Wild animals live freely in nature.

### Common domestic animals
• Cattle: cows, bulls and calves.
• Sheep.
• Goats.
• Poultry: birds kept for eggs and meat, such as chickens, ducks and turkeys.

There are also other domestic animals such as pigs, rabbits, donkeys and, in dry areas, camels. Dogs and cats are kept as pets and to guard the home.

### Young and adult animals
Every animal has names for the male, the female and the young. Knowing them helps you talk clearly about animals.
• Cattle: bull (male), cow (female), calf (young).
• Sheep: ram (male), ewe (female), lamb (young).
• Goats: billy goat (male), nanny goat (female), kid (young).
• Chickens: cock or rooster (male), hen (female), chick (young).

### Discuss
Which of these animals are kept in your neighbourhood? Which are kept by your relatives in the village? Which ones have you seen at a market or on the road?

## PAGE 2: What cattle give us
Cattle are kept in many parts of Kenya. Some breeds are kept for milk (dairy cattle) and some for meat (beef cattle). Many farmers keep cattle for more than one use.

### Uses of cattle
• Milk: fresh milk is drunk, or made into yoghurt, cheese, butter and ghee. Milk has protein and calcium, which build strong bones and teeth.
• Meat: the meat from cattle is called beef.
• Hides and skins: they are made into leather for shoes, belts and bags.
• Manure: cow dung feeds the soil and can be used to make compost or biogas.
• Draught power: oxen are strong and can pull ploughs and carts, especially where tractors are too costly.
• Income: farmers sell milk, calves or cattle to earn money.

### Animal welfare
A cow needs plenty of clean water and good feed every day, a clean shelter, shade, and regular treatment for diseases. Cows that are well cared for produce more milk.

### Activity
Ask an adult how much milk a cow at a nearby home gives in a day, and what the family does with it. Write two sentences about what you find.

## PAGE 3: Sheep and goats
Sheep and goats are smaller than cattle and easier to keep. They can live in dry places where cattle would find little to eat, and are kept in many parts of Kenya.

### Uses of sheep
• Meat: sheep meat is called mutton.
• Wool: in cool highlands, some sheep have thick coats of wool that is sheared and made into blankets and sweaters. Many sheep in Kenya are kept mainly for meat.
• Skins: used to make leather.
• Manure: adds plant food to soil.
• Income: sold in markets.

### Uses of goats
• Meat: goat meat is a favourite in many homes.
• Milk: goat milk is drunk fresh and is easy to digest. Dairy goats give a good amount of milk.
• Skins: used to make leather, drums and bags.
• Manure: goat droppings make good manure for gardens.
• Income: goats are sold when a family needs money for fees or for other needs.

### A helpful fact
A goat can eat leaves, shrubs and grass and can live where the land is dry. Goats are good animals to keep when there is little land, but they must be kept away from crops and young trees.

### Compare
Sheep mostly eat grass. Goats like to browse on leaves and shoots from bushes and trees. This is one difference between them.

## PAGE 4: Poultry
Poultry are birds kept at home for food. Chickens are the most common in Kenya. Ducks, turkeys and geese are also kept.

### Uses of chickens
• Eggs: a laying hen gives eggs, which are full of protein and vitamins. Eggs can be boiled, fried or used in cooking.
• Meat: chicken meat is a popular dish for special meals.
• Manure: chicken droppings are strong manure for crops.
• Feathers: used to make pillows, dusters and decorations.
• Income: eggs and birds are sold. It is easy to start with a few chickens.

### Breeds
Indigenous chickens (kienyeji) scavenge for food, are tough, and give fewer eggs. Improved breeds are kept for more eggs or more meat, but need better feed and care.

### Caring for poultry
Chickens need a clean, dry house that keeps out rats, snakes and thieves. They need clean water and feed every day, and shelter from rain. Vaccinate them against common diseases when the animal doctor (veterinary officer) advises.

### Keep hens healthy
Wash your hands after touching chickens or collecting eggs. Wash eggs before cooking and cook them well.

### The food and farm cycle
Animals eat grass and crop leftovers. Their dung goes back to the crops as manure. Crops grow well and give food for people and for animals. This makes a cycle on the farm where nothing is wasted.

## PAGE 6: Caring for animals and sharing what we know
Domestic animals give us so much that we must care for them. This is called animal welfare. Every animal needs five things.

### Basic needs of domestic animals
• Clean water always available.
• Enough good feed.
• A clean, dry shelter with shade.
• Treatment when they are sick and regular vaccination and deworming.
• Kind handling. We should never beat or overwork an animal.

### Preparing a class presentation
In small groups, choose one domestic animal. Bring pictures from newspapers or drawings you make. Prepare a short talk with:
1. The name of the animal and its young.
2. Three uses of the animal.
3. Two ways to care for it.
4. Why it helps food production.

### Speaking tips
Speak clearly and slowly. Show your pictures. Look at your classmates. Listen to other groups without interrupting, and ask polite questions at the end.

### Match the animal to its product
Milk: cow or goat. Eggs: hen. Wool: sheep. Mutton: sheep. Beef: cattle. Skins for drums: goat. Ploughing: oxen.

## PAGE 7: Summary and common mistakes
Cattle, sheep, goats and poultry are domestic animals. They give milk, meat, eggs, skins, wool, manure, farm power and income. They help to produce food for families.

### Remember
• Beef is from cattle, mutton is from sheep, goat meat is from goats.
• Milk comes from cattle and goats, eggs from poultry.
• Manure from all of them helps crops.
• Give animals water, feed, shelter, health care and kindness.

### Common mistakes
• Saying that all sheep give wool. Many Kenyan sheep are kept for meat, not wool.
• Leaving animals without water in hot weather.
• Keeping too many animals in a small house.
• Using fresh milk that has not been boiled or is dirty. Boil raw milk before drinking unless it has been treated.
• Forgetting to wash hands after handling animals.

### Quick check
Which domestic animals give us eggs? Poultry, especially hens. Which give us leather? Cattle, goats and sheep.

## QUIZ
1. Which of these is a domestic animal?
   A) Lion
   B) Zebra
   C) Goat
   D) Elephant
   Answer: C
   Why: Goats are kept by people at home. The other animals are wild.
2. What is the meat of sheep called?
   A) Beef
   B) Mutton
   C) Pork
   D) Chicken
   Answer: B
   Why: Beef is from cattle, mutton is from sheep, and pork is from pigs.
3. Which of these is a product from poultry?
   A) Wool
   B) Eggs
   C) Beef
   D) Milk
   Answer: B
   Why: Hens lay eggs, which are eaten as food.
4. How does manure from domestic animals help crops?
   A) It scares birds
   B) It gives plant food to the soil
   C) It gives shade
   D) It makes the crops smaller
   Answer: B
   Why: Manure contains plant food, so crops grow better.
5. Give two uses of cattle other than milk. (2 marks)
   Answer: Meat (beef), leather from hides, manure, pulling ploughs and carts, income (any two).
   Why: 1 mark for each correct use.
6. Name the young of a cow, a sheep and a goat. (3 marks)
   Answer: Cow: calf; sheep: lamb; goat: kid.
   Why: 1 mark for each correct name.
7. Explain how animals and crops help each other on a farm. (3 marks)
   Answer: Animals eat crop leftovers and grass. Their manure goes to the soil and helps crops grow. The crops feed people and animals again.
   Why: 1 mark for animals eating crops, 1 mark for manure helping crops, 1 mark for the cycle.
8. List four things a domestic animal needs to stay healthy. (2 marks)
   Answer: Clean water, enough feed, a clean shelter, health care and kind handling.
   Why: Half a mark for each correct need, up to 2 marks.
9. Match each product to the animal that gives it most often: milk, eggs, mutton. (3 marks)
   Answer: Milk: cow or goat; eggs: hen; mutton: sheep.
   Why: 1 mark for each correct match.
