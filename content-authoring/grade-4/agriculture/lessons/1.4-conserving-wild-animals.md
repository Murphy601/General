## PAGE 1: Small wild animals near the shamba
Wild animals are animals that live in nature and are not kept by people. Some are big, like elephants and lions, and live in parks and forests. Others are small and come close to our farms and gardens. Some of these small wild animals eat or damage our crops.

### Small wild animals that destroy crops
• Birds such as weaver birds and queleas eat ripening millet, sorghum and rice. They also pick up newly sown seeds.
• Hares nibble young shoots of vegetables, beans and other crops.
• Squirrels dig up planted seeds and eat maize and fruits.
• Monkeys pick maize cobs, bananas, fruits and vegetables.

These animals are only looking for food. They do not know the crop belongs to a family. A hungry animal will return to where it finds easy food.

### Why it matters
A farmer can lose much of the harvest to birds or monkeys. That means less food and less money. But killing wild animals is not the answer. Wild animals are part of nature and many are protected. We should find ways of keeping them away from crops without hurting them.

### Think about it
Which small wild animals visit your home or school garden? What do they eat? At what time of day do they come?

## PAGE 2: Peaceful ways of protecting crops
There are many safe methods that keep small animals away from crops without killing them.

### Ways of scaring animals
• Scarecrow: a figure that looks like a person, standing in the field.
• Noise: tins with stones inside, or a clapper, tied to strings that shake in the wind.
• Shiny objects: old CDs, mirrors or strips of foil that flash and move in the sun.
• Nets: light nets over fruit trees or vegetables so birds cannot reach the fruits.
• Fences: a strong fence or thick hedge to keep hares and other animals out.
• Guarding: children or adults guard the field at the time of day birds come, often early morning and late afternoon. Remember that school time is for learning, so this should be arranged so children can attend school.

### Why not poison or trap them?
Poison can kill other animals, pets and even people. Traps can injure animals and children. Peaceful methods keep both crops and animals safe.

### Which method?
The best method depends on the animal and the crop. A scarecrow works against birds. A net helps to protect fruit. A fence is needed for hares. Using more than one method usually works better than using only one.

### Discuss
Which methods can your school use to protect the school garden?

## PAGE 3: Making a scarecrow
A scarecrow is a figure made to look like a person, placed in the field to frighten birds and small animals away from crops. It can be made from materials found at home.

### Materials
• Two straight sticks or poles. One is long (about 1.5 to 2 metres) and one shorter (about 1 metre).
• String, strips of cloth or thin rope for tying.
• An old sack, gourd or old pot for the head, stuffed with dry grass or old paper.
• An old shirt, dress or cloth for the body, and dry grass or straw for stuffing.
• An old hat or cap.
• Old tins, bottle tops or CDs to make noise and flash.

### Tools
A hammer or stone to push in the pole, and scissors. Get help from a teacher or an adult when you cut or dig.

### Safety
Do not use sharp wire or broken glass. Wear gloves when handling rough poles. Make sure that nothing on the scarecrow can hurt someone passing by.

## PAGE 4: Steps to build and place the scarecrow
Follow these steps in order.

### Steps
1. Choose the long pole and push it firmly into the ground so it stands straight and does not fall over in the wind. About a third of the pole can be underground.
2. Tie the shorter stick across the long pole, about 30 cm below the top, to make a cross. Tie it tightly with string in several turns. These are the shoulders and arms.
3. Stuff the sack for the head with dry grass and tie it closed at the neck. Fix it on top of the pole. You may draw eyes and a mouth with charcoal or paint.
4. Put the old shirt on the cross like clothes on a hanger, with the sleeves over the arms. Stuff the body with dry grass or straw and tie the shirt bottom shut.
5. Put the hat on the head and tie it so it does not blow off.
6. Tie tins with pebbles, old CDs and strips of shiny cloth to the arms so they move and make noise in the wind.
7. Move the scarecrow slightly every few days. Birds and animals learn quickly that something which never moves is not dangerous.

### Where to place it
Place the scarecrow in the middle of the crop or at the side where animals come from. In a big field, use two or three scarecrows. Check them often and fix any that have fallen.

### Working in a group
One learner brings sticks, one collects stuffing, one ties. Share the tools and take turns. When each person does a task well, the work is finished sooner.

## PAGE 5: Living better with wild animals
Wild animals are part of the environment and have a right to live. If we drive them all away or kill them, nature can go out of balance. This is why we should live peacefully with them.

### How wild animals help us
• Birds eat insects that harm crops.
• Bees, butterflies and some birds carry pollen from flower to flower. Without this, many fruits and seeds would not form.
• Some animals spread seeds in their droppings, so new trees grow.
• Wildlife brings tourists, and this gives Kenya money and jobs.
• Animals and plants together make up biodiversity, the great variety of life on Earth.

### Conservation means protecting
• Do not kill or hurt wild animals.
• Do not destroy their homes by cutting trees or burning bush without reason.
• Plant trees and keep areas of natural bush and forest.
• Keep some wild plants and water sources for them.
• Report poaching to a teacher, parent or wildlife officer.

### Living together
Farmers and wild animals can share the land if farmers use scarecrows, nets and fences. In this way the farmer keeps the harvest and the animals stay alive. This is called coexisting.

### Discuss
Why is it wrong to kill every bird that visits the field? What would happen if all the birds were gone?

## PAGE 6: Summary and common mistakes
Birds, hares, squirrels and monkeys can damage crops. A scarecrow is one simple, safe way of scaring them off. We should use peaceful methods and protect wildlife.

### Remember
• A scarecrow has a pole, a cross, a head, clothes and noisy or shiny things.
• Move it every few days.
• Use more than one method when possible.
• Do not use poison or traps.

### Common mistakes
• Making the pole too short or not fixing it firmly, so the wind knocks the scarecrow down.
• Leaving the scarecrow in the same place and position for weeks. The animals get used to it.
• Using sharp wire or glass that can hurt people.
• Killing the animals instead of scaring them.
• Forgetting to check the scarecrow after storms.

## QUIZ
1. Which of these is a small wild animal that can destroy crops?
   A) Cow
   B) Monkey
   C) Goat
   D) Dog
   Answer: B
   Why: Monkeys are wild animals that pick maize, fruits and vegetables. Cows, goats and dogs are domestic animals.
2. What is the main purpose of a scarecrow?
   A) To feed birds
   B) To frighten birds and small animals away from crops
   C) To measure the crop
   D) To water the plants
   Answer: B
   Why: A scarecrow looks like a person and scares animals away.
3. Why should a scarecrow be moved every few days?
   A) It gets tired
   B) Animals get used to something that never moves
   C) The pole becomes shorter
   D) So it can be washed
   Answer: B
   Why: Animals learn that a scarecrow which does not move is harmless, so it must be moved.
4. Which of these is the best way of dealing with birds eating crops?
   A) Put poison in the field
   B) Set traps
   C) Scare them away using scarecrows and noise
   D) Cut down the trees
   Answer: C
   Why: Scaring is safe and does not kill wildlife, poison and traps harm other animals and people.
5. Name three small wild animals that destroy crops. (3 marks)
   Answer: Birds, hares, squirrels and monkeys (any three).
   Why: 1 mark for each correct animal.
6. List four materials that can be used to make a scarecrow. (2 marks)
   Answer: Sticks or poles, an old shirt, a sack for the head, string, dry grass, an old hat, tins (any four).
   Why: Half a mark for each correct material, up to 2 marks.
7. Describe how to make a scarecrow, from fixing the pole to adding clothes. (4 marks)
   Answer: Push the long pole firmly into the ground; tie a shorter stick across it for arms; fix a stuffed head on top; dress the cross with a stuffed shirt and hat; tie on tins or shiny things.
   Why: 1 mark for each of four correct steps in the right order.
8. Explain two reasons why we should live peacefully with wild animals. (2 marks)
   Answer: Wild animals help nature, for example birds eat harmful insects and bees pollinate flowers. They also bring tourists and are part of biodiversity.
   Why: 1 mark for each reason.
9. Why is using poison against birds not a good idea? (2 marks)
   Answer: The poison can also kill other animals, pets and people, and it harms the environment.
   Why: 1 mark for harming other living things, 1 mark for a second reason.
