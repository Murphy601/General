## PAGE 1: What is personal protective equipment?
Personal protective equipment, or PPE, is the special clothing and gear that we wear to protect our bodies from harm. It protects us from dirt, dust, water, sharp objects, sun, chemicals and germs. We use PPE in the garden, in the kitchen, when cleaning, in workshops, and in many jobs such as farming, building and health care.

### Common PPE
• Gloves: protect the hands.
• Dust mask: covers the nose and mouth to keep out dust.
• Gumboots: protect the feet and legs from mud, water and sharp things.
• Headgear: a cap, hat, scarf or helmet to protect the head.
• Overall or apron: covers the body and clothes.
• Canvas shoes: strong shoes for work and school.

### Why keep it clean?
PPE gets dirty because it takes the dirt instead of our bodies. If it is not cleaned, it can carry germs and smell bad. Dirty gloves can spread germs to your hands. A dirty mask can make it hard to breathe. Mouldy boots can cause foot infections. Clean PPE lasts longer, works better and keeps us healthy.

### Think about it
Which PPE have you seen a farmer, a builder or a doctor wearing? What was it protecting?

### Which PPE for which job?
• Digging, weeding or handling compost: gloves, gumboots, overall, hat.
• Sweeping and dusting: dust mask, gloves, overall.
• Cooking: apron, headscarf or cap, closed shoes.
• Painting or cleaning with chemicals: an adult should choose the right gear.

### Keep it in good state
Check PPE before using it. Do not use torn gloves or gumboots with holes. Tell a teacher or an adult when something is damaged.

## PAGE 3: Cleaning gumboots
Gumboots are made of rubber. They get very muddy in a garden, so they need regular cleaning.

### Materials
A bucket of clean water, soap or detergent, a soft brush, a cloth, and a place with drainage.

### Steps
1. Take the boots outside. Knock them against each other or against a stone to remove lumps of dried mud. Do not knock hard enough to crack them.
2. Rinse the boots in a bucket of clean water to remove loose mud.
3. Scrub the outside with a soft brush and soapy water, especially the soles and the folds.
4. Wash the inside with a cloth and soapy water. Rinse well.
5. Rinse the outside and inside with clean water.
6. Turn the boots upside down on a rack or stick to drain in a shady, airy place. Do not dry them close to a fire or in very strong sun because the rubber can crack.
7. When dry, store them upright in a dry place. Put a clean sock inside, if you like.

## PAGE 4: Cleaning canvas shoes and other items
### Canvas shoes
1. Clap the shoes together outside to knock off loose soil.
2. Remove the laces and insoles if they come out. Wash the laces separately.
3. Brush off dry dirt with a soft brush.
4. Mix soap in a basin of clean water. Dip the brush and scrub the canvas gently, the rubber sides and the soles.
5. Rinse the shoes with clean water. Squeeze the water out gently. Do not twist them hard.
6. Stuff the shoes with clean paper to keep their shape and soak up moisture.
7. Dry them in an airy, shady place, or in gentle sun. Very strong heat can make shoes shrink and lose their shape. Put the laces back when dry.

### Overalls and aprons
Shake them outside. Soak in soapy water, scrub any dirty patches, rinse well and dry in the sun. Sunshine helps to kill germs. Fold or hang them neatly.

### Gloves
Wash rubber gloves while still wearing them, using soap and water. Rinse, then take them off carefully, pulling from the wrist so the dirty outside does not touch your skin. Hang them to dry with the fingers down, and turn them inside out if they are wet inside. Fabric gloves can be washed with soap like clothes.

### Masks and headgear
Cloth masks and headgear are washed with soap and water, rinsed, and dried in the sun. Paper (disposable) dust masks are not washed. They are thrown away when dirty or damp, and a new one is used.

## PAGE 5: Safe cleaning and looking after your PPE
Cleaning has its own risks. Follow the safety rules so that you do not get hurt or make others sick.

### Safety while cleaning
• Wear old clothes or an apron. Wear gloves if the item is very dirty.
• Use only soap and clean water. Do not use strong chemicals unless an adult directs you.
• Clean in an open place with drainage so that the dirty water does not make a puddle where children play.
• Keep brushes and buckets away from small children.
• Wash your hands with soap and clean water when you finish.
• Pour dirty water away from any drinking water source, garden crops or drains that lead to rivers.

### Storing PPE
• Store clean, dry PPE in a cupboard, rack or a clean box.
• Keep gumboots upside down on a stick or upright in a dry place.
• Hang overalls on a peg. Fold aprons.
• Label your own PPE so it does not get mixed with others.
• Check for tears, holes or cracks before you store it. Report damage.

### Make a display
Groups can display clean PPE on a table or a wall with labels and a short card telling what each item protects and how to clean it. Visitors can read the cards and learn.

### Share the tasks
Take turns. One learner scrubs, one rinses, one dries, and one makes the labels. When everyone shares the work, each item is clean quickly.

## PAGE 6: Summary and common mistakes
PPE protects us at work, and cleaning it keeps us healthy. Different items need different care.

### Remember
• PPE: gloves, dust masks, gumboots, headgear, overalls and canvas shoes.
• Clean gumboots: knock off mud, rinse, scrub, rinse, dry upside down in the shade.
• Clean canvas shoes: remove laces, brush, scrub gently, rinse, stuff with paper, dry in the shade.
• Overalls and cloth items: wash with soap, dry in the sun.
• Wash hands after cleaning.

### Common mistakes
• Drying rubber boots near a fire or in very strong sun, which cracks the rubber.
• Putting on damp or dirty PPE.
• Washing paper dust masks, which spoils them.
• Storing PPE wet, so it grows mould.
• Using hard brushes or strong chemicals that damage the material.
• Forgetting to wash your hands after cleaning.

### Quick check
After a day working in the school garden, your gumboots are covered in mud. What should you do first? Take them outside and knock off the lumps of mud, then rinse and scrub them with soapy water.

## QUIZ
1. What is personal protective equipment?
   A) Clothing and gear that protects the body from harm
   B) Food that keeps the body strong
   C) A type of cleaning soap
   D) Tools used for digging
   Answer: A
   Why: PPE is worn to protect us from dirt, injuries, dust and germs.
2. Which PPE protects the feet from mud and water?
   A) Dust mask
   B) Gloves
   C) Gumboots
   D) Headgear
   Answer: C
   Why: Gumboots are waterproof rubber boots that keep the feet dry and protected.
3. How should gumboots be dried after washing?
   A) Close to a hot fire
   B) Upside down in a shady, airy place
   C) Under a heavy stone
   D) In a closed box while wet
   Answer: B
   Why: Drying upside down in the shade drains the water without cracking the rubber.
4. Which item is thrown away rather than washed?
   A) Cloth mask
   B) Overall
   C) Canvas shoes
   D) Paper dust mask
   Answer: D
   Why: A paper (disposable) mask is not washed. It is replaced when dirty or damp.
5. Name four items of PPE. (2 marks)
   Answer: Gloves, dust mask, gumboots, headgear, overall, canvas shoes (any four).
   Why: Half a mark for each correct item, up to 2 marks.
6. Why should PPE be kept clean? (3 marks)
   Answer: Dirty PPE can carry germs and spread infection, it smells and spoils faster, and it may not protect well.
   Why: 1 mark for each correct reason.
7. Describe how to clean a pair of canvas shoes. (4 marks)
   Answer: Remove laces; brush off loose dirt; scrub with soapy water and a soft brush; rinse; stuff with paper; dry in the shade.
   Why: 1 mark for each of four correct steps in order.
8. State two safety rules to follow when cleaning PPE. (2 marks)
   Answer: Use only soap and clean water unless an adult says otherwise; keep brushes and buckets from small children; wash your hands after cleaning; clean in an open place with drainage.
   Why: 1 mark for each correct rule.
9. A learner stores gloves while they are still wet. What problem may happen? (2 marks)
   Answer: The gloves can grow mould and smell, and they may spread germs to the next person who wears them.
   Why: 1 mark for mould or bad smell, 1 mark for germs.
