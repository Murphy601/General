## PAGE 1: What is personal hygiene?
Personal hygiene means keeping our own bodies clean so that we stay healthy. It includes washing our hands and bodies, brushing our teeth, keeping our nails short and clean, and wearing clean clothes.

The world around us has tiny living things called germs. We cannot see them, but they are on dirty hands, dirty water, dirty food and dirty surfaces. Some germs make us sick. They can cause diarrhoea, cholera, typhoid, worms, skin infections, coughs and colds. When we keep clean, we stop germs from getting into our bodies and we do not pass them to other people.

### Good personal hygiene practices
• Washing hands with soap and clean water.
• Bathing every day with soap and clean water.
• Brushing teeth twice a day.
• Keeping fingernails and toenails short and clean.
• Wearing clean clothes and washing them often.
• Combing and washing hair.
• Drinking clean and safe water.
• Using a toilet or latrine, and washing hands afterwards.
• Wearing protective equipment such as gloves and shoes when needed.

### Benefits
A clean child feels fresh, has fewer illnesses, misses fewer days at school, and is comfortable playing with friends.

## PAGE 2: Washing hands correctly
Hands touch everything. If hands are dirty, germs move from the hands into our mouths when we eat, rub our eyes or touch our faces. Washing hands with soap and water is one of the best ways of staying healthy.

### When to wash hands
• Before eating or preparing food.
• After using the toilet.
• After playing outside, touching soil or handling animals.
• After coughing, sneezing or blowing your nose.
• After touching rubbish.
• When hands look dirty.

### Steps for washing hands
1. Wet your hands with clean water.
2. Put soap on your hands.
3. Rub palms together to make a lather.
4. Rub the back of each hand.
5. Rub between fingers.
6. Rub the thumbs and the tips of the fingers, and under the nails.
7. Keep rubbing for about 20 seconds. You can hum a song to count time.
8. Rinse well with clean running water.
9. Dry hands with a clean cloth or let them dry in the air.

## PAGE 3: Making a tippy tap

### To make one
1. Take a clean jerrican or large bottle with a cap. Ask an adult to make a small hole near the cap using a hot nail or an awl.
2. Hang the container from a strong stick or beam with string.
3. Tie a string to the cap. Attach it to a stick that you can push down with your foot.
4. Place a basin or a soak pit under the tap to catch dirty water.
5. Tie a bar of soap on a string next to the tap.
6. Press the foot lever. The container tips and water pours out of the hole. Wash hands under the flow. Release the lever to stop the water.

### Keep it working
Refill the container each day. Keep the soap dry. Empty the basin often and never let the wash-water pool where mosquitoes will breed.

## PAGE 4: Body care and clean water
### Bathing
Bathe every day, and after playing sports. Use clean water and soap. Wash the whole body, especially under the arms, behind the ears, between toes and the private parts. Dry with a clean towel. Do not share towels.

### Teeth and mouth
Brush teeth in the morning and before bed for about two minutes using a toothbrush and a little toothpaste. Brush the fronts, backs and tops of the teeth and gently brush your tongue. Rinse the mouth with clean water. A toothbrush belongs to one person only. Eating fewer sweets protects teeth. If there is no toothbrush, a chewing stick from a suitable tree may be used, as advised by an adult.

### Nails and hair
Cut nails short with a nail cutter and clean under them. Germs and worm eggs hide under long nails. Wash and comb your hair. Do not share combs.

### Clothes and shoes
Wear clean clothes each day, especially underclothes and socks. Wash clothes with soap and dry them well, preferably in the sun. Wear shoes to protect the feet from injuries and from worms and jiggers that enter through the skin.

### Clean, safe water
• Drink water that is safe. Boil it to a full rolling boil for at least one minute, then let it cool covered.
• Store drinking water in a clean container with a lid.
• Use a clean cup with a handle or a tap. Do not dip hands into the water.
• Treat water with water purification tablets or a filter if you know how, with an adult.

## PAGE 5: Using personal protective equipment
Personal protective equipment (PPE) is clothing and gear that protects the body from dirt, injury and germs. It is part of personal hygiene.

### Common PPE and uses
• Gloves protect hands from dirt, cuts, chemicals and animal waste while gardening or cleaning.
• Dust mask keeps dust from being breathed in.
• Gumboots protect feet from mud, water and sharp objects.
• Overall or apron protects clothes and skin from dirt.
• Headgear such as a cap, hat or headscarf protects from sun and dust and keeps hair clean.
• Canvas shoes protect feet during school or garden work.

### When to use it
Put on the right PPE before starting messy work such as gardening, cleaning the compound, handling manure or compost, or cleaning the toilet. Take it off after work and put it in its place. Wash your hands after removing gloves.

### Good habits
• Use PPE that fits you.
• Do not share PPE that touches the face, such as masks, unless it has been cleaned.
• Keep it clean, dry and stored away from children's play areas.
• Report any torn or damaged item to the teacher or an adult.

## PAGE 6: A hygiene log and summary
A log is a table that records what you do each day. Keeping a hygiene log helps you to make good habits.

### A simple log
Draw a table in your exercise book with the days of the week down the side. Across the top write: washed hands before meals, brushed teeth twice, bathed, clean clothes, drank safe water. Put a tick when you do each one. After a week, count your ticks. Which practice did you forget most?

### Summary
• Germs cause many illnesses. Cleanliness keeps them away.
• Wash hands with soap and clean water at the right times.
• Bathe, brush teeth, keep nails short and wear clean clothes.
• Drink safe water.
• Use protective equipment for messy or risky work.

### Common mistakes
• Rinsing hands quickly without soap.
• Not drying hands or drying them on a dirty cloth.
• Sharing towels, combs and toothbrushes.
• Biting fingernails.
• Leaving tippy tap water to become dirty or the soap wet.
• Drinking water from an open, dirty container.
• Washing hands only when they look dirty. Germs cannot be seen.

## QUIZ
1. What is personal hygiene?
   A) Keeping our own bodies clean to stay healthy
   B) Cleaning the classroom
   C) Cooking food
   D) Growing vegetables
   Answer: A
   Why: Personal hygiene is looking after our own bodies by keeping them clean.
2. When should you wash your hands?
   A) Only on Sundays
   B) Before eating and after using the toilet
   C) Only when they look dirty
   D) Only after bathing
   Answer: B
   Why: Germs cannot be seen, so we wash before eating and after toilet use, playing or touching animals.
3. About how long should you rub your hands with soap?
   A) 2 seconds
   B) 5 seconds
   C) 20 seconds
   D) 5 minutes
   Answer: C
   Why: Rubbing for about 20 seconds removes germs properly.
4. Which item protects the feet from mud and sharp objects?
   A) Gloves
   B) Dust mask
   C) Gumboots
   D) Headgear
   Answer: C
   Why: Gumboots cover the feet and legs and keep them dry and protected.
5. Give three times when you should wash your hands. (3 marks)
   Answer: Before eating, after using the toilet, after playing outside, after touching animals, after coughing or sneezing (any three).
   Why: 1 mark for each correct time.
6. Describe how to wash hands correctly. (4 marks)
   Answer: Wet hands with clean water, apply soap, rub palms, backs of hands, between fingers, thumbs and nails for about 20 seconds, rinse and dry with a clean cloth.
   Why: 1 mark each for wetting and soap, rubbing all parts, correct time, rinsing and drying.
7. Explain how a tippy tap helps a school without a water tap. (2 marks)
   Answer: It lets children wash hands with a small amount of water without touching the container with dirty hands.
   Why: 1 mark for saving water, 1 mark for keeping the container clean.
8. Name two ways of making drinking water safe. (2 marks)
   Answer: Boiling it and letting it cool covered; using a filter or water treatment tablets.
   Why: 1 mark for each correct way.
9. State three ways in which good personal hygiene helps a child. (3 marks)
   Answer: Prevents diseases; keeps the child feeling fresh and confident; reduces missing school.
   Why: 1 mark for each correct benefit.
