## PAGE 1: Why do we eat?
We eat food to grow, to get energy for work and play, to stay healthy, and to repair the body when it is hurt. Food is made up of different parts called nutrients. No single food gives the body all the nutrients it needs. That is why we must eat a variety of foods.

A balanced meal is a meal that contains foods from all the main food groups, in the right amounts. It has some energy-giving food, some body-building food and some protective food. A balanced meal also includes clean water to drink.

### Two words to remember
• Variety: eating many different kinds of food.
• Proportion: eating the right amount of each kind. It is not enough to eat a mountain of ugali with one spoon of vegetables.

### What happens when we do not eat well?
A child who eats only one kind of food may feel weak and tired, fall sick often, grow slowly, or find it hard to concentrate in class. A child who eats a balanced meal has strong muscles and bones, bright eyes and shining skin.

### Think about it
What did you eat yesterday? Did every meal have more than one kind of food?

## PAGE 2: The food groups
Foods are grouped by what they do in the body. Kenya's health guides often use three simple groups.

### 1. Energy-giving foods
These give the body the energy to move, work and keep warm.
• Ugali (maize, millet, sorghum), rice, chapati, bread and porridge.
• Sweet potatoes, Irish potatoes, cassava, arrowroots and bananas (matoke).
• Fats and oils such as cooking oil, butter, and avocado also give energy.

### 2. Body-building foods
These help the body to grow and to repair itself. They are rich in protein.
• Beans, peas, green grams (ndengu), lentils and groundnuts.
• Meat, fish (including omena and tilapia), chicken and eggs.
• Milk, yoghurt and cheese.

### 3. Protective foods
These have vitamins and minerals that help the body to fight sickness and keep eyes, skin and blood healthy.
• Vegetables such as sukuma wiki (kale), spinach, cabbage, managu, terere, carrots and tomatoes.
• Fruits such as oranges, mangoes, pawpaws, guavas and passion fruits.

### Water and fibre
Water helps the body work and keeps it cool. Fibre from vegetables, fruits and whole grains helps food move through the body. Drink plenty of clean water every day.

### What each group does
Energy-giving foods are like fuel for a car: without them we cannot go far. Body-building foods are like bricks used to build and mend a house. Protective foods are like a guard that keeps sickness away.

### Some foods belong to more than one group
Groundnuts and beans give both protein and energy. Milk gives protein, some fat and minerals. Avocado gives fat and vitamins. That is fine. What matters is that our daily eating has all three groups.

## PAGE 4: Choosing foods for a balanced meal
To make a balanced meal, choose one or more foods from each group. Use foods that grow near you, because they are fresh and cheaper.

### A simple plan
1. Start with an energy-giving food, for example ugali or rice.
2. Add a body-building food, for example beans, fish or eggs.
3. Add a protective food, for example sukuma wiki, spinach or cabbage.
4. Add a fruit if you can, or eat one after the meal.
5. Drink clean water.

### Examples of balanced meals
• Ugali, sukuma wiki and fried fish, with an orange afterwards.
• Rice, beans, cabbage and tomato stew, with a slice of pawpaw.
• Githeri (maize and beans) with avocado, and a carrot salad.
• Boiled sweet potatoes, an egg, spinach and a glass of milk.

### Not a balanced meal
• Chips only.
• Chapati and tea only.
• Ugali with salt only.
These give energy but no body-building or protective foods.

### Cost is not the reason
A balanced meal does not have to be costly. Beans, kale, omena, eggs, local fruits and vegetables from a small garden are low in cost. Growing your own vegetables and fruits helps a family have a balanced meal.

### How to keep the journal
1. Draw a table in your exercise book for each day.
2. Write what you ate at each meal.
3. Write which group each food belongs to.
4. At the end of the week, count. Did you eat foods from all three groups each day? Which group did you eat least?

### What to do with the results
If you find you missed protective foods, add vegetables or fruits next week. Tell your family what you learned, and suggest cheaper local foods.

### Serving sizes
Do not stuff yourself with only one food. Eat enough of each group and stop when you feel full. Wash hands before eating and eat slowly.

## PAGE 6: Summary and common mistakes
A balanced meal has energy-giving, body-building and protective foods in the right amounts, with clean water. It keeps us healthy, strong and active.

### Remember
• Energy: ugali, rice, potatoes, cassava, oil.
• Body-building: beans, fish, eggs, meat, milk.
• Protective: vegetables and fruits.
• Variety and proportion are both important.

### Common mistakes
• Thinking that expensive food is always healthier.
• Leaving out vegetables and fruits.
• Eating only sweets, chips or soft drinks.
• Skipping breakfast.
• Believing that one food group is enough.
• Forgetting to drink water.

### Compose your own
Make up a balanced supper using foods from your home. Name at least one food from each group. For example: sweet potatoes (energy), omena (body-building) and managu (protective).

## QUIZ
1. What is a balanced meal?
   A) A meal with only one kind of food
   B) A meal with foods from all the main food groups in the right amounts
   C) A meal that is very sweet
   D) A large meal
   Answer: B
   Why: A balanced meal has energy-giving, body-building and protective foods.
2. Which food is body-building?
   A) Ugali
   B) Beans
   C) Cooking oil
   D) Orange
   Answer: B
   Why: Beans are rich in protein, which helps the body to grow and repair itself.
3. Which food group do oranges and spinach belong to?
   A) Energy-giving foods
   B) Body-building foods
   C) Protective foods
   D) Drinks
   Answer: C
   Why: Fruits and vegetables give vitamins and minerals that protect the body from illness.
4. Which of these is a balanced meal?
   A) Chips only
   B) Tea and chapati only
   C) Ugali, fish and sukuma wiki
   D) Sweets and soda
   Answer: C
   Why: It has an energy food, a body-building food and a protective food.
5. Name two foods from each of the three food groups. (3 marks)
   Answer: Energy: ugali, rice. Body-building: beans, eggs. Protective: sukuma wiki, mango (any correct foods).
   Why: 1 mark for each group with two correct examples.
6. Explain why we need protective foods. (2 marks)
   Answer: They contain vitamins and minerals that help the body to fight diseases and keep skin, eyes and blood healthy.
   Why: 1 mark for fighting diseases, 1 mark for keeping the body healthy.
7. Plan a balanced lunch using local foods and say which group each food belongs to. (4 marks)
   Answer: For example ugali (energy), beans (body-building), sukuma wiki (protective), and an orange (protective).
   Why: 1 mark for each food correctly placed in a group; must include all three groups for full marks.
8. Explain why a meal of only chips is not balanced. (2 marks)
   Answer: Chips give mainly energy. They lack enough body-building foods and protective foods.
   Why: 1 mark for saying it gives energy only, 1 mark for what is missing.
9. Why is it useful to keep a food journal for a week? (2 marks)
   Answer: It shows what we eat and helps us to see whether we eat foods from all food groups.
   Why: 1 mark for recording meals, 1 mark for checking the balance or improving.
