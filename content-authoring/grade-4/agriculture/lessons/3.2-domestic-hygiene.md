## PAGE 1: Keeping our homes clean
Domestic hygiene means keeping the home and its surroundings clean. A dirty home is a good place for flies, cockroaches, rats and mosquitoes. These carry germs that cause diseases such as diarrhoea, typhoid and malaria. A clean home helps everyone in the family to stay healthy, feel comfortable and be proud of where they live.

### Why a clean home matters
• It stops germs and pests from spreading.
• It protects food from being spoiled.
• It prevents accidents, because floors are dry and clear of clutter.
• It looks and smells pleasant.
• It shows care and responsibility.

### Ways of cleaning the home
The main cleaning methods are:
• Sweeping: removing dry dirt from the floor with a broom.
• Dusting: wiping dust from furniture and shelves with a cloth.
• Mopping: washing the floor with a wet cloth or mop and clean water.
• Disposing of refuse: getting rid of rubbish safely.

We also wash utensils, clean the toilet and keep the compound tidy. In this lesson we learn how to use these methods properly, using tools we find at home or make ourselves.

## PAGE 2: Cleaning tools and materials
You do not need to buy special equipment to clean well. Many tools can be improvised, which means made from things we already have.

### Common cleaning tools
• Broom: made from grass, palm ribs or plastic bristles for sweeping floors and compounds.
• Dustpan: to pick up swept dirt. It can be made from a cut jerrican or a flat tin.
• Duster: a clean dry or damp piece of cloth, often from an old shirt.
• Mop and bucket: to wash the floor. A mop can be made from strips of old cloth tied to a stick.
• Rake: to collect leaves and litter outside.
• Basin and soap: for washing dishes and cloths.
• Rubbish bin with a lid.

### Materials
Soap or detergent, clean water, and, when an adult advises, a small amount of disinfectant. Never mix different cleaning chemicals, because they can give off harmful fumes.

### Personal protection while cleaning
Wear an apron or old clothes, shoes, and gloves if you handle dirty things. If there is much dust, cover your nose with a clean cloth or a dust mask. Keep chemicals away from small children and never taste or smell them.

### Keep tools clean
Wash dusters and mops after use and dry them in the sun. Store brooms with the bristles up so they stay straight.

List three cleaning tools you have at home. Which of them could be made from old materials?

## PAGE 3: Dusting and sweeping
Cleaning has a right order. Start with high places and finish with the floor. Dust falls downward, so if you dust first and sweep afterwards, the dust that falls will be swept away.

### How to dust
1. Open the windows so dust can leave the room.
2. Take a clean, slightly damp cloth. A damp cloth catches the dust. A dry one just moves it about in the air.
3. Start at the highest places: shelves, window ledges, and the tops of cupboards.
4. Wipe the tables and chairs.
5. Shake the duster outside, away from other people, and wash it after use.

### How to sweep
1. Move small furniture aside and clear the floor of toys and shoes.
2. Sweep gently from the corners towards the middle, keeping the broom low so dust does not fly.
3. Sweep towards the door or into a dustpan.
4. Pick up the dirt with a dustpan and put it in the rubbish bin.
5. Do not sweep dust under the bed or the carpet. Clean there too.
Some floors, such as earth floors, can be sprinkled lightly with water to keep dust down before sweeping.

## PAGE 4: Mopping
Mopping is washing a smooth floor with a wet mop. It removes stains and germs that sweeping cannot. Always sweep before mopping.

### Steps for mopping
1. Sweep the floor and pick up any objects.
2. Fill a bucket with clean water and a little soap. Warm water works well.
3. Dip the mop in the bucket. Squeeze out the extra water. The mop should be damp, not dripping.
4. Start at the corner farthest from the door. Mop in gentle sweeps working backwards towards the door, so you do not step on the clean floor.
5. Rinse the mop often in the bucket. If the water becomes very dirty, change it.
6. Leave the floor to dry. Put up a sign or warning that the floor is wet, because a wet floor is slippery.
7. Wash the mop and bucket. Hang the mop outside to dry in the sun.

### Safety
Never mop and leave the floor wet where people are walking. Do not use too much water on wooden or cement floors that can be damaged. Keep buckets out of the way of small children.

## PAGE 5: Disposing of refuse
Refuse means rubbish or waste. If refuse is left lying around, it smells, attracts flies and rats, and spreads diseases. It can also block drains and pollute rivers.

### Types of household refuse
• Organic waste: vegetable and fruit peelings, food leftovers, dry leaves and grass. These rot.
• Paper and cardboard.
• Plastics: bottles, bags and packets.
• Glass and metal: bottles, cans and tins.
• Dangerous waste: broken glass, batteries, sharp objects, and medicine. These must be handled with care.

### What to do with each
• Put organic waste in a compost heap or a rubbish pit and cover it. Compost turns it into manure.
• Reuse or recycle paper, plastics, glass and metal where you can. For example, jerrican for water storage, tins for seedlings.
• Put rubbish in a bin with a lid. When it is full, take it to a rubbish collection point or the dump. Do not throw it in the road, drains, rivers or the bush.
• Wrap broken glass in thick paper before disposing so no one is cut. Tell an adult.
• Do not burn plastics. The smoke is harmful to breathe and to the environment.

### Safety and hygiene
Wear gloves when handling rubbish. Keep the bin closed. Never touch sharp objects with bare hands. Wash your hands with soap after handling waste.

### Reduce, reuse, recycle
The best waste is the waste we never make. Reduce what you throw away. Reuse containers and paper. Recycle materials so that they can be used again.

## PAGE 6: Keeping the whole home healthy, summary and mistakes
A clean home has clean rooms, clean utensils, a clean toilet, a tidy compound and no stagnant water where mosquitoes lay eggs.

### More home cleaning habits
• Wash dishes after each meal with soap and clean water. Wash the least greasy items first, then the greasy ones. Rinse and dry them on a rack, in the air.
• Clean the toilet or latrine regularly and keep it covered.
• Cover food and store it away from flies, rats and cockroaches.
• Empty containers that hold rainwater, such as old tyres and tins, to stop mosquitoes breeding.
• Keep the compound free of long grass and rubbish.

### Summary
• Dust from high to low, then sweep, then mop.
• Use a damp cloth for dusting and a squeezed mop for the floor.
• Sort refuse: compost the organic waste, reuse and recycle the rest.
• Wear protection and wash hands after cleaning.

### Common mistakes
• Sweeping before dusting.
• Mopping with very dirty water.
• Leaving the floor wet so someone slips.
• Throwing rubbish anywhere.
• Burning plastic.
• Mixing cleaning chemicals.
• Leaving tools dirty.

## QUIZ
1. Which is the correct order of cleaning a room?
   A) Mop, sweep, dust
   B) Sweep, mop, dust
   C) Dust, sweep, mop
   D) Mop, dust, sweep
   Answer: C
   Why: Dust falls down, so dust first, sweep next and mop last.
2. Why do we use a damp cloth for dusting?
   A) It makes the room wet
   B) It catches dust instead of spreading it
   C) It is heavier
   D) It cleans the floor
   Answer: B
   Why: A damp cloth traps dust so it does not fly around.
3. Which waste can be put into a compost heap?
   A) Plastic bottles
   B) Vegetable peelings
   C) Tins
   D) Broken glass
   Answer: B
   Why: Vegetable peelings are organic and rot into compost.
4. Why should a floor be swept before it is mopped?
   A) Sweeping removes dry dirt so the mop is not clogged with it
   B) Mops cannot touch dust
   C) The floor must be dry
   D) It is a rule at school only
   Answer: A
   Why: Sweeping picks up loose dirt. Mopping without sweeping turns it into mud.
5. Name three methods of cleaning the home. (3 marks)
   Answer: Sweeping, dusting, mopping, disposing of refuse (any three).
   Why: 1 mark for each method.
6. Describe how to mop a floor. (4 marks)
   Answer: Sweep first; fill a bucket with clean soapy water; dip the mop and squeeze it; mop from the far corner backwards towards the door; rinse the mop; leave the floor to dry and wash the mop.
   Why: 1 mark for each of four correct steps.
7. Explain why refuse should not be thrown into rivers or drains. (2 marks)
   Answer: It pollutes the water and blocks drains, and it can spread diseases.
   Why: 1 mark for pollution or blocking, 1 mark for disease.
8. Give two ways of making cleaning tools from things at home. (2 marks)
   Answer: A duster from an old shirt; a mop from strips of old cloth tied to a stick; a dustpan from a cut jerrican.
   Why: 1 mark for each correct improvised tool.
9. Why should stagnant water be emptied from old containers and tyres around the home? (2 marks)
   Answer: Mosquitoes lay eggs in it, and they spread malaria.
   Why: 1 mark for mosquitoes breeding, 1 mark for the disease.
