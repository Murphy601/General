## PAGE 1: Fruits around us
A fruit is the part of a plant that holds the seeds. Many fruits are sweet or juicy and good to eat. Mangoes, oranges, bananas, pawpaws, avocados, guavas, passion fruits and tree tomatoes are all fruits.

Different places in Kenya have different climates, so different fruits grow well in different areas. Mangoes and pawpaws grow well in warm places such as the coast and lowlands. Tree tomatoes and plums grow well in cooler highlands. Bananas and avocados grow in many areas where there is enough rain. Passion fruit grows in many areas from warm to cool.

### Fruits in our locality
Ask your family and neighbours which fruits grow near your home. Write down the names in a list. Which ones do you eat most? Which ones could you grow at school?

### Why fruits are important
• They are rich in vitamins that help the body fight sickness.
• They have fibre, which helps digestion.
• They have a lot of water, which keeps the body cool and healthy.
• They can be sold, so they bring money to the family.
• A fruit tree also gives shade and cleans the air.

### Two groups of fruit crops
For this lesson we look at two groups of plants:
• Woody fruit trees, which stand on their own with a strong stem. Examples are guava, tree tomato, mango and orange.
• Climbing fruit crops, which need something to hold on to. Examples are passion fruit, grapes, kiwi, blackberries and raspberries.

### What each part does
• Roots hold the tree in the soil and take in water and food.
• Trunk or stem carries water and food up and down the plant.
• Branches hold up the leaves, flowers and fruits.
• Leaves use sunlight to make food for the plant.
• Flowers make the fruits. After a flower is pollinated, the fruit starts to grow.
• Fruits protect the seeds inside and help spread them.

### Fruit trees take time
Some fruits bear early. Pawpaw and passion fruit can start giving fruits within about a year. Guava and tree tomato take longer, and mango can take several years. Patience is important.

### Activity
Draw a fruit tree from your locality and label its parts. Cut open a fruit such as an orange or a tomato and find the seeds inside.

## PAGE 3: Planting a woody fruit tree
The best way to start a fruit tree is to plant a healthy seedling, a young tree already grown in a nursery. Many fruit trees are grafted. This means a branch from a good tree is joined to a strong root, and it gives fruit sooner and gives good quality fruit. Buy your seedlings from a trusted nursery or agrovet. A farm extension officer can advise you which variety suits your area.

### Preparing the planting hole
1. Choose a sunny place, away from buildings and other big trees. Trees need space to spread.
2. Mark the spot. Different trees need different spacing. Big trees like mango need much more space than tree tomato. Follow the advice you are given.
3. Dig a hole about half a metre wide and deep. Put the topsoil (the dark top layer) in one pile and the subsoil in another.
4. Mix the topsoil with a bucket of well-rotted compost or manure.
5. Put a little of this mixture back at the bottom.

### Planting steps
1. Water the seedling in its bag before planting.
2. Remove the bag carefully. If it is polythene, cut it away gently without breaking the ball of soil around the roots.
3. Place the seedling in the middle of the hole. It should stand at the same depth as it was in the bag. The joint of a grafted seedling must stay above the ground.
4. Fill the hole with the mixture, pressing gently around the roots.
5. Water well. Make a small ridge of soil around the tree to hold water.
6. Mulch around the stem with dry grass, leaving a small gap next to the stem.

### Steps for growing a climber
1. Build the trellis first. Fix strong posts about 2 metres tall in the ground and tie strong wires or strings between them. A live fence or a strong stick tied to the plant may also work.
2. Dig planting holes as for trees, about 2 to 3 metres apart along the trellis. Mix soil with compost.
3. Plant the seedling as above and water well.
4. Guide the young stem to the support with a soft string. Tie loosely so the stem does not get cut.
5. As the plant grows, help it climb and spread along the wires. Cut off weak or dead stems, which is called pruning.

## PAGE 5: Caring for fruit crops
Fruit crops need regular care. Good care gives healthy plants and plenty of fruit.

### Care activities
• Watering: water young plants regularly, especially during dry weather. Water near the stem. Mulch keeps soil moist.
• Weeding: remove weeds around the plant, as they take water and food. Be careful not to hurt the stem.
• Feeding: add compost or manure around the plant every few months, spread away from the stem.
• Pruning: remove dead, weak or crossing branches, so light and air reach the plant.
• Support: tie young trees to a stake if it is windy. Keep climbers guided on the trellis.
• Pests and diseases: check leaves and fruits often. Fruit flies, birds and monkeys may attack the ripe fruits. Ask an adult or an agricultural officer for safe ways of controlling problems.
• Protection: fence the young plants so animals do not eat or trample them.

### Working responsibly
Make a chart of who waters, who weeds and who records. Take turns so that every learner learns each job and no plant is forgotten.

### Record keeping
Write in your exercise book: the date of planting, the dates you watered and weeded, the date of the first flower and the first fruit. This shows you how the plant grows.

## PAGE 6: Harvesting and eating fruits
Fruits should be harvested when they are ripe. Ripe fruits taste best and have the most vitamins. Unripe fruits are hard and sour, and some are not safe to eat. Overripe fruits are soft and rot quickly.

### How to know a fruit is ripe
• Passion fruit: purple types turn deep purple and start to wrinkle, and yellow types turn yellow. Ripe fruits often fall and can be picked from the ground.
• Tree tomato: the skin turns deep red or orange, depending on the variety, and the fruit gives slightly when pressed.
• Guava: the skin turns from green to yellowish and the fruit smells sweet and feels slightly soft.
• Pawpaw: the skin turns yellow or orange and the fruit gives slightly under the thumb.

### Harvesting safely
Use both hands. Twist or cut the stalk gently so that the fruit and the branch are not damaged. Use a ladder or a picking pole and never climb thin branches. Wash your hands and the fruit with clean water before eating.

### Why we eat fruits
Fruits give vitamins, fibre and water. Vitamin C in fruits like oranges, guavas and passion fruits helps the body to heal cuts and fight colds. Eating fruits every day keeps us healthy and protects us from illness. Eat fruits as snacks instead of sweets.

### Sharing the harvest
Extra fruits can be sold or shared with neighbours. Do not waste fruits that fall. Some can be made into juice. Fresh juice should be made with clean water and clean hands.

## PAGE 7: Summary and common mistakes
We grow fruits in our locality using seedlings. Woody trees stand alone, while climbers need supports. Careful planting, watering, weeding, feeding and pruning give healthy fruit.

### Remember
• Choose fruits that suit your area.
• Dig a good hole, mix soil with compost, plant at the right depth.
• Give climbers a trellis.
• Water, weed, mulch and prune.
• Harvest ripe fruit and wash it before eating.

### Common mistakes
• Planting in a shady spot where fruit trees cannot get enough sun.
• Burying the graft joint under the soil.
• Not watering young plants in dry weather.
• Putting mulch or manure touching the stem.
• Picking fruit before it is ripe.
• Forgetting to give climbers a support.
• Eating fruit without washing it first.

### Quick check
A passion fruit seedling was planted but has no support and lies on the ground. What should the farmer do? Build a trellis and tie the stem gently to it so that it climbs.

## QUIZ
1. What is a fruit?
   A) The root of a plant
   B) The part of a plant that holds the seeds
   C) The leaf of a plant
   D) The soil around a plant
   Answer: B
   Why: A fruit grows from the flower and holds the seeds inside.
2. Which of these is a climbing fruit crop?
   A) Guava
   B) Mango
   C) Passion fruit
   D) Orange
   Answer: C
   Why: Passion fruit has a soft stem and needs a trellis. Guava, mango and orange are woody trees.
3. Why is a trellis made for a passion fruit plant?
   A) To make the plant shorter
   B) To give the plant support to climb
   C) To keep water away
   D) To stop it from flowering
   Answer: B
   Why: Climbers have soft stems that cannot stand alone.
4. Which part of a fruit tree takes in water from the soil?
   A) Leaves
   B) Flowers
   C) Roots
   D) Fruits
   Answer: C
   Why: Roots grow in the soil and take up water and food.
5. Give three reasons why we should eat fruits. (3 marks)
   Answer: They give vitamins, they give fibre to help digestion, and they contain water. They also help protect the body from sickness.
   Why: 1 mark for each correct reason.
6. Name two woody fruit trees and two climbing fruit crops. (4 marks)
   Answer: Woody: guava, tree tomato, mango, orange. Climbing: passion fruit, grapes, kiwi, blackberries.
   Why: 1 mark for each correct crop, up to 4.
7. Describe the steps for planting a fruit seedling. (4 marks)
   Answer: Dig a hole about half a metre wide and deep; mix topsoil with compost; remove the bag carefully; place the seedling at the same depth; fill the hole and firm the soil; water and mulch.
   Why: 1 mark for each of four correct steps.
8. List three ways of caring for a growing fruit tree. (3 marks)
   Answer: Watering, weeding, mulching, adding compost, pruning, protecting from animals (any three).
   Why: 1 mark for each correct way.
9. Why is it important to harvest fruit only when it is ripe? (2 marks)
   Answer: Ripe fruit tastes best and has the most vitamins, while unripe fruit is hard and sour.
   Why: 1 mark for taste or quality, 1 mark for nutrition or unripe problems.
