## PAGE 1: Water is precious
Every plant needs water to grow. Farmers in many parts of Kenya do not get enough rain all through the year. Carrying water from a river or a tap takes time and effort, so we must not waste it.

To conserve water means to use it carefully so that none is wasted. Irrigation means giving water to crops by ways other than rain. Some ways of irrigating waste a lot of water. If you pour water quickly from a bucket over a bed, much of it runs away over the surface, sinks past the roots, or dries up in the hot sun before the plants can use it.

### Ways of watering crops
• Bucket or watering can: water is poured by hand over the plants.
• Sprinkler: water is thrown into the air like rain.
• Furrow irrigation: water flows along small channels between rows.
• Drip irrigation: water falls slowly, drop by drop, next to the roots.

Drip irrigation is one of the best ways to save water. In this lesson you will learn how it works and how to make a simple drip system from things found at home.

### Think about it
Why do most farmers water their crops early in the morning or late in the afternoon rather than at noon?

## PAGE 2: What is drip irrigation?
Drip irrigation is a way of watering plants by letting water drip slowly from a container or pipe onto the soil close to the roots. The water goes into the soil right where the plant needs it, so very little is wasted.

### Why drip irrigation saves water
• The water falls close to the roots, so the plant uses almost all of it.
• Slow dripping lets the soil soak up the water, so less runs away.
• The top of the soil between plants stays dry, so fewer weeds grow.
• Less water evaporates in the sun because only a small patch of soil is wet.
• Leaves stay dry, which helps prevent some plant diseases.

### Drip irrigation and normal watering compared
A watering can may wet the whole bed, including the paths where no plants grow. A drip system wets only a small circle around each plant. A farmer may use much less water with drip irrigation and still get good crops.

### Where is it used?
Large farms use plastic pipes with tiny holes or special drippers, joined to a tank or pump. Small farmers and school gardens can make a simple system with bottles, pipes and buckets. Drip irrigation is especially useful in dry areas, and for crops planted in rows such as tomatoes, kale, onions and young fruit trees.

### Activity
Ask an adult if any neighbour uses drip irrigation. Look at how the water reaches the plants and draw what you see.

### The parts
• Container: a bucket, jerrican or drum, placed high on a stand so water flows down by gravity. Even a small height gives the water a push.
• Pipe: a plastic hose or tubing that carries water to the plants. The far end is closed with a plug or knot.
• Openings: tiny holes in the pipe next to each plant. Holes should be small so that water drips rather than sprays.
• Cover or filter: put clean cloth over the water opening so that bits of dirt do not block the tiny holes.

### Safety
Use only clean water. Making holes in pipes or bottles needs a sharp tool such as a nail or an awl, so ask a teacher or an adult to make them. Do not hold the item in your hand while it is being pierced. Put it on a piece of wood.

## PAGE 4: Making a bottle drip system
The bottle drip is the simplest system. It waters one plant, or a plant and its neighbour, and it is good for young fruit trees, tomatoes and kale.

### You need
A plastic bottle with a cap (1 to 2 litres), a nail or awl, a small stick, and clean water.

### Steps
1. Ask an adult to make two or three tiny holes in the bottle cap using the nail. Small holes give slow drips.
2. Cut off the bottom of the bottle only if an adult helps you. This makes it easy to pour water in.
3. Dig a small hole about 10 to 15 cm from the plant. It should be just deep enough to hold the neck of the bottle.
4. Turn the bottle upside down and place the neck in the hole with the cap on. Press soil around the bottle so it stands firm. You can also tie it to a stick.
5. Fill the bottle with clean water and watch the water drip out slowly into the soil.
6. Refill when the bottle is empty.

## PAGE 5: Making a pipe and bucket system, and caring for it
For a row of plants, use a bucket and a length of hose.

### Steps
1. Choose a bucket or jerrican with a cover. Make a hole near the bottom and fix the hose tightly so that it does not leak. An adult should help with this.
2. Place the container on a stand or a strong table about one metre above the ground. The higher the container, the faster the drip.
3. Lay the hose along the row of plants. Close the end by folding it and tying it with string, or by pushing a stick in.
4. Ask an adult to pierce a small hole in the hose beside each plant.
5. Fill the container with clean water and check that each hole drips slowly.
6. If a hole drips too fast, tie a small piece of cloth around it. If a hole is blocked, clear it gently with a thin wire.

### Caring for your drip system
• Fill the container in the morning or late afternoon.
• Look for leaks and blocked holes each day.
• Keep the water covered. This keeps out leaves, mosquitoes and children's toys.
• Water only as much as the plants need. Feel the soil. If it is damp a finger deep, the plant has enough.
• Mulching around plants with dry grass saves even more water.

### Working as a team
One learner fills, one checks holes, one records how many litres are used each week. Everyone shares the work.

## PAGE 6: Summary and common mistakes
Drip irrigation saves water because it gives plants small amounts slowly, right at the roots. It saves time and effort too, since you fill the container and the system does the watering.

### Remember
• Drip means drop by drop, near the roots.
• A container placed higher gives a stronger flow.
• Use clean water and cover the container.
• Small holes make slow drips. Adults make the holes.

### Common mistakes
• Making holes too big, so water pours out and is wasted.
• Placing the holes far from the plants, so the roots do not get the water.
• Using dirty water that blocks the holes.
• Leaving the end of the pipe open so water runs away.
• Watering at midday when the sun is hot and water evaporates quickly.

### Other ways of saving water
Collect rainwater from roofs in tanks. Mulch the soil. Do not leave taps running. Reuse water used for washing vegetables to water plants.

## QUIZ
1. What is drip irrigation?
   A) Pouring water quickly over the whole field
   B) Watering crops slowly, drop by drop, near the roots
   C) Waiting for rain to fall
   D) Spraying water into the air
   Answer: B
   Why: Drip irrigation lets water fall slowly on the soil close to the roots, so little is wasted.
2. Which is a reason why drip irrigation saves water?
   A) It wets all the soil between the rows
   B) It uses only rain
   C) Water goes close to the roots and little evaporates
   D) It makes plants need more water
   Answer: C
   Why: Water goes where the plant needs it, and only a small patch of soil is wet, so less is lost.
3. Why is the bucket placed on a stand in a drip system?
   A) So that water flows down through the pipe
   B) So that it looks tidy
   C) So that it stays clean
   D) So that it can be seen from far away
   Answer: A
   Why: A raised container makes water flow down through the pipe by gravity.
4. Who should make the holes in bottle caps and pipes?
   A) The youngest child
   B) An adult or teacher
   C) Anyone without gloves
   D) Nobody, the holes are not needed
   Answer: B
   Why: Nails and awls are sharp. An adult or teacher should pierce the holes safely.
5. Name three parts of a simple drip system. (3 marks)
   Answer: A container for water, a pipe or hose, and tiny holes that let the water drip out.
   Why: 1 mark for each part.
6. Give two ways in which drip irrigation helps a farmer. (2 marks)
   Answer: It saves water, and it reduces weeds because only the soil near plants is wet.
   Why: 1 mark for each correct benefit. Also accept saves time or reduces plant disease.
7. Describe how to set up a bottle drip beside a young plant. (4 marks)
   Answer: Make tiny holes in the cap, fill the bottle with clean water, dig a small hole near the plant, bury the neck upside down and firm the soil, and refill when empty.
   Why: 1 mark each for holes in the cap, digging a hole near the plant, placing the bottle neck down and firm, and filling and refilling.
8. A learner's drip system has water pouring out very fast from one hole. Explain what is wrong and how to fix it. (2 marks)
   Answer: The hole is too big. Tie a small piece of cloth around the hole or use a new pipe with a smaller hole.
   Why: 1 mark for the cause and 1 mark for a fix.
9. Why is it better to water in the morning or late afternoon? (2 marks)
   Answer: The sun is less hot, so less water evaporates and the plants use more of it.
   Why: 1 mark for cooler time, 1 mark for less evaporation.
