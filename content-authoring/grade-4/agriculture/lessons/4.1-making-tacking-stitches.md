## PAGE 1: What is a tacking stitch?
When we sew a garment, we often need to hold two pieces of cloth together in the right place before we sew them for good. Pins can slip and prick us. Tacking solves the problem. Tacking stitches are large, loose stitches used to hold cloth temporarily. They are also used to mark a line on cloth, such as a fold or a hem. When the permanent sewing is done, the tacking thread is cut and pulled out.

The word tacking is sometimes called basting in other places. In Kenya schools we say tacking.

### Why we tack
• It holds layers of cloth together so they do not move while we sew.
• It marks where the permanent stitches will go.
• It keeps folds and hems flat and in place.
• It helps us to sew straight and neatly.
• It is quick to make and easy to remove, so it does not spoil the cloth.

### Types of tacking stitches
There are two types you will learn:
• Even tacking: all the stitches are the same length and the spaces between them are the same length.
• Long and short tacking (also called uneven tacking): a long stitch is followed by a short one, in a repeating pattern.

### Think about it
Have you seen a tailor with a garment full of white thread stitches before the machine sewing? What do you think those stitches were for?

## PAGE 2: Sewing tools and how to keep safe
Before you start sewing, get to know your tools.

### Tools and materials
• Needle: a thin steel needle with an eye. For tacking, use a medium needle that moves easily through the cloth.
• Thread: cotton thread. Use a colour that shows clearly on the cloth, so it is easy to see and remove.
• Scissors: for cutting cloth and thread.
• Tape measure and ruler: for measuring cloth and stitch lengths.
• Chalk or pencil: for marking lines on the cloth.
• Pins: to hold the cloth before tacking.
• Thimble: a small cap worn on the finger that pushes the needle.
• Pin cushion: a small pad where needles and pins are kept.
• Cloth: cotton cloth is easiest to sew for beginners.

### Safety rules with needles and scissors
• Needles and pins are sharp. Never put them in your mouth or leave them on the floor or on a chair.
• Count your needles at the start and end of every lesson. Always return them to the pin cushion or a needle case.
• Carry scissors with the points held down and pass them handle first.
• Do not play with sewing tools.
• Tell the teacher at once if you prick yourself. Wash the spot with clean water and soap.
• Keep needles away from small children.
• Sit comfortably with good light so you can see clearly.

## PAGE 3: Threading and starting
Good sewing starts with correct threading.

### Preparing the thread
1. Cut a length of thread about as long as from your fingertips to your elbow, or up to about 50 cm. Very long thread tangles and knots.
2. Wet the end lightly or cut it at a slant to make threading easier.
3. Push the end through the eye of the needle and pull about 10 cm through.
4. Make a knot at the other end. Twist the end around your finger, roll it off and pull tight.

### Getting ready to tack
1. Place the two pieces of cloth together with their right sides facing each other, or the right side out, depending on the job. The right side is the good side that will be seen.
2. Use a ruler and chalk to mark the line where you want to tack.
3. Pin the layers at the ends and in the middle, with pins going across the line, not along it.
4. Hold the cloth in your left hand if you are right-handed, and sew with the right hand.

### Starting and finishing
Start with a knot on the right side of the cloth, or with a small back stitch, so the thread stays put. To finish, make a small back stitch. Do not make a hard knot, because tacking thread must be easy to remove.

### Practice
Practise on a scrap of cloth with a coloured thread first. Draw a straight line with chalk and follow it.

## PAGE 4: Even tacking
In even tacking, every stitch is the same length and every gap is the same length. It gives a firm hold and is good for seams that need to be held exactly in place, especially around curves.

### Steps
1. Mark a straight line on the cloth with chalk or pencil.
2. Bring the needle up from the underside of the cloth at the start of the line.
3. Move the needle forward about 1 cm along the line and push it down through the cloth.
4. Bring the needle up again about 1 cm ahead. This leaves a gap of 1 cm on top.
5. Repeat: down 1 cm, up 1 cm, along the line. Keep the stitches and gaps the same size.
6. Keep the thread loose. Do not pull hard or the cloth will wrinkle.
7. At the end, make a small back stitch.

### Where it is used
Even tacking is used where the cloth needs to be held firmly, such as at the edge of a hem, along curved seams and on slippery cloth.

## PAGE 5: Long and short tacking
Long and short tacking has a long stitch on the top of the cloth followed by a short stitch underneath. It is quicker than even tacking because you cover more cloth with each stitch. It is used on long, straight seams where there is not much strain on the stitches, for example when putting two pieces of a scarecrow shirt together.

### Steps
1. Mark a straight line with chalk.
2. Bring the needle up at the start and make a long stitch of about 2 cm on top of the cloth along the line.
3. Push the needle down at the end of the long stitch. Bring it up again about 0.5 cm further along. This makes a short stitch that lies underneath, on the underside of the cloth.
4. Continue: a long stitch on top, then a short stitch underneath, along the line. Keep the pattern regular.
5. Finish with a small back stitch.

### Choosing the right stitch
• Use even tacking when you need a firm hold, for curves and for slippery cloth.
• Use long and short tacking for long, straight seams and for a quick hold.

## PAGE 6: Making an item: a handkerchief
A handkerchief is a good first item. We will fold and tack the edge into a neat hem, which keeps the cloth from fraying while you sew it permanently later.

### Materials
A piece of cotton cloth, thread of a different colour, needle, scissors, ruler, chalk, tape measure and pins.

### Steps
1. Measure and mark a square of cloth 25 cm by 25 cm. Check that all four sides are equal and the corners are square.
2. Cut along the lines with sharp scissors.
3. Fold each edge over about 1 cm towards the wrong side and press with your fingers. Fold it over again by another 1 cm so the raw edge is hidden.
4. Pin the fold in place with pins across the hem.
5. Thread the needle and make a knot.
6. Tack along the hem with even tacking, about 0.5 cm from the folded edge. Make the stitches about 1 cm long.
7. At the corners, fold neatly so they are square. Turn the cloth and continue.
8. When all four sides are tacked, check that the hem is even and flat.
9. Sew the hem permanently with small neat stitches close to the edge. After that, cut the tacking thread and pull it out.

### A scarecrow shirt
You can make a small shirt for a scarecrow from two rectangles of old cloth by using tacking. Tack the two rectangles together along the sides, leaving openings for the neck and arms, then sew them firmly. Fill with dry grass.

### Team work
Share scissors and rulers politely. Help each other to thread needles. Praise each other's neat work.

## PAGE 7: Summary, display and common mistakes
Tacking stitches are temporary. They hold cloth, mark lines and keep hems in place until permanent sewing is done. Even tacking has equal stitches and gaps. Long and short tacking alternates a long and a short stitch.

### Remember
• Use a contrasting thread that is easy to see and remove.
• Keep the stitches loose.
• Start with a knot or a small back stitch, and end with a small back stitch.
• Even tacking: 1 cm stitches and 1 cm gaps.
• Long and short tacking: about 2 cm long stitch and about 0.5 cm short stitch.
• Count needles and keep sharp tools safe.

### Common mistakes
• Making stitches uneven in length when doing even tacking.
• Pulling the thread too tight so the cloth wrinkles.
• Using thread the same colour as the cloth, making it hard to see and remove.
• Using very long thread that tangles.
• Making a hard knot at the end, so removing the thread is difficult.
• Leaving needles on the floor or in the mouth.

### Display and talk
Show your finished handkerchief or sample stitches on a board with cards naming the stitch. Explain to visitors what tacking is, why it is important and how you made your item. Listen to questions and answer politely.

## QUIZ
1. What are tacking stitches used for?
   A) To hold cloth temporarily before permanent sewing
   B) To decorate cloth for ever
   C) To cut cloth
   D) To measure cloth
   Answer: A
   Why: Tacking stitches hold cloth in place and are removed after permanent sewing.
2. Which is a type of tacking stitch?
   A) Even tacking
   B) Cross stitch
   C) Chain stitch
   D) Satin stitch
   Answer: A
   Why: Even tacking and long and short tacking are the two tacking stitches taught in this grade.
3. In even tacking, how are the stitches and gaps?
   A) Stitches are long and gaps are short
   B) Stitches and gaps are all the same length
   C) Gaps are longer than stitches
   D) There are no gaps
   Answer: B
   Why: Even tacking has equal stitches and equal gaps.
4. What should you do with needles when a sewing lesson ends?
   A) Leave them on the floor
   B) Put them in your mouth
   C) Count them and return them to the pin cushion
   D) Give them to young children
   Answer: C
   Why: Sharp needles must be counted and stored safely.
5. Why is it better to use thread of a different colour from the cloth for tacking? (2 marks)
   Answer: The stitches show clearly, so it is easy to follow them and to pull them out later.
   Why: 1 mark for easy to see, 1 mark for easy removal.
6. State three safety rules when sewing. (3 marks)
   Answer: Never put needles in the mouth; count and store needles in a pin cushion; carry scissors with the points down; tell the teacher if pricked (any three).
   Why: 1 mark for each correct rule.
7. Describe how to make even tacking. (4 marks)
   Answer: Mark a line; bring the needle up at the start; move forward about 1 cm and push the needle down; bring it up 1 cm ahead; repeat keeping stitches and gaps equal; finish with a small back stitch.
   Why: 1 mark for each of four correct steps.
8. Explain the difference between even tacking and long and short tacking. (2 marks)
   Answer: In even tacking all the stitches and gaps are equal. In long and short tacking a long stitch is followed by a short stitch.
   Why: 1 mark for each correct description.
9. List the steps for making a handkerchief hem with tacking. (4 marks)
   Answer: Measure and cut a 25 cm square; fold each edge twice by 1 cm; pin the hem; tack along the hem with even tacking; check that it is even; sew permanently and remove the tacking.
   Why: 1 mark for each of four correct steps.
