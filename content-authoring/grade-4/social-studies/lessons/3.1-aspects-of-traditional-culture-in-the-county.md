## PAGE 1: What is traditional culture?
Every family has ways of doing things: how they greet elders, what they cook for visitors, the songs they sing. Whole communities also have ways of life that they have followed for many years. This way of life is called culture.

Traditional culture is the way of life that a community has passed down from grandparents to parents and then to children. It includes what people wear, eat, how they build houses, the objects they make, the games they play, and the special events they celebrate.

Kenya has more than 40 communities. Each one has its own language and customs. Your county may have one main community or several. All are part of the Kenyan family. This is why we say Kenya's culture is rich and varied.

## PAGE 2: Traditional dressing and food
### Dressing
In the past, people made clothes from what they found around them, such as animal skins, bark cloth and later woven cloth. People also wore beads, shells, metal bangles and feathers as decoration. Different communities wore different styles.

Some examples you can check on: The Maasai are well known for their red or blue checked cloth called a shuka and for colourful beaded necklaces. On the Kenyan coast, women wear the kanga, a printed cloth, and men wear the kikoi. Today many people wear modern clothes every day, but they wear traditional dress at weddings, cultural days and festivals.

### Food
Traditional foods come from the crops and animals in each area. Some examples:
• Ugali, made from maize or millet flour, is eaten in many parts of Kenya.
• Githeri is a mixture of maize and beans that is popular in central Kenya.
• Fish and ugali is popular around Lake Victoria.
• Milk, meat and blood from livestock are part of the diet of many herding communities.
• Mursik, a fermented milk, is a drink of the Kalenjin.
• On the coast, pilau, coconut rice and mahamri are common.
• Traditional vegetables such as managu, terere and sukuma wiki are eaten in many homes.

### Activity
Ask your grandparents or parents: what is a traditional dish of our community and how is it prepared? Draw it and write two sentences about it.

## PAGE 3: Housing and artefacts
### Housing
Houses were built from materials found nearby. Many communities built round houses with mud walls and a grass roof. The Maasai build low houses of a wooden frame covered with mud and dung, arranged inside a circle fence called a manyatta. In Lamu and other coastal towns, old houses were built of coral stone with carved wooden doors. Some herding communities in northern Kenya use portable dome-shaped houses covered with mats, so they can be moved when the family moves.

### Artefacts
Artefacts are objects that people make and use. Examples are:
• Baskets such as the kiondo, made from sisal fibre
• Clay pots for cooking and storing water
• Wooden stools, spoons and carvings
• Soapstone carvings from Kisii
• Gourds for carrying milk and water
• Beaded jewellery and traditional tools such as bows and hoes

Artefacts help us to see how people lived and how skilled they were.

## PAGE 4: Sports, games, festivals and ceremonies
### Sports and games
Children and adults in traditional communities enjoyed many games. Examples include wrestling, running races, jumping, and throwing games. In many places people play bao, a board game where seeds or stones are moved between holes in a wooden board. It is also called by other names in different languages. Children play hide and seek, skipping and clapping games. Dances are also a kind of sport, such as the Maasai jumping dance and the Isukuti dance of the Luhya.

### Festivals and ceremonies
A ceremony is a special event that follows customs. Communities hold ceremonies to mark important steps in life such as the naming of a baby, a wedding, a funeral or the welcoming of a young person into adulthood. They also hold events after harvest to give thanks. Songs, dances, food and prayers are part of these events. A festival is a bigger celebration, such as the Lamu Cultural Festival or the Kenya Music and Cultural Festival for schools.

## PAGE 5: Why traditional culture is important
Culture is like the roots of a tree. It keeps us strong and connected.

• Identity: it tells us who we are and where we come from.
• Values: traditional stories, proverbs and songs teach respect, honesty, courage and sharing.
• Unity: ceremonies bring families and neighbours together.
• Learning: elders pass on skills such as farming, weaving and healing.
• Income: cultural centres, crafts and festivals earn money and bring tourists.
• Pride: knowing our culture helps us to be proud and confident.

### Respect all cultures
Each community's culture is valuable. We must never laugh at how other people dress, eat or speak. Learn about other cultures and enjoy them.

### Culture changes
Culture is not frozen. Over time, some ways change. People use modern medicine, phones and electricity. Some old practices were found to harm people, such as female genital mutilation and forcing girls into early marriage. Kenyan law forbids these because they harm children. We keep the good parts of culture, such as respect, songs, foods and crafts, and we leave out harmful practices.

### Think about it
Which part of your culture would you like to keep for your grandchildren?

## PAGE 6: Collecting and showing culture
One good way to learn about culture is to collect and record it. Your class can prepare a culture day.

### Steps
1. Choose an aspect: dress, food, housing, artefacts, games, or ceremonies.
2. Ask elders and parents. Write their answers or record them with permission.
3. Collect pictures, songs, safe old objects and tools.
4. Draw or paint what you learned.
5. Make labels that tell the name, use and community of each item.
6. Present your findings and perform a song or dance.

### Manners for interviews
• Greet the elder respectfully.
• Ask permission before recording or taking photographs.
• Listen carefully and do not interrupt.
• Say thank you at the end.

### Summary
Traditional culture includes dress, food, housing, artefacts, sports and games, and festivals and ceremonies. It is passed down through the generations. It gives identity, values and unity. We respect all cultures and leave out practices that harm children.

### Common mistakes
• Thinking that culture belongs only to the past. We live culture every day.
• Thinking that one community's way is better. Every culture deserves respect.

## QUIZ
1. What is traditional culture?
   A) A way of life passed down from one generation to the next
   B) A new invention
   C) A kind of road
   D) A type of school
   Answer: A
   Why: Culture is the way of life of a community that parents and grandparents teach to children.
2. Which of the following is an aspect of traditional culture?
   A) Sports and games
   B) Weather
   C) Rainfall
   D) Mountains
   Answer: A
   Why: The six aspects are dressing, food, housing, artefacts, sports and games, and festivals and ceremonies.
3. What is an artefact?
   A) A kind of animal
   B) An object made and used by people, such as a basket
   C) A type of weather
   D) A crop
   Answer: B
   Why: Artefacts are made by people. Baskets, pots and carvings are examples.
4. Which of these is a ceremony?
   A) Digging a garden
   B) A wedding
   C) Washing clothes
   D) Buying sugar
   Answer: B
   Why: A wedding is a special event marked by customs, songs and food.
5. Which practice is against Kenyan law because it harms children?
   A) Wearing a shuka
   B) Cooking githeri
   C) Female genital mutilation
   D) Playing bao
   Answer: C
   Why: Female genital mutilation harms girls and is forbidden by law. The other choices are harmless parts of culture.
6. Name three foods that are part of traditional culture in Kenya. (3 marks)
   Answer: Ugali, githeri and fish. Accept pilau, mursik, managu or other correct examples.
   Why: 1 mark for each correct food.
7. Explain why it is important to keep our traditional culture. (3 marks)
   Answer: It gives us identity, it teaches values such as respect and sharing, and it brings people together.
   Why: 1 mark for each valid reason.
8. Describe one way of showing traditional culture in your community. (2 marks)
   Answer: For example, a group can wear traditional dress and perform a dance at a school culture day.
   Why: 1 mark for naming the activity, 1 mark for describing it.
9. State two good manners to follow when you interview an elder about culture. (2 marks)
   Answer: Greet the elder politely and ask permission before recording or taking a photo. Listening carefully and thanking the elder are also correct.
   Why: 1 mark for each correct manner.
