## PAGE 1: Nobody can do everything alone
Think about your morning. You woke up and ate breakfast. Someone grew the maize or the wheat. Someone sold the sugar and the tea leaves at the shop. Your clothes were sewn by a tailor or made in a factory. A driver may have taken you to school. Your teacher was waiting for you when you arrived.

You did not do all these jobs yourself, and you could not. No person can grow every food, make every tool, treat every illness and teach every child. So we depend on each other. When people depend on one another, we say they are interdependent.

### Words to know
• Depend on: need help or goods from someone else.
• Interdependence: people needing each other and helping each other.
• Service: work done to help someone, such as teaching or driving.
• Goods: things we can touch and use, such as food, clothes and books.

## PAGE 2: Farming and trade
Farmers grow food, but a farmer also needs many things that he or she cannot produce alone. A maize farmer needs seeds, a hoe or a tractor, clothes, medicine, school fees and salt. Farmers get these by selling their maize and buying what they need.

Trade means buying and selling goods. Traders help farmers by buying their crops and carrying them to towns. Without traders, a farmer in a village might have too much maize and no one to sell it to. Without farmers, traders and shopkeepers would have nothing to sell and people in towns would go hungry.

Long ago, people used barter, which means exchanging one thing for another, such as a goat for grain. Today we use money. Money makes trade easier.

### Think about it
How many people helped to bring the sugar in your tea to your cup?

## PAGE 3: Hospitals and health workers
When you are sick, you go to a health centre or a hospital. Doctors, nurses, clinical officers, laboratory workers and pharmacists work there. Ambulance drivers carry patients who are very sick. Community health volunteers visit homes and teach about clean water and vaccines.

These health workers depend on other people too.
• Farmers provide their food.
• Builders made the hospital and cleaners keep it clean.
• Teachers taught them how to read and write, and trainers taught them medicine.
• Drivers and traders bring the medicine and equipment.
• The community gives them respect and pays taxes that help to run public hospitals.

In this way, we all help each other to stay healthy. A child who is treated and gets well can go back to school and later work to help others.

### Good manners at a clinic
• Wait for your turn quietly.
• Thank the nurse or doctor.
• Follow the advice you are given.
• Keep the compound clean.

## PAGE 4: Places of worship and community life
People also depend on each other for spiritual care. A church, mosque, temple or shrine is a place where people pray and worship. Religious leaders teach good values, lead prayers and comfort people who are sad. Worshippers give their time and gifts so that the building and its work can continue.

People also help each other during happy or sad times. At weddings, funerals, births and graduations, neighbours bring food, cook, lend chairs, and give money or comfort. In Kenya we call working together to solve a problem harambee, which means "pull together". Communities have used harambee to build schools, clinics and water points.

### More examples of interdependence
• A teacher teaches all children, and parents pay school fees and bring children to school.
• A tailor makes uniforms and a farmer buys them with money from crops.
• A mechanic repairs matatus and drivers pay for the service.
• A police officer keeps peace, and the community gives information about crime.

### Role-play idea
Choose four pupils. One is a farmer, one is a shopkeeper, one is a nurse, and one is a teacher. Act out a day where each one needs something from the others. Then say how it felt to depend on others.

### Benefits
• We get many goods and services that we could not make ourselves.
• People share skills and each one does the job they do best.
• We save time. Instead of doing everything, we buy what we need.
• We help each other in times of trouble, such as illness, floods or drought.
• We live in peace and become friends when we share.
• The county develops faster when everyone does their part.

## PAGE 6: Showing appreciation
If one group of workers stopped working, everybody would feel it. If farmers stopped farming, there would be no food. If drivers stopped, food and medicine could not reach shops and hospitals. Every job is important, and no job is small.

### How to show that you value others
• Say thank you to the cook, the cleaner, the driver and the teacher.
• Return borrowed things and keep promises.
• Do your own part at home and at school.
• Share what you have with people in need.
• Respect all workers, whatever their job.

### A short poem
We need the farmer for our bread,
The teacher fills our mind,
The nurse who cares when we are ill,
Together we are kind.

### Find out
Ask a parent or guardian: which people help our family, and how do we help them? Draw a small chart like Figure 3 and share it in class.

### Common mistakes
• Thinking that some jobs are more important than others. All useful work helps the community.
• Thinking that being independent means never needing anyone. Everyone needs help sometimes.

## QUIZ
1. What does interdependence mean?
   A) Doing everything by yourself
   B) People depending on and helping one another
   C) Living alone
   D) Selling goods far away
   Answer: B
   Why: Interdependence means that people need each other and help each other.
2. Why do farmers need traders?
   A) Traders buy crops and carry them to towns
   B) Traders grow the crops
   C) Traders build hospitals
   D) Traders teach in school
   Answer: A
   Why: Traders buy from farmers and take crops to markets where people need them.
3. Which of these is a service, not a good?
   A) Maize
   B) A book
   C) Treatment by a nurse
   D) A pair of shoes
   Answer: C
   Why: A service is work done to help someone. Maize, books and shoes are things we can touch, which are goods.
4. What is harambee?
   A) Working together to solve a problem
   B) A kind of market
   C) A type of crop
   D) A holiday
   Answer: A
   Why: Harambee means pulling together, when people join efforts to do something for the community.
5. Which is a benefit of interdependence?
   A) People get goods and services they cannot make themselves
   B) Everyone does the same job
   C) People stop trading
   D) Nobody needs money
   Answer: A
   Why: Because people specialise and share, everyone can get many more goods and services.
6. Give three examples of people who depend on each other in a community. (3 marks)
   Answer: A farmer and a trader, a teacher and parents, a nurse and the community. Accept other correct pairs.
   Why: 1 mark for each correct pair.
7. Explain how health workers depend on farmers and teachers. (2 marks)
   Answer: Health workers need food that farmers grow. They were taught to read and study by teachers.
   Why: 1 mark for farmers and food, 1 mark for teachers and education.
8. State three benefits of interdependence in a county. (3 marks)
   Answer: People get goods and services they cannot produce, people help each other in trouble, and the county develops faster.
   Why: 1 mark for each correct benefit.
9. Draw a simple chart with three workers, what each gives, and what each needs from others. (3 marks)
   Answer: A chart showing, for example, farmer gives food and needs clothes; teacher gives learning and needs food; driver gives transport and needs repairs.
   Why: 1 mark for three workers, 1 mark for what they give, 1 mark for what they need.
