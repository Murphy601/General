## PAGE 1: How many people live here?
When you enter a big market on Saturday, you see crowds of people. When you walk in a quiet part of the village at midday, you might see nobody at all. People are not spread evenly across the land. Some places are crowded and others are almost empty.

The number of people living in a place is called its population. Kenya has a population census about every ten years, where trained officers count everyone. In 2019 the census counted about 47.6 million people in Kenya. Every county also has a population, and so does every sub-county.

Population distribution means how the people are spread out over an area. When we talk about population distribution in a county, we ask two questions:
• Where do most people live?
• Where do very few people live?

### Key words
• Population: the total number of people living in a place.
• Population distribution: how people are spread out over an area.
• Densely populated: many people live close together.
• Sparsely populated: few people live in a large area.

### Think about it
Is your school compound crowded at break time? Is it crowded at 6 pm? How does the distribution of people change during the day?

## PAGE 2: Patterns of distribution
There are three main patterns that we can describe.

• Dense (crowded) areas: many people live close to each other. Towns like Nairobi, Mombasa, Kisumu and Nakuru are densely populated. Good farming areas such as the highlands around Mount Kenya and parts of Western Kenya are also crowded.
• Sparse (thinly populated) areas: few people live in a wide area. Very dry places in northern Kenya, such as parts of Turkana, Marsabit, Wajir and Mandera, have few people for their size.
• Moderate areas: not crowded and not empty. Many rural areas fit this description.

A single county can have all three patterns. There may be a crowded town, a moderate farming area and an almost empty dry or forest area.

### Words to describe a pattern
• Clustered: people live in groups close together, for example around a town centre.
• Linear: people live in a line, for example along a road or a river.
• Scattered: people live far apart from each other, each family on its own land.

### Try it
Look out from a hill or a tall building if you can. Do the houses cluster around a trading centre or a road? Or are they scattered in the fields?

## PAGE 4: Why people live where they do
People choose places where life is easier. These are the main reasons.

### Reasons for crowded areas
• Good rainfall and fertile soil, so farming is easy. Food is plentiful.
• Towns and trading centres, because they have jobs, shops, schools and hospitals.
• Water from rivers, lakes or boreholes.
• Good roads and railways make it easy to travel and trade.
• Fishing on the lakeshore or the coast.
• Peace and safety, and a cool healthy climate.

### Reasons for thinly populated areas
• Very little rain, so the land is dry and hard to farm.
• Thick forests or swamps, which are hard to live in.
• Steep mountains and rocky land that is difficult to build on.
• Game parks and forest reserves, where people are not allowed to settle.
• Few roads and services.
• Insecurity, floods or diseases in some places.

### Remember
In dry areas, many families keep livestock and move with their animals to look for water and grass. This is a good way to use dry land, so fewer people live in one place.

### Discussion
Why do you think many families move from villages to cities to find jobs? What problems can crowded places have?

### A shaded map
Another way is to shade a map. Use dark colours where many people live, medium colours for moderate populations and light colours where few people live. Always add a key.

### A dot map
Draw the boundary of your county. Put the dots where people live. Make sure that each dot stands for the same number of people, and write what a dot stands for.

### Display
Put your map of population distribution in the class learning corner. Explain why the dots are where they are.

## PAGE 6: Summary and appreciation
Population distribution tells us where people live in a county and how crowded each place is. The pattern depends on rainfall, soil, water, towns, roads, jobs and safety. People in a crowded place need many schools, clinics and roads. Where people are few, services can be far apart, so county planners must think about how to reach them.

### Common mistakes
• Thinking that a big county always has a big population. Area and population are different.
• Forgetting the key on a dot map or a shaded map. Without it, nobody knows what the dots or colours mean.
• Thinking that sparse means bad. Dry areas still support herders and wildlife and are valuable.

### Appreciate
Every area of the county is important. The crowded town provides jobs and markets. The farmland grows food. The quiet dry lands carry cattle and beautiful wildlife. We should value and serve all people wherever they live.

## QUIZ
1. What is meant by population?
   A) The number of people living in a place
   B) The size of a place
   C) The number of schools in a place
   D) The rainfall of a place
   Answer: A
   Why: Population is the total number of people who live in an area.
2. Which area is likely to be sparsely populated?
   A) A busy town centre
   B) A fertile highland farming area
   C) A very dry area with little rain
   D) A place next to a large market
   Answer: C
   Why: Very dry land is hard to farm and to get water from, so few people settle there.
3. On a dot map where 1 dot = 1,000 people, an area has 5 dots. How many people live there?
   A) 50
   B) 500
   C) 5,000
   D) 50,000
   Answer: C
   Why: 5 dots x 1,000 people = 5,000 people.
4. Which of these is a reason why many people live in towns?
   A) There are no jobs
   B) There are jobs, shops, schools and hospitals
   C) There is no water
   D) There are no roads
   Answer: B
   Why: Services and jobs in towns attract people.
5. In Figure 2, which sub-county has the fewest people?
   A) A
   B) B
   C) C
   D) D
   Answer: D
   Why: Sub-county D has the shortest bar, 15 thousand people.
6. State three factors that make an area densely populated. (3 marks)
   Answer: Fertile soil and good rain, jobs in a town, and a good water supply. Accept good roads or safety.
   Why: 1 mark for each correct factor.
7. Explain why very few people live in a forest reserve. (2 marks)
   Answer: The law does not allow people to settle in a reserve. Thick forest is also hard to farm and live in.
   Why: 1 mark for each valid reason.
8. In Figure 2, how many more people live in sub-county C than in sub-county B? Show your working. (3 marks)
   Answer: 15 thousand more people, which is 15,000.
   Why: C has 45 thousand and B has 30 thousand. 45 - 30 = 15 thousand, which is 15,000 people. 1 mark for each reading, 1 mark for the subtraction.
9. Describe how you would draw a dot map of population in your county. (3 marks)
   Answer: Draw the county boundary, decide how many people each dot stands for, put more dots where more people live and fewer where few live, and add a key and a title.
   Why: 1 mark for the boundary, 1 mark for dots placed by population, 1 mark for the key.
