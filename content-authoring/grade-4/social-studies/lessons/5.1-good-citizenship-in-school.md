## PAGE 1: Who is a citizen?
A citizen is a person who belongs to a country or a community, who enjoys its protection, and who has duties towards it. If you are a Kenyan citizen, you belong to Kenya. You have the right to go to school, to be protected and to be treated fairly. You also have duties, such as obeying the law, respecting others and taking care of public property.

Your school is like a small country. It has leaders, rules, property and a community of people who live and work together. In this community you are a citizen too. A school citizen has rights, such as to learn in a safe place, and duties, such as to follow the rules and care for others.

A good citizen does what is right for the whole community, not only for himself or herself. This is the same at home, in school, and in the country.

### Key words
• Citizen: a member of a country or community with rights and duties.
• Duty: something you are expected to do.
• Right: something you are allowed to have or do, such as learning.
• Community: a group of people who live or work together.

### Think about it
What is one right you have at school? What is one duty you have at school? Do you think they belong together?

## PAGE 2: Qualities of a good citizen in school
Here are the main qualities. Each one has an example.

• Honesty: telling the truth. Example: returning a pen you found to its owner.
• Respect: treating people and things with care. Example: greeting teachers and listening when someone speaks.
• Responsibility: doing your duty without being told. Example: cleaning the board when it is your turn.
• Obedience: following school rules. Example: lining up quietly.
• Cooperation: working well with others. Example: sharing tasks in a group.
• Care and kindness: helping those in need. Example: helping a new pupil find the class.
• Cleanliness: keeping yourself and the school clean. Example: putting litter in the bin.
• Punctuality: arriving on time.
• Patriotism: loving and respecting your country. Example: standing still and singing the national anthem with respect.
• Courage: doing what is right even when it is hard, and telling a teacher about bullying.

## PAGE 3: Showing good citizenship in real situations
Qualities are shown by our actions. Here are some situations. Think what a good citizen would do.

### Situation 1: A lost coin
Kamau finds Ksh 50 near the classroom door. A good citizen hands it to the teacher, so that the owner can be found. This shows honesty.

### Situation 2: The messy compound
After break, there are sweets wrappers on the ground. Even if you did not drop them, you can pick them up and put them in the bin. This shows responsibility and cleanliness.

### Situation 3: The new pupil
A girl has joined your class and does not know anyone. You invite her to play and show her where the toilets are. This shows kindness and welcome.

### Situation 4: The unfair game
In a football match a player breaks the rules. A good citizen accepts the referee's decision and does not fight. This shows respect and peace.

### Situation 5: Group work
In a group activity, one pupil does everything while others chat. A good citizen shares the tasks. This shows cooperation.

### Role-play
In groups of four, pick one situation. One person plays the good citizen. Act it out for the class. Discuss what happened and how each person felt. Then act out a second version where the pupil makes the wrong choice, and say what should change.

## PAGE 4: Being a good digital citizen
Many pupils use phones, tablets or computers at home, at school or at a cybercafe. The internet is a place where we also behave as citizens. A good digital citizen uses technology in a positive and safe way.

### Rules for good digital citizens
• Ask a parent, guardian or teacher before you use a device or go online.
• Be kind. Do not send or post mean messages. Bullying online is still bullying.
• Keep private things private. Do not share your full name, address, school, phone number or password with strangers.
• Do not share pictures of other people without their permission.
• Check facts. Not everything online is true.
• Do not talk to strangers online, and never agree to meet someone you met online.
• Use screen time wisely. Balance it with study, sleep, play and family time.
• Tell a trusted adult if something online makes you scared or uncomfortable.

## PAGE 5: Role models and growing our qualities
A role model is a person whose good behaviour others admire and copy. A role model may be a teacher, a nurse, a religious leader, an elder, a parent, an older pupil or a community leader. You can invite a role model to talk to your class about how they became a good citizen. Ask questions such as: What do you do to be honest? What did you learn from school?

### How to develop good qualities
1. Choose one quality to practise this week, such as punctuality.
2. Set a small goal. For example, arrive five minutes early every day.
3. Practise every day until it becomes a habit.
4. When you make a mistake, say sorry and try again.
5. Ask a friend to remind you and encourage you.
6. Join a school club or duty, such as the environment club.

### Writing about good citizenship
You may be asked to write an essay. Use three parts:
• Beginning: say what a good citizen is.
• Middle: describe two or three qualities and give an example of each.
• End: say how you will be a good citizen.

### Designing a poster
A poster needs a big clear title, a picture, and one or two short sentences. For example, the title "Be Honest" and a picture of a pupil handing in a lost pencil.

## PAGE 6: Why good citizens matter
When every pupil is a good citizen, the school is peaceful, clean and successful. Teachers can teach, pupils can learn, and everyone feels safe. When we practise good citizenship at school, we are getting ready to be good citizens of our county and of Kenya.

### Benefits
• The school is safe and orderly.
• Pupils learn more.
• People trust each other.
• Property lasts longer.
• Our school gets a good name.

### Summary
A citizen is a member of a community with rights and duties. A good citizen in school is honest, respectful, responsible, obedient, cooperative, kind, clean, punctual and patriotic. A good digital citizen uses technology safely and kindly. We learn these qualities by practice and from role models.

### Common mistakes
• Being good only when the teacher is watching. Integrity means being good even when nobody sees.
• Thinking that duties are boring and only rights matter. Both are needed.
• Believing anything you read online without checking.
• Sharing your password with friends.

## QUIZ
1. What is a citizen?
   A) A person who belongs to a country or community and has rights and duties
   B) A person who is visiting from another country
   C) A type of teacher
   D) A kind of law
   Answer: A
   Why: A citizen is a member of a country or community with rights and duties.
2. You find a pencil that is not yours. What should a good citizen do?
   A) Keep it
   B) Throw it away
   C) Return it to the owner or give it to the teacher
   D) Sell it
   Answer: C
   Why: Returning lost items shows honesty.
3. Which of these is an example of bad digital citizenship?
   A) Asking permission to use the phone
   B) Sending unkind messages to a classmate
   C) Keeping your password secret
   D) Telling an adult about something that scares you
   Answer: B
   Why: Mean online messages are cyberbullying. The other choices are safe and good behaviour.
4. What does punctuality mean?
   A) Arriving on time
   B) Speaking loudly
   C) Sharing food
   D) Wearing a uniform
   Answer: A
   Why: Punctual people arrive at the right time.
5. In Figure 2, what should you do before you post something online?
   A) Stop and think
   B) Post quickly
   C) Ask a stranger
   D) Share your password
   Answer: A
   Why: The red light says stop, and the yellow light says think before you share.
6. State three qualities of a good citizen in school. (3 marks)
   Answer: Honesty, respect, responsibility. Accept cooperation, kindness or cleanliness.
   Why: 1 mark for each correct quality.
7. Explain how a pupil can show responsibility in the classroom. (2 marks)
   Answer: Do assigned duties without being reminded, such as cleaning the board, and take care of books and desks.
   Why: 1 mark for naming a duty, 1 mark for showing it is done without being told.
8. List three rules for staying safe online. (3 marks)
   Answer: Ask an adult before going online, never share private details or passwords with strangers, and tell a trusted adult about anything that upsets you.
   Why: 1 mark for each correct rule.
9. Write two sentences to invite a role model to speak in your school about good citizenship. (2 marks)
   Answer: Dear Sir, our class would be honoured if you would speak to us about how to be a good citizen. Please let us know a day that suits you.
   Why: 1 mark for a polite invitation, 1 mark for stating the topic.
