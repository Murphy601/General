## PAGE 1: Choosing a project for our school
An enterprise is a business activity that people start to make goods or provide services and to earn money. A project is a plan of work that has a start, a finish and a goal. An enterprise project at school is a small business that the pupils run with the help of their teachers, for example growing vegetables and selling them.

Why do schools do enterprise projects? They teach us to work together, to be honest with money, and to be creative. The money earned can buy books, seedlings, sports equipment or repair a desk.

### Ideas for school projects
• A vegetable garden growing sukuma wiki, spinach or tomatoes
• A tree nursery selling seedlings
• Keeping chickens or rabbits for eggs or meat
• Making paper bags, bead jewellery or reusable shopping bags
• Collecting and selling clean waste paper or plastic bottles for recycling
• Making safety bibs and armbands for pupils who walk to school

### How to choose a good project
Ask these questions:
1. Do people need or want what we will make?
2. Do we have the land, materials and skills?
3. Is it safe for children?
4. Can we finish it in the time we have?
5. Will we earn more money than we spend?

If the answer to each question is yes, the project is viable, which means it can work.

### Think about it
Which project would suit your school best? What resources already exist in your school compound?

### The plan should say
• What we will make and how many.
• Who will do each job.
• Where and when we will work.
• What materials and tools we need.
• How much money we need and where it will come from.
• Where and to whom we will sell.

### Safety
Sharp tools, fire, chemicals and heavy loads are dangerous for children. Adults must handle them. Wear gloves when handling soil and wash your hands afterwards.

## PAGE 3: Costs, income and profit
### Words about money
• Cost (expenses): money you spend on the project.
• Income (sales): money you receive from selling.
• Profit: the money left after you pay all the costs. Profit = income - costs.
• Loss: when you spend more than you earn.

### Example: a school tree nursery
Costs:
• Seeds: Ksh 300
• Polythene tubes: Ksh 200
• Tools: Ksh 100
Total cost = 300 + 200 + 100 = Ksh 600

Income: 80 seedlings sold at Ksh 20 each = 80 x 20 = Ksh 1,600

Profit = income - costs = 1,600 - 600 = Ksh 1,000

### Example of a loss
Suppose another group spends Ksh 800 and earns only Ksh 500.
Loss = 800 - 500 = Ksh 300.
They should ask why sales were low. Maybe the price was too high, or not enough people knew about their goods.

## PAGE 4: Roles and teamwork
An enterprise works best when everyone has a role. When people share duties, no one is overloaded and the group achieves more.

### Common roles
• Patron: a teacher who guides and advises.
• Project manager: leads the team and makes sure work is on time.
• Treasurer: keeps the money and records it.
• Record keeper (secretary): writes plans, minutes and attendance.
• Production team: grows or makes the goods.
• Sales team: sells and tells others about the product.
• Safety officer: makes sure everyone follows safety rules.

### Rules for teamwork
• Listen to each other and respect ideas.
• Do your part on time.
• Help others who are struggling.
• Share praise for success and share blame for problems.

## PAGE 5: Ethics: doing business the right way
Ethics are the rules of right and wrong behaviour. A project can earn money, but it must also be honest and fair.

### Good practices
• Honesty: do not cheat customers by giving the wrong change or selling poor goods as good ones.
• Accountability: everyone who handles money must record it and be ready to show it.
• Fairness: share work and rewards fairly. Do not favour friends.
• Care for property: look after tools and materials.
• Care for the environment: do not pollute, waste water or leave rubbish behind.
• Safety: never let people work in dangerous ways.
• No child labour: pupils do the work for learning and only in safe ways, and it must not interfere with lessons.
• Wise use of profit: agree together on how profit will be used, for example to buy books, more seeds and equipment.

### Collective effort
Collective effort means working together as a team. One person alone cannot dig a whole garden, water it, harvest, sell and count the money. As a team, the work is quicker and more fun. If you see a team member doing wrong, such as taking money, tell the teacher.

### A test of honesty
Wanjiru sold 10 tomatoes but only recorded the money for 8. Is this right? No. The record must match the sales. She should correct the record and tell the treasurer.

## PAGE 6: Designing safety gear for the road
Many pupils walk to school along roads. Drivers need to see them clearly, especially in the early morning, in the rain and at dusk. Personal protective equipment (PPE) is clothing or gear that protects a person from harm. On the road, bright and reflective items help drivers to notice you.

### Make a safety bib
You will need: bright orange, yellow or green cloth or an old bright cloth bag, shiny reflective tape or strips of silver material, and strong thread or glue. An adult should help with cutting and stitching.

### Steps
1. Sketch your design and decide the size for pupils.
2. Cut the cloth in the shape shown, with an opening for the head.
3. Fix the reflective strips across the front and back.
4. Test it. Is it easy to wear and can it be seen from a distance?
5. Improve it if necessary.

### Other road safety ideas
• Wear light or bright clothes when walking on the road.
• Carry a bright bag.
• Never walk on the road while looking at a phone.

## PAGE 7: Showing and judging our project
When the work is done, we show what we learned. Invite other classes and parents to a project day.

### How to showcase
• Set up a stall with the goods and a poster that explains the project.
• Show your record of costs, income and profit.
• Explain the roles each team member played.
• Let visitors ask questions and give feedback.

### Evaluate the project
Ask these questions:
1. Did we reach our goal?
2. Did we make a profit or a loss? Why?
3. Did everyone do their part?
4. Did we follow the ethical rules?
5. What will we do better next time?

### Summary
An enterprise project is a small business run by pupils. We choose a project that is useful and safe, plan it, share roles, keep records of money, and follow honest practices. Profit is income minus costs. We work as a team and review our results.

### Common mistakes
• Choosing a project without checking costs and skills.
• Forgetting to write down the money.
• Leaving all the work to a few pupils.
• Forgetting that profit is income minus costs, not just the money you received.

## QUIZ
1. What is profit?
   A) All the money you receive
   B) The money left after paying all the costs
   C) Money you borrow
   D) The cost of the goods
   Answer: B
   Why: Profit = income - costs. It is what remains after the costs are paid.
2. A group spent Ksh 500 and earned Ksh 900. What was the profit?
   A) Ksh 400
   B) Ksh 500
   C) Ksh 900
   D) Ksh 1,400
   Answer: A
   Why: 900 - 500 = Ksh 400.
3. Who keeps the money and records it in a school project?
   A) The safety officer
   B) The treasurer
   C) The sales team
   D) The patron
   Answer: B
   Why: The treasurer is responsible for keeping and recording the money.
4. Which of these is an honest practice?
   A) Giving the wrong change on purpose
   B) Recording every sale correctly
   C) Hiding some of the money
   D) Selling poor goods as good ones
   Answer: B
   Why: Honesty means keeping true records and treating customers fairly.
5. What is the purpose of reflective strips on safety gear?
   A) To make it heavy
   B) To help drivers to see the wearer
   C) To keep out rain
   D) To make noise
   Answer: B
   Why: Reflective material shines when light hits it and warns drivers.
6. A group spent Ksh 300 on seeds and Ksh 150 on tools. They sold vegetables for Ksh 700. Work out the profit. (3 marks)
   Answer: Ksh 250.
   Why: Total cost = 300 + 150 = 450. Profit = 700 - 450 = Ksh 250. 1 mark for the total cost, 1 mark for subtracting, 1 mark for the answer with Ksh.
7. Give three questions to ask when choosing a school enterprise project. (3 marks)
   Answer: Do people want the goods? Do we have the materials and skills? Is it safe? Accept: Can we earn more than we spend?
   Why: 1 mark for each sensible question.
8. Explain why it is good for a project team to share roles. (2 marks)
   Answer: Nobody is overloaded and the work is completed faster and better because each person does the job they can do well.
   Why: 1 mark for sharing the load, 1 mark for better results.
9. State two ethical practices a school enterprise should follow. (2 marks)
   Answer: Be honest with money and customers, and be fair in sharing work and rewards. Accept caring for the environment.
   Why: 1 mark for each correct practice.
