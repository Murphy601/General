## PAGE 1: Children have rights
A right is something that every person is allowed to have or to do, and that nobody may take away. Children have rights because they are young and need care and protection while they grow.

The Constitution of Kenya and many laws say that every child in Kenya has the right to:
• A name and to belong to a country
• Free and compulsory basic education
• Enough food and clean water, and a safe home
• Health care
• Care from parents or guardians
• Protection from abuse, neglect and harmful cultural practices
• Protection from hard and dangerous work
• Play, rest and be listened to

Rights come with responsibilities. A responsibility is a duty that you should do. When we enjoy rights, we must also respect other people's rights.

## PAGE 2: Forms of child abuse
Child abuse means treating a child in a way that harms the child's body, feelings or future. Some of the forms of abuse in communities are these.

• Early and forced marriage: marrying a child before adult age, or forcing anyone to marry. In Kenya a person must be at least 18 years to marry.
• Female genital mutilation (FGM): cutting a girl's private parts. It is harmful and is against the law in Kenya.
• Slavery: forcing a person to work without pay and treating them as property.
• Child trafficking: taking children away from their homes by trick or force, in order to make money from them.
• Child labour or forced labour: making children do hard, dangerous or long work that harms their health or stops them going to school.
• Sexual abuse: when an adult or older person touches a child in a private way or makes a child feel unsafe or uncomfortable. It is never the child's fault.
• Abuse of children with special needs: hiding, neglecting, laughing at, or hurting children who have disabilities. They have the same rights as all children.
• Unauthorised school transport: putting children in vehicles that are unsafe, overloaded, not licensed or approved by the school and parents.

### Neglect and other harm
Neglect is not giving a child food, care and a safe place. Beating a child, shouting insults and refusing to send a child to school are also harmful.

### Think about it
Why should we speak about these things? So that children know they can be protected and know where to get help.

## PAGE 3: Causes of child abuse
Why does child abuse happen? There are many reasons. None of them is an excuse.

• Poverty: some families lack money, so they force children to work or marry early for money.
• Harmful beliefs and customs: some people believe old practices such as early marriage or FGM are needed, even though they harm children.
• Lack of knowledge: some adults do not know children's rights or the law.
• Unequal treatment of girls and boys: in some places girls are seen as less important, so they suffer more.
• Greed: traffickers and bad employers want to make money from children.
• Conflict, drought and displacement: children lose their homes or families and become more at risk.
• Alcohol and drug abuse: some adults become violent.
• Lack of supervision: children left alone or travelling with strangers.
• Fear of speaking: children or families keep quiet about abuse because of shame or fear.

### Gender and abuse
Girls and boys have equal rights. Both can suffer abuse. Girls are more at risk of some harmful practices, such as early marriage and FGM, and boys are sometimes forced into hard labour such as herding or work at construction sites. We need to protect all.

### Discussion
What can a community do to reduce poverty so that families do not need to use children to earn money?

## PAGE 4: Effects of child abuse
Abuse harms the child, the family and the community.

### On the child
• Physical harm: pain, injuries, illness, and even death.
• Emotional harm: fear, sadness, shame and loss of self-confidence.
• Lost education: children drop out of school and lose the chance for a good future.
• Health problems: early pregnancy, infections, and long-term pain.
• Bad behaviour: some abused children become angry or withdrawn.

### On the family and community
• Families lose the hope and talents of their children.
• The community remains poor when children are not educated.
• Fear and mistrust spread.
• Some abused children may repeat the harm on others when they grow up if they are not helped.

### The good news
Abuse can be prevented. Children who are protected and encouraged can heal and grow into strong adults. Kenyan law provides protection, and there are people who will help.

## PAGE 5: Staying safe and getting help
Every child can learn how to stay safe.

### Body safety rules
• Your body belongs to you. The parts covered by your underwear are private.
• No one should touch your private parts or ask you to touch theirs. Only a doctor, with a parent present, may check your body when you are ill.
• Do not keep secrets that make you feel scared, sad or confused.
• Trust your feelings. If something feels wrong, it probably is.

### Trusted adults
A trusted adult may be a parent, guardian, teacher, school counsellor, religious leader or a local administrator. If the first person does not help, keep telling until someone does. In Kenya, the child helpline 116 can be called free of charge, to get help and advice. Ask your teacher to confirm the number.

### Safe school transport
• Use only transport that your parents and school have approved.
• Do not accept lifts from strangers or people you do not know well.
• Do not board a vehicle that is overloaded, or where there are no seats, or where the driver seems unwell or unsafe.
• Wear a seat belt when there is one.
• Sit properly and do not put your head or arms outside.

## PAGE 6: Promoting child rights in school
Children can also protect and support each other. Here are some activities.

• Design posters showing the effects of abuse and where to find help.
• Sing songs and recite poems about children's rights and staying safe.
• Set up a suggestion box or a child-friendly reporting corner.
• Tell other pupils about the dangers of unsafe transport.
• Be kind to children with special needs. Include them in games and help them.
• Celebrate the Day of the African Child on 16 June with drama and songs.
• Respect each other's rights, do not bully, and do not laugh at others.

### Laws that protect children
Kenya has laws that protect children, such as the Constitution and the Children Act. The law forbids FGM and child labour, and it says a person must be 18 to marry. People who harm children can be punished.

### Summary
Children have rights, such as education, health care and protection, and matching responsibilities. Forms of child abuse include early and forced marriage, FGM, slavery, trafficking, child labour, sexual abuse, abuse of children with special needs and unsafe school transport. Poverty, harmful beliefs and lack of knowledge are causes. Abuse harms children physically and emotionally. We can say no, go away and tell a trusted adult.

### Common mistakes
• Thinking that a child who is abused is to blame. It is never the child's fault.
• Keeping quiet because of fear. Telling a trusted adult is brave and right.
• Thinking that rights mean we can do whatever we want. Rights come with responsibilities.

## QUIZ
1. Which of these is a child right?
   A) To work all day
   B) To go to school
   C) To marry early
   D) To be left alone
   Answer: B
   Why: Every child has the right to free and compulsory basic education.
2. What is child labour?
   A) Light help at home after school
   B) Hard or dangerous work that harms a child or stops schooling
   C) Doing homework
   D) Playing sports
   Answer: B
   Why: Child labour harms a child's health or stops the child from learning.
3. What is the minimum age at which a person can marry in Kenya?
   A) 12
   B) 15
   C) 16
   D) 18
   Answer: D
   Why: The law says a person must be 18 to marry, so early marriage of children is against the law.
4. If someone makes you feel unsafe, what should you do?
   A) Say no, go to a safe place and tell a trusted adult
   B) Keep it a secret
   C) Fight the person
   D) Stay silent
   Answer: A
   Why: Say no, go away and tell an adult you trust. Keeping it secret allows harm to continue.
5. Which is safe school transport?
   A) An overloaded vehicle
   B) A lift from a stranger
   C) A vehicle approved by the school and parents
   D) A vehicle with no seats
   Answer: C
   Why: Only approved transport with a responsible driver is safe.
6. Name three forms of child abuse. (3 marks)
   Answer: Early and forced marriage, child labour and FGM. Accept trafficking, slavery, sexual abuse or abuse of children with special needs.
   Why: 1 mark for each correct form.
7. Give two causes of child abuse. (2 marks)
   Answer: Poverty and harmful beliefs. Accept lack of knowledge or greed.
   Why: 1 mark for each cause.
8. Explain two ways in which child abuse affects a child. (2 marks)
   Answer: The child may be physically hurt or sick, and may miss school and lose the chance of a good future.
   Why: 1 mark for each valid effect.
9. Describe how your class could promote child rights in your school. (3 marks)
   Answer: Make posters, sing songs about staying safe, and treat children with special needs kindly. Also set up a suggestion box.
   Why: 1 mark for each sensible activity.
