## PAGE 1: Our county and its sub-counties
Kenya is divided into 47 counties. This system was set up by the Constitution of Kenya in 2010. Every county has a name, a county government, and a capital town where its offices are found. Nairobi County contains Kenya's capital city. Kisumu, Mombasa, Nakuru, Kakamega, Turkana and Kitui are also counties.

A county is big, so it is divided into smaller parts called sub-counties. Each sub-county is then divided into wards. This makes it easier to serve people and to plan for schools, roads, health centres and markets.

Some real examples: Mombasa County has sub-counties such as Nyali, Likoni and Mvita. Kisumu County has sub-counties such as Kisumu Central and Nyakach. Your own county has its own list.

### Find out
• What is the name of your county?
• What is the name of your county headquarters town?
• In which sub-county is your school found?
• Can you name two other sub-counties in your county?

### Key words
• County: one of the 47 areas that Kenya is divided into.
• Sub-county: a smaller part of a county.
• Neighbouring county: a county that shares a boundary with yours.

Ask your teacher or a parent to help you list all the sub-counties in your county. You can also look at a county map in the school library or on a computer.

## PAGE 2: Where is a county? Using neighbours to describe location
To describe the location of a county, we say which counties lie around it. We use the four cardinal points to do this. This is called relative location, because we compare the county with its neighbours.

## PAGE 3: Kenya in Africa and the world
Your county belongs to Kenya, and Kenya belongs to Africa. Knowing the bigger picture helps us describe location clearly.

Kenya is in East Africa. The equator, an imaginary line around the middle of the Earth, crosses the country. It crosses the country near the town of Nanyuki. The Indian Ocean is on the south-east side of the country. Kenya has five neighbouring countries:
• Uganda to the west
• Tanzania to the south
• Ethiopia to the north
• South Sudan to the north-west
• Somalia to the east and north-east

Some counties are on the border with another country. Busia is on the border with Uganda. Kwale and Taita Taveta counties are on the border with Tanzania. Turkana borders South Sudan, Uganda and Ethiopia. Other counties are next to the Indian Ocean, such as Mombasa, Kilifi and Lamu. Some counties, like Nyeri and Nairobi, are inland and do not touch a border.

### Try it
Use a map of Kenya. Find your county. Write down:
1. The counties that touch yours.
2. Whether your county touches another country or the ocean.
3. The direction from your county to Nairobi.

## PAGE 4: Size of the county
The size of a county is how much land it covers. Big counties have a lot of land and small counties have less. Size is not the same as population. A county can be large in area and have few people.

In Kenya, the counties are very different in size. Turkana is the largest county. Mombasa is the smallest county. Most counties are somewhere in between.

To estimate size we do not need to measure exactly. We can compare the shapes on a map that has the same scale. Then we say a county is larger than, smaller than, or about the same size as its neighbour.

### The grid-square method
Put tracing paper with squares over the map. Count the squares that each county covers. The county with more squares is bigger.

## PAGE 5: Sketching the map of our county
A sketch map is a simple drawing of an area. It does not have to be exact, but it must be neat and clear. Here are the steps.

1. Look at a map of your county in an atlas, a wall map or on a computer.
2. Draw the outline of the county boundary in the middle of a page. Use a soft pencil first.
3. Draw the boundaries between the sub-counties with thinner lines.
4. Write the name of each sub-county inside its part.
5. Show the neighbouring counties around the edge in a light grey.
6. Add a north arrow, a title and a key.
7. Colour your map. Use one colour for your county and light colours for the others.

### Parts of a good map
• Title: tells what the map is about, for example Map of ______ County.
• North arrow or compass rose: shows direction.
• Key: explains the colours and lines used.

### Display it
Put the finished maps on the class wall. Do a gallery walk. Look at your friends' maps and say one thing you like and one thing they can improve.

## PAGE 6: Why location and size matter
Knowing where the county is and how big it is helps us in many ways.

• Travel: We can plan a journey and know which counties we pass through.
• Planning: A large county with scattered villages needs more roads and more health centres than a small crowded one.
• Sharing resources: Neighbouring counties may share a river, a market or a game park. They must work together to look after these.
• Belonging: When we know our county well, we feel proud of it.

### Class game: Guess the county
One pupil thinks of a county. The others ask questions that can be answered yes or no.
• Is it north of our county?
• Does it touch the ocean?
• Is it bigger than our county?
The pupil who guesses the county correctly gets the next turn.

### Common mistakes
• Saying that a county is big because it has many people. Size means area, not population.
• Mixing up a county and a sub-county. A county is bigger and contains the sub-counties.
• Leaving out the north arrow on a map.

## QUIZ
1. How many counties does Kenya have?
   A) 27
   B) 37
   C) 47
   D) 57
   Answer: C
   Why: The Constitution of Kenya 2010 created 47 counties.
2. A county is divided into smaller parts called what?
   A) Sub-counties
   B) Countries
   C) Continents
   D) Oceans
   Answer: A
   Why: Each county is divided into sub-counties, and sub-counties are divided into wards.
3. In Figure 1, which county lies to the east of the imaginary county?
   A) The one on the left
   B) The one on the right
   C) The one at the top
   D) The one at the bottom
   Answer: B
   Why: East is on the right side of a map when north is at the top.
4. Which county is the smallest in Kenya?
   A) Turkana
   B) Nakuru
   C) Mombasa
   D) Kitui
   Answer: C
   Why: Mombasa is the smallest county by area. Turkana is the largest.
5. County X covers 12 grid squares and County Y covers 6 grid squares. Which statement is true?
   A) County Y is larger than County X
   B) County X is larger than County Y
   C) They are the same size
   D) Size cannot be compared
   Answer: B
   Why: 12 squares is more than 6 squares, so County X covers more land.
6. Explain what the relative location of a county means and give an example. (3 marks)
   Answer: Relative location means describing where a county is by saying which places lie around it, for example the county to the north or to the east of it.
   Why: 1 mark for saying it compares with neighbours, 1 mark for using cardinal points, 1 mark for a correct example.
7. List three things that a good sketch map of your county must have. (3 marks)
   Answer: A title, a north arrow (or compass rose) and a key.
   Why: 1 mark for each correct item. Accept neat boundaries or sub-county names as one of the three.
8. Give two reasons why it is important to know the size and location of your county. (2 marks)
   Answer: It helps in planning journeys and in planning services such as roads and health centres. It also helps neighbouring counties to share and care for resources.
   Why: 1 mark for each sensible reason.
9. Explain why a county with a big area may not have the most people. (2 marks)
   Answer: Size is about the area of land. Population is the number of people. A large county may be very dry or have few settlements, so few people live there.
   Why: 1 mark for separating area from population, 1 mark for a reason.
