## PAGE 1: What is democracy?
Suppose your class wants to choose a game to play on Friday afternoon. The teacher says, "Let everyone say what game they like, and then we will vote." Some pupils choose football, some choose netball, and some choose skipping. After counting the votes, the game with the most votes is chosen. Even those whose choice lost feel that the process was fair, because everyone had a chance to speak and vote.

This is democracy. Democracy is a way of making decisions and choosing leaders where everyone has a say. The word means "rule by the people". In a democracy, people:
• choose their own leaders by voting,
• are free to give their opinions,
• are treated equally, and
• accept the choice of the majority while still respecting those who chose differently.

Democracy is practised at different levels. In Kenya, adults aged 18 and above vote for leaders such as the President, the Governor and members of county assemblies in elections held every five years. In school, pupils can practise democracy too, so that they learn how to be good citizens of tomorrow.

### Key words
• Democracy: rule by the people, where all have a say.
• Vote: to show your choice of leader or idea.
• Election: a process where people vote to choose leaders.
• Majority: the larger number of votes.

### Think about it
Have you ever voted in class? What was it about? Did you accept the result?

## PAGE 2: Democratic processes in our school
A school shows democracy in many ways. Look for these in your school.

• Electing leaders: pupils vote for class monitors, prefects and other leaders.
• Class meetings: pupils and teacher meet to talk about problems and agree on solutions.
• Voting on decisions: pupils vote for the class motto, a trip or a game.
• Suggestion box: pupils drop notes with ideas and complaints. The school listens.
• Debates: pupils discuss two sides of an issue politely.
• Committees: small groups such as the environment committee or games committee include representatives from different classes.
• Children's Government: a group of elected pupils who speak for others.

### Not every decision is a vote
Some decisions are made by teachers and the school management, for example the timetable and safety rules. These are made to keep everyone safe and to follow the law. But even then, teachers should listen to pupils' opinions.

## PAGE 3: Elements of democracy
Democracy works when these principles are followed.

• Equality: every pupil has one vote, and every vote counts the same, whether the person is a girl or a boy, and whatever their tribe, religion or ability.
• Freedom of expression: everyone may give an opinion without being laughed at or punished.
• Participation: everyone takes part, and no one is left out.
• Secret ballot: you vote privately so that nobody can force or influence you.
• Majority rule: the choice with the most votes wins.
• Respect for the minority: those who did not win are still respected and listened to.
• Accountability: leaders must explain and answer for what they do.
• Fairness: rules of the election are the same for all candidates.
• Peaceful acceptance: after the count, everyone accepts the results peacefully.

### Example
In an election for class monitor, pupils must not bribe voters with sweets or threaten them. A candidate who gives sweets for votes is cheating. That would not be a fair election.

### Discuss
Why should the ballot be secret? What could happen if pupils had to vote by raising their hands in front of a friend who wants them to vote in a certain way?

## PAGE 4: A mock election in class
A mock election is a practice election. It helps us to learn how the real one works. Here is how one class of 30 pupils did it.

### Steps
1. The teacher announced an election for class monitor and chose two pupils as election officials.
2. Three pupils were nominated: Amina, Brian and Cheptoo.
3. Each candidate told the class why they would make a good monitor. This is campaigning. They promised to be fair and keep the classroom tidy.
4. Each pupil received a ballot paper, marked one box privately and dropped the paper in a box.
5. The officials counted the votes in front of everyone and wrote tally marks.
6. The winner was announced.

### The results
The officials counted and got: Amina 12 votes, Brian 10 votes, Cheptoo 7 votes and 1 spoilt paper (marked wrongly). Total: 12 + 10 + 7 + 1 = 30, which is the number of pupils who voted.

Amina got the most votes, so she was declared the class monitor. Brian and Cheptoo congratulated her and offered to help. This is peaceful acceptance of results.

## PAGE 5: Benefits of democracy and how to practise it
### Benefits of democracy in school
• Fairness: everyone has an equal chance to speak and vote.
• Ownership: pupils obey rules they helped to make.
• Better decisions: many ideas are heard.
• Peace: problems are solved by talking and voting, not fighting.
• Leaders are answerable to the people who chose them.
• Training: pupils practise skills they will need as adult citizens.

### How to practise democracy
• Listen politely when others talk.
• Wait for your turn to speak.
• Vote honestly and only once.
• Accept the results, even if your choice lost.
• Support the leaders who are elected.
• If you disagree, say so respectfully and give reasons.
• If elected, serve everyone, not just your friends.

### Posters on democracy
A poster on democracy might say: "Every voice counts", "Vote fairly, accept peacefully" or "One pupil, one vote".

### Summary
Democracy is rule by the people. In school we practise it by electing leaders, holding class meetings, voting and using suggestion boxes. Its elements include equality, freedom of expression, secret ballot, majority rule and accepting the results. A mock election teaches us the steps.

### Common mistakes
• Voting more than once or voting for someone else.
• Giving sweets in exchange for votes. That is cheating.
• Laughing at candidates who lose. Everyone who stands should be respected.
• Thinking democracy means having your own way. It means respecting the choice of the majority.

## QUIZ
1. What does democracy mean?
   A) Rule by one person
   B) Rule by the people
   C) Rule by the strongest
   D) No rules at all
   Answer: B
   Why: Democracy means rule by the people, with everyone having a say.
2. Which of these is a democratic process in school?
   A) Electing a class monitor by voting
   B) A bully choosing the leaders
   C) Punishing pupils who speak
   D) Ignoring pupils' ideas
   Answer: A
   Why: Voting for leaders gives everyone a say.
3. Why should a ballot be secret?
   A) So that nobody can force or influence how you vote
   B) So that the votes are lost
   C) So that no one counts them
   D) So that the winner is hidden
   Answer: A
   Why: Secret voting protects your free choice.
4. In Figure 3, who won the election?
   A) Amina
   B) Brian
   C) Cheptoo
   D) There was no winner
   Answer: A
   Why: Amina had 12 votes, more than Brian's 10 and Cheptoo's 7.
5. Which action shows respect for the minority?
   A) Laughing at those who lost
   B) Listening to and helping those who did not win
   C) Refusing to work with them
   D) Sending them away
   Answer: B
   Why: Democracy protects the minority and includes them.
6. State three benefits of democracy in school. (3 marks)
   Answer: It is fair, every pupil has a voice, and pupils obey rules they helped to make. Accept peaceful solutions.
   Why: 1 mark for each correct benefit.
7. In the mock election, Amina had 12 votes and Brian had 10. How many more votes did Amina get than Brian? Show working. (2 marks)
   Answer: 2 votes.
   Why: 12 - 10 = 2 votes. 1 mark for the subtraction and 1 mark for the answer.
8. List the steps of an election from beginning to end. (3 marks)
   Answer: Announce, nominate candidates, campaign, vote, count the votes, and declare the winner.
   Why: 1 mark for the early steps, 1 mark for voting and counting, 1 mark for the correct order.
9. A candidate gives sweets to pupils so that they will vote for him. Explain why this is wrong. (2 marks)
   Answer: It is cheating because votes should be given freely on the basis of the candidate's qualities, not bought. It is not a fair election.
   Why: 1 mark for saying it is cheating, 1 mark for explaining why votes must be free.
