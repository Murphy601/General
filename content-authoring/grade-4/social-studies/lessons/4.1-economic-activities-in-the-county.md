## PAGE 1: Resources around us
A resource is anything found in nature or made by people that we can use to meet our needs and earn a living. The land under our feet is a resource. The water in the river is a resource. The people who work are also a resource.

### Natural resources
Natural resources are gifts of nature. We do not make them.
• Land and soil, used for farming, grazing and building
• Water from rivers, lakes, rain and underground
• Forests that give wood, fruits, honey and medicine
• Minerals, such as soda ash at Lake Magadi and titanium in Kwale
• Wildlife, such as elephants and lions in game parks
• Sunshine, wind and hot rocks underground, which can be used to make electricity. Kenya makes electricity from geothermal steam at Olkaria, near Naivasha, and from wind in Marsabit County.
• Fish in lakes and in the ocean

### Human resources
People's skills, knowledge and labour are resources too. Teachers, farmers, nurses, engineers and carpenters are human resources.

### Think about it
List three natural resources found in your county. Which do people use the most? Which ones might run out if we are careless?

## PAGE 2: What are economic activities?
People use resources to earn money or to get food and goods. The work they do is called an economic activity. An economic activity produces goods or services and earns an income.

### Main economic activities
• Farming: growing crops and keeping animals
• Fishing: catching fish from lakes, rivers and the ocean
• Lumbering (forestry): cutting trees for timber and planting new ones
• Mining and quarrying: taking minerals, stones and sand from the ground
• Trade: buying and selling goods in shops, markets and kiosks
• Tourism: welcoming visitors to game parks, beaches, museums and cultural sites
• Manufacturing: making goods in factories, workshops and jua kali sheds
• Services: teaching, health care, banking, driving, security

## PAGE 3: Where do activities take place?
Different places have different resources, so they have different activities.

• Tea farming in Kericho and other highland areas with cool weather and plenty of rain. Coffee is grown in counties like Nyeri and Kiambu.
• Maize and wheat farming in places like Trans Nzoia, Uasin Gishu and Narok.
• Sugarcane farming in parts of Western Kenya and Nyanza, such as Kakamega, Bungoma and Kisumu.
• Cut flowers and vegetables around Lake Naivasha, sold to countries far away.
• Fishing in Lake Victoria and the Indian Ocean, and in Lake Turkana.
• Keeping cattle, goats, sheep and camels in dry areas such as Turkana, Samburu, Marsabit and Kajiado. This is called pastoralism.
• Tourism at the Maasai Mara in Narok County and on the beaches of Mombasa, Kilifi and Kwale.
• Mining of soda ash at Lake Magadi and titanium in Kwale.

## PAGE 4: Why economic activities are important
Economic activities are the way a county and its people earn their living.

• Food: farmers and fishers provide the food that we eat.
• Income: people earn money, which they use to pay school fees, buy clothes and build homes.
• Jobs: activities give work to many people, such as farmers, drivers, guides and shopkeepers.
• Raw materials: crops such as sugarcane and cotton, and minerals, are used to make goods in factories.
• County income: businesses pay taxes and licences, which help the county to build roads and clinics.
• Money from other countries: tea, coffee, flowers and tourists bring in foreign money.
• Better living: goods and services make life easier and more comfortable.

### Children and work
Children can help with safe, light tasks at home, such as fetching water for the family with a small container or feeding chickens, as long as they can still go to school, rest and play. Children must never be forced to do hard or dangerous work. That is child labour, and it is against the law.

### Participate in your locality
• Take care of the school garden.
• Help your parents at their shop or shamba after school, if it is safe.
• Visit a local market and see how buying and selling take place.
• Ask the traders and farmers how they earn their living.

## PAGE 5: Conserving the resources in our county
Natural resources are not endless. If we waste them, our children will not have enough. Conservation means using resources wisely and protecting them.

### Ways of conserving
• Water: turn off taps, fix leaks, and do not pollute rivers.
• Soil: plant trees and grass to stop erosion, and use manure.
• Forests: plant more trees than we cut. Do not burn forests.
• Fish: follow fishing rules and do not catch very small fish.
• Wildlife: never hunt or buy animal parts. Protect parks and animals.
• Energy: switch off lights and use clean energy where possible.
• Waste: reduce, reuse and recycle.

An old jerrican can be reused as a flower pot. Plastic bottles can be recycled at a collection centre. Using less paper is a way of reducing.

## PAGE 6: Songs, poems and posters
Economic activities are part of daily life, and we can celebrate them. Your class can make posters and poems about the activities in your county.

### Making a poster
1. Choose one activity, such as farming, fishing or tourism.
2. Draw a large picture that shows the people at work.
3. Write a title and two or three important facts.
4. Write one way of conserving the resource used.

### A short poem
In the morning the farmer walks to the field,
The fisher pushes his boat to the shore,
The trader arranges her tomatoes in rows,
We are thankful for what they bring us.

### Summary
Resources are things we use to meet our needs. Economic activities such as farming, fishing, mining, trade, tourism and services use these resources to earn income. They give us food, jobs and money for the county. We must conserve resources so that they last.

### Common mistakes
• Thinking that only farming is an economic activity. Teaching, driving and selling are economic activities too.
• Thinking that resources cannot run out. Fish, forests and water can be used up if not managed well.
• Thinking that children should work all day. Children must go to school and be protected from hard work.

## QUIZ
1. Which of these is a natural resource?
   A) A road
   B) Water in a lake
   C) A school building
   D) A tractor
   Answer: B
   Why: Water in a lake is found in nature. Roads, buildings and tractors are made by people.
2. What is an economic activity?
   A) Work done to earn income or make goods and services
   B) A game played after school
   C) A holiday
   D) A festival
   Answer: A
   Why: Economic activities produce goods or services and bring in income.
3. Cattle keeping is a common activity in which type of area?
   A) A dry plain
   B) The middle of a lake
   C) Inside a city market
   D) A rainforest canopy
   Answer: A
   Why: Dry plains have grass and space for herders, but too little rain for many crops.
4. Which of these is a way of conserving forests?
   A) Cutting trees without planting
   B) Burning the forest
   C) Planting more trees than we cut
   D) Selling all the wood at once
   Answer: C
   Why: Planting trees keeps the forest alive so it continues to provide resources.
5. Which of these is a service?
   A) A maize crop
   B) A fish
   C) Teaching in a school
   D) A block of stone
   Answer: C
   Why: Teaching is work done to help others. The other choices are things.
6. Give three reasons why economic activities are important to a county. (3 marks)
   Answer: They provide food, they give people jobs and income, and they earn taxes that help build roads and clinics.
   Why: 1 mark for each correct reason.
7. Name two economic activities in your county and one resource that each uses. (4 marks)
   Answer: For example, farming uses land and soil; fishing uses a lake.
   Why: 1 mark for each activity and 1 mark for each matching resource.
8. Explain why children must not do hard or dangerous work. (2 marks)
   Answer: Hard work harms their health and stops them going to school. It is child labour and is against the law.
   Why: 1 mark for harm to health or school, 1 mark for saying it is illegal.
9. State three ways of conserving water in your school. (3 marks)
   Answer: Turn taps off after use, fix leaking pipes and do not pollute water sources.
   Why: 1 mark for each valid way.
