## PAGE 1: Why do we need directions?
Imagine a visitor arrives at your school gate and asks, "Where is the headteacher's office?" You could say, "Over there!" and point. But if the visitor cannot see your finger, "over there" does not help at all. You need words that mean the same thing to everybody.

People use direction words to say where a place is. Some words are about you, such as left, right, in front and behind. These change when you turn around. Left for you may be right for the person facing you.

The Earth has directions that never change, no matter which way you face. We call them the cardinal points. They help pilots, drivers, sailors, farmers and mapmakers. A matatu driver going from Nairobi to Mombasa is travelling in a steady direction, and a map shows that direction clearly.

### Think about it
• How do you tell a friend where the school kitchen is?
• Why is "turn left" not enough if the friend is walking towards you?
• Which direction does the sun come up from in the morning?

### Key words
• Direction: the way you must go to reach a place.
• Cardinal points: the four main directions on Earth.
• Compass: a tool with a needle that shows where north is.

Today you will learn the four cardinal points and use them to describe where places are.

## PAGE 2: The four cardinal points
The four cardinal points are north, east, south and west. We write them as N, E, S and W. North and south are opposite each other. East and west are opposite each other.

Look at the compass rose in Figure 1. A compass rose is a drawing that shows the four points. North is at the top. East is on the right. South is at the bottom. West is on the left.

### A way to remember
Start at north and go clockwise, the way the hands of a clock move. You meet north, east, south, west. Many pupils use this sentence: Never Eat Slimy Worms. The first letters are N, E, S, W.

### Try it
Draw your own compass rose in your exercise book. Put N at the top of the page first, then fill in the other three. Colour the north arrow red so that you always notice it first.

## PAGE 3: Finding direction in real life
You do not always have a compass, but nature can help you.

### Use the sun
The sun rises in the east and sets in the west. In Kenya this is true all year round because our country lies across the equator. In the morning, your shadow falls towards the west. In the late afternoon, your shadow falls towards the east.

### Use your body
Stand outside early in the morning with your right hand pointing to where the sun comes up. That is east. Now:
• In front of you is east
• Behind you is west
• Your left hand points to north
• Your right hand points to south

### A compass
A compass has a small magnetic needle that turns freely. One end always points to north. Hold the compass flat, wait until the needle stops, and turn the dial until N lines up with the needle. Then you can read east, south and west.

## PAGE 4: Giving directions on a map
Maps are drawn so that north is at the top of the page. The compass rose or a north arrow on a map tells you this. When you read a map, always find north first.

### Reading the map
• The market is north of the school.
• The church is east of the school.
• The clinic is south of the school.
• The river is west of the school.

To describe two places, compare them. The clinic is south of the market, and the river is west of the church. Always say the place first, then the direction, then the place you are measuring from.

## PAGE 5: In-between directions
Sometimes a place is not exactly north or east, but between them. We use in-between points for this. There are four of them.

• North-east (NE): between north and east
• South-east (SE): between south and east
• South-west (SW): between south and west
• North-west (NW): between north and west

### Examples from Kenya
Looking at a map of Kenya, Mombasa is south-east of Nairobi. Nakuru is north-west of Nairobi. Kisumu is to the west. Mount Kenya is to the north. Ask your teacher to help you check these on a wall map.

### Remember
When you use two letters, say north or south first. We say north-east, not east-north.

## PAGE 6: Using directions every day
Look at this set of instructions for a visitor at your school gate.

"Walk north along the path. Pass the flag pole on your left. Turn east at the water tank. The office is the second door on your right."

The visitor knows which way to walk because the words north and east never change.

### Worked example
On the map in Figure 3, a pupil starts at the market and wants to reach the clinic. 
Step 1: The school is south of the market, so walk south to the school.
Step 2: The clinic is south of the school, so keep walking south.
Answer: Walk south past the school to reach the clinic.

### Everyday uses
• Farmers know which side of the shamba gets morning sun.
• Sailors and fishermen on Lake Victoria and the Indian Ocean use compasses.
• Pilots follow compass headings.
• Phone and computer maps show a small compass so you can turn the map correctly.

### Activity
Draw a simple map of your classroom. Put a north arrow on it. Write three sentences using the words north, east, south and west to say where the door, the blackboard and your desk are.

### Common mistakes
• Putting north at the bottom of a map. Check the north arrow.
• Mixing up east and west. Remember that the sun rises in the east.
• Saying left and right when you should say a cardinal point.

## QUIZ
1. Which direction is opposite to east?
   A) North
   B) South
   C) West
   D) North-east
   Answer: C
   Why: East and west are opposite each other on the compass rose, just as north and south are.
2. On most maps, where is north?
   A) At the top
   B) At the bottom
   C) On the left
   D) On the right
   Answer: A
   Why: Maps are drawn with north at the top unless a north arrow shows something different.
3. In the morning a pupil faces the rising sun. Which direction is behind the pupil?
   A) North
   B) East
   C) South
   D) West
   Answer: D
   Why: The sun rises in the east, so the pupil faces east. Behind is the opposite direction, which is west.
4. A compass needle always points towards which direction?
   A) South
   B) East
   C) North
   D) West
   Answer: C
   Why: The magnetic needle of a compass turns until one end points to north.
5. Which place is between south and west?
   A) North-east
   B) South-west
   C) South-east
   D) North-west
   Answer: B
   Why: South-west lies half way between south and west.
6. Write the four cardinal points in clockwise order, starting at north. (2 marks)
   Answer: North, east, south, west.
   Why: 1 mark for the correct four points, 1 mark for the correct order (clockwise).
7. On Figure 3, the church is east of the school. State where the clinic is in relation to the church. (2 marks)
   Answer: The clinic is south-west of the church.
   Why: The clinic is below (south of) the school and the school is west of the church, so the clinic is to the south and west of the church. Award 1 mark for south and 1 mark for west.
8. Explain how you can find east if you have no compass, early in the morning. (3 marks)
   Answer: Face the direction where the sun is rising. That direction is east. The rising sun always appears in the east.
   Why: 1 mark for using the sun, 1 mark for the sunrise, 1 mark for naming east.
9. Draw a compass rose and label N, E, S and W correctly. (3 marks)
   Answer: A drawing with N at the top, E on the right, S at the bottom and W on the left.
   Why: 1 mark for a neat drawing with four arms, 1 mark for N at the top, 1 mark for E, S and W correctly placed.
