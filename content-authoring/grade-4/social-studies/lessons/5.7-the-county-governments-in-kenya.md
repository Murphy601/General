## PAGE 1: Two levels of government
Kenya has two levels of government: the national government and county governments. The Constitution of Kenya 2010 created this arrangement. The national government looks after matters that affect the whole country, such as defence, security and foreign affairs. Each of the 47 counties has its own county government that looks after matters closer to the people, such as local health services and county roads.

The system of sharing power between the national government and the counties is called devolution. It means that some decisions and money have been moved from the centre to the counties. The first county governments were elected in 2013.

### Why do we have county governments?
• To bring services closer to people, so you do not have to travel far to get health care or use a market.
• To let people take part in decisions about their own area.
• To share development fairly across the country.
• To help each county use its own resources and plan for its own needs.

### Key words
• Devolution: sharing power and resources between national and county government.
• County government: the government that runs a county.
• Governor: the elected head of a county government.
• County assembly: the group that makes laws for the county.

### Think about it
What is the name of your county? What is the name of your governor? Where are the county offices?

## PAGE 2: The structure of a county government
A county government has two main parts. One part makes laws and checks what the government does. The other part carries out the laws and runs services.

• The county assembly makes county laws, approves the budget and oversees the county executive.
• The county executive carries out the laws and runs the county's services, and is led by the governor.

### Who is who?
• Governor: head of the county.
• Deputy governor: helps the governor.
• County executive committee members: each looks after a department such as health, agriculture, roads or water.
• Speaker: leads the county assembly's meetings.
• MCAs: Members of the County Assembly.

## PAGE 3: The Governor and their duties
The governor is the head of the county government. Every five years, adults in the county who are registered voters choose their governor in a general election. The governor is elected together with a deputy governor.

### Duties of the governor
• Leads the county executive and is in charge of running county services.
• Makes sure that county laws are put into practice.
• Chooses the county executive committee members, with the approval of the county assembly.
• Prepares plans and the budget for the county and presents them to the county assembly.
• Represents the county at events and in meetings with other governments.
• Works to bring development, such as better health services, roads, water and markets.
• Tells the public how the county money has been used.
• Works with the national government and with neighbouring counties.

### Qualities of a good governor
A good governor is honest, fair, listens to citizens, works hard and uses county money wisely.

## PAGE 4: Members of the County Assembly (MCAs)
The county assembly is like a parliament for the county. It is made up of people called Members of the County Assembly, or MCAs. Each ward in the county elects one MCA. Some more members are nominated so that women, youth, people with disabilities and other groups are represented. The Speaker leads the assembly. Its meetings take place in the assembly building, usually at the county headquarters.

### Roles of an MCA
• Represents the people of the ward: takes their needs and ideas to the county assembly.
• Makes county laws together with other members.
• Approves the county budget, which decides how money is spent.
• Checks the work of the governor and the county executive to make sure they do it well and honestly.
• Visits the ward to listen to people and reports back.
• Helps to identify projects for the ward, such as a school, road or dispensary.

### Difference between the governor and an MCA
The governor leads the executive and runs the county. An MCA is a member of the assembly that makes laws and checks the governor. The governor is elected by the whole county, but an MCA is elected by a ward.

## PAGE 5: Services provided by county governments
County governments are in charge of many things that touch our daily lives.

• Health: county hospitals, health centres and dispensaries, and ambulance services.
• Agriculture: helping farmers, animal health services and cattle dips.
• County roads and street lighting.
• Early childhood education: pre-primary schools and village polytechnics.
• Markets and trade: building and managing local markets and giving business licences.
• Water and sanitation: helping to supply water and to remove waste.
• Parks, beaches and cultural activities.

### National government services
The national government is in charge of, for example, the police and defence, foreign affairs, national roads, and policy for primary and secondary education.

### Where does the money come from?
County governments receive money shared from the national government. They also collect some fees and taxes locally, such as market fees and business permits. All the money belongs to the public, and it must be used honestly.

## PAGE 6: Ward and village administrators
The county government has a way of reaching people in every corner. The county is divided into sub-counties, wards and villages. The county government appoints officers to look after each level.

• Sub-county administrator: coordinates county services in the sub-county.
• Ward administrator: coordinates county services in the ward and works with the MCA and the community.
• Village administrator: the county officer closest to the people, who works with village elders and families.

### Duties of ward and village administrators
• Bring the services of the county government to people at their level.
• Work with the community to find their needs and pass them on to county leaders.
• Share information about county plans, projects and meetings.
• Help organise public meetings where citizens give views on plans and the budget. This is called public participation.
• Help to look after county property and projects in their area.
• Help to solve local problems, calling on higher officers when needed.

### Citizens have a role
Citizens should attend public meetings, vote in elections, pay fees and taxes, look after public property and report wrong doing.

## PAGE 7: Appreciating the county government
The county government is there to serve you. When the county government works well, health centres have medicine, roads are passable, markets are clean and pupils can learn in good pre-primary classes.

### Ways to appreciate and support the county government
• Obey county rules, such as those on waste and licences.
• Take care of county roads, water points and markets.
• Take part in public meetings and give ideas.
• Keep your neighbourhood clean.
• Respect the governor, MCAs and other county officers.
• Report any misuse of public money to the right authority.

### Summary
Kenya has a national government and 47 county governments. A county government has an executive led by the governor, and an assembly of MCAs led by the Speaker. The governor runs the county and the assembly makes county laws, approves the budget and checks the executive. The county provides services such as health centres, county roads, markets and pre-primary education. Ward and village administrators bring services closer to the people.

### Common mistakes
• Confusing the governor with the president. The president leads the national government, and the governor leads a county.
• Thinking the MCA runs the county. The MCA makes laws and checks the governor.
• Thinking that county money belongs to leaders. It belongs to the public.

   A) 27
   B) 37
   C) 47
   D) 57
   Answer: C
   Why: There are 47 counties, each with its own county government.
2. Who is the head of a county government?
   A) The Speaker
   B) The Governor
   C) The Chief
   D) The MCA
   Answer: B
   Why: The governor leads the county executive and the county government.
3. What does the county assembly do?
   A) It makes county laws and approves the budget
   B) It cooks for pupils
   C) It drives buses
   D) It plays football
   Answer: A
   Why: The county assembly makes county laws, approves the budget and checks the executive.
4. Who elects a Member of the County Assembly (MCA) to represent a ward?
   A) The president
   B) The voters of that ward
   C) The teachers
   D) The chief
   Answer: B
   Why: Each ward has an MCA chosen by the voters in that ward.
5. Which of these is a service of the county government?
   A) Running a county health centre
   B) Defending the country
   C) Issuing passports
   D) Making national exams
   Answer: A
   Why: County health services are a county job. The other choices belong to the national government.
