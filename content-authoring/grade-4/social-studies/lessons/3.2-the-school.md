## PAGE 1: The story of our school
Your school did not appear overnight. Someone had to decide to start it. Land had to be found, classrooms built, teachers found and the first pupils enrolled. This story is called the history of the school. Every school has one, and you are now part of it.

When you tell the history of your school, try to answer these questions:
• In which year was the school started?
• Who started it? It might have been the community, a church, a mosque, the government or a private owner.
• Who was the first headteacher?
• How many pupils and teachers were there at the beginning?
• How has the school changed? Have new classrooms, a library or a playground been added?
• What important events have happened, such as a first prize, a visit or a big celebration?

### Where to find the history
• Ask the headteacher or the deputy.
• Ask teachers who have taught for many years.
• Ask old boys and girls, parents and local elders.
• Look in the school log book, admission register and old photos.
• Read plaques or writing at the school gate or on the buildings.

### Try it
Choose two questions from the list and find the answers this week. Write them neatly and share them with your class.

## PAGE 2: Telling the history in order

### Narrating the history
Use time words to link events: first, then, after that, later, today.

Example: "Mwangaza Primary School was started in 1970. Later, in 1985, a library was built. After that, in 2000, the school got a hall. Today, it has computers."

The sample school is imaginary, so do not copy it. Use the real facts from your own school.

### Activity
Draw a timeline of your school on a long strip of paper. Mark at least four events. Present it in class and explain each event.

## PAGE 3: School values
Values are the good beliefs that guide how we behave. Every school has values, and they are usually written on a poster or on the school wall. Values help the whole school community to live and work together in a good way.

Common school values include:
• Respect: treating teachers, classmates and property with honour.
• Responsibility: doing your duties without being reminded.
• Honesty: telling the truth and not stealing or cheating.
• Unity: working together as one family.
• Integrity: doing the right thing even when nobody is watching.
• Love: caring for others.
• Hard work: trying your best.
• Cleanliness: keeping yourself and the school clean.

### Kenya's national values
The Constitution of Kenya lists national values such as patriotism, unity, sharing, peace, respect for human dignity, fairness, honesty, and good leadership. School values are like small versions of these.

### Think about it
Which values are written on your school poster? Which is your favourite? Give an example of how a pupil showed that value this week.

### Activity
Make a poster on the school values. Draw a picture for each value. Use bright colours and big letters that people can read from far away.

## PAGE 4: The school motto
A motto is a short sentence that tells what a school stands for or hopes to achieve. It reminds pupils and teachers of the school's purpose. It is often written on the school badge or gate, and pupils recite it in assembly.

A good motto is:
• Short and easy to remember.
• Positive, telling us what to do or become.
• True to the school's values.

Examples of ideas in mottos are working hard, striving to succeed, unity, honesty and serving others. These are only examples. Every school has its own motto. Find yours and learn it by heart.

### How to recite the motto
1. Stand straight and look ahead.
2. Say each word clearly and together with others.
3. Understand what the words mean, so you say them with pride.

### Make a motto poster
1. Write the motto in large letters.
2. Draw a picture that matches its meaning.
3. Add the school name and badge if there is one.
4. Display it in the class or corridor.

### Explain it
Talk with a friend about your motto. What does it ask you to do? How can you live by it in class, on the playground and at home?

## PAGE 5: School rules and discipline
Rules are instructions that tell us what we should and should not do. A school with no rules would be unsafe and noisy, and nobody could learn well. Rules protect everyone.

### Examples of rules
• Come to school on time.
• Wear the school uniform neatly.
• Respect teachers and classmates.
• Keep the compound clean and put litter in the bin.
• Do not fight or bully.
• Take care of books, desks and other property.
• Ask permission before leaving the class.

### Discipline
Discipline means controlling yourself and following rules, even when it is hard. A disciplined pupil listens, works hard, and behaves well. Discipline helps you succeed in school and later in life.

If a pupil breaks a rule, the teacher will guide and correct them in a fair way, such as talking with them, counselling them, or asking them to help repair what they spoiled. In Kenya, teachers are not allowed to beat pupils.

### Why we should obey
• Rules keep us safe.
• Rules are fair to everyone.
• Rules help us to learn.
• Obeying rules today makes us good citizens tomorrow.

## PAGE 6: Presenting and peer assessing our work
At the end of this topic your class can hold a gallery walk. Every pupil displays their work: the school history timeline, values poster, motto poster or rules chart. Others walk around, look and comment.

### How to give peer feedback
• Say one thing you like: for example, "Your timeline is neat."
• Say one thing to improve: for example, "Add the year to the last event."
• Be polite and kind.
• Thank the person for their work.

### Summary
The school has a history that we can find from people and records. We can tell it in order using a timeline. School values guide our behaviour. The motto tells us what the school stands for. Rules and discipline keep the school safe and help us learn.

### Common mistakes
• Making up facts about your school. Use only information you have checked.
• Thinking that rules are only to punish. Rules are to protect and help everyone.
• Reciting the motto without knowing what it means.

### Class promise
Together, write a class promise that includes three rules that you all agree to follow. Everyone signs it and it is put on the wall.

## QUIZ
1. What is the history of a school?
   A) The story of how the school began and changed
   B) The list of teachers' salaries
   C) The weather at school
   D) The school menu
   Answer: A
   Why: School history tells when and how the school started and how it has grown.
2. Which of these is a good source of information about the history of your school?
   A) A guess
   B) The headteacher or the school records
   C) A friend's dream
   D) A rumour
   Answer: B
   Why: Reliable facts come from people who know and from records.
3. What is a motto?
   A) A school bell
   B) A short sentence that shows what a school stands for
   C) A school building
   D) A kind of uniform
   Answer: B
   Why: A motto is a short saying that reminds us of a school's aims.
4. Which is a school value?
   A) Honesty
   B) Bullying
   C) Cheating
   D) Laziness
   Answer: A
   Why: Honesty is a positive value. The others are wrong behaviours.
5. Why do schools have rules?
   A) To make pupils unhappy
   B) To keep everyone safe and help learning
   C) To waste time
   D) To stop pupils from playing
   Answer: B
   Why: Rules protect pupils and help the school run well.
6. List the events in the correct order using the timeline in Figure 1. (3 marks)
   Answer: School opens (1970), library built (1985), hall built (2000), computers arrive (2020).
   Why: 1 mark for placing the first event, 1 mark for the middle events in order, 1 mark for the last event.
7. Explain what discipline means and why it helps a pupil. (3 marks)
   Answer: Discipline means controlling yourself and following rules. It helps a pupil to learn well, behave well and be successful.
   Why: 1 mark for the meaning, 2 marks for two valid benefits.
8. Name three values from your school or from the list in this lesson and say how a pupil can show one of them. (3 marks)
   Answer: Respect, honesty and responsibility. For example, a pupil shows honesty by returning a lost pencil to its owner.
   Why: 1 mark for naming three values, 1 mark for an example of a pupil showing one.
9. Write a short paragraph to tell the history of an imaginary school that opened in 1990 and built a library in 2005. Use time words. (3 marks)
   Answer: The school was opened in 1990. Later, in 2005, a library was built. Today, the pupils enjoy reading books there.
   Why: 1 mark for the correct order, 1 mark for using time words, 1 mark for correct dates.
