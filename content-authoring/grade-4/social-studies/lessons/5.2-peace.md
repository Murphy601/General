## PAGE 1: What is peace?
Picture the school during break. Some children are playing football, some are sharing a snack, and others are reading under a tree. Nobody is shouting. Nobody is afraid. This is peace.

Peace is a state of calm and harmony where people live together safely, respect each other and settle their disagreements without fighting. In Kiswahili, peace is called amani.

Peace is not just the absence of war. A school can have no war and still lack peace if children are bullied, if friends keep shouting at one another or if some pupils are left out. Peace means feeling safe, welcome and respected.

Disagreements will always happen because people think differently and want different things. A disagreement is called a conflict. Conflict is normal. What matters is how we deal with it. We can solve it in a peaceful way through talking and listening, or in a harmful way through fighting or insults.

### Key words
• Peace: calm and harmony among people.
• Conflict: a disagreement or quarrel.
• Bullying: repeatedly hurting or frightening someone weaker.
• Forgiveness: choosing to let go of anger against someone who wronged you.

### Think about it
1. How do you feel when there is fighting in the playground?
2. How do you feel when everyone is kind?
3. Which place in your school feels the most peaceful?

### Factors that promote peace
• Respect: treating all people with courtesy, whatever their looks, tribe, religion or ability.
• Sharing: sharing books, play equipment and food.
• Honesty: telling the truth stops rumours and suspicion.
• Forgiveness: saying sorry and forgiving others.
• Listening: giving others time to speak.
• Fairness: treating everyone equally, without favouring friends or people from your own community.
• Tolerance: accepting that others are different.
• Obeying school rules: which are made to keep everyone safe.
• Teamwork: playing and working together.
• Kind leaders: prefects and teachers who guide with patience.

Which of these is easiest for you? Which is the hardest? Why?

## PAGE 3: What spoils peace
Peace is easily broken. The following can spoil it:

• Bullying and name-calling
• Fighting and hitting
• Stealing or damaging other people's things
• Spreading rumours and telling lies
• Treating some pupils unfairly because of their tribe, religion, gender or ability. This is called discrimination.
• Jealousy and bad losing in games or exams
• Cheating
• Refusing to listen to others

When peace is broken, people are hurt, lessons are disturbed, friendships end, and the school gets a bad name. Some children become afraid to go to school.

### What to do if you are bullied or see bullying
1. Stay calm and do not fight back.
2. Walk away to a safe place.
3. Tell a teacher or another adult you trust.
4. Stand with the child being bullied and say, "Stop it."
5. Never join in.

### Honesty check
Have you ever said something unkind that you regret? What could you have done instead? Write your answer in your exercise book. You do not have to show it to anyone.

## PAGE 4: Managing feelings and solving conflicts
Anger is a normal feeling. It is what we do with anger that matters. When we are very angry, we can say or do things we later regret. Learn to notice how strong your anger is and cool down.

### Steps for solving a conflict
1. Stop. Do not hit or shout.
2. Calm down. Breathe slowly.
3. Talk. Each person says what happened and how they feel, using "I" sentences such as "I felt hurt when..."
4. Listen. Let the other person finish without interrupting.
5. Find a solution together. Ask, "What can we do so that both of us are happy?"
6. Say sorry if you were wrong, forgive, and shake hands.

If you cannot solve it, ask a peer mediator, a prefect or a teacher to help.

### Example
Two pupils both want the same ball. Instead of pulling it, they decide to share it. One plays for ten minutes and then gives it to the other. Both are happy and the game continues.

## PAGE 5: Peace-building activities in school
A school can plan activities that build peace. It is important to plan for them so that they run well and everybody knows what to do.

### Examples
• Peace garden or nature trail: a garden or path with flowers and trees where pupils can sit and be calm.
• Peace corner: a quiet place in the classroom where pupils go to calm down, read or talk through a problem.
• Peace clubs: a group that promotes kindness and helps to solve conflicts.
• Peace competitions: essay, poem, song and drawing competitions on the theme of peace.
• Peace days and assemblies: for example the International Day of Peace on 21 September.
• Class rules created by pupils, so that everyone feels they own them.

### How to plan an activity
1. Choose the activity, for example a peace poster competition.
2. Decide on the date, time and place.
3. Share the duties: who will invite, who will make prizes, who will judge.
4. Gather materials.
5. Carry out the activity and enjoy it.
6. Afterwards, discuss what went well and what to improve.

### Peace messages
Write a peace message that is short and strong. For example: "Talk, do not fight." "Peace begins with me." "Be kind, be fair, be friends."

## PAGE 6: Peace at home and in the community
Peace does not stay at school. We carry it home and into the community. Ask your parents or guardians: How do you keep peace in our family and neighbourhood? What can I do to help? Share their answers in class. Many families keep peace by talking calmly, sharing chores, forgiving, and respecting elders.

### Ways to live in peace with others
• Greet everyone politely.
• Share what you have.
• Use kind words.
• Do not tease or laugh at others.
• Accept other people's ideas.
• Solve problems by talking.
• Forgive quickly.

### Summary
Peace is calm and harmony where people feel safe and respected. It grows through respect, sharing, honesty, forgiveness, listening and fairness. Bullying, fighting, rumours and discrimination spoil peace. We can manage anger, solve conflicts in six steps, and join peace-building activities in school.

### Common mistakes
• Thinking that peace means never disagreeing. Peace is solving disagreements well.
• Believing that "getting back" at someone solves a conflict. It only makes it worse.
• Staying silent when someone is being bullied. Tell a trusted adult.

## QUIZ
1. What is peace?
   A) Calm and harmony among people
   B) A loud game
   C) A school bell
   D) A kind of food
   Answer: A
   Why: Peace means people live together safely and respectfully.
2. Which of these promotes peace in school?
   A) Bullying
   B) Sharing
   C) Rumours
   D) Cheating
   Answer: B
   Why: Sharing builds friendship and trust. The other choices spoil peace.
3. What is the first step in solving a conflict?
   A) Shout
   B) Stop and calm down
   C) Hit back
   D) Tell everyone
   Answer: B
   Why: We first stop and cool down so that we can talk sensibly.
4. What is a peace corner?
   A) A quiet place to calm down or talk through a problem
   B) A place to play football
   C) A place to store books only
   D) A place to hide
   Answer: A
   Why: A peace corner gives pupils a calm space to settle down and solve problems.
5. What should you do if you see a pupil being bullied?
   A) Join in
   B) Laugh
   C) Tell a trusted adult and support the pupil
   D) Ignore it
   Answer: C
   Why: Reporting bullying and standing with the victim helps to stop it.

   Answer: Bullying, fighting and spreading rumours. Accept stealing or discrimination.
   Why: 1 mark for each correct point.
7. Explain why forgiveness is important for peace. (2 marks)
   Answer: When we forgive, we let go of anger, so quarrels end and friendships can grow again.
   Why: 1 mark for letting go of anger, 1 mark for the result.
8. List the steps you would follow to solve a quarrel with a classmate over a ball. (3 marks)
   Answer: Stop, calm down, talk and listen to each other, agree to share, and say sorry if needed.
   Why: 1 mark for calming down, 1 mark for talking and listening, 1 mark for finding a fair solution.
9. Write a short peace message for a poster. (2 marks)
   Answer: For example: "Peace begins with me. Be kind, be fair, be friends."
   Why: 1 mark for a positive message, 1 mark for it being short and clear.
