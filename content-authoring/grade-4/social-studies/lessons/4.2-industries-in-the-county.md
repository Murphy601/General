## PAGE 1: What is an industry?
Sugar for your tea, the paper in your exercise book, the bar of soap in the bathroom and the desk you sit at all began as something else. Sugar began as sugarcane. Paper began as wood or plant fibre. A desk began as a tree. People changed these raw materials into things we use. The places where this is done are called industries.

An industry is a place or business where raw materials are made into goods, or where a special service is provided to many people. A raw material is a material from nature that has not yet been made into a finished product. Farm produce, fish, minerals, wood and water can all be raw materials. Finished goods are called products.

There are big industries with large factories and machines, and small ones such as a carpenter's workshop or a tailor's shop.

### Words to know
• Industry: making raw materials into goods, or providing services, at large scale.
• Raw material: a material used to make something new.
• Product: the finished good that is made.
• Factory: a large building with machines where goods are made.

### Find out
Name one factory or workshop near your home. What does it make? What raw material does it use?

## PAGE 2: Types of industries
### Processing farm produce
These are called agro-based industries because they use produce from farms.
• Tea factories turn green tea leaves into packed tea. There are many in Kericho and around Mount Kenya.
• Sugar factories turn sugarcane into sugar. There are many in western Kenya.
• Dairies make fresh milk into packed milk and other products.
• Coffee is cleaned and dried at coffee mills.
• Maize is ground into flour at posho mills and in big flour mills.

### Fish processing
Fish from Lake Victoria is cleaned, dried, or frozen in factories in towns such as Kisumu.

### Using minerals and stone
• Cement factories use limestone. There are cement factories in Mombasa and Athi River.
• At Lake Magadi in Kajiado County, soda ash is taken from the lake and processed.

### Small workshops
Jua kali means "hot sun" in Kiswahili. Jua kali workers make things such as furniture, metal doors, buckets, shoes and charcoal stoves in open-air workshops or sheds.

### Service industries
Hotels, hospitals, banks and transport companies are also industries because they provide services to many people.

## PAGE 3: Benefits of industries
Industries help a county and its people in many ways.

• Jobs: factories employ many workers such as machine operators, drivers, cleaners, clerks and guards.
• Income: workers earn salaries. The business owners earn profits.
• Market for farmers: sugar, tea and dairy factories buy what farmers produce. Farmers do not have to search for buyers.
• Goods available: products such as cement, soap, flour and clothes are available close to where they are needed and are often cheaper.
• Taxes: industries pay taxes that help to build roads, schools and clinics.
• Money from other countries: some products, such as tea, are sold abroad.
• Development: industries lead to new roads, water, electricity, shops and homes.
• Skills: people learn new skills and can start their own businesses.

### Think about it
How would a farmer in a sugarcane area feel if the sugar factory closed? What would happen to the families who work at the factory?

## PAGE 4: Where are industries located?
Industry owners choose a place carefully. They think about these factors.

• Raw materials: it costs less to build a sugar factory near the sugarcane farms than to carry cane a long way.
• Market: many factories are near big towns such as Nairobi, Mombasa and Kisumu where many customers live.
• Transport: roads, the railway and the port help to bring in materials and take out products. Mombasa is a busy port.
• Water and power: many factories need plenty of water and electricity.
• Workers: there must be people living nearby to work in the factory.
• Land: factories need wide space, and the land should not be too costly.

Nairobi's Industrial Area, Thika, Athi River and Mombasa are known industrial areas. In the west, sugar factories are close to the cane fields. On the shores of Lake Victoria there are fish processing factories.

### Reading the chart
• The sugar factory employs the most workers: 7 hundred, which is 700.
• Jua kali employs the fewest: 2 hundred, which is 200.
• The tea and cement factories together employ 4 + 3 = 7 hundred, the same number as the sugar factory.

### Problems caused by industries
• Smoke and waste can pollute air, rivers and soil.
• Machines can be noisy and dangerous.
• If people cut too many trees or take too much from the land, resources become fewer.

### Solving the problems
Factories should treat waste before releasing it, plant trees, use protective clothing, and follow safety rules. Communities and the county government can check that rules are followed.

## PAGE 6: Visiting an industry, and showing what we learned
A visit to a factory or workshop is a good way to learn. Because factories have machines and heat, we must stay safe.

### Before the visit
• Ask the teacher and the factory for permission.
• Write down three questions, for example: What raw materials do you use? How many people work here? How do you look after the environment?

### During the visit
• Stay with your group and follow the guide.
• Do not touch machines or run around.
• Listen, take notes and, if permitted, take pictures.
• Notice the safety clothes workers wear, such as helmets, gloves, boots and masks.

### After the visit
1. Write a short report: what the industry makes, its raw materials, and its benefits.
2. Draw a poster with a picture of the industry and its main facts.
3. Compose a poem about how industries help the county.

### Summary
Industries change raw materials into products and provide services. They provide jobs, income, goods, taxes and development. Industries are placed near raw materials, markets, transport, water, power and workers. We must reduce pollution and stay safe.

### Common mistakes
• Thinking that only large factories are industries. Small workshops such as jua kali also count.
• Building a factory anywhere. Its location matters for cost and for people.
• Forgetting about pollution. Industries must protect the environment.

## QUIZ
1. What is a raw material?
   A) A finished product
   B) A material from nature used to make something new
   C) A machine
   D) A factory worker
   Answer: B
   Why: Raw materials such as sugarcane or wood are changed into finished products.
2. A sugar factory takes sugarcane and makes what?
   A) Sugar
   B) Tea
   C) Cement
   D) Soap
   Answer: A
   Why: Sugarcane is the raw material and sugar is the product.
3. Why is a sugar factory often built near cane farms?
   A) Because cane is heavy and costly to carry far
   B) Because there are no roads
   C) Because farmers dislike factories
   D) Because sugar cannot be sold
   Answer: A
   Why: Building close to the raw material saves transport costs.
4. Which of these is a jua kali activity?
   A) Making metal doors in an open-air workshop
   B) Running a large airport
   C) Flying an aeroplane
   D) Mining gold
   Answer: A
   Why: Jua kali workers make goods such as doors and furniture in small workshops.
5. Look at Figure 3. How many workers does the sugar factory have?
   A) 400
   B) 500
   C) 700
   D) 200
   Answer: C
   Why: The sugar bar reaches 7 hundred, which is 700 workers.
6. State three benefits of industries in a county. (3 marks)
   Answer: They give jobs, they buy farmers' produce, and they pay taxes. Accept goods available locally and development.
   Why: 1 mark for each correct benefit.
7. Give two things a company thinks about before building a factory. (2 marks)
   Answer: Nearness to raw materials and to a market. Accept transport, water, power, workers or land.
   Why: 1 mark for each correct factor.
