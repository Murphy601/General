## PAGE 1: Old places with a story
Think of an old building near your home or a place that your grandparents talk about. Maybe it is a very old church, an old market hall, a stone wall or a monument in the town centre. It has been standing for many years. People have seen many things happen there.

A built environment is everything that people have built: houses, roads, schools, bridges and towers. A historic built environment is a place that people built a long time ago and that still tells us about the past. It is old, and it is important to our history and culture.

The main kinds are:
• Museums
• Monuments
• Cultural centres

### Find out
Name one historic place in your county or nearby. Ask an elder or a teacher what it is and why people remember it.

## PAGE 2: Museums
A museum is a building where important objects are kept safely and shown to the public. The objects are called artefacts. They can be old tools, pots, clothes, weapons, photographs, fossils and documents. A museum also has people who study these things and explain them.

In Kenya, many museums are cared for by the National Museums of Kenya. The Nairobi National Museum shows the natural history, cultures and history of Kenya. There are also museums in other towns, for example the Kisumu Museum and the Kitale Museum. Fort Jesus in Mombasa is a museum too.

### What can you learn in a museum?
• How people lived long ago
• Which tools they used to farm, cook and travel
• Which animals and plants live in Kenya
• How our ancestors dressed and made things

### Visiting a museum
1. Walk quietly and listen to the guide.
2. Do not touch the objects unless allowed.
3. Take notes and draw items that interest you.
4. Take pictures only when permission is given.

### Activity
If you cannot visit a museum, make a class corner museum. Each pupil brings one old but safe item from home, such as a clay pot or a wooden spoon. Write a label for it. Say who used it, what it was for and how old it is.

## PAGE 3: Monuments and old buildings
A monument is a structure built to remind people of a person or an event. It may be a statue, a tall pillar, an old building or a grave. Some monuments are very old. Others are newer.

Here are some examples that are true and well known.
• Uhuru Gardens in Nairobi remembers Kenya's independence in 1963. The Kenyan flag was raised there for the first time when Kenya became independent.
• Fort Jesus in Mombasa is a stone fort built by the Portuguese more than 400 years ago. It was made to guard the harbour. It is recognised by UNESCO as a World Heritage Site.
• Lamu Old Town is one of the oldest towns on the coast. It has narrow streets and stone houses built long ago.
• Gede Ruins, near Watamu in Kilifi County, are the remains of a very old coastal town.
• Thimlich Ohinga in Migori County is a large stone-walled enclosure built by people who lived in that area long ago.

## PAGE 4: Cultural centres
A cultural centre is a place where the culture of one or many communities is kept alive. Visitors can see traditional homes, hear music, watch dances, taste foods and buy crafts.

The Bomas of Kenya in Nairobi is a well known cultural centre. It has traditional homesteads that show how different Kenyan communities built their houses. There are also performances of traditional dances and songs.

Many counties also have cultural centres or community museums run by local people. They may hold cultural festivals where people wear traditional dress and perform songs.

### What happens at a cultural centre?
• People perform traditional dances and songs.
• Elders tell stories and explain customs.
• Craft makers show how to weave baskets or carve wood.
• Visitors learn about foods and traditional medicine.

### How is a cultural centre different from a museum?
A museum mostly keeps objects for people to look at and study. A cultural centre mostly lets people see, hear and take part in living culture. Some places do both.

### Think about it
Which cultural centre or cultural event is the closest to your home? Which dance or craft would you like to learn there?

## PAGE 5: Why historic places are important
Historic built environments are more than old buildings. They are the memory of a community.

• They teach us history. We learn how our ancestors lived, worked and fought for freedom.
• They give us identity. They help us to know who we are and where we come from.
• They bring visitors. Tourists pay to visit places such as Fort Jesus and Lamu, which earns money and gives people jobs as guides, cooks, drivers and craft sellers.
• They help researchers. Scientists and students study the past by visiting old sites.
• They bring people together. Communities meet at cultural events and share their heritage.
• They give us pride. We feel proud of our country when we see how much history it has.

### A world honour
UNESCO is an organisation of the United Nations that helps to protect important places. Lamu Old Town, Fort Jesus and Thimlich Ohinga have been named World Heritage Sites. This means that the whole world agrees they are precious.

### Think about it
What would happen if we let an old building fall down and nobody remembered it?

We must protect these places so that our children and grandchildren can also enjoy them. Protecting them is called conservation.

### Things we can do
• Sweep the compound and collect litter.
• Never write, scratch or draw on old walls.
• Do not climb on or break old structures.
• Report anyone who is damaging a historic place.
• Pay any entrance fee, because it helps to repair the place.
• Learn and tell the story of the place to others.
• Take part in service learning: visit the site with your teacher and clean up together.

### Summary
Historic built environments are places built long ago that help us remember history. They include museums, monuments and cultural centres. They teach us, bring income, and give us pride. We must keep them clean and safe.

### Common mistakes
• Thinking that only very tall or big buildings can be historic. Small places can also be important.
• Touching or climbing on old objects and walls. This damages them.

## QUIZ
1. What is a museum?
   A) A place where old objects are kept and shown
   B) A place where crops are sold
   C) A place where cars are repaired
   D) A place where trees are planted
   Answer: A
   Why: A museum keeps artefacts safely and shows them to visitors so that people can learn.
2. A monument is built to do what?
   A) Sell goods
   B) Remind people of a person or event
   C) Keep animals
   D) Store water
   Answer: B
   Why: Monuments help people to remember important people or events.
3. Which of these is a cultural centre in Nairobi?
   A) Fort Jesus
   B) Gede Ruins
   C) Bomas of Kenya
   D) Thimlich Ohinga
   Answer: C
   Why: The Bomas of Kenya shows traditional homes and dances. Fort Jesus is in Mombasa, Gede is in Kilifi County and Thimlich Ohinga is in Migori County.
4. Fort Jesus in Mombasa was built for what reason?
   A) To keep animals
   B) To guard the harbour
   C) To grow crops
   D) To hold a market
   Answer: B
   Why: The Portuguese built the stone fort to guard the harbour.
5. Which action helps to conserve a historic site?
   A) Writing your name on a wall
   B) Climbing the old walls
   C) Picking up litter around it
   D) Breaking off a small piece
   Answer: C
   Why: Removing litter keeps the site clean and does not damage it.
6. Give three reasons why historic built environments are important. (3 marks)
   Answer: They teach us history, they bring visitors and income, and they give us pride and identity.
   Why: 1 mark for each correct reason.
7. Explain the difference between a museum and a cultural centre. (2 marks)
   Answer: A museum mainly keeps objects to look at, while a cultural centre lets people watch and take part in living culture such as dance and crafts.
   Why: 1 mark for describing each correctly.
8. Name two rules that visitors should follow at a historic place. (2 marks)
   Answer: Do not litter and do not write on or climb the old walls. Accept do not touch the objects.
   Why: 1 mark for each sensible rule.
9. Describe one service learning activity that your class could do at a historic site. (3 marks)
   Answer: Visit the site with the teacher, sweep and pick up litter, and put the litter in a bin. Then tell others what we learned.
   Why: 1 mark for the visit, 1 mark for a cleaning action, 1 mark for sharing or explaining the purpose.
