## PAGE 1: What is a Children's Government?
A school has hundreds of pupils and only a few teachers. The teachers cannot know every problem in every class, every toilet and every corner of the playground. Pupils can help. Many schools in Kenya have a Children's Government: a group of pupils, elected by their classmates, who help to run the school and speak on behalf of all pupils.

It is called a government because it is organised like a small government, with a leader, helpers, a group of representatives and different departments. But it is not the same as the government of Kenya. In school, the headteacher and teachers are still in charge, and a teacher usually guides the Children's Government as an adviser, often called a patron.

The names used are different from school to school. Some schools use president, others use head boy and head girl, and others use school council. Ask your teacher what your school calls its leaders. In this lesson we describe a typical structure. Your school may have a slightly different one.

### Why do we have a Children's Government?
• It gives pupils a voice.
• It shares responsibility, so pupils learn leadership.
• It helps teachers to keep order and cleanliness.
• It teaches democracy, since the leaders are elected.
• It makes the school a happier place.

### Think about it
Who are the pupil leaders in your school? Who chose them? What do they do?

## PAGE 2: Composition: who are the members?
Composition means the parts that make up a group. A typical Children's Government has these members.

• President (or head pupil): the main leader of the pupils. Represents pupils at school events and meetings.
• Deputy president: helps the president and takes over when the president is absent.
• Speaker of the assembly: leads meetings and makes sure every member is heard.
• Cabinet members (or ministers or prefects): each takes care of one area, such as education, health and sanitation, environment, sports and games, library, discipline and order, or food and dining.
• Class representatives: one or two pupils from each class who bring their classmates' ideas and complaints to the meetings.
• Secretary: writes down what is discussed and decided.
• Patron (teacher adviser): guides the group.

### How are they chosen?
Pupils vote for their leaders in elections like the mock election you have learned about. Candidates are usually from higher classes and must have good behaviour, honesty and good attendance. Teachers may check and guide the process to make sure it is fair.

## PAGE 3: Functions: what does it do?
A function is a job that someone has to do. The Children's Government does these jobs.

• Represent pupils: takes pupils' ideas, needs and complaints to the teachers.
• Help to keep order: leaders help with lining up, assemblies, silent study and behaviour in the dining hall.
• Promote cleanliness: leaders check classrooms, toilets and the compound and organise cleaning.
• Look after pupils' welfare: they help sick pupils, welcome new pupils and notice those who need help.
• Organise activities: sports days, debates, clubs and talent shows.
• Manage resources: the library, the games equipment or the school garden, with teachers' guidance.
• Model good behaviour: leaders show good example to others.
• Solve small disagreements: they may help pupils to settle conflicts peacefully. Serious problems are passed to teachers.
• Receive visitors: they greet guests and show them around.

### What the Children's Government must not do
They must not punish or beat other pupils, take money or favour their friends. They must not act as if they are teachers. Serious matters must be reported to teachers.

### Match the work to the leader
• A pupil finds that the toilets have no water. The health and sanitation minister reports it to the teacher on duty.
• Class 4 wants a football match against Class 5. The sports minister organises it.
• Pupils complain that the library is too noisy. The library prefect looks into it.

## PAGE 4: Participating in the Children's Government
Every pupil belongs to the Children's Government, even if not every pupil is an office bearer. This means everybody has a part to play.

### Ways to participate
• Vote in the election for leaders.
• Stand as a candidate if you have the qualities needed.
• Give ideas to your class representative.
• Attend class meetings and speak politely.
• Carry out the duties given to you, such as cleaning.
• Help leaders to do their work.
• Follow the decisions made fairly.

### Simulation
Your class can hold a mock session. Choose a Speaker, a secretary, three ministers and class representatives. Practise the agenda and discuss a real problem in your school, such as litter. Vote on a plan and write the minutes.

### Compose
Write a composition about how you will participate in your school's Children's Government. Use an opening paragraph, a middle paragraph with three ways, and a closing paragraph.

## PAGE 5: Respecting and being a good leader
Leaders can only do their work well if they are respected.

### How to respect the Children's Government
• Obey the instructions of leaders when they are fair and follow school rules.
• Use polite language, even when you disagree.
• Do not insult or laugh at leaders.
• Take complaints through the proper channels, such as the class representative.
• Support leaders' plans, such as clean-up days.
• Report a leader who is misusing power to a teacher.

### Qualities of a good pupil leader
• Honest and fair
• Responsible and reliable
• Humble and polite
• Good listener
• Hard working and punctual
• A good example to others
• Brave enough to report wrongdoing

### For leaders
Leaders have duties, not special privileges. Serve everyone equally. Do not shout at or bully those you lead. Take advice from your patron. Remember that leadership is a service.

### Summary
The Children's Government is a group of elected pupils who help to run the school with guidance from teachers. It typically has a president, a deputy, a speaker, cabinet members and class representatives. Its functions include representing pupils, keeping order, promoting cleanliness and organising activities. Every pupil participates and respects the leaders.

### Common mistakes
• Thinking that leaders are more important than other pupils. They serve everyone.
• Thinking that only leaders should keep the school clean. All pupils share the duty.
• Leaders punishing pupils by themselves. Only teachers can decide on discipline.

## QUIZ
1. What is a Children's Government?
   A) A group of elected pupils who help to run the school
   B) The government of Kenya
   C) A group of teachers only
   D) A sports team
   Answer: A
   Why: It is made up of pupils chosen by their classmates and guided by teachers.
2. Who usually guides the Children's Government?
   A) A teacher patron
   B) A visitor
   C) A politician
   D) A parent
   Answer: A
   Why: A teacher adviser or patron helps the pupils so that the work is done well.
3. Which member of the Children's Government leads a meeting and makes sure that members are heard?
   A) The Speaker
   B) The cook
   C) The gatekeeper
   D) The driver
   Answer: A
   Why: The Speaker leads the meeting and gives everyone a chance to talk.
4. Which is a function of the Children's Government?
   A) Beating pupils
   B) Helping to promote cleanliness
   C) Taking pupils' money
   D) Closing the school
   Answer: B
   Why: Leaders organise cleaning, while punishing pupils or taking money is wrong.
5. Which action shows respect for pupil leaders?
   A) Laughing at them
   B) Ignoring their fair instructions
   C) Using polite language and following fair rules
   D) Insulting them
   Answer: C
   Why: Respect means following fair instructions and speaking politely.
6. Name three members of a typical Children's Government. (3 marks)
   Answer: President, deputy president and class representatives. Accept Speaker, secretary or cabinet members.
   Why: 1 mark for each correct member.
7. Give three functions of the Children's Government. (3 marks)
   Answer: Represent pupils, help keep order, and promote cleanliness. Accept organising activities.
   Why: 1 mark for each correct function.
8. Explain why a Children's Government should not punish other pupils. (2 marks)
   Answer: Only teachers are allowed to decide on discipline. Pupil leaders may treat others unfairly, so serious problems must be given to teachers.
   Why: 1 mark for saying teachers decide, 1 mark for the reason.
9. Describe two ways in which an ordinary pupil can participate in the Children's Government. (2 marks)
   Answer: By voting in the elections and by giving ideas to the class representative. Accept doing duties or helping the leaders.
   Why: 1 mark for each valid way.
