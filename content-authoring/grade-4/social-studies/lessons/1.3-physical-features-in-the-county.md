## PAGE 1: What are physical features?
Look out of the window or think about the road you walk to school. You may see a hill in the distance, a stream at the bottom of a slope, or a wide flat field. Nobody built these. They are natural. We call them physical features.

Physical features are natural features on the surface of the Earth. They were there long before towns and roads were made. Mountains, hills, valleys, plains, lakes, rivers and the ocean are all physical features.

They are different from man-made features. A school, a bridge, a road, a dam and a market are made by people. We call these built features. Sometimes the two work together. A bridge is built across a river, which is natural.

### Two groups
• Landforms: features made of land, such as mountains, hills, valleys, plains and plateaus.
• Water features: features made of water, such as lakes, rivers, streams, waterfalls and the ocean.

### Think about it
1. Name one physical feature that you can see from your home or school.
2. Is a dam a physical feature or a built feature?
3. Which physical feature do you think is the most important in your county? Why?

Every county in Kenya has some physical features. Some counties have a mountain. Some have a lake or a long river. Others have wide plains. In this lesson we learn to name them, show them and explain why they matter.

## PAGE 2: Landforms
Landforms are the shapes of the land. Learn these five.

• Mountain: very high land with steep sides and a narrow top called the peak. High mountains can be cold at the top and may have snow.
• Hill: land that is higher than the land around it, but lower and smoother than a mountain.
• Valley: low land between hills or mountains. Rivers often flow along valleys.
• Plain: wide, flat land. It is easy to farm and to build roads and towns on plains.
• Plateau: high land that is flat on top.

### Kenya examples
• Mount Kenya is the highest mountain in Kenya. It is 5,199 metres high and is the second highest mountain in Africa.
• Mount Elgon lies on the border between Kenya and Uganda.
• The Aberdare Range and the Ngong Hills are also highlands.
• The Great Rift Valley runs through Kenya from north to south. It is a very long, wide valley with steep sides.
• The Kenyan coast has a low coastal plain next to the Indian Ocean.

## PAGE 3: Water features
Water features hold or carry water.

• Lake: a large area of water with land all around it.
• River: water flowing in a long channel from high land to low land. Small rivers are called streams.
• Waterfall: a place where a river drops over a cliff.
• Ocean: a huge body of salt water. The Indian Ocean touches the coast of Kenya.
• Swamp: low land that is wet most of the year.

### Kenya examples
• Lake Victoria is the largest lake in Africa. Kenya shares it with Uganda and Tanzania. Kisumu, Homa Bay, Siaya, Busia and Migori counties have shores on the lake.
• Lake Turkana is in the far north, in a dry area. It is a very large lake.
• Lake Naivasha has fresh water and lies in the Rift Valley. Lake Nakuru is a salty lake, famous for flamingos.
• The Tana River is the longest river inside Kenya. It flows from the highlands to the Indian Ocean.
• Rivers such as the Nzoia and the Yala flow towards Lake Victoria.
• Thomson's Falls in Nyahururu and Fourteen Falls near Thika are waterfalls.

### Find out
Ask an elder or a parent: What are the names of the rivers, hills or lakes near our home? Are there any stories about them? Write the answers and share with the class.

### How to draw a river
A river starts on high land and flows to low land. It begins as a thin line and gets wider as it goes, because more water joins it. It ends in a lake or an ocean.

### Map task
Draw a small map of the area around your school. Show at least two physical features, for example a stream and a hill. Use the correct colours. Add a key, a title and a north arrow.

### Examples
• Mountains catch rain. Mount Kenya and the Aberdare Range are sources of rivers that give water to many towns. Their rich soils grow tea, coffee and food crops.
• Lakes give fish. Lake Victoria supports many fishing families. Lake Nakuru attracts visitors who come to see flamingos.
• Rivers give water for drinking and irrigation. The Tana River has dams at Masinga and Kiambere that make electricity, which is called hydroelectric power.
• Plains are flat and good for farming, building roads and railways, and for keeping animals.

## PAGE 6: Caring for physical features
If we do not care for physical features, they can be spoiled. Then people and animals will suffer.

### Dangers
• Cutting trees on mountains and near rivers makes rivers dry up.
• Throwing rubbish or chemicals into lakes and rivers kills fish and makes water unsafe to drink.
• Farming right at the river bank causes soil to wash into the water.
• Digging and building on steep hills can cause landslides.

### What we can do
• Plant trees on slopes and along river banks.
• Keep rubbish out of rivers and drains.
• Wash clothes and animals a good distance from the water source.
• Help clean up beaches, lake shores and streams.
• Tell adults when someone is polluting.

### Summary
Physical features are natural. Landforms are mountains, hills, valleys, plains and plateaus. Water features are lakes, rivers, waterfalls and oceans. They give us water, food, power, transport and beauty. We show them on maps using colours and symbols, and we must look after them.

### Common mistakes
• Calling a dam a physical feature. A dam is built by people, even if it holds a river's water.
• Colouring rivers green on a map. Water is coloured blue.
• Thinking that all lakes have fresh water. Some, such as Lake Nakuru, are salty.

## QUIZ
1. Which of the following is a physical feature?
   A) A bridge
   B) A school
   C) A lake
   D) A road
   Answer: C
   Why: A lake is natural. A bridge, school and road are built by people.
2. What is a valley?
   A) A very high mountain peak
   B) Low land between hills or mountains
   C) A large body of salt water
   D) A wide flat farm
   Answer: B
   Why: A valley is a low area that lies between higher land such as hills or mountains.
3. Which is the highest mountain in Kenya?
   A) Mount Elgon
   B) Ngong Hills
   C) Mount Kenya
   D) The Aberdare Range
   Answer: C
   Why: Mount Kenya is 5,199 metres high and is the highest mountain in Kenya.
4. On a physical map, what colour is used to show water?
   A) Blue
   B) Green
   C) Brown
   D) Red
   Answer: A
   Why: Blue is the colour used for lakes, rivers and the ocean on maps.
5. Which physical feature is very useful for fishing?
   A) A plain
   B) A hill
   C) A lake
   D) A plateau
   Answer: C
   Why: Fish live in lakes such as Lake Victoria, so people catch them there.
6. Name two landforms and two water features found in Kenya. (4 marks)
   Answer: Landforms: Mount Kenya, the Great Rift Valley (or Ngong Hills). Water features: Lake Victoria, the Tana River (or Lake Turkana).
   Why: 1 mark for each correct example, two landforms and two water features.
7. Explain two ways in which mountains are important to people. (2 marks)
   Answer: Mountains catch rain and give rivers that supply water. Their soils are rich for growing crops such as tea, and they attract tourists.
   Why: 1 mark for each correct point.
8. State three ways in which people can care for rivers and lakes. (3 marks)
   Answer: Not throwing rubbish into water, planting trees along river banks, and not washing or farming too close to the water.
   Why: 1 mark for each sensible way.
9. Draw and label a sketch of a mountain, a river and a lake as they would look on a map, with a key. (3 marks)
   Answer: A map with a triangle for a mountain, a wavy blue line for a river, a blue shape for a lake, and a key explaining each symbol.
   Why: 1 mark for correct symbols, 1 mark for correct colours, 1 mark for a key.
