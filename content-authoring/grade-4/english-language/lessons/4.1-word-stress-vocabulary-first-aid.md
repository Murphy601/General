## PAGE 1: Words for accidents and first aid
Accidents can happen anywhere: at home, at school and on the road. When they do, we need to know what to say and what to do. First aid is the first help we give to a person who is hurt or ill before a doctor or nurse takes over. Learning the words helps us speak clearly in an emergency.

### Vocabulary
• emergency: a sudden, serious event that needs quick action.
• injury: harm done to a body, such as a cut or a broken arm.
• bleed: to lose blood from a cut.
• bandage: a strip of cloth used to cover a wound.
• antiseptic: a liquid or cream that kills germs on a wound.
• first aid kit: a box with the things we use for first aid.
• treatment: the care given to a sick or hurt person.
• painkiller: a medicine that reduces pain.
• sting: a sharp hurt from an insect such as a bee.
• snake bite: a wound made by a snake.
• choke: to be unable to breathe because something is stuck in the throat.
• poison: something that can make you very ill or kill you if you swallow or touch it.
• patient: a person who is receiving treatment.
• health centre and hospital: places where sick or hurt people are treated.

### Road safety words
• underpass: a road or path that goes under another road.
• foot bridge: a bridge for people to walk over a busy road.
• roundabout: a circular junction where cars go around in one direction.
• railway crossing: the place where a road crosses a train line.
• cycling lane: a part of the road kept for people on bicycles.
• number plate: the plate on a vehicle showing its registration number.

## PAGE 2: The sounds /ɒ/, /eɪ/ and /aɪ/
Let us practise three vowel sounds that appear in first aid words.

### /ɒ/
This is a short sound with your mouth slightly rounded, as in "hot". Words: hospital, doctor, hot, stop, box, bottle, cough, pot, drop, cotton.
Sentence: The doctor stopped the cough with hot water.

### /eɪ/
This is a sound that glides from "eh" to "ee". Words: pain, ache, break, snake, safe, stay, wait, danger, tray.
Sentence: A snake can cause great pain, so stay safe and wait for help.

### /aɪ/
This sound glides from "ah" to "ee". Words: bite, sign, ice, life, right, side, ride, light, mind.
Sentence: Put ice on the bite, and ride to the hospital right away.

### Sorting
Put each word in the correct group: stop, pain, bite, hot, safe, ice, box, break.
/ɒ/: stop, hot, box.
/eɪ/: pain, safe, break.
/aɪ/: bite, ice.

### Listen and repeat
Listen to your teacher read the words. Repeat each word twice. Then read the sentence aloud, slowly and clearly.

## PAGE 3: Clusters sn, tr and st
A consonant cluster is two or three consonants that come together, and we say the sound of each one quickly.

### sn
snake, sneeze, snort, snack, snail, snow
Say a hissing "s" and glide into "n": snnn-ake.

### tr
treatment, tree, train, truck, trip, track, tray, trouble
Say /t/ and slide quickly into /r/.

### st
stop, stand, sting, stomach, stitch, star, still, first, list
The cluster st can also come at the end of a word, as in "first" and "list". Do not drop the /t/. Say "first aid", not "firs aid".

### Tongue twisters
• A snake will sneeze if it smells snow.
• Take the tray to the train and try to trim the tree.
• Stand still, Stella, and stop the stinging stomach.
• The doctor's treatment stops the sting.

Say each twister slowly, then quickly. Which words were hard? Repeat them alone until they feel easy.

### Rhythmic poem
Read this poem, clapping on each stressed beat.
Stop, look, listen, cross the road with care,
Use the foot bridge or the underpass to get there.
At the railway crossing, wait for the train,
Safe and sound, we walk home again.

## PAGE 4: Word stress
Every word of more than one syllable has one syllable that we say more strongly than the others. We say it louder, longer and a little higher. This is called word stress. A syllable is a beat in a word: hos-pi-tal has three syllables, ban-dage has two.

### Stress can change how a word sounds
If you put the stress in the wrong place, people may not understand you, or may hear another word. Listen to how the stress goes in these words. Capital letters show the stressed syllable.
• HOS-pi-tal (first syllable)
• BAN-dage (first syllable)
• TREAT-ment (first syllable)
• e-MER-gen-cy (second syllable)
• AM-bu-lance (first syllable)

### How to practise
1. Say the word slowly and clap the syllables.
2. Say the stressed syllable louder.
3. Say the word again naturally.
4. Check the stress in a dictionary. It is shown with a small mark ' before the stressed syllable, for example /ˈbændɪdʒ/.

## PAGE 5: When stress changes the meaning
Some words are both a noun and a verb, and they are spelt the same. The stress tells us which one we mean.
• Noun: the stress is on the first syllable.
• Verb: the stress is on the second syllable.

### Word pairs
• OB-ject (noun: a thing) and ob-JECT (verb: to say you do not agree)
• PRE-sent (noun: a gift) and pre-SENT (verb: to give or show)
• RE-cord (noun: a written note) and re-CORD (verb: to keep or make a record)
• IN-crease (noun: a rise in number) and in-CREASE (verb: to become more)
• PER-mit (noun: a paper of permission) and per-MIT (verb: to allow)

### Sentences
1. The nurse will re-CORD the patient's name in the RE-cord book.
2. Please pre-SENT the PRE-sent to your mother.
3. There is an IN-crease in the number of bicycles. Bicycle lanes will in-CREASE road safety.
4. I ob-JECT to leaving litter, because it is an OB-ject that can hurt someone.

### Practise
Say these sentences with the correct stress. Then make your own sentence using one word as a noun and then as a verb.

Remember that not every noun and verb pair changes stress. Learn the words listed above and listen to how other people say new words. When you are not sure, check a dictionary that also has sound.

## PAGE 6: Using the words and recording yourself
### Give an accident report
When there is an accident, you must give clear information. Read this model aloud:
"Help! There is an emergency. A boy has fallen from a tree near the school. His arm is bleeding and he is in pain. Please call a doctor and bring the first aid kit."

### Ask and answer
• What should we put on a small cut? An antiseptic and a bandage.
• Where should a person go if a snake bites? To the hospital or health centre quickly.
• Where do we cross a busy road safely? At the foot bridge, the underpass or the zebra crossing.

### Build sentences
Use these words in sentences with a partner: injury, treatment, painkiller, roundabout, number plate, cycling lane.
Example: The boy's injury needed treatment at the health centre.

### Record yourself
With your teacher's permission, record yourself on a phone or tablet reading a short passage with first aid words. Listen to it. Are the sounds clear? Is the stress on the right syllable? Share your recording with the class and ask for advice.

### Remember
Say each syllable, stress the correct one and make each sound clear. Then your words will be easy to understand, even in an emergency.

## QUIZ
1. In the word "hospital", which syllable is stressed?
   A) The first
   B) The second
   C) The third
   D) None
   Answer: A
   Why: We say HOS-pi-tal with the stress on the first syllable.
2. Which word has the stress on the second syllable?
   A) hospital
   B) bandage
   C) emergency
   D) treatment
   Answer: C
   Why: The stress is e-MER-gen-cy.
3. Which pair is a noun and a verb with a change of stress?
   A) OB-ject and ob-JECT
   B) hot and cold
   C) cat and cats
   D) run and running
   Answer: A
   Why: The noun is stressed on the first syllable and the verb on the second.
4. Which word has the cluster sn?
   A) stop
   B) treatment
   C) snake
   D) stomach
   Answer: C
   Why: Snake begins with s and n together.
5. What does the word "antiseptic" mean?
   A) A place to rest
   B) A liquid that kills germs on a wound
   C) A road sign
   D) A strip for bandages
   Answer: B
   Why: An antiseptic cleans wounds by killing germs.
6. Say which words have the sound /aɪ/: bite, stop, ice, hot. (2 marks)
   Answer: bite and ice
   Why: 1 mark for each correct word.
7. Explain what word stress is, and give one example. (3 marks)
   Answer: Word stress is the syllable we say more strongly in a word, for example the first syllable in HOS-pi-tal.
   Why: 1 mark for the idea of one syllable being stronger, 1 mark for saying it is louder or longer, 1 mark for a correct example.
8. Write two sentences using "present" once as a noun and once as a verb, showing the stress with capital letters. (3 marks)
   Answer: For example: My uncle gave me a PRE-sent. I will pre-SENT the prize to the winner.
   Why: 1 mark for each correct sentence and 1 mark for the correct stress marking.
9. A friend has been stung by a bee. Write three sentences you would say to ask for help, using at least two words from this lesson. (3 marks)
   Answer: For example: Help! My friend has a bee sting. Please bring the first aid kit. We need an antiseptic and a bandage.
   Why: 1 mark for each clear sentence and 1 mark for a suitable lesson word, up to 3 marks.
