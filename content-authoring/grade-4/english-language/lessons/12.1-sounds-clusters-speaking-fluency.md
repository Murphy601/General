## PAGE 1: Speaking accurately, with expression and at the right speed
Fluent speakers do three things well. They speak accurately, so each word can be understood. They use expression, so their voice shows meaning and feeling. And they speak at the right speed, so listeners can follow.

### Accuracy
Say every sound in a word. Do not leave off endings, for example "walked" not "walk", and "hands" not "han".

### Expression
• Your voice goes up at the end of a question.
• Your voice is louder for excitement and softer for gentle ideas.
• You stress important words: "Please put the rubbish in the BIN."

### Right speed
• Too fast: listeners miss words.
• Too slow: listeners get bored.
• Just right: about like a calm talk with a friend.

### Minimal hesitation
Hesitation means stopping or saying "um" and "er". Prepare what you want to say and practise it, and you will hesitate less.

### The theme: a clean environment
This unit uses words like compound, bin, bush, tree, conserve, water, sweep, rubbish pit, broom, protect, rivers, drain, neighbourhood, hills, green and grass. Say them slowly, then at normal speed.

### Nonverbal cues
Use your face and hands. A smile, eye contact and gentle gestures make your speech more interesting. A hand held out can mean "please give" or "stop"; a nod can mean "yes".

### Try it
Say this sentence with expression: "Please keep our compound clean!" First say it softly, then loudly, then as a question. How does the meaning change?

## PAGE 2: The vowel sounds /ʌ/ and /ɔː/
### /ʌ/ (as in "cup")
This is a short vowel. The mouth is open a little and relaxed. It is found in:
• cup, bus, sun, mud, run, fun
• mother, brother, love, come, some, month
• money, monkey, country, other, does
The letter "u" and sometimes "o" can make this sound. Learn them as word groups.

### /ɔː/ (as in "door")
This is a long vowel. Round your lips and hold the sound. It is found in:
• door, four, more, floor, store, sport
• ball, call, all, wall, small, tall
• saw, draw, paw, straw, law
• walk, talk, chalk
• water (in careful British English), horse, born, corn, storm

### Contrast
• cut and caught
• bus and boss
• luck and lock
• cup and cop
The sound /ʌ/ is short and the mouth is unrounded; /ɔː/ is long and the lips are round.

### Sentences
1. "My mother put some rubbish in the bin."
2. "We sweep the floor and clean the wall."
3. "The sun shines on the small, green hills."
4. "Four boys walked through the door to sort the bottles."

### Note
Accents differ. A few of these words may be said with slightly different vowels in different parts of the country. Follow your teacher's model.

## PAGE 3: The clusters br, sw and pr
A consonant cluster is a group of two or three consonant sounds together, each still heard. Say them quickly but clearly.

### br
• bring, brush, bread, bridge, brown
• broom, brother, branch, break, bright
Example: "Bring the broom and brush the bridge."

### sw
• swim, sweep, sweet, swing, swan
• swallow, sweat, switch, swell
Example: "Sweep the swing area."

### pr
• print, price, proud, protect, prepare
• press, prize, present, problem, produce
Example: "Protect the water and prepare a prize."

### Common mistakes
• Adding a vowel inside the cluster: "buh-room" for "broom". Say the two consonants together.
• Leaving out one sound: "bing" for "bring", or "seet" for "sweet".

### Practice
Say each pair slowly, then fast.
• brush / bush
• sweep / seep
• pride / ride
• bread / red

### Tongue twister
"Bring the broom and brush the bridge, then sweep the swing before the sweet bread comes."

### Environment sentence
"We are proud to protect the bright, green grass and the brown river."

## PAGE 4: The clusters cl and gr
### cl
• clean, clap, class, clear, cloud
• climb, close, clock, club, clay
Example: "The clear water clears the clay."

### gr
• green, grass, grow, ground, grain
• great, group, grandmother, grin, grab
Example: "Green grass grows on the ground."

### Say and compare
• clean and lean
• clue and loo (avoid)
• grass and ass (avoid)
• glass and grass: notice that "gl" and "gr" are different. In "glass", the tongue touches the top of the mouth; in "grass", it curls back.
Practise the pair: glow / grow, glad / grad. Take care not to mix them.

### A poem to recite in groups
"Green grass grows on the ground,
Clean water flows, clear and sound.
Clap your hands, and climb the hill,
Keep our world both clean and still."

### Rules for reciting
• Everyone starts and ends together.
• Follow the beat.
• Speak clearly and at the right speed.
• Use hand actions for verbs: clap, climb, grow.

### Try it
Choose one cluster. Write five words that begin with it. Make a sentence with at least three of them. Read it aloud.

## PAGE 5: Yes/no questions and short speeches
### Yes/no questions
A yes/no question can be answered with "yes" or "no". Start with a helping verb such as Is, Are, Do, Does, Did, Can, Will.
• Is the compound clean? Yes, it is. / No, it is not.
• Do you sweep every day? Yes, I do. / No, I do not.
• Can we plant more trees? Yes, we can.
• Will you help clean the river bank? Yes, I will.
• Did you put rubbish in the bin? Yes, I did.
Your voice goes up at the end of these questions.

### Use vocabulary in questions
Make yes/no questions with the theme words: bin, broom, rubbish pit, drain, hills.
• Is the rubbish pit covered?
• Did you sweep the compound with a broom?

### A short speech on the environment
Plan a short speech with a greeting, three points and a closing.
Example:
"Good afternoon, friends. Today I want to talk about keeping our compound clean. First, we should sweep every day. Second, we should put rubbish in the bin. Third, we should plant trees and grass. A clean environment keeps us healthy. Thank you."

### Delivery tips
• Stand up straight and look at the audience.
• Speak clearly, not too fast.
• Use your hands to show the size of things or to count points.
• Practise several times.

## PAGE 6: Repeating oral texts without hesitation
Practise repeating a text after a teacher or a recording.

### Text to repeat
"The river is our friend. It gives us water to drink. Let us protect it. We must not throw rubbish in the water. We must sweep our compound and plant trees. Together we can keep our neighbourhood green and clean."

### Steps
1. Listen to the whole text once.
2. Repeat it phrase by phrase after the speaker.
3. Repeat it whole, without stopping.
4. Say it to a friend from memory.

### Marking a text for expression
Use marks in your book to remind yourself: a slash (/) for a short pause, two slashes (//) for a long pause, and an underline for words to stress.
"The river / is our FRIEND. // It gives us water to DRINK. //"

### Poem to recite together
"Clean the drain and sweep the ground,
Keep the rubbish off the ground,
Plant a tree and watch it grow,
Let the river clearly flow."
Every learner takes a line. The class says the last line together.

### Summary
• Fluent speech is accurate, expressive and at the right speed.
• /ʌ/ as in cup; /ɔː/ as in door.
• Clusters: br, sw, pr, cl and gr.
• Yes/no questions start with a helping verb.
• Practise and repeat to reduce hesitation.

## QUIZ
1. Which word has the sound /ɔː/?
   A) cup
   B) bus
   C) door
   D) mud
   Answer: C
   Why: "Door" has the long vowel /ɔː/. The others have the short /ʌ/.
2. Which word begins with the cluster "sw"?
   A) sweep
   B) broom
   C) clean
   D) green
   Answer: A
   Why: "Sweep" begins with s and w together.
3. Which of these begins with "pr"?
   A) protect
   B) brush
   C) clap
   D) grow
   Answer: A
   Why: "Protect" begins with p and r.
4. Which is a yes/no question?
   A) Where is the bin?
   B) What did you sweep?
   C) Is the compound clean?
   D) Who is the captain?
   Answer: C
   Why: It starts with the helping verb "Is" and can be answered "yes" or "no".
5. Write two words for each cluster: (a) br, (b) gr. (4 marks)
   Answer: (a) bring, broom (or bread, brush) (b) green, grass (or grow, ground)
   Why: One mark for each correct word.
6. Explain what it means to speak with expression. (2 marks)
   Answer: It means using the voice to show meaning and feeling, such as by changing loudness, stress and pitch.
   Why: One mark for using the voice and one mark for showing feeling or meaning.
7. Write three yes/no questions about keeping the compound clean. (3 marks)
   Answer: For example: Do you sweep the compound? Is the bin covered? Can we plant a tree?
   Why: One mark for each correct question.
8. Write a short speech of four sentences about protecting a river. (4 marks)
   Answer: A greeting, two points such as "Do not throw rubbish in the river" and "Plant trees on the bank", and a closing.
   Why: One mark for each element.
