## PAGE 1: What is a factual text?
A factual text gives true information. It tells facts about a subject. Examples are textbooks, encyclopaedias, news reports, health leaflets and notices. A story, in contrast, may be made up.

### Features of factual texts
• A heading or title that names the topic.
• Sub-headings that divide the text into parts.
• Sentences that state facts, usually in the present tense.
• Often a list, a table, a picture or a diagram.
• A clear order of ideas.

### Reading habits for factual texts
1. Read the title and sub-headings first to see what the text is about.
2. Read at a steady pace. If you rush, you miss details; if you are too slow, you forget the start.
3. Read a paragraph, then stop and say the main idea to yourself.
4. Underline key words.
5. Look at pictures and their captions.
6. Read the text again to check your answers.

### Fact and opinion
• A fact can be checked: "Water boils at a high temperature."
• An opinion says what someone thinks: "Cold water is nicest."
Factual texts contain mostly facts.

### Try it
Look at a page from your Science or Social Studies book. Say the heading, one sub-heading and one fact from the page.

## PAGE 2: A factual text, part 1
Read this article carefully.

Keeping Clean to Stay Healthy

Hygiene means the things we do to keep our bodies and surroundings clean. Good hygiene protects us from germs. Germs are tiny living things that are too small to see. Some germs cause illnesses such as diarrhoea, cholera and skin infections.

Washing hands
Hands touch many things each day, such as door handles, money and animals. Germs can stay on our hands. When we touch our mouth, eyes or food, the germs can enter our bodies. To stop this, we should wash our hands with soap and clean water:
• after using the toilet,
• before preparing or eating food,
• after playing outside,
• after touching animals or rubbish.
Rub your hands together for about twenty seconds, including between the fingers and under the nails. Then rinse them well.

Clean water
Water from rivers and ponds may contain germs. It should be boiled until it bubbles well, or treated with a water treatment product, before drinking. Store treated water in a clean, covered container. Do not dip dirty cups into it.

### Answer these
1. What is hygiene? (The things we do to keep our bodies and surroundings clean.)
2. Give two illnesses caused by germs. (Diarrhoea, cholera, skin infections.)
3. Name two times when we should wash our hands. (After using the toilet, before eating.)

## PAGE 3: A factual text, part 2
Continue reading.

Toilets and waste
Cholera and diarrhoea spread when human waste, called faeces, gets into water or food. This is why every home and school needs a clean latrine or toilet. A good latrine has a cover or a door, is far from water sources and is kept clean. After using it, wash your hands with soap. Rubbish should be put in a bin or pit, not thrown around. Flies land on waste and then on food, spreading germs. A covered pit or bin keeps flies away.

Keeping the body and home clean
• Bathe often with soap and clean water.
• Brush your teeth in the morning and before bed.
• Cut your nails short, so that dirt cannot hide beneath them.
• Wash your clothes, and sleep under a clean blanket.
• Sweep and mop the floor, and wipe dust off surfaces.
• Keep food covered and wash fruit and vegetables before eating them.

What happens if we do not keep clean?
People can fall sick. They may have stomach pains, diarrhoea and high fever. They may miss school or work. If a person has diarrhoea, they should drink plenty of clean, safe water and go to a health worker.

Summary
Good hygiene is easy and costs little. Soap, clean water, a clean toilet and covered food help to keep everyone healthy.

### Answer these
4. Why do flies spread germs? (They land on waste and then on food.)
5. How long should you rub your hands when washing them? (About twenty seconds.)
6. What should a person with diarrhoea do? (Drink plenty of safe water and see a health worker.)

## PAGE 4: Inferring the meaning of new words
Sometimes the text does not explain a word, but the sentence gives clues. This is called inferring meaning from context.

### Steps
1. Find the word you do not know.
2. Read the sentence before and after.
3. Look for clues: examples, opposites or explanations.
4. Guess a meaning that fits.
5. Check with a dictionary.

### Practice with the passage
• "Germs are tiny living things that are too small to see." The sentence explains "germs".
• "Cholera and diarrhoea spread when human waste, called faeces, gets into water." The words "human waste" and "called" explain faeces.
• "Rub your hands... then rinse them well." "Rinse" comes after the soap, so it means to wash off the soap with clean water.
• "A good latrine has a cover or a door, is far from water sources." From "cover or door" and "toilet" earlier, latrine means a simple toilet.

### Use a dictionary or encyclopaedia
A dictionary gives the meaning and spelling of a word. Words are arranged in alphabetical order. An encyclopaedia gives detailed information on a topic. Look up a topic such as "cholera" with the help of a teacher.

### Word building
• hygiene, hygienic (clean and healthy)
• infect, infection, infected
• sanitation, sanitary
• clean, cleaner, cleanliness

### Try it
Look at the sentence: "Store treated water in a clean, covered container." What might "container" mean? (Something that holds water, such as a bucket or jerrycan.)

## PAGE 5: Factual and inferential questions
### Factual questions
The answer is in the text.
• What is hygiene?
• When should we wash our hands?
• What should a latrine have?

### Inferential questions
You must think and use clues.
• Why does the writer say soap and water are important? (Because they remove germs that cannot be seen.)
• Why should water from rivers be boiled? (Because it may contain germs that could make us ill.)
• What can you tell about the writer's opinion of hygiene? (It is easy, cheap and important.)

### Answering tips
1. Read the question twice.
2. Find the paragraph.
3. Answer in a full sentence.
4. Use words from the text to start.
5. For "why" questions, use "because".

### Pick out specific information
Practise finding details quickly. Where does the article say that fruit and vegetables should be washed? (In "Keeping the body and home clean".)

### Watch a video and note down
Watch a short health video with your teacher. Note down three facts about hygiene. Then compare with a partner.

### Main idea and details
Main idea: Good hygiene keeps us healthy.
Supporting details: washing hands, clean water, clean toilets, covered food, a clean body.

## PAGE 6: Using what you have read
### Make a poster
Design a poster to remind people to wash hands. Use a heading, a picture and short sentences: "Wash before you eat. Wash after the toilet. Use soap and water."

### Note-making
Copy this table into your notebook and complete it.
Hygiene action: Why it matters
Washing hands: removes germs
Boiling water: kills germs in it
Covering food: keeps flies away
Using a latrine: stops waste from reaching water
Cutting nails: dirt cannot hide

### Speak
In groups, take turns explaining one hygiene rule to the class. Give a reason.

### Summary
• Factual texts give true information.
• Read the title and sub-headings first.
• Use context to work out new words.
• Answer factual questions from the text and inferential questions from clues.
• Use reference books such as dictionaries and encyclopaedias to find more.

## QUIZ
1. What is a factual text?
   A) A made-up story
   B) A text that gives true information
   C) A poem
   D) A joke
   Answer: B
   Why: Factual texts share true information about a subject.
2. According to the text, when should you wash your hands?
   A) Only on Sundays
   B) After using the toilet
   C) When they look clean
   D) Never with soap
   Answer: B
   Why: The text lists "after using the toilet" as one of the times.
3. Why should river water be boiled before drinking?
   A) It tastes sweet
   B) It may contain germs
   C) It looks brown
   D) It is too cold
   Answer: B
   Why: The text says water from rivers may contain germs.
4. What does "rinse" mean in the passage?
   A) To wash off soap with clean water
   B) To dry
   C) To cook
   D) To hide
   Answer: A
   Why: In the hand-washing steps, you rinse after using soap.
5. Give two ways flies can spread germs, according to the text. (2 marks)
   Answer: They land on waste and then on food.
   Why: One mark for landing on waste, one mark for landing on food afterwards.
6. Write three hygiene habits mentioned in the text. (3 marks)
   Answer: For example: wash hands with soap; brush teeth; cut nails short; cover food; bathe often.
   Why: One mark for each correct habit.
7. Explain the difference between a fact and an opinion. Give one example of each. (3 marks)
   Answer: A fact can be checked, for example "Germs are tiny." An opinion says what someone thinks, for example "Cold water is nicest."
   Why: One mark for each definition and one mark for examples.
8. Write four sentences to advise your classmates on how to keep their school toilets clean. (4 marks)
   Answer: Any four sensible sentences, such as: Wash your hands after using the toilet. Do not throw rubbish in the latrine. Keep the door closed. Report any damage to the teacher.
   Why: One mark for each sensible sentence.
