## PAGE 1: What is a narrative?
A narrative is a story that tells what happened. It has people or animals (characters), a place (setting) and events that happen one after another. When we tell a friend how our weekend went, we tell a narrative. When we write it down, we write a narrative composition.

Why do you enjoy listening to stories? Because they carry us to other places, make us laugh or teach us something. When you write, you can make a reader feel the same way.

### Kinds of narratives
• A true story about something that happened to you: a visit, a journey, a day at school.
• An imagined story: an animal that talks, a hero, a magic drum.
• A retelling of a story you heard on the radio, on television or from an elder.

### Parts of a narrative composition
1. Beginning (introduction): tells who, where and when. It introduces the story.
2. Middle (events): tells what happened, in order. This may include a problem or a surprise.
3. End (conclusion): tells how it finished and how the writer felt or what was learnt.

### Tell it aloud first
Before writing, tell your story to a friend in three parts: beginning, middle and end. If you can tell it, you can write it.

## PAGE 2: Planning a story
Why is it important to plan our composition? A plan keeps your story in order and stops you from forgetting what comes next.

### Steps
1. Choose a topic. Example: "A visit to the supermarket".
2. Brainstorm ideas. Write words and short phrases: bus, trolley, bread, fruit, cashier.
3. Choose the most interesting ideas.
4. Arrange them in order.

### Sample plan: A trip to the supermarket
Beginning: Saturday, Mama and I go to the supermarket in town.
Events: We push the trolley, buy bread and milk, choose vegetables and fruit.
Exciting part: I find a big pile of oranges.
End: We pay, go home and eat happily.

Write your plan in note form, not in full sentences. A plan is a guide.

## PAGE 3: Making writing lively with similes and time words
### Similes
A simile compares two different things with "like" or "as". Similes help the reader to picture your story.
• The shelves were as tall as giants.
• The oranges were as round as balls.
• The cashier worked like a machine.
• Mama moved through the shop like a bird.
• The bread was as soft as a pillow.

Create your own. Complete: as cold as ______, as bright as ______, ran like ______. Use only one or two similes in a short story, or the story becomes crowded.

### Time and order words
Use these words so that the reader can follow the order of events.
first, then, next, after that, soon, later, suddenly, at last, in the end

### Paragraphs
Write the beginning in one paragraph, the events in another and the end in a last paragraph. If you write only 60 to 80 words, your paragraphs will be short, but keep them separate.

### Choose exact words
• Instead of "went", say ran, walked, hurried or rushed.
• Instead of "nice", say delicious, sweet, soft or pleasant.
• Instead of "said", say asked, shouted, whispered or replied.

### Use the senses
Tell the reader what you saw, heard, smelt and felt. "I could smell the fresh bread" helps the reader to be there with you.

## PAGE 4: A model narrative
The composition below has about 65 words, which fits the length you are asked to write. Read it and look at its parts.

### A Trip to the Supermarket
On Saturday, Mama took me to the supermarket in town. The shelves were as tall as giants and full of colourful food. First, we bought bread and milk. Then we chose fresh vegetables. Suddenly, I saw a huge pile of oranges. I picked the sweetest ones. At last, Mama paid and we went home. I was happy because I had helped to choose our food.

### Study the model
• Beginning: "On Saturday, Mama took me to the supermarket in town." Who, where and when.
• Middle: what they bought and the surprise of the oranges.
• End: they went home and the writer felt happy.
• Simile: "as tall as giants."
• Time words: first, then, suddenly, at last.
• Length: about 65 words.

### Rearrange jumbled sentences
Put these sentences in the correct order to make a paragraph.
a) She opened the gate and walked in.
b) Grace woke up early on Monday.
c) Finally, she sat on her seat and smiled.
d) She wore her uniform and walked to school.
Correct order: b, d, a, c.

Notice how the time words and the ideas help you to put the sentences in order.

## PAGE 5: Write, check and share
### Write your narrative
Choose one topic:
• A visit to the supermarket, a food store or a grocery shop near school.
• A day I helped in the kitchen.
• A story that I heard on the radio or television.

Follow these steps.
1. Make a plan.
2. Write a draft of 60 to 80 words. Include one simile and at least three time words.
3. Read your draft aloud and improve it.
4. Write a clean copy in neat handwriting.

### Retell a story you heard
Listen to a story on the radio, on television or from an elder. Then write it in your own words. Do not copy word for word. Remember the beginning, middle and end.

### Checklist
• Does my story have a beginning, middle and end?
• Are the events in order?
• Did I use time words?
• Did I use a simile?
• Does each sentence start with a capital letter and end with a full stop?
• Are the names spelt with capital letters?
• Is it 60 to 80 words?

### Act it out
Watch or listen to a story with a group. Then act it out. Actors show what happens, and writers can do the same with words.

### Share
Read your story to a group and listen to theirs. Say one thing that you liked about each story.

## QUIZ
1. What is a narrative?
   A) A story that tells what happened
   B) A list of words
   C) A letter to a friend
   D) A form to fill in
   Answer: A
   Why: A narrative tells events in the order they happened.
2. Which part of a narrative tells how the story finished?
   A) The beginning
   B) The middle
   C) The end
   D) The title
   Answer: C
   Why: The end tells how the story finished and what the writer felt or learnt.
3. Which sentence contains a simile?
   A) The shelves were tall.
   B) The shelves were as tall as giants.
   C) We bought bread.
   D) Mama paid.
   Answer: B
   Why: It compares shelves with giants using as ... as.
4. Which word helps to show the order of events?
   A) suddenly
   B) blue
   C) mango
   D) green
   Answer: A
   Why: Time words such as first, then and suddenly show order.
5. About how long should your narrative be in Grade 4?
   A) 5 to 10 words
   B) 20 words
   C) 60 to 80 words
   D) 500 words
   Answer: C
   Why: The composition should be about 60 to 80 words.
6. Write the sentences in the correct order: (a) Then she paid. (b) Grace entered the shop. (c) Finally, she went home. (2 marks)
   Answer: b, a, c
   Why: 1 mark for starting with b and 1 mark for the correct order of the rest.
7. Write two similes about a food you like. (2 marks)
   Answer: For example: The bread was as soft as a pillow. The juice was as sweet as honey.
   Why: 1 mark for each correct simile.
8. Explain why a plan is important before writing a story. (3 marks)
   Answer: A plan puts ideas in order, shows the beginning, middle and end and makes sure nothing is forgotten.
   Why: 1 mark for each valid reason, up to 3 marks.
9. Write a narrative of about 60 words about a visit to a shop. Include one simile and at least two time words. (5 marks)
   Answer: A composition of about 60 words that has a beginning, middle and end, one simile, at least two time words, and correct punctuation.
   Why: 1 mark for a clear beginning, 1 mark for events in order, 1 mark for the simile, 1 mark for time words and 1 mark for punctuation and neatness.
