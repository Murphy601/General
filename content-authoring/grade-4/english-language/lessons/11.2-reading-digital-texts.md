## PAGE 1: What are digital texts?
A digital text is any text you read on a screen: a phone, tablet, computer or e-reader. You might read it online, when you are connected to the internet, or offline, when the text has been saved on the device.

### Kinds of digital texts
• Animated stories: stories with moving pictures and sound.
• Comics and cartoons: stories told through pictures and speech bubbles.
• Digital poems and narratives: the same as those in books, but on a screen.
• Web pages and articles: information on a topic.
• Videos and mimes: stories told by acting and pictures, sometimes without words.

### How are they like and unlike printed books?
Alike: both have words, pictures and a message; both need careful reading.
Unlike: digital texts can move, have sound, let you click a link and can be updated. They may also have advertisements that distract you.

### Reading digital texts well
1. Sit at a comfortable distance from the screen, with good light.
2. Read the title and any menu first.
3. Scroll slowly so that you do not miss lines.
4. Read every word, not only the pictures.
5. Take breaks to rest your eyes.
6. Stay on the page your teacher or parent has approved.

### Try it
Ask your teacher for a short animated story. Watch it once, then say who, where and what happened.

## PAGE 2: Reading comics and cartoons
A comic tells a story in a series of pictures called panels. Words appear in speech bubbles (what people say) and in boxes (what the narrator tells). The panels are read from left to right and top to bottom.

### Read this comic as text
Panel 1. A boy called Wafula is holding a football. He is standing at the edge of a muddy field.
Wafula (speech bubble): "I cannot wait for the match!"
Panel 2. Clouds fill the sky and it begins to rain.
Box: "Then the sky turned dark."
Panel 3. Wafula's coach waves from the classroom door.
Coach: "Come inside, team! We will practise in the hall."
Panel 4. The team sits in a circle, learning a new pass.
Wafula: "Rain cannot stop a good team!"

### Reading interpretively
To read interpretively means you think about feelings and meaning, not just words. Notice:
• How does Wafula feel at the start? (Excited.)
• Why does the coach call the team inside? (It is raining and the field is muddy.)
• What does Wafula's last sentence show? (He is positive and will not give up.)

### Speech bubbles and voice
When you read aloud, use a different voice for each character and for the narrator. Read Wafula's words with excitement, and the coach's words in a firm, kind tone.

### Try it
Create a four-panel comic about a game you enjoy. Write in each panel what the picture shows, and what people say.

## PAGE 3: Reading a poem on a screen
Read this poem carefully, then discuss it.

The Whistle
The whistle blows, the game begins,
The players run, each wants to win.
The crowd is loud, the ball flies high,
It sails right through the clear blue sky.

A goal! A goal! We jump and cheer,
The coach is smiling, the team is near.
But if we lose, we will not cry,
We shake their hands and say, "Good try."

### Reading a poem interpretively
• Read it once for enjoyment.
• Read it again, using your voice to show feelings: excitement in the first verse, joy in "A goal! A goal!", kindness in the last line.
• Notice the rhyme: begins/win, high/sky, cheer/near, cry/try.
• Notice the exclamation marks: they tell you to speak with feeling.

### Questions
1. What does the poem say happens when the whistle blows? (The game begins.)
2. How do the players behave when they lose? (They shake hands and say "Good try.")
3. What lesson does the poem teach? (To be a good sport: to lose without crying and to respect the other team.)

### Different emotions
Poems can be joyful, sad, funny or calm. Practise reading a line first happily, then sadly, to see how your voice changes.

### How to read a mind map
1. Find the centre: that is the main topic.
2. Follow each branch outward.
3. Read the words on each branch.
4. Say how they connect to the centre.
Here: Football, netball, athletics and handball are all kinds of sport.

### Make your own
Draw a mind map with "Keeping Fit" in the middle. Add branches: exercise, food, sleep and water. Below each, write one or two words.

### Other visuals
Pictures, diagrams, charts and maps are visuals too. Read the title, labels and captions to understand them.

## PAGE 5: Searching for information online
You can do simple research online, but always with an adult's help and permission.

### Steps for a safe search
1. Decide what you want to find out: "How is netball played?"
2. Choose key words, such as "netball rules for children".
3. Type the key words into a search box on a site your teacher has approved.
4. Look at the list of results. Pick a reliable one, such as a school, library or well-known encyclopaedia site.
5. Read the page carefully.
6. Write down the facts in your own words.
7. Note where you found them.

### Staying safe online
• Do not share your name, school, address or password.
• Do not click on pop-up advertisements or strange links.
• Tell a trusted adult if something you see worries you.
• Be kind in anything you write or share.

### Judging a page
Ask:
• Is it about my topic?
• Is it easy to read?
• Does it agree with other places that I have read?
Not everything online is true. Check with a book or a teacher.

### Crossword from a story
After reading an animated story, make a crossword puzzle. Choose six words from the story, write clues (for example, "Official who blows a whistle" for "referee"), and give your puzzle to a friend to solve.

### Try it
Write three key words you could type to find out about the history of football. For example: "football history for children".

## PAGE 6: A short digital passage and practice
Read this text as if you found it on a children's website.

Netball: A Fast and Fun Game

Netball is a team game played by two teams of seven players. It is played on a rectangular court with a tall post and ring at each end. The aim of the game is to throw the ball through the ring of the other team to score a goal.

Players cannot run with the ball. When a player catches the ball, she must pass it to a team-mate within three seconds. Each player has a position and stays in certain parts of the court. There is a referee, who blows a whistle to start the game and to stop it when a rule is broken.

Netball helps children to keep fit. It teaches teamwork, quick thinking and good sportsmanship. Many schools in Kenya play netball, and pupils often practise with their coach after lessons.

### Answer these
1. How many players are in a netball team? (seven)
2. Can a player run with the ball? (No.)
3. What is the aim of the game? (To throw the ball through the other team's ring.)
4. What does netball teach? (Teamwork, quick thinking and good sportsmanship.)

### Summary
• Digital texts include animated stories, comics, poems and web pages.
• Read comics from left to right, top to bottom.
• Use mind maps to organise ideas.
• Search safely and choose reliable sources.

## QUIZ
1. What is a digital text?
   A) A text on a printed page only
   B) A text you read on a screen
   C) A spoken poem only
   D) A letter in an envelope
   Answer: B
   Why: Digital texts are read on screens such as phones, tablets and computers.
2. In which order do we read comic panels?
   A) Right to left
   B) Left to right and top to bottom
   C) Bottom to top
   D) Any order
   Answer: B
   Why: We read a comic the same way as a book: left to right and top to bottom.
3. What is at the centre of a mind map?
   A) The main topic
   B) The last idea
   C) A picture
   D) The author's name
   Answer: A
   Why: The main topic is in the middle and branches lead to related ideas.
4. Which action is safe when searching online?
   A) Telling a stranger your address
   B) Clicking every pop-up
   C) Using a site approved by your teacher
   D) Sharing your password
   Answer: C
   Why: Using approved sites and keeping details private keeps you safe.
5. In "The Whistle", how do players behave when they lose? Answer in a sentence. (2 marks)
   Answer: They do not cry; they shake hands with the other team and say "Good try."
   Why: One mark for not crying and one mark for shaking hands.
6. Give two safety rules for using the internet. (2 marks)
   Answer: For example: do not share personal details; tell an adult if something worries you.
   Why: One mark for each rule.
7. Draw a mind map in words for the topic "Keeping Fit" with four branches. (4 marks)
   Answer: Centre: Keeping Fit. Branches: exercise, healthy food, sleep, clean water.
   Why: One mark for the centre and one mark for each branch, up to four marks in all.
8. Explain the difference between a printed book and a digital text. (3 marks)
   Answer: A book is printed on paper. A digital text is on a screen and may have moving pictures, sound and links.
   Why: One mark for each kind and one mark for a clear difference.
