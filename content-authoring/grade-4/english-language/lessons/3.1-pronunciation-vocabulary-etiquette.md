## PAGE 1: What is etiquette?
Etiquette means the polite way to behave and speak with other people. Another word for it is good manners. When we use good manners, people feel respected and welcome. When we are rude, people feel hurt.

Words are a very important part of good manners. The words we choose and the way we say them show how much we respect a person. A small word such as "please" can change a demand into a polite request.

### Polite words and phrases
• Please: used when we ask for something. "Please pass the salt."
• Thank you: used when someone gives or does something for us.
• You are welcome: our answer when someone thanks us.
• Excuse me: used to get someone's attention, or to pass politely in front of someone.
• I am sorry: used when we have made a mistake or hurt someone.
• I beg your pardon: used when we did not hear or understand, or when we need to say sorry.
• May I borrow your pencil? used when we ask permission.
• Congratulations: used when someone has done well or something good has happened.
• Good morning, good afternoon, good evening: greetings for different times of the day.
• Welcome: used when a guest arrives.

### Rude or polite?
Compare: "Give me water!" and "May I have some water, please?" The second is polite. It uses "may", "please" and a gentle voice.

## PAGE 2: Using polite words in different places
The right polite word depends on the situation. Think about where you are and who is with you.

### At home
• When you wake up: "Good morning, Mother."
• When your father gives you your school bag: "Thank you, Father."
• When you want to pass through: "Excuse me, Grandmother."
• After you spill water: "I am sorry, Mother. I will clean it."

### At school
• To your teacher: "Good morning, teacher."
• When you need to borrow something: "May I borrow your ruler, please?"
• When you are late: "Excuse me, teacher. I am sorry I am late."
• When a friend wins a prize: "Congratulations!"

### In public places
• On a matatu, when you want to sit: "Excuse me, is this seat free?"
• At a shop: "Good afternoon. Please may I have a loaf of bread?"
• When a visitor comes: "Welcome. Please sit down."

### How you say it also matters
• Smile and look at the person.
• Speak in a soft and friendly voice.
• Say the word clearly, so that the person can hear you.

### Try it
Act out these short scenes with a partner: (1) You meet a visitor at the gate. (2) You knock over your friend's book. (3) A classmate helps you carry your bag.

## PAGE 3: The sounds /e/ and /eɪ/
The vowel /e/ is short, as in "pen". The vowel /eɪ/ is a diphthong, meaning it glides from one sound to another. It is the sound in "pain". Open your mouth slightly for /e/. For /eɪ/, start with the same sound and slide upward as if you are saying "ee" at the end.

### Words with /e/
pen, bed, red, met, set, get, help, welcome, friend, yes, well
Careful: the word "excuse" does not begin with /e/. It begins with the short sound /ɪ/, so we say "ik-SKYOOZ".

### Words with /eɪ/
pain, made, take, wait, may, day, say, make, great, break, late, gate, mate

### Minimal pairs
• pen, pain
• wet, wait
• men, main
• met, mate
• get, gate
• let, late
• sell, sale

Say each pair slowly. Change only the vowel.

### Sentences
• Please wait at the gate.
• May I help you, my friend?
• It is late, so let us say goodbye.
• The men sell red pens.

## PAGE 4: The sounds /p/ and /b/
These sounds are made with both lips. First press your lips together. Then let them open quickly. The difference is in the voice.

• /p/ is whispered. You blow out a little puff of air. Hold a strip of paper in front of your lips and it moves.
• /b/ is voiced. You feel your throat buzz. The paper hardly moves.

### Minimal pairs
• pin, bin
• pat, bat
• pig, big
• pear, bear
• park, bark
• pay, bay
• cap, cab
• rope, robe

Ask a partner to read one word. You point to the one you hear.

### Polite words with p and b
please, pardon, polite, borrow, behave, believe, beg, both, brave, brother

### Tongue twisters
• Please pass Peter's purple pen.
• Big Ben borrowed both brown books.
• Polite Paul behaves and begs pardon.

Say them slowly at first and speed up as your mouth learns. If you say "big" and "pig" the same way, the meaning changes! "I saw a big pig" is clear only when both sounds are right.

### Dictation
Ask a friend to read these words while you write them: pen, pain, bin, pin, please, borrow. Then check your spelling together.

## PAGE 5: A polite conversation and a poem
### Read this dialogue aloud in pairs
Mark: Good morning, Mrs Wambui.
Mrs Wambui: Good morning, Mark. How are you?
Mark: I am well, thank you. May I borrow a pen, please?
Mrs Wambui: Yes, here you are.
Mark: Thank you very much.
Mrs Wambui: You are welcome.
(Later, Mark bumps into a chair.)
Mark: I am sorry. I did not see you there, Peter.
Peter: That is all right. Be careful.

Notice the polite words: good morning, thank you, may I, please, you are welcome, I am sorry.

### A poem about good manners
Say "please" when you ask,
Say "thank you" when you take.
Say "sorry" when you are wrong,
And a friend you will make.

Say "excuse me" if you pass,
Say "welcome" at the door.
With every kindly word you say,
Your friends will love you more.

### Activity
In a small group, recite the poem together, with actions. Then make up two more lines. Underline the words with /e/, /eɪ/, /p/ or /b/. Finally write three sentences using polite words, for example: "Please may I borrow your book? Thank you for helping me."

## QUIZ
1. Which word is used to ask for something politely?
   A) Give
   B) Please
   C) Hurry
   D) Quickly
   Answer: B
   Why: Please turns an order into a polite request.
2. What do we say when someone thanks us?
   A) You are welcome
   B) Excuse me
   C) I beg your pardon
   D) Congratulations
   Answer: A
   Why: You are welcome is the polite answer to thank you.
3. Which pair of words is a minimal pair for /p/ and /b/?
   A) pen, pain
   B) pin, bin
   C) men, main
   D) wet, wait
   Answer: B
   Why: Pin and bin differ only in the first sound.
4. Which word has the diphthong /eɪ/?
   A) pen
   B) bed
   C) wait
   D) help
   Answer: C
   Why: Wait has /eɪ/ spelt ai. The other words have /e/.
5. Which phrase would you use when you did not hear what someone said?
   A) Congratulations
   B) I beg your pardon
   C) Good evening
   D) You are welcome
   Answer: B
   Why: I beg your pardon is used when we did not hear or understand.
6. Write a polite request to a classmate to borrow a ruler. (2 marks)
   Answer: May I borrow your ruler, please?
   Why: 1 mark for using may I or could I and 1 mark for please.
7. Name three polite words or phrases and say when you use each. (3 marks)
   Answer: Any three, for example: Please, when asking; thank you, when receiving; sorry, when we make a mistake.
   Why: 1 mark for each phrase with its use.
8. Write two minimal pairs, one for /e/ and /eɪ/ and one for /p/ and /b/. (2 marks)
   Answer: For example: pen, pain and pat, bat.
   Why: 1 mark for each correct pair.
9. Your friend wins a prize at school. Write what you say and explain why it is a polite thing to say. (3 marks)
   Answer: "Congratulations!" It shows that we are happy for our friend and respect the effort made.
   Why: 1 mark for the correct phrase and 2 marks for a reason.
