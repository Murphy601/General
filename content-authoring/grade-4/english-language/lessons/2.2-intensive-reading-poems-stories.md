## PAGE 1: Getting ready to read
Intensive reading means reading a short text slowly and carefully so that you understand it well. You read it more than once, think about the words and answer questions. Good readers prepare before they read.

### Before reading
1. Read the title. It tells you what the text is about.
2. Look at the pictures. What do they show?
3. Predict. Guess what will happen or what the poem will say. It does not matter if you are wrong. Guessing wakes up your mind.
4. Think about what you already know. Have you been to a party like this?

### While reading
• Read the text once for the general idea.
• Read it again slowly. Stop at new words. Use the sentences around the word to guess its meaning, or look it up in a dictionary.
• Note the events in the order they happen.

### After reading
• Answer questions. Some questions are direct: the answer is written in the text. Other questions are indirect: the answer is not written, but you can work it out from clues. We call this inference.
• Retell the text in your own words.

Direct question: What time did the guests arrive? Indirect question: Why do you think the girl was excited? Both kinds show that you understand.

## PAGE 2: A poem about a celebration
Read the poem aloud, slowly and with joy. Then read it again.

### The Day of Songs
The sun came up like a golden coin,
And Mama sang while she stirred the pot.
The yard was full of balloons and ribbons,
And the whole house smelt of pilau, hot.

My cousins came in a rattling van,
Grandmother came with a gift in a bag.
The little ones danced on the dusty grass,
While Father waved a bright red flag.

We clapped and laughed and sang out loud,
As we ate and drank until the night.
The moon looked down on our happy home,
And the stars kept watch with silver light.

### Words to check
• ribbons: long thin strips of cloth used to decorate.
• pilau: a spiced rice dish with meat.
• rattling: making a noise as it moves.

### Questions
1. Direct: What was Mama doing when the sun came up?
2. Direct: Who came in the van?
3. Indirect: Was it a happy day? Which words show this?
4. Indirect: Why do you think the moon "looked down" at the end?
Answers: 1. She was singing and stirring the pot. 2. The cousins. 3. Yes, because they clapped, laughed and sang. 4. The party lasted until night, so the moon was already up.

## PAGE 3: Similes
A simile compares two different things using the word "like" or "as". It makes writing and speaking more colourful. The poem says "The sun came up like a golden coin". The sun is not really a coin, but it is round and shiny like one.

### Two patterns
1. as + adjective + as + noun: as brave as a lion.
2. verb + like + noun: ran like the wind.

### Common similes
• as busy as a bee
• as light as a feather
• as quiet as a mouse
• as cold as ice
• as slow as a tortoise
• as tall as a giraffe
• as strong as an ox
• sing like a bird
• sleep like a log

### What does a simile show?
Each simile picks a special quality. A bee is always working, so "as busy as a bee" means very busy. A feather weighs almost nothing, so "as light as a feather" means very light.

### Make your own
Complete the ideas: as bright as ______, as sweet as ______, as loud as ______. Example: as bright as the sun, as sweet as sugar, as loud as thunder.

Use them in sentences: "The birthday cake was as sweet as honey." "The children were as quiet as mice when Grandmother began her story."

Avoid too many similes in one sentence. Use one that fits well, and your reader can picture it easily.

## PAGE 4: Sayings
A saying is a short sentence that people repeat because it holds a piece of wisdom. A proverb is a very old saying. Sayings are found in every language. We often use them to advise or to explain something briefly.

### Some English sayings and their meanings
• Practice makes perfect. The more you do something, the better you become.
• Slow and steady wins the race. Working carefully and continuously is better than rushing.
• Unity is strength. People are stronger when they work together.
• Haste makes waste. If you hurry, you may make mistakes and waste things.
• A stitch in time saves nine. Fix a small problem early so it does not become a big problem.
• Actions speak louder than words. What we do matters more than what we say.

### Using sayings
We can use a saying to end a short story or to give advice.
• "You have started to save a little money each week. Slow and steady wins the race."
• "We cleaned the compound together in ten minutes. Unity is strength."

### Activity
Ask an elder at home for a saying in your mother tongue or in Kiswahili. Write it down, translate it to English if you can and explain what it teaches. Share it with the class. Then compare it with an English saying that gives the same lesson.

## PAGE 5: A story to read intensively
Read the story twice. On the second reading, underline new words and look at the events.

### The Big Day
Neema woke up early. Today was her sister Zawadi's graduation from primary school, and the family was going to celebrate. Neema was as excited as a child on the first day of the holidays.

In the kitchen, Grandmother was frying mandazi. Father was tying blue and white ribbons to the gate. Mother was ironing Zawadi's best dress. Neema helped by arranging the chairs in the yard.

At ten o'clock the family went to the school hall. Zawadi walked on the stage and received her certificate. When her name was read, everyone clapped like thunder. Neema was so proud that she cried a little.

After the ceremony, the family returned home for a feast. Uncle Peter gave Zawadi a present wrapped in gold paper. Inside was a pen and a notebook. "Keep learning," he said. "Practice makes perfect."

That night, Neema wrote in her diary, "I want to work hard like my sister."

### Questions
1. What was the family celebrating?
2. What was Grandmother doing in the kitchen?
3. What did Uncle Peter give Zawadi?
4. Find one simile in the story.
5. Why did Neema cry a little? What does this tell you about her feelings?
6. Which saying did Uncle Peter use, and what does it teach?

Answers: 1. Zawadi's graduation. 2. Frying mandazi. 3. A pen and a notebook. 4. "as excited as a child on the first day of the holidays" or "clapped like thunder". 5. She was proud, so she was moved. 6. "Practice makes perfect": the more you practise, the better you become.

## PAGE 6: Reading together and telling the story
### Readers' theatre
In readers' theatre, a group reads a story aloud like actors, but without costumes. Each person reads a part.

For "The Big Day" you need a narrator, Neema, Grandmother, Father, Mother, Zawadi and Uncle Peter. The narrator reads the story lines. The others read only the words their characters speak. You can also read some lines together, such as the sound of clapping.

### Tips for a good performance
• Practise your lines beforehand. Read them slowly.
• Use your voice to show feelings: happy, proud, shy.
• Speak loudly and clearly.
• Wait for your turn and listen to others.
• Be fair. Everyone in the group should have a turn to read.

### Retell events in order
Put the events of "The Big Day" in the correct order.
1. Neema wakes up early.
2. The family prepares the house.
3. The family goes to the school hall.
4. Zawadi receives her certificate.
5. They return home for a feast.
6. Neema writes in her diary.

Using words such as first, then, next, after that and finally, tell the story to a friend. For example, "First Neema woke up early. Then the family prepared the house."

### Remember
Read the title and pictures, predict, read slowly, use clues to find meaning and answer both direct and indirect questions.

## QUIZ
1. What is a simile?
   A) A short poem
   B) A comparison using like or as
   C) A question with no answer
   D) A word that means the opposite
   Answer: B
   Why: A simile compares two different things with the word like or as.
2. Which is a simile?
   A) The moon is bright.
   B) She was as quiet as a mouse.
   C) We ate pilau.
   D) Practice makes perfect.
   Answer: B
   Why: It compares a person with a mouse using as ... as.
3. What does "Unity is strength" teach?
   A) People are stronger when they work together
   B) Strong people do not need friends
   C) Everyone should work alone
   D) Only leaders are strong
   Answer: A
   Why: The saying teaches that working together makes us stronger.
4. Which question is an indirect question?
   A) Who came in the van?
   B) What did Mama stir?
   C) Why do you think the children were happy?
   D) Where was the party?
   Answer: C
   Why: The answer is not written in the text. We work it out from clues.
5. What should you do before you read a story?
   A) Read the title and look at the pictures
   B) Write the answers
   C) Close the book
   D) Read only the last page
   Answer: A
   Why: The title and pictures help you predict what the story is about.
6. Complete the simile with a suitable word and explain it: "as light as ______". (2 marks)
   Answer: as light as a feather, because a feather weighs almost nothing.
   Why: 1 mark for a suitable comparison and 1 mark for the explanation.
7. Explain the saying "Haste makes waste". (2 marks)
   Answer: If you hurry too much you make mistakes and waste things or time.
   Why: 1 mark for the idea of hurrying and 1 mark for the result of mistakes or waste.
8. From "The Big Day", list the events that happened after the ceremony. (3 marks)
   Answer: The family returned home for a feast, Uncle Peter gave Zawadi a present, and Neema wrote in her diary.
   Why: 1 mark for each event.
9. Write two sentences using a simile of your own about a party. (3 marks)
   Answer: For example: The cake was as sweet as honey. The children danced like leaves in the wind.
   Why: 1 mark for each sentence with a proper simile and 1 mark for a sensible meaning.
