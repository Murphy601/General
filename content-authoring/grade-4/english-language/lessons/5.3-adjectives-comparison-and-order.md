## PAGE 1: Describing words
An adjective is a word that describes a noun. It tells us more about a person, an animal, a place or a thing. Which words do we use to talk about how someone looks or feels? Adjectives! How do we describe things? With adjectives.

### Kinds of description
• Size: big, small, tall, short, long, huge, tiny.
• Shape: round, square, flat, straight, curved.
• Colour: red, green, yellow, brown.
• Feeling or looks: happy, tired, hungry, plump, thin, healthy.
• Opinion: lovely, delicious, ugly, kind, clever.
• Number or amount: three, many, few.

### Where do adjectives go?
1. Before a noun: a ripe mango, a tall boy.
2. After the verb "be" and similar verbs: The mango is ripe. The boy looks tired.

### Try it
Look around the classroom or your kitchen. Describe five things using adjectives. For example: "The chalkboard is big and black." "The cup is small and round."

### Spot the adjectives
"Wanjiku bought a big yellow pawpaw and some sweet oranges. She was hungry and happy." The adjectives are big, yellow, sweet, hungry and happy. Notice that "big yellow" describe the pawpaw, "sweet" describes the oranges and "hungry" and "happy" describe Wanjiku.

## PAGE 2: Comparing things: regular adjectives
We use adjectives to compare two or more things. An adjective has three forms.
• The plain form describes one thing: tall.
• The comparative form compares two things: taller. We usually add "than".
• The superlative form compares three or more things and points to the top one: tallest. We use "the" before it.

### Rules for regular adjectives
1. Short adjectives: add er and est. tall, taller, tallest. small, smaller, smallest. long, longer, longest.
2. Adjectives ending in e: add r and st. nice, nicer, nicest. large, larger, largest.
3. Short adjectives with one vowel and one consonant at the end: double the last letter. big, bigger, biggest. hot, hotter, hottest. thin, thinner, thinnest. fat, fatter, fattest.
4. Adjectives ending in y: change y to i and add er, est. happy, happier, happiest. healthy, healthier, healthiest. heavy, heavier, heaviest.
5. Long adjectives (two or more syllables, not ending in y): use more and most. beautiful, more beautiful, most beautiful. delicious, more delicious, most delicious. careful, more careful, most careful.

### Sentences
• Ben is taller than Ali.
• Chege is the tallest boy in the group.
• This mango is sweeter than that one. This is the sweetest mango.

## PAGE 3: Irregular adjectives
Some adjectives do not follow the rules. Their comparative and superlative forms are quite different. We must learn them.

### The main irregular adjectives
• good, better, best
• bad, worse, worst
• many, more, most (for things we can count)
• much, more, most (for things we cannot count)
• little, less, least
• far, farther (or further), farthest (or furthest)

### Examples
• This is a good meal. That meal is better. The meal at grandmother's house is the best.
• The weather was bad on Monday, worse on Tuesday and worst on Wednesday.
• Kamau has many books. Njeri has more books. Otieno has the most books.
• I drink little milk. He drinks less. She drinks the least.
• The market is far. The bus stage is farther. The hospital is the farthest.

### Mistakes to avoid
• "gooder" and "more good" are wrong. Say better.
• "badder" and "more bad" are wrong. Say worse.
• "more better" is wrong. Say better. Do not use more with the er form.
• "the most tallest" is wrong. Say the tallest.

### Practise
Fill the blanks. (1) Fruit is good for us, but vegetables are ___ (good) for our health. (2) This is the ___ (bad) road in the county. (3) She has ___ (many) sweets than I do. Answers: better, worst, more.

## PAGE 4: Adjectives of size and shape in the right order
Sometimes we use more than one adjective before a noun. We cannot put them in any order. English speakers follow a natural order:
opinion, size, shape, colour, then the noun.

### Examples
• a lovely big round table (opinion, size, shape)
• a small round green apple (size, shape, colour)
• a long thin red carrot (size, shape, colour)
• two big square white boxes
• a tall thin man

### How to check
Size comes before shape. We say "a big round ball", not "a round big ball". Shape comes before colour. We say "a round red tomato", not "a red round tomato". If there is an opinion word such as lovely, ugly or delicious, put it first.

### Examples with food
• a huge round yellow pawpaw
• a small thin brown sausage
• a delicious big red apple
• a sweet round orange

### Put in order
1. (round, small, red) tomato → a small round red tomato
2. (green, long, thin) bean → a long thin green bean
3. (big, lovely, flat) plate → a lovely big flat plate
4. (white, square, large) box → a large square white box

### Speaking practice
In pairs, describe items in your classroom in the right order. "This is a long thin blue ruler." "That is a big square brown desk."

## PAGE 5: Using adjectives in writing
Write sentences using comparison and adjectives in order. Use a comparative when comparing two things and a superlative when comparing three or more.

### Sample paragraph
"At the market Mama bought a big round watermelon and some small red tomatoes. The watermelon was heavier than the pumpkin. It was the heaviest fruit in her basket. The tomatoes were sweeter than the ones from last week. That was the best shopping trip ever."

Find the adjectives: big, round, small, red, heavier, heaviest, sweeter, best.

### Write your own
Describe your school bag, your classroom or a picture using at least five adjectives. Use one comparative and one superlative. For example: "My bag is bigger than Ali's bag, but Amina's bag is the biggest."

### On a device
Type your sentences on a tablet, computer or phone with permission, and share them with a friend. Ask them to underline the adjectives.

### Describe a scene
Look at a picture, a comic or a scene in a book. Describe the characters using how they look and feel. "The plump woman is happy. The thin boy looks hungry."

### Summary
• Adjectives describe nouns.
• Comparative: er or more + than. Superlative: est or most, with the.
• Irregular: good, better, best; bad, worse, worst; many, more, most; little, less, least.
• Order: opinion, size, shape, colour.

## QUIZ
1. What does an adjective do?
   A) It describes a noun
   B) It names a person
   C) It shows action
   D) It joins two sentences
   Answer: A
   Why: Adjectives describe nouns, for example tall boy.
2. What is the comparative form of "big"?
   A) biger
   B) bigger
   C) more big
   D) biggest
   Answer: B
   Why: Big has one vowel and one consonant, so the last letter doubles: bigger.
3. What is the superlative form of "good"?
   A) goodest
   B) gooder
   C) best
   D) better
   Answer: C
   Why: Good is irregular: good, better, best.
4. Which phrase has the adjectives in the correct order?
   A) a round small red ball
   B) a red round small ball
   C) a small round red ball
   D) a round red small ball
   Answer: C
   Why: The order is size, shape, colour.
5. Choose the correct sentence.
   A) This is more better than that.
   B) This is better than that.
   C) This is gooder than that.
   D) This is best than that.
   Answer: B
   Why: The comparative of good is better, and we do not add more.
6. Write the comparative and superlative forms of: happy, beautiful. (2 marks)
   Answer: happier, happiest; more beautiful, most beautiful.
   Why: 1 mark for each adjective with both forms.
7. Complete the table with the missing forms: bad, worse, ___ ; little, ___ , least. (2 marks)
   Answer: worst; less.
   Why: 1 mark for each correct word.
8. Put the adjectives in the right order: a (red, round, big) balloon. Explain your order. (3 marks)
   Answer: a big round red balloon. Size comes first, then shape, then colour.
   Why: 1 mark for the correct phrase and 2 marks for the explanation.
9. Write three sentences about your family or school: one using a comparative, one using a superlative and one using two adjectives in the right order. (3 marks)
   Answer: For example: My brother is taller than I am. He is the tallest boy in his class. He has a big round bag.
   Why: 1 mark for each correct sentence.
