## PAGE 1: What is a dialogue?
A dialogue is a conversation between two or more people. When you read a dialogue, you read the words that the people say to each other. Dialogues are found in stories, plays, comics and lessons. They help us learn how people speak, ask and answer.

### How a dialogue is written
• The name of the speaker comes first, followed by a colon (:).
• Then come the words that the person says.
• A new line starts each time another person speaks.

Example:
Neema: Good morning, Uncle.
Uncle: Good morning, Neema. How are you?

In stories, we sometimes write dialogue with speech marks: "Good morning, Uncle," said Neema. The words inside the marks are the words spoken.

### Useful things to look for
• Greetings and polite expressions: Good morning, please, thank you.
• Questions and answers: What is your name? My name is Neema.
• New words and sentence structures: click here, I will send it, could you show me?
• Events: what happens first, next and last.

### Reading tips for dialogues
1. Read the title and predict what the talk is about.
2. Read it aloud with a partner. Each person takes one role.
3. Use your voice to show feelings, such as surprise or worry.
4. Read again and underline new words.
5. Answer the questions in the text.

## PAGE 2: Dialogue 1 — Sending an email
Read the dialogue with a partner. Achieng is ten years old. Her cousin Baraka is nine. They are at Achieng's home in Kisumu, and her father has allowed them to use his computer.

### Dialogue
Baraka: Achieng, I miss Grandmother. Can we talk to her?
Achieng: We cannot call her now, but we can send her an email.
Baraka: What is an email?
Achieng: It is a message that you type on a computer or a phone. It travels over the internet to the person you write to, in a few seconds.
Baraka: That is quick! How do we start?
Achieng: First, we open Father's email. Then we click on "Compose".
Baraka: I see a box where I must type the address.
Achieng: Yes. That is the email address. It shows where the message must go. Father wrote Grandmother's address on this paper. Type it carefully.
Baraka: Every letter must be right?
Achieng: Yes, one wrong letter and the message will not reach her.
Baraka: Now what do I write?
Achieng: Write a short greeting and your news. Then click "Send".
Baraka: Dear Grandmother, we are well. We miss you. Please come and see us soon. Achieng and Baraka. Now I press "Send"?
Achieng: Wait. Read it again to check the spelling. Now you can press "Send".
Baraka: Done! Will she reply?
Achieng: When she reads it, she can click "Reply" and write back to us. We will see her message in the inbox.

## PAGE 3: Questions on Dialogue 1
Factual questions have answers that are written in the text. Inferential questions need you to think and use clues.

### Factual questions
1. Who wanted to talk to Grandmother?
2. What is an email, according to Achieng?
3. Which button do they click first to write a new message?
4. What did Father write on the paper?
5. Where will they see Grandmother's reply?

Answers: 1. Baraka. 2. A message you type on a computer or phone that travels over the internet. 3. Compose. 4. Grandmother's email address. 5. In the inbox.

### Inferential questions
6. Why did Achieng say "We cannot call her now, but we can send her an email"?
   Answer: A phone call was not possible at that moment, so she offered another way to reach Grandmother.
7. Why must every letter of the address be right?
   Answer: If a letter is wrong, the message will go to the wrong place or not arrive.
8. Why did Achieng tell Baraka to read the message again before pressing "Send"?
   Answer: To find mistakes in spelling so that the message would be correct and clear.
9. How do you think Baraka felt at the end? Give a reason.
   Answer: Happy and excited, because he said "Done!" and was eager to know if Grandmother would reply.

### Put the events in order
a) Baraka types the email address.
b) Achieng explains what an email is.
c) They click on Compose.
d) Baraka presses Send.
Correct order: b, c, a, d.

## PAGE 4: Dialogue 2 — Staying safe online
Read this second dialogue about safety on the internet. Mama and her daughter Neema are talking after school.

### Dialogue
Mama: Neema, why do you look worried?
Neema: A message came on the school computer. It said, "You have won a prize! Click here and give your name and address."
Mama: Did you click on it?
Neema: No. I told my teacher, Mrs Atieno.
Mama: You did well. What did she say?
Neema: She said it may be a trick. Some people send messages to steal information.
Mama: She is right. You must never give your name, address, school or password to someone you do not know.
Neema: What if a friend sends me a message?
Mama: You may answer if you know the person and an adult allows it. But if the message makes you afraid, or asks for secrets, show a grown-up at once.
Neema: I understand. I will also keep my password secret.
Mama: Good. Choose a password that is hard to guess, and do not share it, even with friends.

### Questions
1. What did the message say?
2. Whom did Neema tell?
3. What must a child never give to someone unknown online?
4. Why do some people send such messages? Give a reason from the dialogue.
5. What should Neema do if a message frightens her?

Answers: 1. That she had won a prize and must click and give her name and address. 2. Her teacher, Mrs Atieno. 3. Name, address, school or password. 4. To steal information. 5. Show a grown-up at once.

## PAGE 5: New words, sentence structures and role-play
### New words from the dialogues
• compose: to write a new message.
• address: the place a message is sent to.
• inbox: where received messages are kept.
• reply: to answer a message.
• password: a secret word that only you know, used to open your account.
• trick: something done to cheat someone.
• prize: something you win.

### Useful sentence structures
• Can we + verb...? Can we talk to her?
• We cannot... but we can... We cannot call her, but we can send an email.
• First, ... Then, ... Now you can... (steps in order)
• You must never... You must never share your password.
• What if...? What if a friend sends me a message?

### Work out meaning
Look at the sentence "Some people send messages to steal information." The word "steal" means to take something that does not belong to you. It goes with "trick" in the story, so you can guess the message was a bad one.

### Role-play
In pairs, act out Dialogue 1 and then Dialogue 2. Use expressive voices.
1. Read each part aloud first.
2. Then try to say the lines from memory.
3. Change some words to make your own dialogue: for example, Baraka wants to send an email to his teacher.

### Safety reminders
• Use the internet with a trusted adult.
• Keep your password secret.
• Do not open messages from strangers.
• Tell an adult if something makes you uncomfortable.

### Summary
Dialogues show people talking. To understand them, read aloud, note who says what, find new words and answer factual and inferential questions.

## QUIZ
1. How is a speaker's name usually shown in a written dialogue?
   A) At the start of the line, followed by a colon
   B) At the end in brackets
   C) In the middle
   D) Not shown at all
   Answer: A
   Why: Each line begins with the speaker's name and a colon.
2. In Dialogue 1, which button do Achieng and Baraka click first to write a new message?
   A) Send
   B) Reply
   C) Compose
   D) Save
   Answer: C
   Why: They click "Compose" to start a new message.
3. Why must the email address be typed carefully?
   A) So that the message reaches the right person
   B) So that the computer stays cool
   C) To make the message longer
   D) To change the colour of the screen
   Answer: A
   Why: If one letter is wrong, the message will not reach the person.
4. What does "reply" mean?
   A) To answer a message
   B) To delete a message
   C) To open a computer
   D) To type an address
   Answer: A
   Why: To reply is to answer a message that you received.
5. What should a child do if a message online frightens him or her?
   A) Keep it a secret
   B) Reply quickly
   C) Show a grown-up at once
   D) Give a password
   Answer: C
   Why: A trusted adult can help keep the child safe.
6. List three things a child must never give to a stranger online. (3 marks)
   Answer: Name, home address, school name, password.
   Why: 1 mark for each correct item, up to 3.
7. Why did Achieng ask Baraka to read the message again before sending it? (2 marks)
   Answer: To check the spelling and to make sure the message was correct and clear.
   Why: 1 mark for checking and 1 mark for a reason.
8. Put these events from Dialogue 1 in order: Baraka presses Send, Achieng explains what an email is, they click Compose. (2 marks)
   Answer: Achieng explains what an email is; they click Compose; Baraka presses Send.
   Why: 1 mark for the first correct step and 1 mark for the other two in order.
9. Explain why Neema's teacher said the message might be a trick. (3 marks)
   Answer: The message promised a prize but asked for personal details, and some people send such messages to steal information.
   Why: 1 mark for the prize, 1 mark for personal details and 1 mark for the purpose of stealing information.
