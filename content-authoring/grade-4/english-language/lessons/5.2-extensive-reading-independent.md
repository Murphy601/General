## PAGE 1: What is independent reading?
There are two ways of reading. Intensive reading is slow reading of a short text to understand it deeply. Extensive reading is reading many books, stories, poems and articles for pleasure and for information, usually on your own. When you read on your own, without a teacher telling you what to do, we call it independent reading.

Why should we read widely? The more you read, the better you become at reading. You meet new words. You learn about people, places and ideas. You improve your spelling and writing. You also enjoy wonderful stories. Many of the best writers were people who read a lot when they were young.

### What can we read?
• Narratives: stories about people or animals, such as folktales and adventures.
• Poems: short pieces in lines, often with rhyme and rhythm.
• Newspapers and magazines for children.
• Information books about animals, health, nutrition and science.
• Comics and picture books.
• Electronic texts on a tablet or computer, read with permission.

### Where can we find them?
In the school library, the class reading corner, the community library or at home. Ask your teacher, librarian or parents to help you find good books. Share and exchange books with friends, and always return borrowed books on time and in good condition.

What materials do you enjoy reading? Tell a partner the name of a book you like and why.

## PAGE 2: Choosing a book that suits you
A good independent reading book is not too easy and not too hard. It should interest you.

### The five-finger test
1. Open the book at any page and begin to read.
2. Every time you meet a word you do not know, hold up one finger.
3. At the end of the page count your fingers.
• 0 or 1 finger: the book is easy. It is good for fun and speed.
• 2 or 3 fingers: the book is just right.
• 4 or 5 fingers: the book is too hard for now. Try it again later.

### Other ways of choosing
• Look at the cover and title. Does it make you curious?
• Read the short description on the back.
• Look at the pictures.
• Read the first page. Do you want to continue?
• Ask a friend or teacher for a suggestion.

### Choose by your purpose
• To enjoy: pick a story or a poem.
• To learn: pick an information book or a junior encyclopaedia.
• To find out the news: pick a newspaper or magazine for children.
• To practise speed: pick an easy book you have read before.

### Try it
Go to the reading corner. Select two books and try the five-finger test on each. Which one is right for you? Tell a friend why.

## PAGE 3: How to read on your own
When no one is with you, use these strategies to keep understanding.

### Strategies
• Find a quiet place with good light.
• Read the title and look at the pictures. Predict what will happen.
• Read a few pages at a time. Stop and think: What has happened so far?
• If you meet a new word, read the sentence again. The rest of the sentence can give you a clue. If you still do not understand, write the word down and check it in a dictionary later.
• Picture the story in your mind like a film.
• Read with your eyes only. Try not to move your lips or point at each word, so that you can read faster.
• Use a bookmark so that you can continue easily.

### Read silently
Silent reading means reading quietly in your head. It helps you focus and go at your own speed. Read for about ten to fifteen minutes every day. Little by little your speed and fluency will grow. Fluency means reading smoothly, with few stops.

### Keep a reading log
Write in your notebook after each book:
Title: ________
Author: ________
Date finished: ________
What I liked: ________
New words: ________

A reading log shows you how much you have read and helps you remember.

## PAGE 4: A text to read on your own
Try reading this short passage silently. Then answer the questions from memory before you check.

### A Pot of Vegetables
Mama Zuri grows vegetables behind her house. She has spinach, tomatoes, onions and sukuma wiki. Every morning she waters them before she goes to the market. Her neighbours say that her vegetables are the greenest in the village.

One day a boy called Juma asked, "Mama Zuri, why do you eat so many vegetables?" She smiled and said, "Vegetables give us vitamins. Vitamins protect the body from sickness. When I eat them, I feel light and strong."

Juma went home and asked his mother to add vegetables to his lunch. After a few weeks, he noticed that he was not getting tired at football practice. He also began to plant a small garden of his own, using an old bucket.

### Questions
1. What vegetables does Mama Zuri grow?
2. When does she water them?
3. Why does Mama Zuri say vegetables are good for us?
4. What did Juma do after that?
5. What can you learn from Juma?

Answers: 1. Spinach, tomatoes, onions and sukuma wiki. 2. Every morning before she goes to the market. 3. They give vitamins that protect the body from sickness. 4. He asked for vegetables in his lunch and started a small garden. 5. We can learn to eat healthy food and try new things ourselves.

### Retell
Close the book. Tell the story in your own words using first, then and at the end. Then compare with a partner.

## PAGE 5: Reading clubs and sharing what we read
Reading is more fun when we share it. Many schools have an after-school reading club where learners meet regularly and read different books.

### Setting up a reading club
1. Choose a meeting day and time, for example every Friday after lessons.
2. Choose a place, such as the library or under a tree.
3. Give each member a role: leader, time keeper, book keeper and reporter.
4. Agree on rules: listen politely, take turns and look after the books.
5. Each meeting, everyone reads silently for a while and then shares.

### What to share
• Retell the story you have read: who, where, what happened and how it ended.
• Say what you liked or disliked and why: "I liked the story because it was funny."
• Say if you agree with the ideas in a text. Respect other opinions, even when they are different from yours.
• Read your favourite lines aloud.
• Recommend a book to a friend.

### Sentence starters
• The story is about...
• My favourite part was...
• I learnt that...
• I would like to read more about...

### Why reading matters for life
People who read often learn faster, speak better and understand the world. Reading can help you become anything you would like to be. Choose to read something every day, even for a short time.

### Activity
Plan your class reading club. Give it a name and choose a motto, such as "Read to grow". Write three rules and share them with the class.

## QUIZ
1. What is independent reading?
   A) Reading on your own without being told what to read
   B) Reading only for the teacher
   C) Copying words from a book
   D) Reading aloud in a group only
   Answer: A
   Why: Independent reading is choosing and reading materials on your own.
2. In the five-finger test, what does it mean if you count four or five fingers on a page?
   A) The book is easy
   B) The book is too hard for now
   C) The book is just right
   D) The book has no words
   Answer: B
   Why: Four or five unknown words on a page means the book is too difficult at present.
3. Which is an example of extensive reading?
   A) Reading many story books in your free time
   B) Reading one paragraph over and over for an exam
   C) Writing a letter
   D) Learning a poem by heart
   Answer: A
   Why: Extensive reading means reading widely for pleasure and information.
4. What can you do when you meet a new word while reading alone?
   A) Stop reading forever
   B) Use clues from the sentence and check the dictionary later
   C) Tear the page
   D) Skip the whole book
   Answer: B
   Why: Context clues and a dictionary help you understand new words.
5. What does fluency mean?
   A) Reading smoothly with few stops
   B) Reading very loudly
   C) Reading backwards
   D) Reading only pictures
   Answer: A
   Why: Fluent readers read smoothly and easily.
6. Name three materials you can read for pleasure and information. (3 marks)
   Answer: Any three: story books, poems, newspapers, magazines, comics, information books.
   Why: 1 mark for each correct material.
7. Explain how the five-finger test helps you choose a book. (3 marks)
   Answer: You open a page and count the words you do not know. Few words mean the book suits you. Many words mean it is too hard.
   Why: 1 mark for counting unknown words, 1 mark for the meaning of a few, 1 mark for the meaning of many.
8. Write three things you can share in a reading club. (3 marks)
   Answer: Any three: retell a story, say what you liked, read favourite lines aloud, recommend a book, share new words.
   Why: 1 mark for each.
9. Explain why reading widely helps a person to succeed in school. (2 marks)
   Answer: Reading widely builds vocabulary, improves spelling, speed and understanding, and gives knowledge for all subjects.
   Why: 1 mark for a benefit of reading and 1 mark for linking it to learning.
