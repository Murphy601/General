## PAGE 1: Sounds are not the same as letters
When we speak, we make sounds. When we write, we use letters. Sometimes one letter can make different sounds, and sometimes two letters make one sound. To speak clearly, we must listen to the sound first and then copy it with our mouth.

In this lesson we practise four sounds that many learners mix up: /t/, /d/, /f/ and /v/. We also practise the letter pair "tw" as in twin. A sound is written between slanted lines, like /t/, to show that we mean the sound and not the letter name.

Try this test. Put your fingers gently on your throat. Say "sss" and then "zzz". With "zzz" you feel a buzz. That buzz is your voice. Sounds with a buzz are called voiced sounds. Sounds with no buzz are whispered sounds.

### The four sounds
• /t/ is whispered. Examples: ten, table, mat.
• /d/ is voiced. Examples: den, dinner, mad.
• /f/ is whispered. Examples: fan, family, leaf.
• /v/ is voiced. Examples: van, visit, five.

Why does it matter? If you say "den" when you mean "ten", your listener may not understand you. Clear speech helps us share ideas and shows respect for the people who listen.

## PAGE 2: /t/ and /d/
To say /t/ and /d/, touch the tip of your tongue on the bumpy ridge just behind your top front teeth. Then let the tongue drop quickly. For /t/ you push out a small puff of air and make no buzz. For /d/ you make the same shape but add your voice.

Hold a strip of paper in front of your mouth. It should move a little when you say "ten". It should move much less when you say "den".

### Minimal pairs
A minimal pair is two words that are different in only one sound. Say each pair slowly and listen to the difference.
• tin, din
• ten, den
• town, down
• tie, die
• two, do
• tip, dip

The sounds can also come at the end of a word.
• bat, bad
• mat, mad
• bet, bed
• hat, had
• cart, card
• seat, seed

### Practise
Say each pair three times. Ask a friend to say one word from a pair, without showing you which. Point to the word you heard. Swap places.

Tongue twister: Ten tired tigers took Tom's tiny toys. Then say: Dina's dad did a dance in the dark. Start slowly and speed up only when you make no mistakes.

## PAGE 3: /f/ and /v/
To say /f/ and /v/, gently touch your top teeth to your lower lip and blow air out. For /f/ you only blow air, so there is no buzz. For /v/ you add your voice, so your lip and throat tickle.

### Minimal pairs
• fan, van
• fine, vine
• fairy, very
• few, view
• ferry, very
• fast, vast

At the end of a word, the difference is important because it can change the meaning.
• leaf, leave
• safe, save
• half, halve
• proof, prove
• belief, believe

Notice that the word leaf ends with /f/, so it is a whispered sound at the end. The verb leave ends with /v/. Do not skip the last sound. Say "leave" fully, not "lee".

### Say and write
Read these sentences aloud. Listen for /f/ and /v/.
• My family visited a farm five times.
• Vera fetched fresh vegetables for the feast.
• The van was full of fruit.

Tongue twister: Five fat frogs fried five fine fish.

## PAGE 4: The tw sound
The letters "tw" together make a quick blend. Say /t/ and then slide straight into a "w" sound, as if you were saying "tuh-w" very fast. Words that start with tw often have something to do with two of something.

### Words with tw
• twin: one of two children born at the same time to the same mother.
• twelve: the number 12.
• twenty: the number 20.
• twice: two times.
• twist: to turn something around.
• twig: a thin small branch of a tree.
• tweet: the short high sound of a small bird.
• twinkle: to shine with quick little flashes of light.
• between: in the middle of two things. Here tw is in the middle of the word.

Use them in sentences. My twin sisters are twelve years old. The stars twinkle at night. A bird tweeted on a twig.

### Tongue twisters
• Twin Tom twists ten twigs.
• Twelve twins twirl twice between two trees.

Say each one slowly three times. Then try for speed and clear sounds. If you stumble, start again. It is good to stumble in practice, because that is how your mouth learns.

## PAGE 5: Family words and how to use them
The theme of this unit is the family. A family is a group of people who are related by blood, marriage or adoption and who care for one another. Here are useful words. Read the meaning and then say the word aloud, paying attention to the target sounds.

### Vocabulary
• relative: a person who belongs to your family, such as an aunt or a cousin.
• sibling: a brother or a sister.
• care-giver: a person who looks after a child or a sick person.
• orphan: a child whose parents have both died.
• widow: a woman whose husband has died.
• clan: a large group of families who share the same ancestor.
• ancestor: a person in your family who lived long before your grandparents.
• mother-in-law: the mother of your husband or wife.
• father-in-law: the father of your husband or wife.
• sister-in-law: the sister of your husband or wife, or the wife of your brother.
• adopt: to take a child into your family and raise the child as your own.
• elder: older than someone else, for example your elder brother.
• chore: a small job you do regularly at home, such as sweeping.

## PAGE 6: Listening comprehension and using a dictionary
### Checking pronunciation in a dictionary
A dictionary shows how to say a word. Look beside the word for letters between slanted lines, like /twɪn/ for twin. Online dictionaries also have a small speaker button. Press it, listen carefully, and repeat. Then say the word inside a whole sentence, because real speaking happens in sentences.

### Listening passage (your teacher or a friend reads it aloud)
The Otieno family lives near Kisumu. Mr Otieno drives a van, and Mrs Otieno sells fruit at the market. They have five children. The twins, Vera and Victor, are twelve years old. Their elder brother Daniel is fifteen, and their little sister Faith is four. On Saturdays every person does a chore. Daniel feeds the chickens. Vera and Victor wash the dishes. Faith puts the spoons on the table. In the evening their grandfather visits and tells stories about their ancestors. The children love those stories because they are funny and full of surprises.

### Listen and answer
1. Where does the Otieno family live?
2. What does Mrs Otieno sell?
3. How old are the twins?
4. Who tells stories in the evening?

Now retell the passage to a partner in your own sentences, using at least three words from this lesson.

## QUIZ
1. Which pair of words differs only in the first sound, /t/ and /d/?
   A) ten, den
   B) fan, van
   C) leaf, leave
   D) twin, twelve
   Answer: A
   Why: Ten begins with /t/ and den begins with /d/. The other pairs use /f/ and /v/ or are different words.
2. Which sound is voiced, meaning you feel a buzz in your throat?
   A) /t/
   B) /f/
   C) /v/
   D) /p/
   Answer: C
   Why: /v/ uses the voice. /t/ and /f/ are whispered sounds.
3. Which word ends with the /v/ sound?
   A) leaf
   B) safe
   C) half
   D) leave
   Answer: D
   Why: Leave ends with /v/. Leaf, safe and half end with /f/.
4. What is a sibling?
   A) A brother or a sister
   B) A mother's mother
   C) A child with no parents
   D) A large group of families
   Answer: A
   Why: Siblings are brothers and sisters. An orphan has lost both parents and a clan is a large group of families.
5. Which word starts with the blend "tw"?
   A) three
   B) twelve
   C) trip
   D) tell
   Answer: B
   Why: Twelve begins with t and w together. The others begin with th, tr and t.
6. Write two minimal pairs that use /f/ and /v/. (2 marks)
   Answer: Any two correct pairs, for example fan, van and safe, save.
   Why: 1 mark for each pair in which the two words differ only in /f/ and /v/.
7. Explain what a minimal pair is and give one example. (3 marks)
   Answer: A minimal pair is two words that differ in only one sound, such as tin and din.
   Why: 1 mark for the idea of two words, 1 mark for only one sound different, 1 mark for a correct example.
8. Use "orphan", "twin" and "chore" in three separate sentences about a family. (3 marks)
   Answer: Any three correct sentences, for example: An orphan is cared for by a relative. My twin sister and I share a chore. It is my chore to sweep.
   Why: 1 mark for each sentence that uses the word correctly.
9. Say why it is important to pronounce the last sound of the word "leave". (2 marks)
   Answer: Without the /v/ sound at the end, the listener may hear "lee" or think we said "leaf", so the message may be misunderstood.
   Why: 1 mark for saying the meaning can change, 1 mark for naming the last sound /v/.
