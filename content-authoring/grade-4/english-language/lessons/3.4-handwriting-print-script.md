## PAGE 1: Why neat writing matters
Writing is how we send our thoughts to other people who cannot hear us. If a reader cannot read your writing, your message is lost. Think about how you feel when you cannot read a friend's note. You may feel confused or annoyed. Your reader will feel the same way about your work.

Legible writing is writing that can be read easily. Neat writing is clean, tidy and pleasant to look at. In Grade 4 you write in print script, which means separate letters, each written on its own.

### Getting ready to write
• Sit with your back straight and your feet flat on the floor.
• Hold the pencil between your thumb and first finger, and rest it on your middle finger. Do not squeeze hard.
• Place the book at a slight angle so that you can see the line you write.
• Use a pencil first. When your letters are steady and neat, your teacher will let you use a pen.
• Keep your book clean and your pencil sharp.

### What makes writing neat?
1. Letters of the correct size and shape.
2. Even spacing between letters and between words.
3. Letters sitting on the line.
4. Correct spelling.
5. No smudges or torn pages.

## PAGE 2: Letter size and height
Letters live on lines like people live in houses. Some are tall, some are short and some hang down.

### Three kinds of letters
• Tall letters reach the top line: b, d, f, h, k, l, t. All capital letters are tall too.
• Short letters stay in the middle: a, c, e, m, n, o, r, s, u, v, w, x, z.
• Tail letters hang below the line: g, j, p, q, y.

The letters i and j have a dot above them. The letters t and f have a crossbar. Put the dot and the crossbar in the right place: not too high and not touching the next letter.

### Use the same size every time
Each letter of the same kind should be the same height. Do not write some 'a's big and some small. When all letters are even, the word looks tidy.

### Spaces
• Leave a small gap between letters when you print.
• Leave a gap between words. A good gap is about the size of one small letter 'o' or a finger width.
• Leave a line at the end of a paragraph, or begin a new line with a small indent.

### Capital letters
Use them at the start of a sentence, for the names of people and places, and for the days and months. Write them full height, without curls.

### Try it
Copy this sentence three times, watching the size and spacing: Kamau kept a tall kite.

## PAGE 3: b and d, p and q
Some letters look alike, and we may mix them up. The most confusing are b and d, and p and q. The shapes are the same, but the direction is different.

### Memory tips
• b: stick first, then the round part on the right. Think of a bat and a ball: b.
• d: round part first, then the stick on the right. Think of a drum and a stick.
• p: the stick goes down below the line, and the round part sits on the right at the top.
• q: the round part is on the left, and the stick goes down on the right.

### Practise
Write a row of each letter. Then write these words: bed, did, bad, dad, pad, pop, quit, quiz.

Read them aloud and check that b and d face the correct way.

## PAGE 4: Silent letters
Some words have a letter that we write but do not say. We call it a silent letter. If you do not know it is there, you may forget it when you spell.

### Common silent letters
• k before n: know, knee, knife, knock
• w before r: write, wrong, wrist, wrap
• b after m: lamb, comb, climb, thumb
• h at the start: hour, honest
• l before k or f: walk, talk, half
• t in some words: listen, castle
• g before n: gnat, sign

### Practise with polite sentences
Copy carefully and circle the silent letters.
• Please listen.
• I beg your pardon.
• May I borrow your comb?
• Write your name here.
• I know an honest man.

### Tips to remember
Say the word slowly, pronouncing every letter, as you copy it: "k-nife", "w-rite". This helps your memory. Then say it the right way. When you are unsure, look it up in a dictionary or on a device that has a dictionary. Type the word and check the spelling.

## PAGE 5: Blends and double consonants
Many words have two or three consonants together, and each makes its own sound. We call them blends. Write each letter clearly so that no letter is missing.

### Common blends
• bl: blue, black, blanket
• br: brown, bread, bridge
• cl: clap, class, clean
• dr: drink, drum, dress
• fl: flag, flower, floor
• gr: green, grass, grow
• pl: plate, play, plant
• sn: snake, snail, snack
• st: stand, stone, stop
• tr: tree, train, truck
• tw: twin, twelve, twenty

### Double consonants
Some words have the same consonant twice. Both letters must be written, but we say the sound only once.
• dinner, letter, butter, summer, happy, funny
• apple, bottle, ribbon, yellow, hello
• grass, class, address, bell, doll

Spelling tip: after a short vowel sound, many words double the consonant before an ending, for example: hop → hopping, sit → sitting.

### Write it neatly
Copy this passage in your best print script. Watch your letter size, your spaces and your spelling.
"A funny little snake sat in the grass. It saw a yellow butterfly and a green apple on the bridge."

### Check your work
1. Are all letters sitting on the line?
2. Are the tall letters and tail letters the right length?
3. Are there spaces between the words?
4. Are all blends and double letters present?
5. Are the silent letters there?
When you are proud of your writing, sign your name in neat letters at the bottom.

## QUIZ
1. Which of these letters is a tail letter?
   A) m
   B) p
   C) t
   D) o
   Answer: B
   Why: P has a tail that hangs below the line. M, t and o do not.
2. Which letter is silent in the word "knife"?
   A) k
   B) n
   C) f
   D) e
   Answer: A
   Why: We say "nife", so the k is silent.
3. Which word has a double consonant?
   A) stop
   B) letter
   C) plate
   D) drink
   Answer: B
   Why: Letter has two t letters in the middle.
4. Which correctly describes the letter d?
   A) The round part is on the right and the stick is on the left
   B) The round part is first, on the left, and the stick is on the right
   C) It has a tail below the line
   D) It has a dot on top
   Answer: B
   Why: A d has its round part on the left and its tall stick on the right.
5. What do we use first when we start to learn neat writing?
   A) A felt pen
   B) A pencil
   C) A marker
   D) A brush
   Answer: B
   Why: We begin with a pencil, so mistakes can be rubbed out, and use a pen when writing is steady.
6. Copy this sentence neatly, keeping the silent letters: "I know a wise man." (2 marks)
   Answer: I know a wise man.
   Why: 1 mark for the silent k and 1 mark for neat spacing and correct spelling.
7. List three things that make handwriting easy to read. (3 marks)
   Answer: Any three: letters of the correct size, letters sitting on the line, even gaps between words, correct spelling, tidy page.
   Why: 1 mark for each correct point.
8. Write four words with silent letters and circle the silent letter in each. (4 marks)
   Answer: For example: know (k), write (w), lamb (b), hour (h).
   Why: 1 mark for each word with its silent letter correctly identified.
9. Explain how you can remember the difference between b and d. (2 marks)
   Answer: For b, write the stick first and put the round part on its right, like a bat and ball. For d, make the round part first and then the stick on the right.
   Why: 1 mark for each letter described correctly.
