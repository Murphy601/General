## PAGE 1: Two more punctuation marks
You already know that a sentence begins with a capital letter and often ends with a full stop. Two other marks help us to write clearly:
• the comma ( , ), which tells the reader to make a short pause
• the question mark ( ? ), which shows that a sentence asks something

Compare these:
"Let's eat Grandma."
"Let's eat, Grandma."
In the first sentence it seems we might eat Grandma! The comma in the second sentence shows that we are talking to her. A tiny mark can change the meaning.

### Why punctuate?
Punctuation marks help the reader to read your sentences the way you meant them. When you speak, your voice goes up or down and you pause. In writing, punctuation does this job.

### Where do we see them?
• In your storybooks: commas in lists and dialogue, and question marks after questions.
• On signs: "Where is the clinic?"
• In letters, messages and emails.

### Try it
Look at a page of your reading book. Find one comma and one question mark. Say why each one is used.

## PAGE 2: Commas in lists
Use commas to separate three or more items in a list. Put "and" before the last item.

### Examples
• We bought tomatoes, onions, kale and carrots.
• Baraka, Neema and Omondi played football.
• She put on her shoes, picked up her bag and left.
Notice that there is no comma before "and" in these examples. (Some writers use one, called a serial comma. Follow your teacher's style.)

### Commas with describing words
When two or more describing words come before a noun, we separate them with commas.
• It was a tall, green tree.
• A cold, wet, windy day.
But if two words are used together as a unit, no comma: "a big red bus" is usual without a comma.

### Common mistake
Do not use a comma between only two items joined by "and".
• Wrong: I like rice, and beans.
• Right: I like rice and beans.

### Practice
Punctuate: "For lunch we ate rice beans and cabbage."
Answer: For lunch we ate rice, beans and cabbage.

### Days and dates
Use a comma in dates: "Monday, 5 May". In addresses, use commas between lines when written across: "Kisumu, Kenya".

## PAGE 3: More uses of the comma
### After words like Yes, No and Well
• Yes, I am ready.
• No, thank you.
• Well, that is a good idea.
• Oh, I see.

### When you speak to someone (addressing)
• Mama, may I go out?
• Thank you, Mr. Otieno.
• Come here, please, Achieng.

### After an opening word or phrase
• In the morning, we do exercises.
• Yesterday, my uncle visited us.
• After lunch, we went to the field.
The comma marks the pause after the introduction. When the phrase is very short, the comma is not always needed.

### Before a spoken sentence
• Mother said, "Please close the door."
• The teacher asked, "Are you ready?"
The comma comes after the word said or asked and before the quotation marks.

### Joining two sentences with "but" or "so"
When you join two full sentences with but, so, or, put a comma before the joining word.
• I wanted to go, but it was raining.
• The shop was closed, so we went home.

### Practice
Punctuate: "Yes Mary I will come after lunch." Answer: Yes, Mary, I will come after lunch.

## PAGE 4: The question mark
A question asks something and needs an answer. We put a question mark at the end.

### Examples
• What is your name?
• Where do you live?
• Did you eat breakfast?
• Is the clinic open today?
• How many books have you read?

### Question words
who, what, when, where, why, which, whose, how
Many questions begin with a helping verb: Is, Are, Do, Does, Did, Can, Will.

### Do not use a question mark after a statement
• Wrong: I asked where the clinic is?
• Right: I asked where the clinic is.
When we tell what someone asked, it is a statement, so we use a full stop.
• Direct question: "Where is the clinic?" she asked.
• Reported: She asked where the clinic was.

### Never combine
Use one question mark. Not two. "What is it?" is enough.

### Tone of voice
When you read a question aloud, your voice usually goes up at the end. Practise:
• "Are you coming?" (voice goes up)
• "I am coming." (voice goes down)

### Practice
Add the correct end punctuation:
1. What time does the bus leave (?)
2. The bus leaves at nine (.)
3. Can you help me carry this sack (?)

## PAGE 5: Choosing the right mark
### Statement, question or command?
• Statement: tells something. End with a full stop. "The market opens at six."
• Question: asks something. End with a question mark. "When does the market open?"
• Command: tells someone to do something. Usually a full stop. "Come early."
• Exclamation: shows strong feeling. End with an exclamation mark. "What a big crowd!"

### Punctuation card game
Make cards: commas, full stops, question marks, capital letters. Give a group a sentence without punctuation and ask them to place the cards.
Example: "when did you arrive yesterday" becomes "When did you arrive yesterday?"
"mary tom and grace went home" becomes "Mary, Tom and Grace went home."

### Read the passage and correct it
"is the clinic near the market yes it is near the market we can walk there mama do you want to come"

Corrected: Is the clinic near the market? Yes, it is near the market. We can walk there. Mama, do you want to come?

### Check
Number of commas used: 2 (after Yes, after Mama). Number of question marks: 2.

### Typing
On a tablet or phone, the punctuation marks may be on a second keyboard page. Take care to place them right after the word without a space before, and a space after.

## PAGE 6: Practice and summary
### Write correctly
1. what is your favourite fruit
   What is your favourite fruit?
2. I bought books pens and rulers
   I bought books, pens and rulers.
3. no I have not finished
   No, I have not finished.
4. peter did you feed the goats
   Peter, did you feed the goats?
5. we can play outside but it may rain
   We can play outside, but it may rain.

### Write your own
Write a short conversation of four lines between two friends. Include at least two questions and at least two commas.
Example:
"Amina, are you going to the market?"
"Yes, I am."
"What will you buy?"
"I will buy maize, beans and tomatoes."

### Summary
• A comma separates items in a list and marks small pauses.
• Use commas after Yes/No, when addressing a person, and before but or so between sentences.
• A question mark ends a direct question.
• Do not use a question mark after a reported question.
• Always read your work aloud to check the pauses.

## QUIZ
1. Which sentence is punctuated correctly?
   A) We bought rice beans and maize.
   B) We bought rice, beans and maize.
   C) We bought, rice beans and maize.
   D) We bought rice beans, and maize
   Answer: B
   Why: Commas separate the items in the list; "and" comes before the last one.
2. Which sentence needs a question mark?
   A) I like apples
   B) Please close the door
   C) Where is the school
   D) We went home
   Answer: C
   Why: "Where is the school" is a question.
3. Which sentence is correct?
   A) Yes I will help.
   B) Yes, I will help.
   C) Yes I, will help.
   D) Yes I will, help.
   Answer: B
   Why: A comma follows the word "Yes".
4. Which sentence should end with a full stop, not a question mark?
   A) She asked where the clinic was
   B) Where is the clinic
   C) What is her name
   D) Are you ready
   Answer: A
   Why: It reports a question instead of asking one, so it is a statement.
5. Punctuate: "mama may i go to the shop" (2 marks)
   Answer: Mama, may I go to the shop?
   Why: One mark for the comma and capitals, one mark for the question mark.
6. Punctuate this list sentence: "We planted maize beans and sorghum." (2 marks)
   Answer: We planted maize, beans and sorghum.
   Why: One mark for each comma.
7. Explain when to use a comma, giving two examples. (3 marks)
   Answer: Use a comma to separate items in a list ("rice, beans and cabbage"), after Yes or No ("Yes, I can") and when addressing a person ("Mama, come here").
   Why: One mark for each correct use with an example (maximum 3).
8. Write a short conversation of four lines between two friends. Include two questions and use commas correctly. (4 marks)
   Answer: Any conversation with two correctly punctuated questions and correct commas.
   Why: One mark for each question and one mark for each correct comma use.
