## PAGE 1: How to read a narrative closely
Intensive reading means reading a text carefully to understand it fully. You read slowly, think about the meaning and answer questions. In this lesson you will read a narrative of about 320 words.

### Before you read
1. Read the title: "The Barber's New Blade". What might the story be about?
2. Look at the first line and last line.
3. Think about what you already know about the theme: HIV and AIDS.

### While you read
• Read one paragraph at a time.
• Underline words you do not know.
• Ask yourself: Who is in the story? Where are they? What is happening?
• Look for the order of events.

### After you read
• Answer questions in full sentences.
• Find the meanings of new words.
• Say what the story teaches.

### Why do we read narratives?
Stories help us to understand other people's feelings. They show us how people solve problems and they teach lessons in a memorable way.

### Facts to keep in mind
HIV is a virus that weakens the body's defence against illness. AIDS is the illness that can develop when HIV has not been treated. A person can live a long and healthy life with HIV if they take the right medicine from a health worker. Learning the facts helps us care for one another and stay safe.

## PAGE 2: The story, part 1
Read carefully. The story is in two parts.

The Barber's New Blade

On Saturday morning, Baraka's father took him to Mzee Juma's barbershop in the market. Baraka was excited. He was getting his first haircut of the school holidays.

As they waited, Baraka noticed something on the shelf. Mzee Juma opened a small packet and put a new blade into his razor. "Why do you use a new blade?" asked Baraka.

"Good question," the barber said kindly. "Blood can carry germs. Some germs, like the virus that causes HIV, can pass from one person's blood to another if a sharp object is shared. So I use a new blade for every customer, and I clean my tools carefully."

Baraka's father nodded. "That is why I brought you here. This barber cares about his customers."

After the haircut, Baraka walked home. On the way, he saw his classmate Omondi standing alone at the edge of the football field. Baraka remembered that the other boys were saying that Omondi's mother had HIV. Some children refused to play with him.

"Omondi looks sad," Baraka thought.

### Check your understanding
1. Where did Baraka go on Saturday? (to Mzee Juma's barbershop in the market)
2. Why does the barber use a new blade for every customer? (Blood can carry germs, and sharing sharp objects can spread them.)
3. How did Baraka feel about the haircut? (excited)

## PAGE 3: The story, part 2
Baraka walked over to Omondi. "Why aren't you playing?" he asked.

"Nobody wants me on their team," Omondi said quietly. "They say I will make them ill because of my mother."

Baraka shook his head. "That is not true. Mwalimu Sarah told us that you cannot get HIV by playing, shaking hands, sharing a plate or hugging someone. It passes through blood, and from a mother to her baby, and in a few other ways adults will learn about later."

"Really?" Omondi asked.

"Yes. And my father told me that people with HIV can stay healthy if they take medicine every day. Your mother can still cook, work and laugh."

Omondi's face lit up. "She does. She is at the clinic today to get her medicine."

Baraka smiled. "Come and play. You can be on my team."

When the other boys saw them, some were surprised. "Are you sure?" asked one boy.

"I am," said Baraka. "Come, I will explain." He told them what Mwalimu Sarah had taught. One by one, the boys nodded and made space for Omondi.

That evening, Omondi ran home with a smile. "Mama, I scored a goal!" he shouted.

His mother hugged him. "I am proud of you, and I am proud of your friend Baraka too."

### Check your understanding
4. Why was Omondi alone? (The other children refused to play with him because his mother had HIV.)
5. What did Baraka know that the other boys did not? (HIV cannot be caught by playing, hugging or sharing a plate.)
6. Where was Omondi's mother that day? (at the clinic getting her medicine)

## PAGE 4: Words and meanings
Find each word in the story and work out its meaning from the words around it.

• blade: the thin, sharp piece of metal in a razor
• customer: a person who buys something or uses a service
• virus: a tiny germ that can make a person ill
• sharp object: a thing that can cut or pierce the skin
• medicine: something that helps fight illness
• clinic: a place where people get health care
• refused: said no to doing something

### Word study
The story uses the word "refused" in "Some children refused to play with him." The word "refused" suggests that the children said no on purpose.

### Using words in sentences
1. "The barber changes the blade for every customer."
2. "She took her medicine at the clinic."
3. "The boys refused to share the football."

### Prefixes and suffixes
• customer: from custom + er (a person who does something)
• careful: care + ful (full of care)
• carefully: careful + ly (in a careful way)
• kindly: kind + ly (in a kind way)
Finding the base word can help you understand new words.

### Try it
Find two describing words in the story and two action words. Examples: excited, sad; nodded, smiled.

## PAGE 5: Factual and inferential questions
There are two main kinds of comprehension questions.

### Factual questions
The answer is stated in the text. You can point to the line.
• What did the barber put into his razor? (A new blade.)
• Who told Baraka that HIV is not spread by playing? (Mwalimu Sarah.)
• Who was standing alone at the edge of the football field? (Omondi.)

### Inferential questions
The answer is not stated, but you work it out from clues.
• How did Omondi feel at the start? (Sad and lonely. Clue: he stood alone and spoke quietly.)
• Was Baraka a good friend? (Yes. He went to Omondi, explained the facts and invited him to play.)
• Why did Omondi's face light up? (He felt accepted, and he was glad someone believed his mother was fine.)

### How to answer
1. Find the part of the text the question is about.
2. For factual questions, copy the key words into a sentence.
3. For inferential questions, use clues and say "because".
4. Write a full sentence.

### The order of events
Put these in order:
(a) Omondi scored a goal. (b) Baraka went to the barber. (c) Baraka explained the facts. (d) Baraka saw Omondi alone.
Correct order: (b), (d), (c), (a).

## PAGE 6: The lesson and what to do
### What does the story teach?
• Use facts, not rumours.
• Do not leave someone out because of their family's illness.
• Sharp objects should never be shared: use clean, new ones.
• Speaking up kindly can change people's minds.

### Stay safe
Do not touch blood or sharp objects that are not yours. If you find a needle or razor blade, do not touch it. Tell a trusted adult. Only a health worker can give a test or medicine. If you are worried about your health, talk to a teacher, a parent or a health worker.

### Written practice
Write three sentences about how you can be a good friend to someone who is sad. Use the words "kind", "listen" and "include".
Example: "I can be kind to a sad friend. I will listen to what he says. I will include him in our games."

### Summary
• Read the title, think, read closely, then answer.
• Factual questions have answers in the text; inferential questions need clues.
• Look at events in order.
• Use new vocabulary in your own sentences.

## QUIZ
1. Why does the barber use a new blade for each customer?
   A) It looks better
   B) Sharing blades can pass germs through blood
   C) Blades are free
   D) The customers ask for it
   Answer: B
   Why: The barber explained that sharing sharp objects can spread germs in blood.
2. Why was Omondi standing alone?
   A) He was tired
   B) He had lost his shoes
   C) Other children would not play with him because of his mother's illness
   D) He did not like football
   Answer: C
   Why: The story says some children refused to play with him.
3. Which of these can you do without any risk of getting HIV?
   A) Share a razor blade
   B) Share a plate of food
   C) Touch a needle you found
   D) Use a used blade
   Answer: B
   Why: The story says HIV is not spread by sharing a plate. Sharing blades or touching needles is unsafe.
4. What does the word "customer" mean?
   A) A person who sells goods
   B) A person who buys something or uses a service
   C) A teacher
   D) A doctor
   Answer: B
   Why: In the story, people who visit the barber are customers.
5. Give two facts from the story about how HIV cannot spread. (2 marks)
   Answer: It cannot spread by playing, shaking hands, sharing a plate or hugging.
   Why: One mark for each correct fact.
6. What kind of person is Baraka? Give one reason from the story. (3 marks)
   Answer: He is kind and brave, because he went to talk to Omondi, explained the facts and invited him to join the team.
   Why: One mark for the quality, two marks for the evidence.
7. Put the events in order: (a) Omondi scored a goal. (b) Baraka spoke to Omondi. (c) The barber changed the blade. (3 marks)
   Answer: (c), (b), (a)
   Why: One mark for each correct position.
8. Write two sentences to say what you learnt from the story. (2 marks)
   Answer: For example: I learnt that people with HIV should not be left out. I learnt that we should not share sharp objects.
   Why: One mark for each correct lesson.
