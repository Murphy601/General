## PAGE 1: Asking questions
We ask questions to find out information. Some questions can be answered with "yes" or "no": "Is the match today?" But many questions need more information, and we start them with special words called interrogatives, or question words.

This lesson covers five of them:
• when: asks about time
• where: asks about place
• whose: asks who owns something
• who: asks about a person
• what: asks about a thing, an idea or an action

### Each question word tells what kind of answer to give
• "When is the match?" — "It is on Friday." (a time)
• "Where is the match?" — "It is at the school field." (a place)
• "Whose ball is this?" — "It is Achieng's." (an owner)
• "Who is the captain?" — "Mercy is the captain." (a person)
• "What is the referee holding?" — "He is holding a whistle." (a thing)

### End with a question mark
Every direct question ends with a question mark (?).

### Why do we ask questions?
Questions help us learn, check facts, find things and take part in conversations. Good questions show interest and help us to understand.

### Try it
With a partner, ask each other three questions using different question words. Listen for the type of answer each one needs.

## PAGE 2: When and where
### When (time)
Answers include: at five o'clock, on Monday, in June, yesterday, after lunch, next week.
• When does the match start? It starts at ten o'clock.
• When did you last play netball? I played last Friday.
• When will the team travel? It will travel tomorrow morning.

### Where (place)
Answers include: at school, in the classroom, near the river, on the field, to the stadium.
• Where is the stadium? It is near the town.
• Where do you keep your kit? I keep it in my bag.
• Where did they play? They played on the school field.

### Sentence pattern
question word + helping verb + subject + main verb?
• When + will + the team + travel?
• Where + did + they + play?
Notice that the helping verb (is, are, do, does, did, will, can) comes before the subject in a question.

### Compare statements and questions
• The match starts at ten. → When does the match start?
• They played at the stadium. → Where did they play?

### Note about "does" and "did"
After does or did, use the base form of the main verb.
• "When does she practise?" (not "practises")
• "Where did he go?" (not "went")

### Practice
Make a question with when or where.
1. The race is on Saturday. (When is the race?)
2. The coach is in the office. (Where is the coach?)

## PAGE 3: Who and whose
### Who (asks about a person)
Who can be the subject: "Who scored the goal?" — "Kamau scored the goal." Who is followed straight by the verb.
• Who is the referee? Mr. Otieno is the referee.
• Who won the race? Wanjiru won the race.
• Who coaches the netball team? Mrs. Akinyi coaches it.
• Who are the captains? Mercy and Peter are the captains.

### Whose (asks about ownership)
Whose is followed by a noun. The answer often includes a name with 's or a possessive word such as mine, hers or theirs.
• Whose boots are these? They are Peter's.
• Whose bag is under the bench? It is mine.
• Whose turn is it to bowl? It is hers.
• Whose team is winning? Ours is winning.

### Do not confuse who's and whose
• who's = who is or who has: "Who's the captain?" (Who is the captain?)
• whose = belonging to whom: "Whose ball is this?"
Try replacing with "who is". If it makes sense, use who's.

### Practice
Fill in who, whose, or who's.
1. ____ is coming to practice? (Who)
2. ____ shoes are these? (Whose)
3. ____ the coach? (Who's)
4. ____ won the match? (Who)

## PAGE 4: What
"What" asks about a thing, a fact, an idea or an action.

### Examples
• What is your favourite game? My favourite game is football.
• What do you wear when you play? I wear shorts and a jersey.
• What did the referee say? He said the match had ended.
• What is a goal? It is a point scored when the ball goes into the net.
• What happened? Our team scored.

### What with a noun
• What game do you like? I like netball.
• What time is it? It is half past nine.
• What colour is your kit? It is blue.

### Other question words
There are more question words: why (reason), which (choice), how (way, or amount) and how many. You will meet them in later lessons, but you already know they follow the same pattern.
• Why do we practise? Because it helps us improve.
• Which ball is yours? The red one.
• How many players are on the team? Eleven in football.

### Matching questions and answers
1. Who scored? — Kamau.
2. What did he score? — A goal.
3. When did he score? — In the last minute.
4. Where did he score? — In front of the crowd.
5. Whose team won? — Ours.

### Try it
Look at the pattern above. Write a question for each answer: "In the school hall." (Where did they practise?) "Yesterday." (When did they play?)

## PAGE 5: Responding to questions and asking them from pictures
### Listen first
Before you answer, listen to the question word. It tells you what to give.
• A "when" question needs a time.
• A "where" question needs a place.
• A "who" question needs a person.
• A "whose" question needs an owner.
• A "what" question needs a thing or an event.

### Answer in a complete sentence
• Question: When does the match begin? Answer: The match begins at two o'clock.
• Short answer: At two o'clock.
Both are fine. In writing, use complete sentences.

### Ask questions from a picture
Imagine a picture of a school sports day with children running, a teacher with a whistle and a crowd cheering. Here are some questions:
• Who is holding the whistle? (the teacher)
• What are the children doing? (running)
• Where are the children running? (on the school field)
• Whose parents are watching? (the children's)
• When do you think the picture was taken? (during sports day)

### Reading real texts
Look at a newspaper sports page, a school notice or a magazine. Pick out sentences containing when, where, whose, who and what. Underline them and say what kind of answer each needs.

### Write and type
Write five question sentences in your exercise book, one for each question word, or type them on a computer. Remember the capital letter at the beginning and the question mark at the end.

## PAGE 6: Practice and summary
### Correct the sentences
1. Where you live? → Where do you live?
2. Who did scored the goal? → Who scored the goal?
3. When you will come? → When will you come?
4. What is the referee's name. → What is the referee's name?
5. Whose is these boots? → Whose boots are these?

### Complete with the right question word
1. ____ is the captain of your team? (Who)
2. ____ do you keep the football? (Where)
3. ____ does the game start? (When)
4. ____ is the winner's prize? (What)
5. ____ bag is on the bench? (Whose)

### Interview game
Choose a partner. Ask them: "Who is your favourite sports person?" "What game do you like most?" "Where do you practise?" "When do you practise?" Write down their answers in full sentences.

### Summary
• Interrogatives are question words.
• When: time. Where: place. Who: person. Whose: owner. What: thing or idea.
• Helping verb comes before the subject: "Where did they play?"
• Who's = who is; whose = belonging to whom.
• End with a question mark.

## QUIZ
1. Which question word asks about ownership?
   A) Who
   B) Whose
   C) When
   D) Where
   Answer: B
   Why: "Whose" asks who owns or has something.
2. Which is the best answer to "When does the match begin"?
   A) On the field
   B) At ten o'clock
   C) The referee
   D) It is mine
   Answer: B
   Why: "When" asks for a time.
3. Which question is correct?
   A) Where you keep the ball?
   B) Where do you keep the ball?
   C) Where do you keeps the ball?
   D) Where you do keep the ball?
   Answer: B
   Why: The helping verb "do" comes before the subject and the main verb is in its base form.
4. Which word completes "____ the captain of the team?" meaning "Who is the captain?"
   A) Whose
   B) Who's
   C) When's
   D) Whos
   Answer: B
   Why: "Who's" is the short form of "who is".
5. Write a question for each answer. (a) "It is at the stadium." (b) "The coach." (2 marks)
   Answer: (a) Where is it? (or Where is the match?) (b) Who is the man with the whistle? (or Who trains the team?)
   Why: One mark for each suitable question.
6. Use the question words when, where, who and whose in four questions about a sports day. (4 marks)
   Answer: For example: When is sports day? Where is it held? Who is the guest of honour? Whose team is winning?
   Why: One mark for each correct question with a question mark.
7. Correct these sentences: (a) Who did scored the goal? (b) When you will come? (3 marks)
   Answer: (a) Who scored the goal? (b) When will you come?
   Why: One mark for each correction and one mark for question marks and capital letters.
8. Explain the difference between "who" and "whose". Give one question for each. (3 marks)
   Answer: "Who" asks about a person: "Who is the coach?" "Whose" asks about the owner: "Whose ball is this?"
   Why: One mark for each meaning and one for examples.
