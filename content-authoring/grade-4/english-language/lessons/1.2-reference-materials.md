## PAGE 1: Books that help us find answers
Some books are read from the first page to the last, like a story. Other books are used only when we need to find something out. They are called reference materials. You do not read them from beginning to end. You look inside them for the exact thing you need.

Two reference books are very useful for you in Grade 4. A dictionary tells you how to spell a word, how to say it and what it means. A junior encyclopaedia gives facts about many subjects, for example animals, countries, plants and famous people, in simple language for children.

### Where to find them
• The school library or reading corner.
• Print copies you can hold in your hands.
• Electronic versions on a tablet, computer or phone with a teacher's permission.

Ask yourself: What do I want to know? If the answer is "the meaning of a word", use a dictionary. If the answer is "facts about giraffes", use an encyclopaedia. Choosing the right book saves time and helps you learn independently.

## PAGE 2: Alphabetical order
In a dictionary, all the words are arranged in alphabetical order, which is the order of the letters of the alphabet: a, b, c, d and so on to z. This is why you can find a word quickly without reading every page.

### How to put words in order
Look at the first letter of each word. The word whose first letter comes earlier in the alphabet goes first.
• goat, cow, hen → cow, goat, hen

When two words begin with the same letter, look at the second letter.
• city, cheese, chalk, chair → chair, chalk, cheese, city
All four begin with c. Three of them have h as the second letter, and city has i, which comes later, so city goes last. Among chair, chalk and cheese, look at the third letter: a, a and e. Chair and chalk have a, so they come before cheese. Then the fourth letter separates chair (i) from chalk (l).

Keep going letter by letter until you find a difference. If one word is the beginning of another, the shorter word comes first: sun comes before sunny.

### Try it
Arrange these family words in alphabetical order: uncle, aunt, sister, brother, cousin.
Answer: aunt, brother, cousin, sister, uncle.

Now arrange these: mother, market, mango, mat. The first letter m is the same in all four, so use the second letter. Mango, market and mat share m-a, so look at the third letter: n, r, t. The order is mango, market, mat, mother.

## PAGE 3: Guide words and entries
At the top of each dictionary page you will see two words in bold print. These are guide words. The left guide word is the first word on that page. The right guide word is the last word on that page. Any word that comes alphabetically between them is on that page.

For example, if the guide words are "farm" and "feast", then "fashion" is on that page, because fashion comes after farm and before feast. So does "father": compare f-a-r (farm), f-a-s (fashion), f-a-t (father) and f-e-a (feast). They are in that order.

### What a dictionary entry shows
Each word is called a headword. Beside it you will find:
• the spelling, exactly how to write it;
• the part of speech, such as noun (n.), verb (v.) or adjective (adj.);
• the meaning, sometimes more than one, numbered 1, 2, 3;
• an example sentence;
• how to say the word.

## PAGE 4: Using a dictionary step by step
Follow these steps to find the meaning of a word such as "ancestor".

1. Look at the first letter. It is "a", so open near the front of the dictionary.
2. Look at the guide words at the top of the page. Are you before or after your word? Move forward or backward.
3. When the guide words surround your word, run your finger down the list.
4. Read the entry: spelling, part of speech, meaning and example.
5. Say the word aloud and write the meaning in your own notebook.

### Which meaning?
Some words have more than one meaning. Read all the meanings and choose the one that fits your sentence. Take the word "bark". It can mean the outer covering of a tree, or the sound a dog makes. In "The dog began to bark", it is the sound of a dog.

### Using a dictionary to spell
If you are not sure how to spell "relative", try the sounds you hear at the start. Look at the r words until you find it. You can also check spelling on a digital device that has a dictionary. Type your best guess and see whether the device suggests the correct spelling.

### Practice
Find these words and copy each spelling and one meaning: sibling, orphan, adopt, elder. Write one sentence for each.

## PAGE 5: The junior encyclopaedia
An encyclopaedia is a set of books, or an online collection, that gives facts about many topics. In a junior encyclopaedia the words are simple, and there are many pictures. Topics are also usually arranged in alphabetical order, so to find "Lion" you look in the L volume.

### How to find information
1. Choose your topic. Example: "Elephant".
2. Choose the correct volume or type the topic in the search box.
3. Find the topic using the guide words or the index at the back.
4. Read the passage. Look at pictures, headings and captions.
5. Write short notes in your own words. Do not copy long parts.

### Sample encyclopaedia entry
Elephant. The elephant is the largest land animal in the world. An adult can weigh several tonnes. It uses its long trunk to smell, drink, pick up food and greet other elephants. Elephants live in family groups led by an older female. Their diet is made up of grass, leaves, bark and fruit.

From this entry you can write notes: "largest land animal; uses trunk; lives in family groups; eats plants."

### When online, be careful
When searching on the internet, use only websites suggested by your teacher, and never share personal details. Check the same fact in a second book or site to be sure it is correct.

## QUIZ
1. What is a reference material?
   A) A book you look inside to find specific information
   B) A story book read from beginning to end
   C) A notebook for homework
   D) A picture on the wall
   Answer: A
   Why: Reference materials such as dictionaries and encyclopaedias are used to find information, not read cover to cover.
2. Which list is in alphabetical order?
   A) hen, cow, goat
   B) cow, goat, hen
   C) goat, hen, cow
   D) cow, hen, goat
   Answer: B
   Why: c comes before g and g comes before h.
3. What do the guide words at the top of a dictionary page show?
   A) The author of the book
   B) The first and last words on that page
   C) The number of pages
   D) The meaning of every word
   Answer: B
   Why: The left guide word is the first entry and the right guide word is the last entry on the page.
4. Where would you look to find facts about giraffes?
   A) A junior encyclopaedia
   B) A birthday card
   C) A map key
   D) A calendar
   Answer: A
   Why: An encyclopaedia gives facts about animals and many other subjects.
5. Which word comes first in a dictionary?
   A) market
   B) mango
   C) mat
   D) mother
   Answer: B
   Why: All begin with m. Mango has n as its third letter, market has r and mat has t, and mother has o as its second letter, so mango comes first.
6. Arrange in alphabetical order: uncle, aunt, sister, brother. (2 marks)
   Answer: aunt, brother, sister, uncle
   Why: 1 mark for aunt and brother in the right order, 1 mark for sister and uncle in the right order.
7. List three things a dictionary entry can tell you about a word. (3 marks)
   Answer: Any three: spelling, part of speech, meaning, example sentence, pronunciation.
   Why: 1 mark for each correct point.
8. Describe the steps you would follow to find the word "orphan" in a dictionary. (4 marks)
   Answer: Look for the letter o, open near the middle, check the guide words, run a finger down the list to find orphan and read the meaning and spelling.
   Why: 1 mark for each of four sensible steps in the right order.
9. Why should you check a fact on the internet in a second source? (2 marks)
   Answer: Some websites are wrong, so checking a second book or site shows whether the fact is correct.
   Why: 1 mark for saying a source can be wrong, 1 mark for the idea of checking another.
