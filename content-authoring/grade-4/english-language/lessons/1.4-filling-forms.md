## PAGE 1: Why forms matter
A form is a paper or a screen with spaces where a person writes facts about himself or herself. People fill forms for many reasons. A parent fills a form to enrol a child in school. A child fills a form to join the school library or a club. A patient fills a form at a clinic. A traveller fills a form at a border.

The person who reads the form knows only what you write there. If you write your name wrongly or leave a space empty, the reader may not be able to find you, help you or send you a message. This is why we must be careful and honest when we fill in a form.

### Kinds of information on forms
• Personal details: your full name, your date of birth, your age, your sex, your grade or class.
• School details: name of the school, its address, the name of your class teacher, the name of the head teacher.
• Contact details: a telephone number of a parent or guardian, a home address.

### Talk about it
Have you seen someone fill a form? What was it for? What happened when a mistake was made? Discuss with a partner.

## PAGE 2: Reading the instructions first
Most forms have short instructions at the top. Read them before you write anything. They tell you how to write and what to use.

### Common instructions
• Use BLOCK LETTERS. This means capital letters such as AMINA, not Amina. Block letters are easy to read.
• Use a blue or black pen. Some forms do not allow a pencil.
• Tick the correct box. Put a mark like a tick in the box next to your choice.
• Delete what does not apply. Cross out the wrong word, for example "Male / Female".
• Write the date as day, month and year, for example 05/03/2016 means 5 March 2016.
• Do not write in the shaded area. That part is for the office.

### Words you will meet
• Surname: your family name. If your name is Amina Njoroge, Njoroge is the surname.
• First name: the name that comes first, such as Amina.
• Date of birth (DOB): the day, month and year you were born.
• Address: the place where you live or receive letters.
• Signature: your name written in your own handwriting. Children often ask a parent to sign for them.
• Tel or Phone: your telephone number.

## PAGE 4: Filling a form correctly step by step
### Steps
1. Read the whole form first. See how many parts it has.
2. Read the instructions. Do they ask for block letters, a pen or a tick?
3. Get the facts ready. If you do not know your parent's phone number by heart, ask an adult or check a record.
4. Write neatly inside the lines, one letter in each box if there are boxes.
5. Fill every space. If something does not apply, write N/A (not applicable) or draw a short line.
6. Check the spelling of names. Ask a friend to read the form back to you.
7. Sign if needed and hand the form to the right person.

### Mistakes to avoid
• Leaving a space empty by accident.
• Writing the date in the wrong order.
• Giving false information. This is dishonest and can cause problems later.
• Using short forms nobody understands.
• Scribbling over mistakes. If you make an error, draw one neat line through it and write the correct word beside it.

### Digital forms
Some forms are online. You click in a box, type your answer and press "Submit" or "Send". Online forms also need careful spelling. Only fill a form online with a parent or teacher, and never give your home address, phone number or a password to a website you do not know.

## PAGE 5: Practise making and filling forms
### Fill this form
Copy the form below into your exercise book and fill in the blanks about yourself using BLOCK LETTERS.

Name: __________________
Grade: __________________
Date of Birth: __________________
School: __________________
Address: __________________
Name of class teacher: __________________
Name of head teacher: __________________

### Design a form
Imagine your class starts a reading club. Design a joining form for the club. First decide what the club leader needs to know. She needs the member's name, grade, and the kind of books the member likes. She may also need a parent's telephone number. Then make a title, leave enough space for each answer and write a short instruction, such as "Use block letters."

### Exchange and check
Swap forms with a partner. Check that each space is filled, the writing can be read and the spelling is right. Then give kind feedback. Say one thing that is good and one thing to improve.

### Remember
A form is only useful if the information on it is complete, correct and neat. That is why we fill forms carefully.

## QUIZ
1. What should you do first before you write anything on a form?
   A) Read the instructions
   B) Sign your name
   C) Fold the form
   D) Hand it in
   Answer: A
   Why: Instructions tell you whether to use block letters, a pen or ticks.
2. Which is written in block letters?
   A) Amina Njoroge, with capital letters only at the start of each name
   B) amina njoroge, in small letters
   C) AMINA NJOROGE, in capital letters only
   D) Amina, written in joined letters
   Answer: C
   Why: Block letters are capital letters.
3. What does "date of birth" mean?
   A) The day, month and year you were born
   B) The date today
   C) The day you started school
   D) The date of your last test
   Answer: A
   Why: Date of birth is when a person was born.
4. In the name Wanjiru Kamau, which is the surname?
   A) Wanjiru
   B) Kamau
   C) Both names
   D) Neither name
   Answer: B
   Why: The surname is the family name, which comes last in this name.
5. A section of a form asks "Sex: Male / Female" and you are a girl. What should you do?
   A) Cross out Male
   B) Cross out Female
   C) Leave it empty
   D) Write your name there
   Answer: A
   Why: When you delete what does not apply, you cross out Male and keep Female.
6. Write the date 5 March 2016 as day, month and year in numbers. (2 marks)
   Answer: 05/03/2016
   Why: 1 mark for the day and month in the right order, 1 mark for the year.
7. Give three pieces of school information that a form may ask for. (3 marks)
   Answer: Any three: name of the school, address, class or grade, name of class teacher, name of head teacher.
   Why: 1 mark for each correct item.
8. Explain two reasons why we must fill in forms carefully. (4 marks)
   Answer: The reader depends on the information, so mistakes can cause confusion or lost messages. Wrong or missing facts can delay help or services. Giving false details is dishonest.
   Why: Award 2 marks for each reason that is clearly explained.
9. Why should you not give your home address or a password to an unknown website? (2 marks)
   Answer: Strangers could misuse the information and harm you or your family, so only share details with a trusted adult's help.
   Why: 1 mark for the danger, 1 mark for the safe action.
