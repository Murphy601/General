## PAGE 1: What is a diary?
A diary is a personal record of what happens to you each day. You write what you did, what you saw, and how you felt. Many people keep diaries so that they can remember events and think about their lives.

### Why keep a diary?
• To remember important days and events.
• To practise writing every day.
• To share your feelings in private.
• To learn from your experiences.
• To plan for tomorrow.

### A diary is private
A diary is usually written for yourself. You may share it when you choose to. If someone else writes in a class diary, such as a class-visit diary, it can be shared.

### Features of a diary
• A date at the top of each entry.
• Written in the first person: I, me, my, we.
• Mostly in the past tense for what happened, and sometimes in the present tense or future for feelings and plans.
• Often begins with "Dear Diary" (this is not required).
• Contains events, feelings and thoughts.
• An informal, friendly style, with contractions such as I'm, we've and didn't.

### Why record what we do?
When you write an event down, you remember it better. Later, you can look back at your diary to see how you have grown.

### Try it
Tell a partner three things you did yesterday. Say how you felt about each one. That is the material for a diary entry.

### Day and date
Write the day and the date at the top. Example: Saturday, 14 June. The year may be added.

### Greeting
"Dear Diary," (optional).

### The body
This is the main part. Write:
1. What happened. Say it in order.
2. Who was there.
3. Where it happened.
4. How you felt.

### Ending
You can end with what you plan for tomorrow, a wish or a short sign-off. Example: "Goodnight." You can sign your name.

### Time words
morning, afternoon, evening, first, then, after that, later, by evening.

### Note
The date in the figure is an example. Your entries use the real date on which you write.

## PAGE 3: A model diary entry
Read this diary entry from a Grade 4 learner about a clean-up day.

Saturday, 14 June

Dear Diary,

Today was our school clean-up day, and I am very tired but happy. We arrived at eight o'clock. Mrs. Kariuki gave each group a job. My group swept the paths with brooms. Then we collected rubbish from under the trees and put it in the big pit.

At ten o'clock, we planted five young trees near the fence. I dug the hole for one of them, and I got mud on my shoes! We watered the trees with cans. Our teacher said, "Many hands make light work." She was right, because we finished before lunch.

I felt proud when I saw the clean compound. I hope the trees grow tall. Tomorrow I will tell my mother all about it.

Goodnight,
Wanjiru

### Study the entry
• Day and date at the top.
• "Dear Diary" greeting.
• Events in time order: arrived, swept, collected, planted.
• Feelings: tired, happy, proud.
• Plans for tomorrow.
• Sign-off with a name.
• First person: I, we, my.
• Past tense for events: arrived, swept, planted. Present for feelings: I am very tired but happy.

### The word count
Diary entries can be short or long. For this lesson, aim for 60 to 100 words.

## PAGE 4: Tenses and style in a diary
### Past tense for events
• "We swept the paths."
• "I dug a hole."
• "The teacher gave us a broom."

### Present tense for feelings now
• "I am tired."
• "I feel happy."
• "I hope the trees grow."

### Future for plans
• "Tomorrow I will visit my grandmother."
• "I am going to write to my cousin."

### Present perfect
You can also use the present perfect for a result that matters now: "I have planted my first tree."

### Feelings words
happy, proud, excited, tired, nervous, sad, surprised, worried, thankful.

### Sentence starters
• "Today I..."
• "This morning we..."
• "I was surprised when..."
• "The best part of the day was..."
• "I learnt that..."

### Informal style
A diary is friendly. You may use short forms: I'm, we've, didn't. But keep your writing neat, with capital letters and full stops.

### Try it
Write three sentences about your day: one in the past tense, one about a feeling, one about tomorrow.

## PAGE 5: Writing your own diary
### Steps
1. Choose a day or an event: a trip, a visit, a special lesson, a clean-up day.
2. Jot down key points: what happened first, next and last.
3. Write the day and the date.
4. Begin with "Dear Diary".
5. Write the events in order.
6. Add feelings and what you learnt.
7. End with a plan or a sign-off.
8. Read it and correct mistakes.

### Word bank
first, next, then, after that, later, finally, at last, in the evening.

### Examples of topics
• "A day at the river clean-up."
• "The day I helped at home."
• "Planting trees at school."
• "When I visited my grandmother."

### A short frame to help you
"[Day, date]
Dear Diary,
Today I [what you did]. First, [event]. Then, [event]. I felt [feeling] because [reason]. Tomorrow I will [plan].
Goodnight, [name]"

### Keep it private or share
If you write about your feelings, you may choose to keep it private. When a diary is for the class, write only what you are happy to share.

## PAGE 6: Checking, sharing and summary
### Checklist
• Did I write the day and date?
• Did I write in the first person?
• Are the events in the right order?
• Did I say how I felt?
• Did I use capital letters and full stops?
• Did I sign at the end?

### Swap and display
Swap diaries with a partner (if it is a class diary). Read theirs and say one thing you liked. You can make a class display of the best entries.

### Using electronic devices
You can look up how to write a diary in printed books or on a device with an adult's help. Only read pages that a teacher or parent has approved.

### Diary or letter?
• A diary is for yourself and has no address or "Yours sincerely".
• A letter is sent to another person.

### Summary
• A diary records events and feelings.
• It has a date, greeting, body and sign-off.
• Write in the first person and mostly the past tense.
• Keep events in order and say how you felt.

## QUIZ
1. What must a diary entry begin with?
   A) The name of a friend
   B) The day and date
   C) A question
   D) A title in capital letters
   Answer: B
   Why: Every entry is dated so that you know when it happened.
2. In which person is a diary usually written?
   A) First person (I, we)
   B) Second person (you)
   C) Third person (he, she)
   D) None
   Answer: A
   Why: A diary is about what "I" did and felt.
3. Which sentence describes a feeling?
   A) We swept the paths.
   B) I felt proud of our work.
   C) It was Saturday.
   D) We planted five trees.
   Answer: B
   Why: "Proud" is a feeling.
4. Which tense do we usually use for events that happened earlier in the day?
   A) Future tense
   B) Past tense
   C) Command
   D) Question form
   Answer: B
   Why: Events that have already happened are written in the past tense.
5. List three parts of a diary entry. (3 marks)
   Answer: Day and date; greeting ("Dear Diary"); body (events and feelings); sign-off. Any three.
   Why: One mark for each correct part.
6. Write two reasons why people keep diaries. (2 marks)
   Answer: For example: to remember events; to practise writing; to record feelings.
   Why: One mark for each reason.
7. Rewrite in the past tense: "Today we sweep the compound and plant a tree." (2 marks)
   Answer: Today we swept the compound and planted a tree.
   Why: One mark for "swept" and one mark for "planted".
8. Write a diary entry of about 60 words about a day at school. Include the date, feelings and a plan for tomorrow. (5 marks)
   Answer: An entry in the first person with a date, events in order, feelings, a plan and a sign-off.
   Why: One mark for the date, one for events in order, one for feelings, one for a plan and one for correct tense and punctuation.
