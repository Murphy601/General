## PAGE 1: Skimming and scanning
We do not read every text in the same way. We choose a way that fits our purpose. Two quick reading skills are skimming and scanning.

### Skimming
To skim is to run your eyes quickly over a text to get the main idea. Look at the title, pictures and headings, then read the first sentence of each paragraph. Skimming answers the question, "What is this text about?"

### Scanning
To scan is to look quickly for one particular fact, such as a name, number or word. You do not read every word. Your eyes move like a torch searching in the dark for one thing. Scanning answers the question, "Where is the answer to my question?" For example, if you want to know at what time a bus leaves, scan the timetable for the time.

### Intensive reading
When you must understand a text well, you read intensively. That means reading slowly, more than once, and thinking about it. Try this:
1. Skim to get the general idea.
2. Read carefully from beginning to end.
3. Note the events in order.
4. Read the questions and go back to the text to find the answers.

### Why do we read?
We read to learn, to enjoy stories and to find out how to do things. Reading regularly helps us to read faster and to write and speak better.

Practise skimming: Look at the title of a chapter in your reader, glance at the pictures and tell your partner in one sentence what you think it is about.

## PAGE 2: The passage
Read the passage below silently. Then read it again more slowly.

### The Purse in the Market
On Saturday morning, Mama Grace sent her ten-year-old daughter Neema to the market to buy tomatoes and onions. Neema greeted the vegetable seller, "Good morning, Mama Rose. May I have two kilograms of tomatoes, please?"

"Good morning, my dear," said Mama Rose with a warm smile. She weighed the tomatoes and put them in a basket.

As Neema turned to leave, she saw a small green purse near a stall. She picked it up and opened it carefully. Inside were some coins and a card with the name "Mrs Achieng Odhiambo". Neema looked around. A worried woman was moving quickly from stall to stall, looking at the ground.

"Excuse me, madam," said Neema politely. "Have you lost something?"

"Yes, my purse!" said the woman. "I have been looking for it for a long time."

"Is this your purse?" asked Neema, holding it out.

The woman took it and sighed with relief. She checked the coins. "Thank you very much, my child! It is all here. You are an honest girl."

She took out a fifty-shilling coin and offered it to Neema, but Neema shook her head. "I am glad I could help. Thank you, but I cannot take it," she said.

The woman smiled. "Your mother has taught you good manners. I hope you remain like this."

On her way home, Neema thought about the purse. She remembered her teacher's words at school: "Whatever does not belong to you, return to its owner." She felt happy and light, as if she were carrying feathers instead of a heavy basket.

When Neema reached home, she told her mother what had happened. Mama Grace hugged her and said, "I am proud of you, Neema. Being honest is a great treasure."

## PAGE 3: Factual questions
Factual questions ask about facts that are stated plainly in the passage. The answer can be found in the words of the text. Use scanning: look for key words from the question, then read that part carefully.

### How to answer a factual question
1. Read the question. Underline the key words.
2. Scan the passage for those words.
3. Read the sentence and the sentences around it.
4. Write the answer in a full sentence.

### Examples from "The Purse in the Market"
Question: What did Mama Grace send Neema to buy?
Key words: send, buy. In the first paragraph: "to buy tomatoes and onions".
Answer: Mama Grace sent Neema to buy tomatoes and onions.

Question: What was inside the purse?
Key words: inside, purse. Answer: There were some coins and a card with the name Mrs Achieng Odhiambo.

Question: How much money did the woman offer Neema?
Answer: She offered a fifty-shilling coin.

Question: Who told Neema that being honest is a great treasure?
Answer: Her mother, Mama Grace.

### Practise
Answer in full sentences: (1) How old was Neema? (2) Who did Neema greet at the market? (3) How many kilograms of tomatoes did she ask for? (Answers: ten years old; Mama Rose; two kilograms.)

## PAGE 4: Inferential questions
Inferential questions ask you to think. The answer is not stated directly. You use clues from the text and what you already know. This is called inference, or reading between the lines.

### How to make an inference
1. Find the clues in the passage.
2. Think: what do these clues tell me?
3. Say your answer and give a reason. "I think ... because ..."

### Examples
Question: Why do you think the woman was moving quickly from stall to stall?
Clues: She was "worried" and "looking at the ground".
Answer: She was looking for her lost purse, because she was worried and looking at the ground.

Question: What kind of girl is Neema? Give a reason.
Clues: She greeted the seller politely, she returned the purse and refused money.
Answer: Neema is honest and polite, because she returned the purse and refused the reward.

Question: Why did Mama Grace hug Neema?
Answer: She was proud that her daughter had been honest.

Question: Why did the woman say "Your mother has taught you good manners"?
Answer: Neema spoke politely and acted well, so the woman guessed she had been well brought up.

### Work out meaning from context
When you meet an unknown word, look at the sentences around it. "She sighed with relief" — the woman was worried but now is happy, so relief means the good feeling when a worry is gone. "She shook her head" — Neema refused. Use clues in the passage to explain "honest": someone who tells the truth and does not take things that are not theirs.

## PAGE 5: Putting the events in order and talking about the story
### Events in order
1. Neema goes to the market.
2. She buys tomatoes politely.
3. She finds a purse.
4. She finds the owner and returns it.
5. The woman offers money, but Neema refuses.
6. Neema tells her mother, who is proud.

Use these events to retell the story in your own words, using "first", "then", "next" and "at last".

### Vocabulary from the passage
• greeted: said hello.
• stall: a small table or stand where things are sold.
• purse: a small bag for money.
• relief: the good feeling when a worry ends.
• reward: a gift for doing something good.
• treasure: something very valuable.

### Talk about it
• What would you have done if you found the purse?
• What polite words did Neema use?
• Why is honesty important in a community?

### Role-play
Act out the story in a group of four. One learner is Neema, one is Mama Rose, one is Mrs Odhiambo and one is Mama Grace. Use expressions such as "May I have...", "Excuse me", "Thank you" and "You are welcome".

### Remember
• Skim for the main idea. Scan for facts.
• A factual answer is stated. An inferential answer is worked out from clues.
• Always answer in a full sentence and give a reason for an inference.

## QUIZ
1. What does it mean to scan a text?
   A) To read every word slowly
   B) To look quickly for one particular fact
   C) To copy the text
   D) To read only the title
   Answer: B
   Why: Scanning means quickly searching for a name, number or fact.
2. What did Mama Grace send Neema to buy?
   A) Bread and milk
   B) Tomatoes and onions
   C) Rice and beans
   D) Eggs and sugar
   Answer: B
   Why: The first paragraph says she was sent to buy tomatoes and onions.
3. How much money did the woman offer Neema?
   A) Ten shillings
   B) Twenty shillings
   C) Fifty shillings
   D) One hundred shillings
   Answer: C
   Why: The passage says she offered a fifty-shilling coin.
4. Which word best describes Neema?
   A) Greedy
   B) Rude
   C) Honest
   D) Lazy
   Answer: C
   Why: She returned the purse and refused a reward, which shows honesty.
5. Which type of question needs you to think and use clues?
   A) A factual question
   B) An inferential question
   C) A spelling test
   D) A copying task
   Answer: B
   Why: Inferential questions have answers that are not stated directly.
6. Why do you think Mrs Odhiambo was worried at the start? (2 marks)
   Answer: She had lost her purse and could not find it.
   Why: 1 mark for saying she lost her purse and 1 mark for a clue, such as looking at the ground.
7. Explain what "sighed with relief" means. (2 marks)
   Answer: She let out a long breath because she was no longer worried after getting her purse back.
   Why: 1 mark for the idea of a breath or feeling and 1 mark for the end of worry.
8. Give two polite expressions that Neema used in the story. (2 marks)
   Answer: Any two: "Good morning", "May I have", "please", "Excuse me", "Thank you".
   Why: 1 mark for each correct expression.
9. What lesson does the story teach? Give a reason from the passage. (3 marks)
   Answer: It teaches that honesty and good manners are important. Neema returned the purse and refused the reward, and her mother said honesty is a great treasure.
   Why: 1 mark for naming the lesson and 2 marks for supporting details from the passage.
