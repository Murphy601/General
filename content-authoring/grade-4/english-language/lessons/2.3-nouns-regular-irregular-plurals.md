## PAGE 1: Singular and plural nouns
A noun is a word that names a person, an animal, a place or a thing. Examples are teacher, goat, Nairobi, table and love. Nouns can show how many there are.

• Singular means one: a book, a child, a goat.
• Plural means more than one: books, children, goats.

How do we show the number of things we have? In English, we usually change the end of the noun. Look at the pairs: cup and cups, boy and boys. But some nouns change in a special way: child becomes children, not "childs". So there are two kinds of plurals.

### Two kinds of plural nouns
• Regular nouns follow a rule. Most of them add s or es.
• Irregular nouns do not follow the rule. They change in another way, or do not change at all.

### Look around your classroom
Which things are in your classroom? Chair, desk, window, door, book, pencil. Say each one in singular and plural: one chair, two chairs; one book, ten books. Now count them in your room. There are thirty desks, six windows, one door. Which words change and which stay the same? Only nouns you can count change to plurals.

## PAGE 2: Regular plurals: the rules
Regular nouns follow five main spelling patterns.

### Rule 1: Add s to most nouns
book, books. cat, cats. chair, chairs. goat, goats. mango, mangoes? no. See Rule 5.
Examples: banana, bananas; teacher, teachers.

### Rule 2: Add es after s, x, z, ch, sh
These endings make a hissing sound, so we need an extra syllable.
• bus, buses
• box, boxes
• church, churches
• dish, dishes
• watch, watches

### Rule 3: Words ending in y
• If a consonant comes before the y, change y to i and add es: baby, babies; story, stories; city, cities; party, parties; lorry, lorries.
• If a vowel (a, e, i, o, u) comes before the y, just add s: boy, boys; key, keys; monkey, monkeys; day, days.

### Rule 4: Words ending in f or fe
Many change f or fe to v and add es: leaf, leaves; knife, knives; wife, wives; loaf, loaves; shelf, shelves; wolf, wolves; thief, thieves.
A few just add s: roof, roofs; cliff, cliffs; chief, chiefs.

### Rule 5: Words ending in o
• Add es to some: potato, potatoes; tomato, tomatoes; mango, mangoes; hero, heroes.
• Add s to others: photo, photos; piano, pianos; radio, radios; zoo, zoos.

## PAGE 3: Irregular plurals
Irregular nouns do not follow the rules above. You must learn them one by one, just as you learn the names of your friends.

### Nouns that change inside
• man, men
• woman, women
• foot, feet
• tooth, teeth
• goose, geese
• mouse, mice

### Nouns with a special ending
• child, children
• ox, oxen
• person, people

### Nouns that stay the same
The singular and plural look the same. Only the number word or verb shows the difference.
• one sheep, ten sheep
• one deer, three deer
• one fish, five fish (fish is the usual plural)

### Nouns that are always plural
Some things have two parts joined together, so the noun is plural. We say "scissors", "trousers" and "shorts". We say "My trousers are clean" and "Where are the scissors?" To count them we say "a pair of scissors" or "two pairs of trousers".

### Learn them with a game
Make cards with the singular on one side and the plural on the other. Test a partner. Or make a puzzle: write clues such as "I have two of these and I use them to walk: feet" and ask friends to guess the word.

## PAGE 4: Using plurals in sentences
A plural noun needs a plural verb form. Look at the change.
• The child is playing. → The children are playing.
• The goose is loud. → The geese are loud.
• The man works here. → The men work here.
Notice that is becomes are, and works becomes work.

### Worked examples
1. Write the plural of "baby". The word ends in y after the consonant b, so change y to i and add es: babies.
2. Write the plural of "knife". It ends in fe, so change to ves: knives.
3. Write the plural of "photo". It ends in o, but it is one of the words that just add s: photos.
4. Write the plural of "tooth". It is irregular: teeth.
5. Write the plural of "bus". It ends in s, so add es: buses.

### Complete the sentences
• There are three (child) in the yard. → children
• The (woman) are selling (tomato). → women, tomatoes
• My grandmother has two (loaf) of bread. → loaves
• The farmer has ten (sheep) and five (goose). → sheep, geese

### Common mistakes
• "childs" for children; "mans" for men; "foots" for feet.
• "sheeps" for sheep.
• "babys" for babies.

## PAGE 5: Nouns in your speaking and writing
Now use nouns in your own sentences. Try a family theme.

### Model sentences
• My aunt bought six loaves, four boxes of biscuits and ten tomatoes.
• The children in our compound have two pianos? no. They have two radios and three balls.
Use sentences that are true and simple.

### Listen and write
Ask a friend to read a short passage. Listen for plural nouns, and write them. For example: "The boys carried baskets of mangoes. The women sold onions and potatoes at the stalls." Plurals: boys, baskets, mangoes, women, onions, potatoes, stalls.

### Search and record
Look for plural nouns in your reader or a newspaper. Sort them into two columns: regular and irregular. Then ask which rule each regular one follows.

### Make a puzzle
Make a word search or crossword using ten plurals: babies, boxes, leaves, wives, mice, teeth, children, women, sheep, feet. Swap with a friend and solve.

### Summary
• Regular plurals follow spelling rules: add s or es, change y to ies, f/fe to ves.
• Irregular plurals must be memorised: children, men, women, feet, teeth, mice, sheep.
• Use a plural verb with plural nouns.

## QUIZ
1. What is the plural of "box"?
   A) boxs
   B) boxes
   C) boxies
   D) boxen
   Answer: B
   Why: Nouns ending in x add es.
2. Which is an irregular plural?
   A) books
   B) chairs
   C) teeth
   D) goats
   Answer: C
   Why: Tooth changes to teeth and does not follow the add-s rule.
3. What is the plural of "story"?
   A) storys
   B) stories
   C) storyes
   D) stores
   Answer: B
   Why: A consonant comes before y, so change y to i and add es.
4. Which word stays the same in the plural?
   A) child
   B) leaf
   C) sheep
   D) woman
   Answer: C
   Why: The plural of sheep is sheep.
5. Choose the correct sentence.
   A) The childs are singing.
   B) The children is singing.
   C) The children are singing.
   D) The child are singing.
   Answer: C
   Why: The plural of child is children and it takes the plural verb are.
6. Write the plural of each word: knife, monkey, photo, woman. (2 marks)
   Answer: knives, monkeys, photos, women
   Why: Half a mark for each correct plural.
7. Explain why the plural of "boy" is boys but the plural of "baby" is babies. (3 marks)
   Answer: In boy, a vowel (o) comes before y, so we only add s. In baby, a consonant (b) comes before y, so we change y to i and add es.
   Why: 1 mark for the vowel case, 1 mark for the consonant case and 1 mark for the change of y to i.
8. Rewrite in the plural: "The woman put a loaf and a tomato in the box." (3 marks)
   Answer: The women put loaves and tomatoes in the boxes.
   Why: 1 mark each for women, loaves and tomatoes with boxes.
9. Give two examples of irregular plurals and two examples of regular plurals. (2 marks)
   Answer: Irregular: children, mice. Regular: books, dishes.
   Why: 1 mark for two correct irregular plurals and 1 mark for two correct regular plurals.
