## PAGE 1: Why we write letters and messages
People pass on information to others every day. We may tell our friends news, thank a relative, invite a cousin or ask how someone is. When the person is far away, we cannot say it face to face, so we write. Two common ways are the friendly letter and the SMS.

A friendly letter is a personal letter to someone you know well, such as a friend, a sibling, a parent or a grandparent. Its language is warm and simple. It is different from a formal letter, which we write to a head teacher or an office.

An SMS (short message service) is a short text message sent from one mobile phone to another.

### Which one to choose?
• Use a letter when you have more to say, want to share feelings or news, or when you want to keep something to read again.
• Use an SMS when the message is short and needs to get there quickly.

What kind of information do we share with friends? Good news, news about school, invitations, thanks, advice and greetings. We also share things we have learnt, such as new words about road safety.

### Before you write
Ask yourself three questions: Who am I writing to? Why am I writing? What must I say? Your answers will guide your choice of words.

### The five parts
1. Address and date: Write your own address at the top right. Below it write the date, for example 14th May 2026. This tells your friend where to reply.
2. Greeting (salutation): Write "Dear" and your friend's name, followed by a comma: Dear Achieng,
3. Body: This is the message. Begin with a greeting such as "I hope you are well." Then write your news in short paragraphs. End by asking your friend a question or sending good wishes.
4. Closing: A friendly ending such as "Yours lovingly," "Your friend," or "With love," followed by a comma.
5. Name: Write your first name under the closing.

### Punctuation reminders
• A comma follows the greeting and the closing.
• Begin each sentence with a capital letter.
• Begin the names of people and places with capital letters.

## PAGE 3: A model friendly letter
Read this letter from Neema to her friend Achieng in another school.

P.O. Box 12,
Nakuru.
14th May 2026

Dear Achieng,

I hope you and your family are well. I am fine and so is my family.

This week my class learnt new words about road safety. We learnt the words roundabout, railway crossing, foot bridge, cycling lane, underpass and number plate. Our teacher took us for a short walk near the school to see a foot bridge. It was very high! She told us to use it every time we cross the busy road.

I am now more careful when I walk on the road. I look right, left and right again before I cross.

What have you learnt in your school this week? Please write back soon and tell me. Greet your mother for me.

Yours lovingly,
Neema

### Study the letter
• The address and date are at the top.
• The greeting has a comma after it.
• The body has three paragraphs: greeting, news, closing questions.
• The closing "Yours lovingly," is followed by the name.
• The letter is friendly, neat and clear. It uses "I" and "you" because she is talking to a friend.

### Compare with formal letters
A friendly letter does not need the name and address of the reader or a subject line. Those belong to formal letters written to an office.

## PAGE 4: Writing your own friendly letter
### Steps
1. Choose who you will write to: a brother, a friend, a cousin, a parent or a grandparent.
2. Plan your ideas. Write three points: one about the person, one about your news, one about a question.
3. Write the address and date.
4. Write the greeting: Dear ______,
5. Write the body in short paragraphs.
6. Write a friendly closing and your name.
7. Read it aloud and correct your mistakes.

### Useful phrases
• Beginning: I hope you are well. How are you and the family? Thank you for your last letter.
• News: This week we... Last Saturday I... I want to tell you about...
• Ending: Please write back soon. Greet everyone at home. I miss you.

### Ideas for letters
• A letter to your elder brother at boarding school about a school trip.
• A letter to a grandparent to thank her for a gift.
• A letter to a friend in another school telling about new words on road safety.

### Checklist
• Is my address and date at the top right?
• Did I use Dear and a comma?
• Did I write in paragraphs?
• Did I use capital letters and full stops?
• Is my writing neat?

### Practice
Write a letter of about five sentences to your best friend telling about your favourite school activity. Swap with a partner and check each other's work.

## PAGE 5: Writing an SMS
An SMS is a short message. You write it on a mobile phone. Because it is short, every word must count.

### Parts of an SMS
• The receiver: the phone number or name of the person who gets the message.
• The message: the words you write.
• The sender: your name, so the receiver knows who wrote (the phone also shows the sender's number).

### Rules for a good SMS
1. Start with a greeting and the receiver's name if you can.
2. Keep it short and clear.
3. Use correct spelling as much as possible. Avoid making short forms nobody understands.
4. Be polite. Use please and thank you.
5. End with your name.
6. Use a phone only with permission from your parent or teacher, and send messages only to people you know.

### Model SMS
To: Uncle Peter
Message: Good evening, Uncle. Thank you for the gift you sent me. I love the book. Please greet Aunt Mary. From Neema.

Another example:
To: Baraka
Message: Hi Baraka. Please bring your reader to school tomorrow. Thanks. Amina.

### Compare letters and SMS
• A letter has five parts and can be long. An SMS is short and has a receiver, a message and a sender.
• A letter is better for news and feelings. An SMS is better for quick reminders.
• Both must be polite and clear.

### Activity
In pairs, write an SMS to invite a friend to your birthday. Include the day, time and place. Then read each other's messages and check that they are complete.

## QUIZ
1. Where does the sender's address go in a friendly letter?
   A) Top right
   B) Bottom left
   C) After the name
   D) In the body
   Answer: A
   Why: The address and date go at the top right of the letter.
2. What punctuation mark follows "Dear Achieng"?
   A) A full stop
   B) A question mark
   C) A comma
   D) An exclamation mark
   Answer: C
   Why: A comma follows the greeting.
3. Which closing is suitable for a friendly letter?
   A) Yours faithfully
   B) Yours lovingly
   C) To whom it may concern
   D) Dear Sir
   Answer: B
   Why: Yours lovingly is a friendly ending. The others belong to formal letters.
4. Which is a part of an SMS?
   A) The postal stamp
   B) The message
   C) The envelope
   D) The salutation date
   Answer: B
   Why: An SMS has a receiver, a message and a sender.
5. Which of these should you do when writing an SMS?
   A) Write it in secret code
   B) Be polite and keep it short and clear
   C) Send it to strangers
   D) Leave out your name
   Answer: B
   Why: A good SMS is short, clear and polite, and it ends with the sender's name.
6. List the five parts of a friendly letter. (3 marks)
   Answer: Address, date, greeting, body, closing and name (address and date may be given together).
   Why: 3 marks for all parts: 1 mark for the address and date, 1 mark for greeting and body, 1 mark for closing and name.
7. Write the greeting and the closing for a friendly letter to your friend Wanjiku. (2 marks)
   Answer: Dear Wanjiku, and Yours lovingly, followed by your name.
   Why: 1 mark for the greeting with a comma and 1 mark for a friendly closing.
8. Write an SMS to invite a friend to a birthday party on Saturday at 3 o'clock. (3 marks)
   Answer: For example: Hi Kamau. Please come to my birthday party on Saturday at 3 o'clock at my home. Thank you. Ann.
   Why: 1 mark for greeting or receiver, 1 mark for the day, time and place, 1 mark for politeness and the sender's name.
9. Explain one difference between a friendly letter and an SMS. (2 marks)
   Answer: A friendly letter is longer and has five parts, while an SMS is short and has a receiver, a message and a sender.
   Why: 1 mark for the difference and 1 mark for a clear explanation.
