## PAGE 1: Email and internet words
The internet is a huge network that joins computers and phones all over the world. With it we can search for information, watch videos and send email. Email is short for electronic mail, which means messages sent from one computer or phone to another.

### Vocabulary
• internet: a worldwide network that connects computers.
• email: a message sent electronically.
• email address: the special name that shows where an email goes, such as neema@example.com.
• computer: a machine that stores and processes information.
• mobile phone: a phone that you can carry with you.
• keyboard: the set of keys that we press to type.
• type: to write using a keyboard.
• search: to look for information.
• click: to press a button on a mouse or screen.
• compose: to write a new message.
• send: to make a message go to someone.
• reply: to answer a message.
• save: to keep your work so that it is not lost.
• inbox: the place where messages you receive are kept.
• outbox: the place where messages waiting to be sent are kept.

### Use them in sentences
• I type my name on the keyboard.
• Please click the send button.
• Compose an email to your teacher.
• I will reply to my cousin's message tonight.

### Safety first
Use the internet only with a parent or teacher. Do not tell strangers your name, school, address or password.

## PAGE 2: The sounds /ʊ/, /uː/, /f/ and /v/
Here are sounds from our theme words.

### /ʊ/ and /uː/
The sound /ʊ/ is short: put, full, good, foot, book, look, push.
The sound /uː/ is long: computer, user, do, you, new, view, video, soon.

Compare full and fool, and pull and pool. Say each word carefully.

### /f/ and /v/
/f/ is whispered and /v/ is voiced. Your top teeth touch your lower lip for both.
• /f/: file, find, forward, fast, few, phone, information
• /v/: view, video, save, very, voice, have, over

Minimal pairs: fan and van, fine and vine, few and view, leaf and leave.

### Sentences to read aloud
• Find the file and save it before you go.
• Look at the video on the new computer.
• A few users have a very fast phone.
• Please put the book on the full desk.

### Tongue twister
Five fine friends found a few new videos.

Say it slowly three times and then quickly. Check that each /f/ is a whisper and each /v/ has a buzz.

## PAGE 3: Word stress in email words
Remember that each word of two or more syllables has one strong syllable. A wrong stress can make a word difficult to understand.

### Stress in theme words (the capital letters show the stress)
• IN-ter-net
• EE-mail
• com-PU-ter
• KEY-board
• com-POSE
• in-BOX
• out-BOX
• SEARCH
• re-PLY
Say each word aloud twice with the stress on the right syllable.

### Stress for noun and verb
Some words have a different stress when they are nouns and when they are verbs. Recall: the noun has stress on the first syllable and the verb on the second.
• IN-crease (noun) and in-CREASE (verb): There was an IN-crease in the number of users. Sales will in-CREASE.
• RE-cord (noun) and re-CORD (verb): I keep a RE-cord of my emails. I will re-CORD my voice.
• PRE-sent (noun) and pre-SENT (verb).
• OB-ject (noun) and ob-JECT (verb).

Some words, such as "address", can be said in more than one way. Listen to your teacher and check the stress mark in your dictionary.

### Practise
Say these sentences with the correct stress:
1. There is an IN-crease in the number of children who use email.
2. The number of learners will in-CREASE next year.

## PAGE 4: Intonation
Intonation is the rise and fall of the voice when we speak. It works like a tune. The same words can mean different things when they are said with different tunes. Intonation tells us whether a person is asking a question, giving information, feeling happy, surprised or sad.

### Three simple rules
1. Statements: the voice falls at the end. "I sent the email." (falling)
2. WH questions: they begin with who, what, where, when, why or how. The voice usually falls at the end. "Where is the file?" (falling)
3. Yes or no questions: they are answered by "yes" or "no". The voice usually rises at the end. "Is the file on the computer?" (rising)

### Practise
Say these aloud. Watch your voice go down or up.
• My name is Neema. (falls)
• Who sent the message? (falls)
• When will you reply? (falls)
• Can I use the computer? (rises)
• Did you save the file? (rises)

Ask a partner to listen and tell you whether your voice goes up or down.

## PAGE 5: Intonation shows feelings
We can hear how a person feels from the tune of the voice. A voice that goes up and down widely sounds happy or excited. A flat, low voice can sound sad or bored.

### One word, many meanings
Say the word "Really?" in different ways.
• With a high, rising voice: you are surprised.
• With a low voice that falls: you are not interested.
Say "Oh!" in a happy voice and in a sad voice.

### Listen for the feeling
Read "I have a new email address." three times:
1. As good news, with a bright, lively voice.
2. As a simple fact, with an even voice that falls.
3. As a question, with a rising voice: "I have a new email address?"

### Recite a poem with questions and statements
Who has sent a message to me?
Where in the inbox can it be?
I click and read, I type a reply.
Send it fast, and wave goodbye.

Read the poem in a group. Let your voice fall at the end of WH questions and statements. Ask a partner: Did my voice go up or down?

### Work in pairs
Ask each other questions about email.
A: Do you have an email address? (rising)
B: Yes, I do. (falling)
A: What is it? (falling)
B: Sorry, I will show you. (falling)

### Remember
Statements and WH questions fall. Yes or no questions rise. Use your voice to show your feelings, and always speak clearly so that people understand you.

## QUIZ
1. What is intonation?
   A) The rise and fall of the voice when we speak
   B) The spelling of a word
   C) The length of a sentence
   D) The meaning of a word
   Answer: A
   Why: Intonation is the tune of our voice.
2. How does the voice usually move at the end of a yes or no question?
   A) It falls
   B) It rises
   C) It stays flat
   D) It stops
   Answer: B
   Why: In yes or no questions, the voice usually goes up at the end.
3. Which sentence normally ends with a falling voice?
   A) Is the file on the computer?
   B) Where is the file?
   C) Can I use the phone?
   D) Did you send it?
   Answer: B
   Why: WH questions usually fall at the end. The others are yes or no questions.
4. In the noun "IN-crease", which syllable is stressed?
   A) The first
   B) The second
   C) Both
   D) Neither
   Answer: A
   Why: The noun has the stress on the first syllable and the verb on the second.
5. What does "inbox" mean?
   A) A place for messages waiting to be sent
   B) The place where messages you receive are kept
   C) A kind of keyboard
   D) A type of phone
   Answer: B
   Why: The inbox holds messages you have received.
6. Say whether the voice falls or rises at the end of each: (a) Who sent the message? (b) Did you save the file? (2 marks)
   Answer: (a) falls (b) rises
   Why: 1 mark for each correct answer.
7. Write two sentences using the words "compose" and "reply". (2 marks)
   Answer: For example: I will compose an email to my uncle. He will reply tomorrow.
   Why: 1 mark for each sentence with the word used correctly.
8. Explain how intonation can change the meaning of "Really?" (3 marks)
   Answer: With a high rising voice it shows surprise, but with a low falling voice it shows that the speaker is not interested.
   Why: 1 mark for each way of saying it and 1 mark for linking intonation to feeling.
9. Write one statement, one WH question and one yes or no question about email, and say how your voice moves at the end of each. (3 marks)
   Answer: For example: I sent the email (falls). Where is the message? (falls) Did you get it? (rises)
   Why: 1 mark for each sentence with the correct voice movement.
