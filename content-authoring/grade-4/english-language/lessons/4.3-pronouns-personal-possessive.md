## PAGE 1: Words that stand in for nouns
Read this: "Amina saw a car. Amina looked at the car. Amina liked the car." It sounds strange because the names come again and again. Now read it another way: "Amina saw a car. She looked at it. She liked it." It sounds much better. The words she and it are pronouns.

A pronoun is a word that takes the place of a noun. Which words replace names of people or things in a sentence? Pronouns! They help us avoid repeating the same noun.

### Kinds of pronouns in this lesson
• Personal pronouns: I, you, he, she, it, we, they (and me, him, her, us, them). They stand for people or things.
• Possessive pronouns: mine, yours, his, hers, ours, theirs. They show who owns something.

### First look
• Mr Otieno drives a van. He drives it to town. (He takes the place of Mr Otieno. It takes the place of the van.)
• Ali and I are friends. We play together. (We takes the place of Ali and I.)
• The teacher called Ann and Eve. She called them to the front. (She stands for the teacher, them for Ann and Eve.)

Pronouns make speaking and writing smooth. In the next pages you will learn how to choose the right pronoun.

## PAGE 2: Personal pronouns as subjects
The subject is the person or thing that does the action in a sentence. It usually comes first. Personal pronouns used as subjects are: I, you, he, she, it, we, they.

### Table of subject pronouns
• I: the person who is speaking. I am nine years old.
• you: the person spoken to. You are my friend.
• he: one boy or man. He is a driver.
• she: one girl or woman. She is a nurse.
• it: one thing or animal. It is a bridge.
• we: the speaker and other people. We are in Grade 4.
• they: more than one person or thing. They are on the road.

### Choosing the right pronoun
1. Look at the noun you want to replace.
2. Is it one or more than one? Is it a male, a female or a thing?
3. Choose the pronoun.
• Baraka is careful. He looks both ways. (one boy)
• The bridge is long. It crosses the river. (one thing)
• The children are outside. They are playing. (more than one)
• Mama and I walked. We went to the clinic. (speaker and another)

### Note
The pronoun I is always written with a capital letter, wherever it is in a sentence. "Ali and I went home" (not "Ali and me went home").

### Try it
Fill each blank with a pronoun: (1) Wanjiru is a doctor. ___ helps sick people. (2) The car has a wiper. ___ cleans the windscreen. Answers: She, It.

## PAGE 3: Personal pronouns as objects
The object is the person or thing that receives the action. It usually comes after the verb, or after a word such as to, for or with. The object forms of the pronouns are different: me, you, him, her, it, us, them.

### Subject and object forms
• I → me: The nurse helped me.
• you → you: I saw you.
• he → him: The doctor treated him.
• she → her: We visited her.
• it → it: He fixed it.
• we → us: The teacher told us.
• they → them: I gave them a bandage.

### Compare
• She hurt her leg. The nurse helped her. (subject, then object)
• They ran to the bridge. A guard stopped them.
• We are safe. The foot bridge protects us.

### Two people
When there are two people, take one away to check.
• "Ali and me went to school" is wrong. Check: "Me went to school" sounds wrong. Use "Ali and I went to school."
• "The teacher gave sweets to Ann and I" is wrong. Check: "gave sweets to I" sounds wrong. Use "to Ann and me."

### Fill in the blank
1. The nurse called (I / me) to the room.
2. (He / Him) is a driver.
3. Give the book to (they / them).
Answers: me, He, them.

## PAGE 4: Possessive pronouns
Possessive pronouns tell us who owns something. They stand alone and take the place of a noun that shows ownership. They are: mine, yours, his, hers, ours, theirs.

### Compare with my, your, his, her, our, their
Words like my and your go before a noun. They are possessive adjectives.
• This is my bag. (my comes before the noun bag)
• The bag is mine. (mine stands alone)

### Pairs
• my bag → the bag is mine
• your pencil → the pencil is yours
• his bicycle → the bicycle is his
• her umbrella → the umbrella is hers
• our house → the house is ours
• their car → the car is theirs

### Examples in sentences
• That first aid kit is ours.
• "Whose bag is this?" "It is mine."
• Is this coat yours or hers?
• The number plate is theirs.
• I have my bag and he has his. (his can be an adjective or a pronoun; here it replaces "his bag")

### Notes
• Possessive pronouns are never written with an apostrophe: yours, hers, ours, theirs.
• Do not write "mine bag" or "yours book". Say "my bag" or "your book".

### Try it
Complete: (1) This is your pen. It is ___. (2) That is our car. It is ___. (3) She has her umbrella. It is ___. Answers: yours, ours, hers.

## PAGE 5: Using pronouns to talk about a picture
Look at Figure 1. Imagine a car near a bridge. Here are sentences using personal and possessive pronouns.

### Describe the picture
• The car has two head lights. They light the road at night.
• It also has a wiper. It cleans the windscreen when it rains.
• The fog lights are small. They help the driver see in mist.
• The driver checks the signal lights before he turns.
• "Is this car yours?" "No, it is my uncle's. It is his."
• We stood on the foot bridge. It was high and safe. It kept us away from the vehicles.

### Pronouns in a short paragraph
"Salma and her father went to town. They travelled by bus. The bus passed a bridge, and she looked at it for a long time. Her father said, 'We use that bridge every week. It is ours to look after.' Salma smiled at him."

Find the pronouns: they, she, it, we, it, ours, him. The word "her" before "father" is a possessive adjective. Notice that they replace names so the paragraph is not repetitive.

### Digital activity
Type five sentences about your family or road safety on a computer or tablet, using at least three pronouns in each. Then check that each pronoun matches the noun it replaces. Share with a friend.

### Reading hunt
Read a newspaper, a magazine or a poem, and find ten pronouns. Sort them into personal (subject), personal (object) and possessive.

### Summary
• Personal pronouns: I, you, he, she, it, we, they (subject); me, you, him, her, it, us, them (object).
• Possessive pronouns: mine, yours, his, hers, ours, theirs.
• Use pronouns to avoid repeating nouns.

## QUIZ
1. Which word is a pronoun?
   A) bridge
   B) she
   C) quickly
   D) green
   Answer: B
   Why: She takes the place of a noun such as a girl's name.
2. Choose the correct sentence.
   A) Him is a doctor.
   B) He is a doctor.
   C) Hims is a doctor.
   D) His is a doctor.
   Answer: B
   Why: He is the subject form for one man or boy.
3. Which pronoun completes the sentence? "The nurse helped ___ after the fall." (a girl)
   A) she
   B) her
   C) hers
   D) they
   Answer: B
   Why: Her is the object form, used after the verb.
4. Which is a possessive pronoun?
   A) my
   B) me
   C) mine
   D) I
   Answer: C
   Why: Mine stands alone and shows ownership.
5. Fill in: "This is our car. It is ___."
   A) our
   B) ours
   C) us
   D) we
   Answer: B
   Why: Ours is the possessive pronoun that stands alone.
6. Replace the second pair of names and the second "the bridge" with pronouns: "Baraka and Amina saw the bridge. Baraka and Amina liked the bridge." (2 marks)
   Answer: Baraka and Amina saw the bridge. They liked it.
   Why: 1 mark for They and 1 mark for it.
7. Rewrite correctly: "Ali and me went to the clinic." (2 marks)
   Answer: Ali and I went to the clinic.
   Why: 1 mark for I and 1 mark for keeping the rest of the sentence correct.
8. Explain the difference between "my" and "mine". Use each in a sentence. (3 marks)
   Answer: My comes before a noun (This is my bag) while mine stands alone (The bag is mine).
   Why: 1 mark for each correct sentence and 1 mark for explaining the difference.
9. Write three sentences about a car near a bridge. Use one subject pronoun, one object pronoun and one possessive pronoun. (3 marks)
   Answer: For example: It has bright head lights. The bridge protects us. The car is theirs.
   Why: 1 mark for each correct pronoun type used properly.
