## PAGE 1: Small words before nouns
Nouns are the names of people, places, animals and things, for example girl, market, goat and book. Very often a small word comes just before a noun to tell us which one or how many. Such small words are called determiners.

The most common determiners are the articles. There are only three articles in English: a, an and the.
• a girl
• an egg
• the market

Articles always come before a noun, or before an adjective that describes the noun. We say "a book" and also "a red book". The article stays at the front.

### Two kinds of articles
• Indefinite articles: a and an. We use them when we talk about any one thing, not one that is already known.
• Definite article: the. We use it when we talk about a particular thing that the speaker and the listener both know.

Compare: "I want a mango." (any mango) and "I want the mango on the table." (one special mango).

Read aloud with a partner: a brother, an uncle, the family, an aunt, a cousin, the twins. Listen to how each article sounds when you say it with its noun.

## PAGE 2: Indefinite articles a and an
Use a or an with one thing that can be counted, when it is mentioned for the first time or when it does not matter which one.
• She has a sister.
• We saw a hen in the yard.
• He is an orphan.

### The rule for a and an
The choice depends on the sound at the start of the next word, not on the letter.
• Use "a" before a consonant sound: a boy, a table, a cup.
• Use "an" before a vowel sound (the sounds of a, e, i, o, u): an apple, an egg, an ink pen, an orange, an umbrella.

Some words are tricky because the sound is not the same as the first letter.
• an hour: the h is silent, so the word starts with the sound "ow".
• an honest man: the h is silent again.
• a uniform: uniform starts with the sound "yoo", a consonant sound.
• a university, a useful tool: they also start with "yoo".

### Add an adjective
When an adjective comes between the article and the noun, choose the article for the adjective. We say "an old man" but "a tall man". We say "an interesting story" but "a long story".

### Note
We do not use a or an with plural nouns. We cannot say "a books". We say "books" or "some books".

## PAGE 3: The definite article the
We use "the" when we speak about a known or particular person, place or thing. It can be used with singular and plural nouns.

### When to use the
1. When the noun has already been mentioned: "I bought a chicken and a cabbage. The chicken was fat." (We already know which chicken.)
2. When there is only one: the sun, the moon, the sky, the equator, the head teacher of our school.
3. When the listener knows which one you mean: "Please close the door."
4. Before superlative words: the tallest boy, the best game.
5. Before some names of rivers, oceans and mountain ranges: the Nile, the Indian Ocean.

### When we do not use the
• Before names of people: Wanjiku, Mr Kamau.
• Before most names of countries or towns: Kenya, Nairobi, Mombasa.
• Before meals and languages in general: We eat lunch at one o'clock. She speaks Kiswahili.

### Pronouncing the
Say "thuh" before a consonant sound (the boy, the family) and "thee" before a vowel sound (the egg, the aunt). Practise with: the uncle, the mother, the ancestor, the twins.

### Example paragraph
Njeri has a brother and an aunt. The brother is called Kevin. The aunt lives in Nakuru. Notice that "a" and "an" introduce the two people, and "the" is used when we speak of them again.

## PAGE 4: Choosing the right article
To choose an article correctly, ask three quick questions.

### Question guide
1. Do I mean one special thing that we both know? Use the.
2. Do I mean any one thing, said for the first time? Use a or an.
3. Does the next word start with a vowel sound? Use an. Does it start with a consonant sound? Use a.

### Worked examples
Fill the blank with a, an or the.
• Sara has ___ uncle in Kisumu. The next word is uncle, which begins with the vowel sound "uh". First mention, so we use an. Answer: an uncle.
• My aunt bought ___ cake. Then ___ cake was shared. First mention: a cake. Second mention: the cake.
• ___ sun rises in the east. There is only one sun. Answer: The sun.
• He waited for ___ hour. Hour begins with the sound "ow". Answer: an hour.

### Common mistakes
• "I have a egg." This should be "an egg".
• "She is an teacher." This should be "a teacher".
• "The Kenya is beautiful." This should be "Kenya is beautiful".
• "He is a honest boy." This should be "an honest boy".

### Try it yourself
Complete the sentences: (1) I ate ___ orange. (2) ___ moon shone brightly. (3) Baraka is ___ clever boy. (4) She wore ___ old dress. Answers: an, The, a, an.

## PAGE 5: Using articles in your own writing
Now use articles in your own sentences and stories. Here is a short paragraph about a family. Read it aloud and find the articles.

"Mama Achieng has a small shop near the bus stage. Every morning she opens the shop at seven o'clock. She sells eggs, bread and milk. One day an old man came and bought a loaf of bread. The old man thanked her and left. Then a little girl came. She wanted an egg for her mother. Mama Achieng gave her the biggest egg."

### Count the articles
In order, the nine articles are a, the, the, an, a, the, a, an, the. Notice that "a" and "an" appear when new people or things arrive. "The" appears when we speak of something already known, or a special one such as "the biggest egg".

### Activity ideas
• Pick a paragraph from a newspaper, magazine or a story book and circle all the articles. Sort them into a, an and the.
• Write five sentences about your family, using each of the articles at least once.
• On a phone, tablet or computer, type your sentences and share them with a friend, but only through a device and platform that your teacher or parent has approved.

### Remember
• a before consonant sounds, an before vowel sounds, the for particular things.
• Never use a or an with plural nouns.
• Do not use the before the names of people or of most towns and countries.

## QUIZ
1. Which sentence is correct?
   A) She ate a orange.
   B) She ate an orange.
   C) She ate the an orange.
   D) She ate orange an.
   Answer: B
   Why: Orange starts with a vowel sound, so we use an.
2. Choose the correct article: "He waited for ___ hour."
   A) a
   B) an
   C) the
   D) no article
   Answer: B
   Why: The h in hour is silent, so the word starts with a vowel sound and needs an.
3. Which word is a definite article?
   A) a
   B) an
   C) the
   D) some
   Answer: C
   Why: The is the only definite article. A and an are indefinite articles.
4. Which sentence uses an article wrongly?
   A) The moon is bright.
   B) I saw a hen.
   C) She has a books.
   D) He is an honest man.
   Answer: C
   Why: A cannot be used with a plural noun such as books.
5. Fill in the blank: "I bought a mango. ___ mango was sweet."
   A) A
   B) An
   C) The
   D) Some
   Answer: C
   Why: The mango has already been mentioned, so we say the mango.
6. Write a or an before each word: (a) ___ uniform (b) ___ egg (c) ___ umbrella (d) ___ tall boy. (2 marks)
   Answer: (a) a uniform (b) an egg (c) an umbrella (d) a tall boy
   Why: Half a mark each. Uniform starts with the consonant sound "yoo".
7. Explain why we say "an hour" but "a house". (3 marks)
   Answer: We choose a or an by the first sound, not the first letter. Hour starts with a vowel sound because the h is silent, so it takes an. House starts with the consonant sound /h/, so it takes a.
   Why: 1 mark for saying the sound matters, 1 mark for hour, 1 mark for house.
8. Rewrite the paragraph with the correct articles: "Chebet has ___ aunt. ___ aunt lives in Eldoret and works as ___ nurse." (3 marks)
   Answer: Chebet has an aunt. The aunt lives in Eldoret and works as a nurse.
   Why: 1 mark for each correct blank: an, The, a.
9. Write three sentences about your family, using a, an and the once each. (3 marks)
   Answer: Any three correct sentences, for example: I have a sister. She has an umbrella. The umbrella is blue.
   Why: 1 mark for each sentence that uses the required article correctly.
