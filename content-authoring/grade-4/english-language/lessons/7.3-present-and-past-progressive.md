## PAGE 1: Actions that are in progress
Some actions are done in a moment: "She opened the door." Other actions go on for some time: "She is reading." The progressive form of the verb shows that an action is going on, or was going on, at a certain time. Some books call it the continuous form.

Look at these two sentences:
• Kamau reads a book. (He does this often, or in general.)
• Kamau is reading a book. (He is doing it right now.)

The second sentence uses the present progressive. It tells us the action is happening now.

### How to form the present progressive
Use am, is or are, and add -ing to the main verb.
• I am + reading = I am reading.
• He, she, it, or one person + is + verb-ing: She is cooking. The baby is sleeping.
• You, we, they, or many people + are + verb-ing: They are playing. We are singing.

### Examples
• I am writing a letter.
• The teacher is marking our books.
• The children are listening to the radio.
• The matatu is waiting at the stage.

### Try it
Look around your classroom. Say what three people are doing right now, using am, is or are and a verb with -ing.

## PAGE 2: Spelling verbs with -ing
Most verbs just add -ing: play, playing; read, reading; walk, walking. But some verbs change a little. Learn these three rules.

### Rule 1: Verbs ending in silent e drop the e
• make, making
• write, writing
• dance, dancing
• bake, baking
Careful: be, being; see, seeing. Here we keep the e.

### Rule 2: Short verbs ending in one vowel and one consonant double the last letter
• run, running
• sit, sitting
• swim, swimming
• stop, stopping
• hop, hopping
Compare "hopping" and "hoping". Hopping means jumping. Hoping comes from hope, and means wishing.

### Rule 3: Other verbs just add -ing
• play, playing (a verb ending in y keeps the y)
• visit, visiting
• open, opening
• wash, washing

### Special case
• lie, lying
• die, dying
The letters ie change to y before -ing.

### Practice
Write the -ing form of: come, jump, put, plant, dig, use, cut, drive.
Answers: coming, jumping, putting, planting, digging, using, cutting, driving.

## PAGE 3: Questions and negatives in the present progressive
To make a question, put am, is or are before the subject.
• You are listening. Are you listening?
• She is reading. Is she reading?
• They are working. What are they doing?

To make a negative sentence, put not after am, is or are.
• I am not sleeping.
• He is not running. (He isn't running.)
• We are not shouting. (We aren't shouting.)

### Short answers
• Are you eating? Yes, I am. / No, I am not.
• Is the bus coming? Yes, it is. / No, it is not.

### Using the present progressive
1. For an action happening now: "The farmer is digging."
2. For a temporary situation: "We are learning about computers this week."
3. For a plan in the near future: "We are visiting the museum tomorrow."

### Verbs we rarely use with -ing
Some verbs describe states, not actions, such as know, like, want, love and need. We say "I know the answer", not "I am knowing the answer". We say "I want water", not "I am wanting water".

### Try it
Look at this sentence: "The girls sing in the choir every Friday." Change it to show the girls are singing right now. The answer is: "The girls are singing in the choir."

## PAGE 4: The past progressive
The past progressive shows an action that was going on at some time in the past. Use was or were and add -ing.
• I was reading.
• He, she or it was reading.
• You, we or they were reading.

### Questions and negatives
• Was he sleeping? Yes, he was. / No, he was not (wasn't).
• Were they playing? Yes, they were. / No, they were not (weren't).
• I was not watching TV.
• We were not talking.

### Two actions at once
Use while to link two actions happening together:
• I was cooking while my sister was washing the plates.
• While Mother was sweeping, Father was fixing the gate.

### An action interrupted
A longer action may be interrupted by a shorter one. The long action uses the past progressive; the short one uses the simple past.
• I was walking to school when it started to rain.
• The children were singing when the teacher came in.

### Try it
Complete: "When the phone rang, I ____ (do) my homework." Answer: was doing.

## PAGE 5: Present or past progressive?
Choose was or were for the past and am, is or are for now.

### Time clues
• Now, at the moment, today, these days: present progressive.
• Yesterday, last night, at six o'clock yesterday, while, when: past progressive.

### Read this passage
It was Saturday afternoon. Baraka was sitting under a tree with his cousin Neema. She was showing him a video on a tablet. A friend was standing nearby. Baraka said, "What are you watching, Neema?" She answered, "I am watching a video on internet safety. Yesterday, I was watching a cartoon, but today I am learning something new."

### Find the forms
• Past progressive: was sitting, was showing, was standing, was watching.
• Present progressive: are you watching, I am watching, I am learning.

### Practise
Fill in the blank with the correct form.
1. Right now, the pupils ____ (read) silently. Answer: are reading.
2. Last night at eight o'clock, we ____ (eat) supper. Answer: were eating.
3. Look! The cows ____ (cross) the road. Answer: are crossing.
4. I ____ (sleep) when the storm began. Answer: was sleeping.
5. Why ____ she ____ (cry) when you saw her? Answer: was, crying.

### Remember
• Progressive means "in progress": the action is not finished.
• Present: am, is, are + verb-ing.
• Past: was, were + verb-ing.
• Check the spelling of the -ing word.

## QUIZ
1. Which sentence is in the present progressive?
   A) He plays football.
   B) He is playing football.
   C) He played football.
   D) He was playing football.
   Answer: B
   Why: The present progressive uses is + verb-ing, showing an action happening now.
2. Choose the correct -ing form of "swim".
   A) swiming
   B) swimeing
   C) swimming
   D) swimmng
   Answer: C
   Why: Short verbs ending in one vowel and one consonant double the last consonant.
3. Complete: "They ____ watching a film when the power went off."
   A) is
   B) was
   C) were
   D) are
   Answer: C
   Why: "They" takes "were" in the past progressive.
4. Which verb is not usually used in the progressive form?
   A) run
   B) know
   C) write
   D) sing
   Answer: B
   Why: "Know" describes a state, not an action in progress.
5. Write the -ing form of: make, run, play, lie. (2 marks)
   Answer: making, running, playing, lying
   Why: Half a mark each: drop the e, double the n, keep the y, change ie to y.
6. Rewrite as a question: "The children are singing." (2 marks)
   Answer: Are the children singing?
   Why: One mark for putting "Are" first, one mark for correct spelling and question mark.
7. Write a sentence with "while" using the past progressive. (3 marks)
   Answer: For example: Mother was cooking while I was doing my homework.
   Why: One mark for "while", one mark for "was/were", one mark for verb-ing forms used correctly.
8. Explain the difference between "I read a book" and "I am reading a book." (3 marks)
   Answer: "I read a book" (simple present) tells of a habit or a fact. "I am reading a book" tells us the action is happening now.
   Why: One mark for each meaning and one mark for a correct example.
