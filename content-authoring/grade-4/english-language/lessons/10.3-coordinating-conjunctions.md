## PAGE 1: Joining words and sentences
Imagine speaking only in short sentences: "I washed my hands. I ate lunch. I went to play." It sounds jumpy. We can join ideas with small linking words called conjunctions.

"I washed my hands and ate lunch, and then I went to play."

A conjunction is a word that joins words, phrases or sentences. Today you will learn the seven coordinating conjunctions:
and, but, or, yet, so, for, nor

A short way to remember them is FANBOYS:
F for, A and, N nor, B but, O or, Y yet, S so.

### What does "coordinating" mean?
It means the conjunction joins two parts that are equal in importance, such as two words of the same kind or two complete sentences.

### Examples
• Soap and water (joins two nouns)
• slowly but surely (joins two adverbs)
• Do you want tea or milk? (joins two choices)
• The floor is wet, so be careful. (joins two sentences)

### Try it
Find the conjunction in each sentence.
1. We swept and mopped the room. (and)
2. It was raining, but we played outside. (but)
3. Wash your hands, or you may fall ill. (or)

## PAGE 2: And, but, or
### and: adds
• I brush my teeth and wash my face.
• Amina and Juma cleaned the classroom.
• We swept the floor, and we wiped the desks.

### but: shows a difference or surprise
• The water looked clean, but it was not safe to drink.
• I wanted to play, but I had to finish sweeping.
• She is small but strong.

### or: gives a choice
• Will you use soap or ash to wash?
• We can walk to school or take a matatu.
• Wash your hands, or the germs will spread.

### Punctuation with joining sentences
When a conjunction joins two complete sentences, put a comma before it.
• The tap is broken, but the bucket is full.
• I finished my chores, and I went out to play.
When it only joins two words or short parts, no comma is needed.
• We need brooms and buckets.

### Examples for practice
Fill in and, but or or:
1. The toilet is clean ____ tidy. (and)
2. Do you want to sweep ____ mop? (or)
3. I am tired, ____ I will finish the job. (but)

## PAGE 3: So, for, yet, nor
### so: shows a result
• The floor was dirty, so we mopped it.
• It was late, so we went home.
The first part is the reason, and the second is the result.

### for: gives a reason (means "because")
• We washed our hands, for we did not want to spread germs.
• She stayed at home, for she was ill.
"For" is used in careful writing. In everyday speech, we usually say "because".

### yet: shows a surprise, like "but"
• The room was small, yet it was very clean.
• He was tired, yet he kept sweeping.

### nor: joins two negative ideas
"Nor" is used after a negative. The verb comes before the subject in the second part.
• She did not wash her hands, nor did she use soap.
• I have no broom, nor do I have a bucket.
• The tap does not work, nor can we find water.

### Compare "so" and "for"
• The rain fell, so the ground was wet. (rain is the reason; wet is the result)
• The ground was wet, for the rain had fallen. (wet is the effect; rain is the reason)

### Practice
Complete with so, for, yet or nor.
1. We had no soap, ____ did we have water. (nor)
2. The bin was full, ____ we emptied it. (so)
3. He is young, ____ he works hard. (yet)

### Question to ask
Ask yourself what the two parts are doing to one another.
• Adding? Use and.
• Different or opposite? Use but or yet.
• Choice? Use or.
• Result? Use so.
• Reason? Use for (or because).
• Both negative? Use nor.

### Not too many "and"s
Compare:
"I woke up and I washed and I ate and I went to school and I met my friends."
"I woke up, washed and ate. Then I went to school, and I met my friends."
Use different linking words and sentences of different lengths.

### Do not start too many sentences with a conjunction
In careful writing, keep conjunctions in the middle of sentences.

### Practise
Join each pair with a suitable conjunction.
1. I like tea. I do not like coffee. → I like tea, but I do not like coffee.
2. The water was dirty. We boiled it. → The water was dirty, so we boiled it.
3. You can walk. You can ride. → You can walk or you can ride.

## PAGE 5: Conjunctions in dialogue and speech
Read this dialogue in pairs. Look for the conjunctions.

Neema: Did you sweep the compound, or did your brother?
Juma: We both did. I swept, and he collected the rubbish.
Neema: The compound looks tidy, but the drain is blocked.
Juma: I saw it, yet I did not have a tool to clear it.
Neema: Let us ask Father, so he can help us.
Juma: Good idea. He has no time in the morning, nor does he like a dirty drain!

### Conjunctions found
or, and, but, yet, so, nor.

### Ask and answer
Practise in pairs. One learner asks a question and the other answers using a conjunction.
• "What will you do if the water is dirty?" "I will boil it, or I will not drink it."
• "Why did you wash the plates?" "They were dirty, so I washed them."
• "Why do you feel tired?" "I swept the whole house, and I washed the clothes."

### Typing sentences
If you type sentences on a tablet or computer, take care with the commas. Read them aloud after typing to check that they make sense.

### Word game
"Sentence bridge": one player says one half of a sentence, the next says a conjunction and the third finishes the sentence. "I was tired... but... I finished my work."

## PAGE 6: Writing with conjunctions
### Complete the sentences
1. We washed the dishes, ____ we swept the floor. (and)
2. The toilet was dirty, ____ we cleaned it. (so)
3. She wanted a bath, ____ there was no water. (but)
4. Will you clean the desk ____ the board? (or)
5. He did not eat, ____ did he drink. (nor)
6. The water was clear, ____ it was full of germs. (yet)

### Write a paragraph
Write four sentences about cleaning your classroom. Use and, but, so and or at least once.
Example: "We sweep the classroom, and we wipe the desks. The floor is dusty, so we use a wet cloth. Some pupils use brooms, or they use brushes. It takes time, but the room looks tidy."

### Common mistakes
• "Because" is not one of the seven, but it also joins sentences. Do not begin a sentence with "but" in formal writing.
• Do not use a comma between two short items: "soap, and water" is wrong; write "soap and water".
• "Nor" needs the verb before the subject in the second part: "nor did she", not "nor she did".

### Summary
• Conjunctions join words and sentences.
• The seven are for, and, nor, but, or, yet, so (FANBOYS).
• Use a comma before the conjunction when joining two full sentences.
• Choose the conjunction that matches the meaning.

## QUIZ
1. Which word is a coordinating conjunction?
   A) quickly
   B) but
   C) tidy
   D) broom
   Answer: B
   Why: "But" joins ideas. The other words are an adverb, adjective and noun.
2. Which conjunction shows a result? "The floor was dirty, ____ we mopped it."
   A) or
   B) nor
   C) so
   D) yet
   Answer: C
   Why: "So" shows that mopping was a result of the floor being dirty.
3. Which conjunction gives a choice?
   A) and
   B) or
   C) so
   D) yet
   Answer: B
   Why: "Or" offers two choices.
4. Which sentence is correct?
   A) She has no soap, nor does she have water.
   B) She has no soap, nor she does have water.
   C) She has no soap, nor water has she.
   D) She has no soap nor does have water she.
   Answer: A
   Why: After "nor" the helping verb comes before the subject.
5. Join with a suitable conjunction: "I was tired. I finished sweeping." (2 marks)
   Answer: I was tired, but I finished sweeping.
   Why: One mark for a suitable conjunction (but or yet) and one mark for the comma.
6. Name the seven coordinating conjunctions. (3 marks)
   Answer: for, and, nor, but, or, yet, so.
   Why: Award 3 marks for all seven, 2 marks for five or six, 1 mark for three or four.
7. Write one sentence for each: (a) and, (b) so. (2 marks)
   Answer: For example: (a) We swept and mopped the floor. (b) The tap was broken, so we used a bucket.
   Why: One mark for each correct sentence.
8. Explain the difference between "but" and "so" with an example of each. (3 marks)
   Answer: "But" shows a difference: "It was late, but we stayed." "So" shows a result: "It was late, so we left."
   Why: One mark for each explanation, one mark for correct examples.
