## PAGE 1: Words that tell how much or how many
Look at the two sentences. "I have books." "I have many books." The second one tells us more: it tells us how many. Words that tell us the amount or number of something are called quantifiers. They belong to the group called determiners, and they come before a noun.

In this lesson we learn four common quantifiers: much, many, some and any. To use them correctly we must first know two kinds of nouns.

### Countable nouns
These are things we can count one by one. They have a singular and a plural form.
• one book, two books
• a chair, ten chairs
• an egg, six eggs
• a pupil, thirty pupils

### Uncountable nouns
These are things we cannot count one by one. We measure them or think of them as a mass. They have no plural form.
• water, milk, rice, sugar, flour, sand
• money, time, homework, information

We say "some water", not "two waters". We can measure uncountable things with a container: a cup of milk, a kilogram of rice, a litre of water.

How can we quantify nouns we cannot count? We use words such as much, some and a lot of, or we measure them.

### Sort them
Sort these nouns: mango, oil, chair, salt, cow, homework, cup, air. Countable: mango, chair, cow, cup. Uncountable: oil, salt, homework, air.

## PAGE 2: Many and much
### many
Use many before plural countable nouns.
• There are many books in the library.
• How many eggs do you need?
• We do not have many chairs.

### much
Use much before uncountable nouns.
• There is not much milk in the jug.
• How much water do you drink?
• I do not have much time.

### Where we use much and many
Much and many are mostly used in questions and negative sentences.
• Do you have many friends? No, I do not have many friends.
• Is there much sugar? No, there is not much sugar.
In positive sentences we often say "a lot of" instead: We have a lot of books. There is a lot of sugar.

### Also useful
• too much / too many: more than is good. Too much sugar is bad for your teeth.
• so many / so much: a very large amount.

## PAGE 3: Some and any
### some
Use some in positive sentences with plural countable nouns and with uncountable nouns.
• I have some books.
• There is some water in the jug.
• She bought some mangoes and some rice.

Some is also used in questions when we offer or request something politely.
• Would you like some tea?
• Can I have some water, please?
• May I borrow some paper?

### any
Use any in questions and negative sentences, with plural countable nouns and uncountable nouns.
• Do you have any pencils?
• Is there any milk?
• I do not have any money.
• There are not any eggs left.

### Compare
• I have some sugar. (positive)
• Do you have any sugar? (question)
• I do not have any sugar. (negative)

### Not any and no
"There is not any milk" and "There is no milk" mean the same. Do not use both: never say "There is not no milk".

### Fill in some or any
1. She gave me ___ bread. (some)
2. Do you have ___ brothers? (any)
3. We do not have ___ chalk. (any)
4. Would you like ___ juice? (some)

## PAGE 4: Choosing the right quantifier
Ask yourself two questions.
1. Is the noun countable or uncountable?
2. Is the sentence a positive statement, a question or a negative statement?

### Decision guide
• Countable plural, question or negative: many, any.
• Uncountable, question or negative: much, any.
• Positive statement: some or a lot of.
• Offers and polite requests: some.

### Worked examples
• "There are ___ chairs in the hall." Positive statement, countable plural. Answer: some (or many chairs / a lot of).
• "Do we have ___ sugar?" Question, uncountable. Answer: any.
• "How ___ time do we have?" Time is uncountable. Answer: much.
• "How ___ pupils are in your class?" Pupils are countable. Answer: many.
• "She has not eaten ___ rice." Negative, uncountable. Answer: much or any.

### Common mistakes
• "How many water?" Water cannot be counted. Say "How much water?"
• "How much books?" Say "How many books?"
• "I do not have some money." Say "I do not have any money."
• "There is many milk." Say "There is a lot of milk."

### Maths link
In mathematics we ask "How many?" when we count and "How much?" when we measure. How many mangoes are in the basket? How much rice is in the bag?

## PAGE 5: Using quantifiers in speech and writing
### Poem
Read this rhyme aloud in a group.
How many cups? How much tea?
Some for you and some for me.
Any sugar? Just a little.
Many friends in the middle.
How much time? Not too much.
Enough to talk and enough to touch.

### Role-play a dialogue
Shop assistant: Good morning. Would you like some help?
Customer: Yes, please. Do you have any flour?
Shop assistant: Yes, we have some. How much do you need?
Customer: Two kilograms, please. Also, how many eggs are in a tray?
Shop assistant: There are thirty eggs in a tray.
Customer: I only need some eggs. I do not want many. Thank you.

Underline the quantifiers in the dialogue: some, any, some, much, many, some, many.

### Write a paragraph
Use much, many, some and any in a paragraph about your kitchen at home or about your classroom. For example: "There are many chairs in our classroom. We do not have much space, but we have some books and a few pictures. Is there any chalk on the desk?"

### On a device
Compose a short poem or paragraph on a computer or tablet. Include each quantifier at least once. Share with a friend and check each other's sentences.

### Summary
• many + plural countable nouns; much + uncountable nouns.
• some for positive sentences and polite offers; any for questions and negatives.
• A lot of can go with both kinds of nouns in positive sentences.

## QUIZ
1. Which noun is uncountable?
   A) chair
   B) egg
   C) milk
   D) pupil
   Answer: C
   Why: We cannot count milk one by one.
2. Choose the correct sentence.
   A) How many water do you drink?
   B) How much water do you drink?
   C) How much waters do you drink?
   D) How many waters do you drink?
   Answer: B
   Why: Water is uncountable, so we use much.
3. Complete: "Do you have ___ pencils?"
   A) any
   B) much
   C) a
   D) an
   Answer: A
   Why: Any is used in questions with plural countable nouns.
4. Which sentence is a polite offer?
   A) Would you like some tea?
   B) I have any tea.
   C) There is many tea.
   D) Do not have some tea.
   Answer: A
   Why: We use some in offers and polite requests.
5. Which is correct?
   A) I do not have some money.
   B) I do not have any money.
   C) I do not have many money.
   D) I have any money not.
   Answer: B
   Why: In negative sentences we use any, and money is uncountable.
6. Sort these into countable and uncountable: rice, mango, time, book. (2 marks)
   Answer: Countable: mango, book. Uncountable: rice, time.
   Why: 1 mark for the countable pair and 1 mark for the uncountable pair.
7. Fill in the blanks with much or many: (a) How ___ eggs? (b) How ___ sugar? (2 marks)
   Answer: (a) many (b) much
   Why: 1 mark for each correct word.
8. Correct each mistake: (a) "How much books are there?" (b) "There is not no milk." (2 marks)
   Answer: (a) How many books are there? (b) There is not any milk. (or There is no milk.)
   Why: 1 mark for each correction.
9. Write three sentences about your classroom using some, any and many. (3 marks)
   Answer: For example: There are many desks in our classroom. We have some pictures on the wall. Is there any chalk?
   Why: 1 mark for each correct sentence.
