## PAGE 1: Short and long vowels: /ɪ/ and /iː/
English has short vowels and long vowels. Changing the length changes the word, so we must listen carefully.

### /ɪ/ (short)
The mouth is relaxed and the sound is quick. It is the sound in:
• sit, ship, bit, live, fill, list
• pin, hit, chip, give, dish, wish

### /iː/ (long)
The lips spread a little, as in a smile, and the sound is held longer. It is the sound in:
• seat, sheep, beat, leave, feel, least
• pea, heat, cheap, green, sleep, three

### Minimal pairs
These pairs differ only in the vowel. Say each word slowly.
• sit and seat
• ship and sheep
• bit and beat
• live and leave
• fill and feel
• hill and heel
• chip and cheap

### In sentences
1. "The ship is near the sheep." (short then long)
2. "I will leave now, but I live here."
3. "Please sit on the seat."
4. "She will fill the bucket and feel happy."

### Common mistake
Some speakers say "ship" like "sheep". Listen to the difference. In "ship" you finish quickly. In "sheep" you smile and hold the sound.

### Try it
A partner says a word from a pair. You point to the picture or write down which word you heard.

## PAGE 2: The sounds /s/, /ʃ/ and /əʊ/
### /s/ and /ʃ/
Both sounds have no voice. For /s/, the tongue is close to the teeth and air hisses. For /ʃ/ (the sound of "sh"), the lips are a little rounded, the tongue is pulled back, and the sound is softer and fuller, as when you say "shh, be quiet".

/s/: sea, sock, sun, soap, seat, sell, sip, sign, mess
/ʃ/: she, shock, shoe, shop, sheet, shell, ship, sure, wash, fish

### Pairs
• sea and she
• sock and shock
• seat and sheet
• sip and ship
• Sam and sham
• sort and short

### Wash and clean
For our theme on hygiene, say these: soap, wash, shower, brush, clean, sweep, dish, hands, germs, rinse.

### /əʊ/
This vowel glides: begin with a relaxed "uh" and move to "oo" with rounded lips. It is the sound in:
• go, no, so, home, phone, open
• soap, boat, road, toe, coat
• slow, know, show, window

### Sentences
1. "She washes her hands with soap."
2. "Sam sips soup on the ship."
3. "Open the window and show me the road."
4. "Go slowly and know your way home."

## PAGE 3: Riddles
A riddle is a puzzle in words. It describes something without naming it, and the listener has to guess. Riddles are fun, and they teach us to listen to clues.

### Riddles to try
1. "I am used to wash the body. I make bubbles. I get smaller each time you use me. What am I?" (soap)
2. "I have a face but no eyes. I have hands, but I cannot clap. What am I?" (a clock)
3. "The more you take, the more you leave behind. What am I?" (footsteps)
4. "I come down but never go up. What am I?" (rain)
5. "I have a mouth but never speak. I run but never walk. What am I?" (a river)

### Sounds in riddles
Riddle 1 has words with /s/, /ʃ/ and /əʊ/: soap, wash, smaller. Say them carefully.

### How to tell a riddle
1. Speak clearly and slowly.
2. Look at your audience.
3. Give one clue at a time.
4. Pause to let the audience think.
5. Ask "What am I?"
6. Do not say the answer until they have tried.

### Make your own
Choose an object from the hygiene theme, such as a toothbrush, a broom or a bucket. Write three clues. Say the riddle to a partner and see if they can guess.
Example: "I have hairs but no head. I clean your teeth. What am I?" (a toothbrush)

### Be kind
If someone guesses wrongly, say "Nice try" and give another clue.

## PAGE 4: Listening actively to a speaker
Listening is not only about hearing. Active listening means you pay attention, show interest and think about what is said.

### Verbal cues (with words)
• "I see."
• "That is interesting."
• "Really?"
• "Could you say that again, please?"

### Non-verbal cues (without words)
• Look at the speaker.
• Nod your head.
• Smile or look serious to match the topic.
• Keep your body facing the speaker.
• Keep your hands still and quiet.

### An oral presentation
When someone gives a talk, follow these steps as a listener.
1. Listen for the main idea.
2. Note down key words.
3. Wait until the end to ask questions.
4. Ask a question or make a comment about what you heard.

### When you are the presenter
• Greet your audience.
• Speak clearly and look at everyone, not only at one person.
• Ask a question to involve the audience: "Has anybody here washed their hands today?"
• Use gestures to show what you mean.
• Thank your audience at the end.

### Try it
Give a one-minute talk on "Why we wash our hands." Ask your partners to nod, look at you and then ask one question at the end.

## PAGE 5: Interrupting politely
Sometimes you need to interrupt a speaker. Perhaps you did not hear, or there is an emergency. Do it politely.

### When it is fine to interrupt
• You did not hear or understand.
• Someone needs urgent help.
• You must leave.
Otherwise, wait for your turn.

### Polite phrases
• "Excuse me, may I say something?"
• "Sorry to interrupt, but could you repeat that?"
• "Pardon me, may I ask a question?"
• "Excuse me, I did not hear the last part."

### How to do it
1. Raise your hand or lift a finger.
2. Wait for the speaker to notice you.
3. Say "Excuse me" in a calm voice.
4. Speak briefly.
5. Say "Thank you."

### Rude interruptions to avoid
• Shouting over the speaker.
• Saying "Stop! You are wrong!"
• Talking to your neighbour while someone speaks.

### Role play
Two learners act. One is a teacher explaining how to wash hands. The other is a pupil who did not hear the second step.
Pupil: "Excuse me, Teacher, may I ask a question?"
Teacher: "Yes, go ahead."
Pupil: "Could you please repeat the second step?"
Teacher: "Of course. Wet your hands and add soap."
Pupil: "Thank you."

### Turn-taking in interviews and debates
In an interview, one person asks and the other answers. In a debate, speakers take turns and each has a fixed time. Keep to the time and respect the rules.

## PAGE 6: Vocabulary and practice
### Words for hygiene and sanitation
• hygiene: keeping yourself and things clean to stay healthy
• sanitation: clean toilets, drains and waste systems that protect health
• germs: tiny living things that may cause illness
• latrine: a simple toilet
• waste: things we throw away
• infection: an illness caused by germs
• scrub: to rub hard to clean
• rinse: to wash with clean water to take off soap
• dust: tiny dry bits that lie on things
• tidy: clean and in good order

### Use them in questions and answers
1. "Why should we scrub our hands with soap?" "To remove germs."
2. "What is sanitation?" "It is keeping toilets, drains and waste clean."

### An interview
Interview a classmate. Ask:
• "What do you do every morning to keep clean?"
• "How does your family get rid of waste?"
• "Why is a clean latrine important?"
Take turns, listen carefully, and remember to interrupt politely if you need to.

### Summary
• /ɪ/ is short (sit); /iː/ is long (seat).
• /s/ hisses (sea); /ʃ/ is softer (she).
• /əʊ/ glides from "uh" to "oo" (go).
• Riddles are clue puzzles; tell them clearly.
• Listen actively and interrupt politely.

## QUIZ
1. Which word has the long vowel /iː/?
   A) ship
   B) sit
   C) sheep
   D) bit
   Answer: C
   Why: "Sheep" has a long vowel. "Ship", "sit" and "bit" have a short /ɪ/.
2. Which pair differs in /s/ and /ʃ/?
   A) sea and she
   B) sit and seat
   C) go and no
   D) live and leave
   Answer: A
   Why: "Sea" has /s/ and "she" has /ʃ/.
3. Which phrase is a polite way to interrupt?
   A) Stop talking!
   B) You are wrong!
   C) Excuse me, may I ask a question?
   D) Be quiet!
   Answer: C
   Why: It is polite: it uses "Excuse me" and asks permission.
4. Which word has the sound /əʊ/?
   A) soap
   B) sip
   C) ship
   D) bit
   Answer: A
   Why: "Soap" has the gliding /əʊ/ vowel sound.
5. Give one riddle with its answer. (2 marks)
   Answer: For example: "I have hairs but no head, and I clean your teeth. What am I?" A toothbrush.
   Why: One mark for a clear riddle and one mark for the correct answer.
6. Write two ways to show a speaker that you are listening. (2 marks)
   Answer: For example: look at the speaker; nod your head; smile.
   Why: One mark for each correct way.
7. Explain when it is right to interrupt a speaker. (3 marks)
   Answer: When you did not hear or understand, when someone needs urgent help, or when you must leave. Do it politely, for example: "Excuse me, may I say something?"
   Why: Two marks for correct situations and one mark for a polite phrase.
8. Write the minimal pairs for these words: ship, seat, sock. (3 marks)
   Answer: ship and sheep (or sip); seat and sheet (or sit); sock and shock.
   Why: One mark for each correct partner word.
