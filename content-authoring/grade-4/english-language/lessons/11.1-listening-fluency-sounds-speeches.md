## PAGE 1: Listening fluently
Listening fluency means listening with attention, following the ideas, and understanding what you hear without missing important parts. Just as fluent readers read smoothly, fluent listeners take in a speaker's message smoothly.

### How to listen well to a speech
1. Sit comfortably and face the speaker.
2. Put away anything that could distract you.
3. Listen for the topic: what is the speech about?
4. Listen for the main points, often signalled by words such as first, second, finally.
5. Listen for the speaker's feelings, shown by the voice.
6. Remember at least three facts.
7. Clap or thank the speaker at the end.

### Short speeches on sports
A short speech may be given at a school sports day, to thank a team, or to welcome guests. Here is an example.

Listening script (read aloud to your class):
"Good morning, everyone. Welcome to our sports day. Today, all of us will run, jump and throw. Some will win, and some will not, but everyone who tries is a winner. Please cheer for every team. Thank you, and let the games begin."

### After listening, ask
• What was the occasion? (a school sports day)
• What did the speaker want us to do? (cheer for every team)
• What did the speaker mean by "everyone who tries is a winner"? (Taking part and trying hard matters, not only winning.)

### Try it
Listen to your teacher read the script. Then tell a partner two things the speaker said.

## PAGE 2: The diphthong /aʊ/
A diphthong is a vowel sound that glides from one sound to another within a single syllable. The sound /aʊ/ starts with an open mouth, as in "ah", and glides to a rounded "oo".

### Words with /aʊ/
• cow, now, how, brown, down, town
• house, mouse, out, about, shout, round
• ground, sound, found, crowd, loud, proud
• mouth, south, count, scout

### Sports words with /aʊ/
• crowd (people who watch a game)
• ground (the pitch)
• shout (to call loudly)
• round (the ball is round)
• count (to say numbers in order)

### Sentences
1. "The crowd shouted loudly when our team scored."
2. "The brown cow walked down to the town."
3. "We found the ball on the ground."
4. "The players were proud of the team."

### Careful
Do not shorten the sound to "o" as in "hot". Say "house", not "hos". Open your mouth first, then round your lips.

### Try it
Say each sentence twice. Ask a partner to clap once for each /aʊ/ sound they hear. In sentence 1, they clap for "crowd", "shouted", "loudly" (three claps).

## PAGE 3: The sounds /ð/ and /θ/
These two sounds are both written "th", but one is voiced and one is not.

### /ð/ (voiced)
Put your tongue lightly between your teeth. Let the air move, and use your voice. You should feel a buzz in your throat.
• this, that, these, those
• the, then, there, they
• mother, father, brother, weather, together
• with, smooth

### /θ/ (unvoiced)
Put your tongue lightly between your teeth. Blow air, without using your voice. There is no buzz.
• think, thank, thin, thick
• three, throw, thumb, thirsty
• mouth, month, bath, fourth, both, earth, path

### Common mistakes
• Saying "dis" for "this" (/d/ instead of /ð/).
• Saying "tink" or "sink" for "think" (/t/ or /s/ instead of /θ/).

### Pairs to practise
• then and thin
• they and thigh
• breathe and breath (breathe has /ð/; breath has /θ/)

### Sports sentences
1. "The third player threw the ball through the goal."
2. "Thank them for their help in the game."
3. "This month, my brother and I run together on the path."
4. "They threw the ball, then they thanked the coach."

## PAGE 4: The cluster "sk" and vocabulary of sports
### The cluster /sk/
Say /s/ and /k/ quickly together. It can be at the start, middle or end.
• skip, skate, sky, skin, skill
• school, skirt, sketch
• ask, task, desk, mask, risk, basket
• basketball, discus (a throwing event in athletics)

### Sentences
1. "Ask the skilled skater to show a skill."
2. "I skip every day on the school ground."
3. "Put the ball in the basket on the desk."

### Vocabulary: sports words
• win: to be the best in a contest
• lose: to not win
• football, netball, handball: team games played with a ball
• athletics: sports such as running, jumping and throwing
• race: a contest to see who is fastest
• match: a game between two teams
• referee: the official who controls a game
• score: to get a point or goal
• cheer: to shout to encourage
• whistle: a small instrument that makes a sharp sound
• kit: the clothes and equipment for a game
• team: a group who play together
• captain: the leader of a team
• coach: a person who trains the team
• competition: a contest
• stadium: a big place where sports are played
• field: a piece of open land for sports

### Sentences
1. "The referee blew the whistle to begin the match."
2. "The captain led the team onto the field."
3. "The coach told the team to practise hard."

## PAGE 5: Interpreting nonverbal cues
Not everything a speaker says is in words. The face, the eyes, the hands and the body send messages too. These are nonverbal cues.

### Facial expressions
• A smile: happy, friendly
• A frown: worried or cross
• Wide eyes: surprised
• A stare at the floor: shy or sad

### Gestures
• A thumbs-up: "well done" or "good"
• A raised hand: "stop" or "I have a question"
• Clapping: praise
• Pointing: "look there" (not polite when pointing at people)

### Posture
• Standing tall: confident
• Slouching: tired or bored
• Arms crossed: may show closed feelings, but sometimes only cold

### In sports
• A referee raises a hand to signal a foul or to stop the game.
• Players clap to show teamwork.
• A coach waves an arm to tell players to move.

### Using cues well
When speaking, make sure your cues match your words. If you say "Congratulations!" with a frown, the listener will be confused. In some cultures, gestures may mean different things, so be careful with people from other places.

### Try it
In pairs, one learner acts a feeling without words (happy, tired, surprised). The other guesses.

## PAGE 6: Making a speech and practising
### Planning a three-minute speech
A three-minute speech is about 300 words spoken slowly. For a first attempt, prepare a shorter speech of about 100 words and practise it.

Use this plan:
1. Greeting: "Good morning, everyone."
2. Topic: "Today I will talk about my favourite game."
3. Point 1: Name the game and how it is played.
4. Point 2: Why you like it.
5. Point 3: What it teaches (teamwork, health, fairness).
6. Closing: "Thank you for listening."

### Model
"Good morning, everyone. Today I will talk about netball. Netball is played by two teams, with seven players on each side. Players pass the ball and try to shoot it through a ring. I like netball because it is fast and fun. It teaches us to work together. Thank you for listening."

### Poem about games
Recite this together.
"Run and jump and throw and catch,
Every game is a fair match.
Win or lose, we shake a hand,
Sport unites our happy land."

### Checklist
• Did I use the sounds /aʊ/, /ð/ and /θ/ correctly?
• Did I speak clearly, and not too fast?
• Did I use my face and hands?
• Did I look at the audience?

### Summary
• Listen for the topic, main points and feelings.
• /aʊ/ as in "house"; /ð/ as in "this"; /θ/ as in "think".
• Nonverbal cues are messages too.
• Plan your speech: greeting, points, closing.

## QUIZ
1. Which word has the sound /aʊ/?
   A) hot
   B) crowd
   C) cat
   D) bell
   Answer: B
   Why: "Crowd" has the diphthong /aʊ/, as in "cow".
2. Which word begins with the voiced /ð/ sound?
   A) think
   B) three
   C) this
   D) thumb
   Answer: C
   Why: In "this" the "th" is voiced, but in the other words it is not.
3. Which word contains the cluster /sk/?
   A) ball
   B) desk
   C) goal
   D) race
   Answer: B
   Why: The word ends with "sk".
4. What does a referee do?
   A) Sells tickets
   B) Controls a game
   C) Trains the team
   D) Cleans the field
   Answer: B
   Why: A referee is the official who makes sure the game is played by the rules.
5. Give two words with /θ/ and two with /ð/. (4 marks)
   Answer: /θ/: think, three, thank, mouth. /ð/: this, that, the, mother.
   Why: One mark for each correct word.
6. Explain what a nonverbal cue is and give two examples. (3 marks)
   Answer: A nonverbal cue is a message sent without words. Examples: a smile shows happiness; a thumbs-up shows approval.
   Why: One mark for the meaning and one mark for each example.
7. Write four sentences for a short speech about your favourite game. (4 marks)
   Answer: Any speech with a greeting, the name of the game, a reason and a closing.
   Why: One mark each for the greeting, the game, the reason and the closing.
8. Why should our gestures match our words? (2 marks)
   Answer: If they do not match, the listener may be confused about what we mean.
   Why: One mark for confusion and one mark for a clear reason.
