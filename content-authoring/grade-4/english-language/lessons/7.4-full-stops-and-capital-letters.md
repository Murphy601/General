## PAGE 1: Why we punctuate
Imagine talking without stopping to breathe. Your listener would be lost. Punctuation marks are the small signs that let a reader "breathe" and understand where ideas begin and end.

Compare these two pieces of writing.
Without punctuation: my name is wanjiru i live in nakuru i go to school by bus
With punctuation: My name is Wanjiru. I live in Nakuru. I go to school by bus.

The second piece is much easier to read. It uses two things:
• Capital letters at the beginning of sentences and for special names.
• Full stops at the end of statements.

### Punctuation marks you already know
• Full stop . (also called a period)
• Comma ,
• Question mark ?
• Exclamation mark !
This lesson is about the full stop and the capital letter. You will meet the comma and the question mark in a later lesson.

### Try it
Read the group of words below aloud: "the bus came the driver stopped we got in". Where would you pause? Write it again with capital letters and full stops. Answer: The bus came. The driver stopped. We got in.

## PAGE 2: The full stop
A sentence is a group of words that makes complete sense. It has a subject (who or what) and a verb (what is done).

We put a full stop at the end of:
1. A statement, which tells something: "The sun is hot."
2. A command, which tells someone to do something: "Close the door."
3. Many abbreviations: "Dr. Achieng", "Mr. Kamau". Some books write these without the full stop, so follow what your teacher says.

### Examples
• Farmers plant maize in March.
• Please pass me the chalk.
• We washed our hands before lunch.

### Full stop inside initials
When we write a person's name with initials, we put a full stop after each initial.
• J. K. Mwangi
• D. W. Otieno
• A. Otieno

### Full stops or none?
In modern writing, many people leave out the full stop in short forms which are made of capital letters, such as KBC, TV and USA. You can write them without full stops.

### Common mistake: the run-on sentence
Wrong: I went to the market I bought tomatoes and onions we went home.
Right: I went to the market. I bought tomatoes and onions. We went home.

### Practice
Add full stops and capital letters. "mother cooked ugali we ate together" Answer: Mother cooked ugali. We ate together.

## PAGE 3: Capital letters: the first rules
A capital letter is a big letter such as A, B, C. We use a capital letter in these places.

### 1. The first word of every sentence
• The rain has stopped.
• Open your books.

### 2. The word I
• Yesterday I read a story.
• Peter and I went home.

### 3. Names of people
• Amina, Wafula, Grace, Mr. Ochieng
Also use capitals for a person's title with the name: Teacher Mary, Doctor Salim.

### 4. Days, months and holidays
• Monday, Friday
• January, October
• Christmas, Eid, Easter, Madaraka Day

Notice that the names of the seasons (dry season, rainy season) and the word "week" do not need capitals.

### Try it
Write correctly: "on monday i met wambui at school." Answer: On Monday I met Wambui at school.

### Careful
Do not use capital letters for the middle of a word. "wAter" is wrong. Every word starts with a small letter unless one of the rules above applies.

## PAGE 4: Proper nouns
A common noun is a general name, such as boy, town, river, mountain. It starts with a small letter.
A proper noun is the special name of a particular person, place or thing. It always starts with a capital letter.

### More proper nouns
• Countries and towns: Kenya, Uganda, Mombasa, Eldoret
• Rivers, lakes and mountains: River Nzoia, Lake Victoria, Mount Elgon
• Schools and buildings: Green Hills Primary School
• Languages and nationalities: Kiswahili, English, Kenyan
• Names of organisations and brands.

### In sentences
• The girl lives near a river. (common nouns)
• Akinyi lives near River Nzoia. (proper nouns)
• We climbed a mountain. We climbed Mount Longonot.

### Try it
Write the correct form: "we travelled from nairobi to kisumu on saturday." Answer: We travelled from Nairobi to Kisumu on Saturday.

## PAGE 5: Abbreviations and dictation
An abbreviation is a short way of writing a word or group of words.

### Common abbreviations
• Mr. (Mister), Mrs. (a married woman), Dr. (Doctor)
• St. (Saint or Street), Rd. (Road)
• a.m. (before noon), p.m. (after noon)
• PTA (Parents' Teachers' Association), TV (television)
• KBC (Kenya Broadcasting Corporation)

### Your school
Write your school's full name and then its short form. For example: St. Mary's Primary School may be shortened to St. Mary's Primary. If your school has initials, write each with capitals.

### Punctuating sentences from dictation
When your teacher dictates a sentence:
1. Listen to the whole sentence once.
2. Write it, starting with a capital letter.
3. Add capitals for names.
4. Put a full stop at the end.
5. Read it back and check.

### A short dictation to practise
"On Saturday, Mr. Otieno took us to Lake Naivasha. We saw birds near the water. I took photographs."
Check: There are capitals at Saturday, Mr., Otieno, Lake Naivasha, We and I. Each sentence ends with a full stop.

### Digital tip
When you type on a phone or tablet, the first letter of a sentence often becomes a capital automatically. But it will not fix names. Always check proper nouns yourself.

## PAGE 6: Putting it all together
Read this paragraph and correct it.

"last friday our class visited the national museum in nairobi. we saw old tools and pictures. mr. juma told us about the history of kenya. i enjoyed the visit."

### Corrected version
Last Friday our class visited the National Museum in Nairobi. We saw old tools and pictures. Mr. Juma told us about the history of Kenya. I enjoyed the visit.

### Checklist
• Does each sentence begin with a capital letter?
• Does each sentence end with a full stop?
• Are names of people and places written with capitals?
• Are the days and months written with capitals?
• Is the word I always a capital?

### A game
Punctuation ladder: A group writes a sentence without punctuation on a card. Another group adds the capital letters and full stops. Each correct card moves your team one step up the ladder.

### Summary
• A full stop ends a statement or a command.
• A capital letter begins a sentence and every proper noun.
• Days, months and holidays need capitals.
• Always check your work.

## QUIZ
1. Which sentence is punctuated correctly?
   A) the boy ran home
   B) The boy, ran home
   C) The boy ran home.
   D) The boy ran home,
   Answer: C
   Why: It begins with a capital letter and ends with a full stop.
2. Which word needs a capital letter?
   A) river
   B) monday
   C) week
   D) rainy
   Answer: B
   Why: Days of the week begin with capital letters: Monday.
3. Which of these is a proper noun?
   A) mountain
   B) girl
   C) Kisumu
   D) market
   Answer: C
   Why: Kisumu is the name of a particular town, so it is a proper noun.
4. What is the correct way to write the initials of a person named Grace Wanjiku Kamau?
   A) G W K
   B) G. W. K.
   C) g.w.k.
   D) G,W,K
   Answer: B
   Why: Capital letters followed by full stops are used in initials.
5. Rewrite with correct capital letters and full stops: "we visited lake victoria in august" (2 marks)
   Answer: We visited Lake Victoria in August.
   Why: One mark for capitals at We, Lake Victoria and August; one mark for the full stop.
6. Explain the difference between a common noun and a proper noun, with one example of each. (3 marks)
   Answer: A common noun is a general name, for example "town". A proper noun is the special name of a particular person, place or thing, for example "Nakuru". A proper noun starts with a capital letter.
   Why: One mark for each meaning and one mark for the examples.
7. Give the full stop and capital letter version: "mother went to the market she bought fish and vegetables" (3 marks)
   Answer: Mother went to the market. She bought fish and vegetables.
   Why: One mark for the first capital and full stop, one mark for the second capital, one mark for the last full stop.
8. Write three sentences about your school. Use capital letters and full stops correctly. (3 marks)
   Answer: Any three correct sentences with capitals at the start, capitals for proper nouns, and full stops at the end.
   Why: One mark for each correctly punctuated sentence.
