## PAGE 1: Celebration words and listening for sounds
Families celebrate many special days. We hold birthday parties, weddings, graduations and baptisms. We sing, dance, share food and give gifts. In this lesson you will learn words about celebrations and practise six sounds so that you can speak clearly at any party.

### Celebration vocabulary
• celebrate: to do something special because a day or event is important.
• ceremony: a special event with set actions, such as a wedding or a graduation.
• invitation: a card or message that asks a person to come to an event.
• decorate: to make a place look pretty with ribbons, balloons or flowers.
• feast: a big and special meal.
• present or gift: something you give to a person to show love.
• guest: a person who is invited to a party or a home.
• graduation: a ceremony when a person finishes a course of study and gets a certificate.

### Target sounds
• /ɜː/ as in bird, girl, work, turn, birthday
• /ɑː/ as in car, star, party, farm, market
• /ɔɪ/ as in boy, toy, coin, enjoy, noise
• /aɪ/ as in five, time, kite, invite, cry
• /l/ as in light, load, play
• /r/ as in right, road, cry

Listen as your teacher reads a sentence: "The girl in the purple shirt heard a bird at the party." Which words have the sound /ɜː/? Girl, purple, shirt, heard, bird. Clap when you hear it.

## PAGE 2: The vowel sounds /ɜː/ and /ɑː/
Both sounds are long, so hold them a little. For /ɜː/, your lips are relaxed and your tongue is in the middle of your mouth. It is the sound in "bird". For /ɑː/, open your mouth wide as if a doctor is looking at your throat. It is the sound in "star".

### Words with /ɜː/
bird, girl, first, third, world, work, worm, turn, burn, nurse, church, birthday, purple
Notice that the sound can be spelt in different ways: ir, or, ur, er. Do not say the r strongly after the vowel.

### Words with /ɑː/
car, card, farm, park, star, arm, dark, smart, party, market, father, calm, after
Practise: We are at a party in a large yard. The cards are on the far table.

### Contrast pairs
These pairs sound different only because of the vowel. The first word has /ɜː/ and the second has /ɑː/.
• hurt, heart
• firm, farm
• burn, barn
Say each pair slowly and listen to the difference.

### Say it aloud
The first girl turned at the third bird. The smart farmer parked his car at the market.

## PAGE 3: The vowel sounds /ɔɪ/ and /aɪ/
These are diphthongs. A diphthong is a sound that begins with one vowel and glides into another in a single syllable.

### /ɔɪ/
Begin with your lips rounded as if you say "or", then slide to "ee". You hear it in boy, toy, joy, coin, join, noise, voice, enjoy, oil, soil.
Sentence: The boy enjoyed the noise at the party.

### /aɪ/
Begin with an open mouth "ah", then slide to "ee". You hear it in five, time, kite, ride, bike, light, night, my, cry, fly, invite, smile.
Sentence: I invite five guests at nine at night.

### Spelling can trick you
Different letters can make the same sound. In "boy" and "coin", /ɔɪ/ is spelt oy and oi. In "cry", "five" and "night", /aɪ/ is spelt y, i-e and igh.

### Sort the words
Put each word under the correct sound: enjoy, kite, coin, smile, toy, light, oil, bike.
/ɔɪ/: enjoy, coin, toy, oil.
/aɪ/: kite, smile, light, bike.

## PAGE 4: The sounds /l/ and /r/
Many learners mix up /l/ and /r/. The tongue does a different job for each.

### How to say /l/
Touch the tip of your tongue on the ridge behind your top front teeth and let the air flow around the sides of your tongue. Your tongue tip stays touching the ridge while the sound is made.

### How to say /r/
Pull your tongue back a little and curl the tip up, but do not touch the roof of the mouth. Round your lips slightly. The tongue tip touches nothing.

### Minimal pairs
• load, road
• light, right
• lake, rake
• long, wrong
• lice, rice
• fly, fry
• play, pray
• clown, crown
• glass, grass
• collect, correct

Say both words in each pair. Ask a friend to read one word without showing you, and you point to the word you heard.

### Sentences
• Read the right book in the light.
• The long road is wrong for a lorry.
• The clown wore a crown at the party.
• Bring a load of rice to the wedding.

### Tongue twister
Red lorry, yellow lorry. Say it three times slowly. Then faster. Watch that each /r/ and /l/ is clear.

## PAGE 5: Using the words in real speaking
Now put the words and sounds to work in a short talk about a celebration. Speak in full sentences and pay attention to your sounds.

### Model talk (read aloud)
"On Saturday, our family held a birthday party for my little brother. My aunt came in a car and brought a big cake. My father hung balloons and ribbons to decorate the yard. The guests arrived at five. We sang, danced and enjoyed the feast. My brother received a toy lorry as a present. It was the best party of the year."

### Listen and identify
Read the talk again. Find one word for each sound.
• /ɜː/: birthday
• /ɑː/: party, car, yard
• /ɔɪ/: enjoyed, toy
• /aɪ/: arrived, five
• /l/: lorry, little
• /r/: ribbons, arrived, received

### Activities
1. Work in a group. Plan a small celebration for your class. Choose a name for it and decide what guests will do.
2. Write an invitation using at least five words from this lesson.
3. Role-play a party. One learner is the host, and others are guests. Use polite words and correct sounds.
4. Record yourselves on a device, if your teacher allows, and listen to check that your sounds are clear.

### Remember
Clear sounds make clear speech. Say every word fully, listen to others, and ask for help if you are not sure how to say a word.

## QUIZ
1. Which word has the sound /ɔɪ/?
   A) bird
   B) toy
   C) car
   D) kite
   Answer: B
   Why: Toy has the diphthong /ɔɪ/ spelt oy.
2. Which pair of words is a minimal pair for /l/ and /r/?
   A) load, road
   B) boy, toy
   C) bird, girl
   D) five, nine
   Answer: A
   Why: Load and road are different only in the first sound, /l/ and /r/.
3. Which word contains the sound /ɑː/?
   A) coin
   B) light
   C) farm
   D) burn
   Answer: C
   Why: Farm has the long vowel /ɑː/. Burn has /ɜː/.
4. What does the word "invitation" mean?
   A) A meal
   B) A message that asks someone to come to an event
   C) A gift
   D) A song at a party
   Answer: B
   Why: An invitation invites a guest to an event.
5. Which word has the sound /aɪ/?
   A) night
   B) noise
   C) girl
   D) park
   Answer: A
   Why: Night has /aɪ/ spelt igh.
6. Sort these words under /ɔɪ/ and /aɪ/: boy, kite, coin, bike. (2 marks)
   Answer: /ɔɪ/: boy, coin. /aɪ/: kite, bike.
   Why: 1 mark for each correct pair.
7. Write two words that differ only by /l/ and /r/ and use one in a sentence. (3 marks)
   Answer: For example: light and right. The right book is under the light.
   Why: 1 mark for each of the two words in a correct minimal pair and 1 mark for a correct sentence.
8. Describe how the tongue is placed to make the /l/ sound. (2 marks)
   Answer: The tip of the tongue touches the ridge behind the top front teeth and air flows around the sides.
   Why: 1 mark for the tongue tip on the ridge and 1 mark for air flowing around the sides.
9. Write an invitation of three sentences to a birthday party. Use the words "invite", "decorate" and "feast". (3 marks)
   Answer: For example: I invite you to my birthday party on Saturday. We shall decorate the yard with balloons. There will be a feast at four o'clock.
   Why: 1 mark for each sentence that uses its word correctly.
