## PAGE 1: What is a preposition?
A preposition is a word that shows the relationship between a noun (or pronoun) and another word in the sentence. Often it tells where something is, or where it is going.

• The book is on the table. (position: where is the book?)
• The boy walked to the market. (direction: where did he walk?)
• The bank is near the school.

A preposition comes before a noun or pronoun. The noun or pronoun after it is called its object. In "on the table", "on" is the preposition and "the table" is its object.

### Why we need prepositions
Compare: "The money is the box." This does not make sense. "The money is in the box." "The money is under the box." "The money is behind the box." Each preposition gives a different meaning. Prepositions help us to say exactly where people and things are.

### Two main groups in this lesson
• Prepositions of position: they tell where something is.
• Prepositions of direction: they tell where something is going.

### Try it
Look at your desk. Say where three things are. Example: "My pencil is on the desk. My bag is under the desk."

## PAGE 2: Prepositions of position
Here are common prepositions of position.

• in: inside something. "The coins are in the purse."
• on: touching the top surface. "The receipt is on the counter."
• under: below something, with space between. "The cat is under the table."
• above: higher than, not touching. "The clock is above the door."
• below: lower than. "The floor is below the desk."
• behind: at the back of. "The shop is behind the bank."
• in front of: at the front of. "The bus stopped in front of the market."
• between: in the middle of two things. "The bank is between the shop and the school."
• beside / next to: at the side of. "The teller sits beside the door."
• near: close to. "I live near the market."
• opposite: facing. "The post office is opposite the bank."
• among: in a group of more than two. "The girl stood among the vendors."
• at: at a point. "We met at the gate."
• inside / outside: within or not within. "Wait outside the shop."

### Careful
"In" is inside; "on" is on a surface; "at" is at a point or place. Say "in the classroom", "on the wall", "at the gate".

## PAGE 3: Prepositions of direction
Prepositions of direction show movement from one place to another.

• to: towards a place. "She went to the bank."
• from: the starting point. "He came from the market."
• into: moving to the inside. "The boy ran into the shop."
• out of: moving from the inside to the outside. "She took the coins out of her pocket."
• through: from one side to the other, within. "We walked through the gate."
• across: from one side to the other, on the surface. "They walked across the road."
• along: following a line. "We walked along the river."
• up / down: to a higher or lower place. "Climb up the hill. Walk down the stairs."
• towards: in the direction of. "The car drove towards the town."
• past: going by. "The bus goes past the school."
• around: in a circle or on all sides. "They ran around the field."
• over: from one side to the other, above. "The ball flew over the fence."
• off: away from a surface. "The book fell off the table."

### Position or direction?
• Position: "The cat is on the roof." (where it is)
• Direction: "The cat jumped onto the roof." (where it went)
• Position: "The boy is in the shop." Direction: "The boy went into the shop."

### Examples on a trip to the bank
"We left our home and walked along the road. We went past the school and across the bridge. Then we walked through the market and into the bank."

### Try it
Give directions from your classroom to the school gate. Use at least three prepositions: "Go out of the classroom, walk along the corridor, and go past the office."

## PAGE 4: Using prepositions in sentences
### Fill in the gaps
Choose from: in, on, under, between, behind, to, from, through.
1. The coins are ____ the box. (in)
2. The letter is ____ the table. (on)
3. The cat sleeps ____ the bed. (under)
4. The bank is ____ the post office and the school. (between)
5. The boy hid ____ the tree. (behind)
6. We walk ____ school every day. (to)
7. She came ____ the market. (from)
8. The bus went ____ the tunnel. (through)

### Prepositions that go with words
Some words go with certain prepositions.
• arrive at (a small place) or in (a town): "We arrived at the bank. We arrived in Nairobi."
• good at: "She is good at maths."
• afraid of: "I am afraid of thieves."
• pay for: "I paid for the book."
• depend on: "It depends on the price."
• listen to: "Listen to the teacher."
• look at: "Look at the picture."
• wait for: "Wait for me."
• borrow from: "I borrowed a book from the library."

### Preposition of time
Some prepositions also show time: at six o'clock, on Monday, in June.

### A common mistake
• Wrong: "I am afraid from dogs." Right: "I am afraid of dogs."
• Wrong: "He is in the bus." Right: "He is on the bus." (We say "on the bus", "on the train", "in the car".)

## PAGE 5: Reading and matching
### Read this passage
"After school, Neema walked along the path to the market. She stopped in front of a shop and looked at the fruit on the shelves. There were mangoes beside the bananas and oranges under the table. She took some coins out of her bag and paid for two mangoes. Then she walked past the bank and across the bridge to her home."

### Find the prepositions
along, to, in front of, at, on, beside, under, out of, for, past, across, to.
Position: in front of, on, beside, under. Direction: along, to, out of, past, across.

### Match to pictures
Draw a quick picture for each sentence, or match sentences to pictures.
1. The ball is under the chair.
2. The bird is on the wall.
3. The girl is between two boys.
4. The car drove through the tunnel.

### Substitution exercise
Change the preposition and see how the meaning changes:
"The bag is ____ the desk." on / under / beside / behind.
Each gives a new picture.

### Treasure hunt
Hide an object in the classroom. Give directions using prepositions: "Walk to the door. Go past the teacher's table. Look under the box beside the window." Your partner follows.

### Charts
Make a preposition chart. Write the preposition, draw an example and write a sentence. Display it in class.

## PAGE 6: Practice, money link and summary
### Prepositions in a money context
• "The money is in the bank."
• "She pays tax to the government."
• "He borrowed money from his uncle."
• "The parking fee is on the sign."
• "Put the coins into the tin."
• "Queue in front of the counter."

### Complete the story
Complete with a suitable preposition.
"Baraka went (1)____ the bank (2)____ his mother. They walked (3)____ the queue and stood (4)____ the counter. The cashier sat (5)____ a glass window. She put the money (6)____ a bag and gave it (7)____ Baraka's mother."
Possible answers: (1) to (2) with (3) into (4) in front of (5) behind (6) into (7) to.

### Write your own
Write five sentences about your home or school. Use in, on, under, behind and between.
Example: "The chalk is on the board ledge. The bin is under the desk. The bag is behind the door. My desk is between two others."

### Summary
• A preposition shows the relationship between a noun and another word.
• Position: in, on, under, above, behind, between, near, beside.
• Direction: to, from, into, out of, through, across, along, past.
• Use "in" for enclosed spaces, "on" for surfaces and "at" for points.

## QUIZ
1. Which word is a preposition of position?
   A) run
   B) under
   C) quickly
   D) happy
   Answer: B
   Why: "Under" shows where something is.
2. Choose the correct word: "The bank is ____ the school and the post office."
   A) among
   B) between
   C) into
   D) through
   Answer: B
   Why: "Between" is used for the middle position of two things.
3. Which preposition shows movement to the inside?
   A) on
   B) beside
   C) into
   D) behind
   Answer: C
   Why: "Into" shows movement from outside to inside.
4. Which sentence is correct?
   A) He is in the bus.
   B) He is on the bus.
   C) He is at the bus inside.
   D) He is by the bus in.
   Answer: B
   Why: We say "on the bus" for public transport.
5. Underline the prepositions: "The children ran along the road, past the bank and into the market." (3 marks)
   Answer: along, past, into.
   Why: One mark for each.
6. Write four sentences using in, on, under and between. (4 marks)
   Answer: For example: The coins are in the box. The book is on the table. The cat is under the chair. The shop is between the bank and the school.
   Why: One mark for each sentence.
7. Explain the difference between "in" and "into". Give one sentence for each. (3 marks)
   Answer: "In" shows where something is inside: "The coins are in the box." "Into" shows movement to the inside: "She put the coins into the box."
   Why: One mark for each meaning and one mark for examples.
8. Give directions from your classroom to the school gate using at least three prepositions. (3 marks)
   Answer: For example: Go out of the classroom, walk along the corridor and go past the office to the gate.
   Why: One mark for each correct preposition used.
