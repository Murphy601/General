## PAGE 1: What are visuals?
A visual is something we understand by looking at it. Pictures, photographs, cartoons, comics, road signs, maps, charts and videos are all visuals. We read them just as we read words. They give us information, tell stories and warn us of danger.

### Kinds of visuals
• Photographs show real people, places and things.
• Drawings and cartoons show ideas in a funny or simple way.
• Comics tell a story in a row of small pictures, often with speech bubbles.
• Signs tell us what to do or not to do, such as "Stop".
• Maps show where places are.
• Videos and animated stories use moving pictures and sound.

We can find visuals in print, such as books, newspapers and posters, and in digital forms, such as videos, phones and computers.

### Why read visuals carefully?
On the road, a sign or a marking can save a life. In a story, a picture may show a feeling that words do not say. In a science book, a diagram may explain something better than a paragraph. To read visuals well, we must look closely, think and talk about what we see.

### Look and think
Look at a picture in your reader. Ask: What can I see? Who is in it? What is happening? Where is it? How do the people feel? Then tell a partner in full sentences.

## PAGE 2: How to interpret a picture
To interpret means to explain what something means. Follow these steps.

### Steps
1. Look at the whole picture. What is it about?
2. Read the title or caption if there is one. It gives the main idea.
3. Look at details: people, objects, colours, expressions, signs.
4. Think: what is happening, and what has just happened?
5. Predict: what will happen next?
6. Say or write your answer with a reason: "I think... because..."

### Example: a cartoon in words
Imagine a cartoon with three pictures. In the first, a boy on a bicycle is riding fast down a hill. In the second, a big yellow sign shows a railway crossing and a train is coming. In the third, the boy's eyes are wide and the brakes of his bicycle are squealing.

Question: What is happening? Answer: The boy is riding fast towards a railway crossing while a train is coming.
Question: How does the boy feel in the third picture? Answer: He is frightened, because his eyes are wide and he is braking.
Question: What will happen next? Answer: He may stop just in time, and he will learn to slow down near a crossing.
Question: What is the lesson? Answer: We must slow down and look at crossings.

### Use clues
A picture may show a clue such as a raised hand, a frown, a broken fence, a clock. Ask what each clue tells you.

## PAGE 3: Road safety visuals
Road signs are visuals with an important job. Learn what these mean and where they help.

### Places and things to know
• Underpass: a path or road that goes underneath a busy road. Pedestrians can walk through it without meeting cars.
• Foot bridge: a bridge over a road, built for people to walk on.
• Roundabout: cars go around a circular island in one direction. We give way to vehicles already inside.
• Railway crossing: where a road crosses a railway line. Always stop, look and listen.
• Zebra crossing: white stripes on the road where cars must stop for people who are crossing.
• Luggage rack: a shelf or frame on a vehicle or bus that holds bags.
• Number plate: a plate on a vehicle with its letters and numbers.

### Common signs
• A red octagon (eight-sided sign) with the word STOP means stop completely.
• A triangle with a red border is a warning sign. It warns that something is ahead, such as a crossing.
• A circle with a red border tells us what we must not do.
• A blue circle tells us what we must do.

### Talk about it
Which of these have you seen near your school? How do they keep people safe? With a partner, draw a warning sign that would help your school compound. Explain it to the class.

### Safe habits
Cross the road only at safe places. Look right, left and right again. Do not run. Do not play near the road or railway line.

## PAGE 4: Reading a simple map
A map is a picture of a place seen from above. It uses symbols and labels for roads, buildings and landmarks. To read a map, we use directions.

### The four main directions
The four main directions are north (N), south (S), east (E) and west (W). They are called the cardinal points. A compass shows them. The sun rises in the east and sets in the west. If you face the rising sun, north is on your left and south is on your right.

## PAGE 5: Giving and following directions
We use direction words and landmarks to tell someone how to go from one place to another.

### Useful words
• north, south, east, west
• turn left, turn right, go straight
• opposite, next to, near, behind, in front of
• first, then, after that, until you reach

### Direction sentences
• The sun rises in the east.
• The sun sets in the west.
• Go north until you reach the health centre.
• From the school, walk east to the roundabout and then turn right to reach the railway crossing.

### Check the last direction sentence
The school is west. You walk east to the roundabout. When you face east, south is on your right. Turning right takes you south, to the railway crossing. So the directions are correct.

### Practise
Using the map in Figure 1, answer:
1. Which place is north of the roundabout? (Health Centre)
2. In which direction do you walk from the market to the school? (West)
3. What do you find on the path between the roundabout and the market? (A foot bridge)

### Digital maps
A digital map on a phone or computer works in the same way. You can zoom in, search for a place and see the way to it. Use them with a teacher or parent, and do not share your home location with strangers.

### Activity
Draw a simple sketch map showing your classroom door, the school gate and the playground. Add a compass with N at the top. Use the words north, south, east and west to describe where they are. Exchange maps with a friend and give each other directions.

## QUIZ
1. What is a visual?
   A) Something we understand by looking at it
   B) A long word
   C) A kind of song
   D) A type of food
   Answer: A
   Why: Pictures, signs, maps and videos are visuals because we read them with our eyes.
2. In which direction does the sun rise?
   A) West
   B) North
   C) East
   D) South
   Answer: C
   Why: The sun rises in the east and sets in the west.
3. What does a foot bridge help people to do?
   A) Cross a road safely by walking over it
   B) Ride bicycles fast
   C) Park cars
   D) Carry luggage
   Answer: A
   Why: A foot bridge goes over the road so that people do not meet vehicles.
4. In the map in Figure 1, which place is west of the roundabout?
   A) Market
   B) School
   C) Health Centre
   D) Railway crossing
   Answer: B
   Why: The school is drawn on the left, which is west when north is at the top.
5. What should you do at a railway crossing?
   A) Run across quickly
   B) Stop, look and listen
   C) Play on the track
   D) Ride faster
   Answer: B
   Why: Trains are fast and cannot stop quickly, so we must stop, look and listen before crossing.
6. Name two visuals that you can find in a newspaper. (2 marks)
   Answer: Any two: photographs, cartoons, maps, charts, advertisements.
   Why: 1 mark for each correct visual.
7. Describe how you would interpret a cartoon. (3 marks)
   Answer: Look at the whole picture, read the caption, notice details and expressions, think about what is happening and what will happen next.
   Why: 1 mark for looking at the picture and caption, 1 mark for details or clues, 1 mark for explaining or predicting.
8. Write directions from the school to the health centre using the map in Figure 1. (3 marks)
   Answer: Walk east to the roundabout, then turn left and go north until you reach the health centre.
   Why: 1 mark for east to the roundabout, 1 mark for the turn or direction north, 1 mark for the health centre.
9. Explain why it is important to interpret road signs correctly. (2 marks)
   Answer: Road signs warn us of danger and tell us how to behave. If we misunderstand them, we could cause an accident.
   Why: 1 mark for saying signs warn or instruct and 1 mark for the danger of misreading.
