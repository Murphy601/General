## PAGE 1: What is an adverb?
An adverb is a word that tells us more about a verb, which is an action word. It answers questions such as How? When? and Where?

Look at these sentences:
• The nurse spoke gently. (How did she speak? Gently.)
• The clinic opens early. (When does it open? Early.)
• The children played outside. (Where did they play? Outside.)

The words gently, early and outside are adverbs. There are three main groups you will learn:
• Adverbs of manner tell how something is done.
• Adverbs of time tell when something is done.
• Adverbs of place tell where something is done.

### Why adverbs are useful
Compare "She walked" with "She walked slowly to the clinic this morning." The second sentence tells us much more. Adverbs make our speech and writing clear and interesting.

### Try it
Find the adverb in each sentence.
1. The doctor listened carefully. (carefully)
2. We shall meet tomorrow. (tomorrow)
3. Please sit here. (here)

## PAGE 2: Adverbs of manner (how)
Adverbs of manner tell how an action is done.

### Many end in -ly
Add -ly to an adjective:
• quick, quickly
• slow, slowly
• careful, carefully
• kind, kindly
• loud, loudly
• quiet, quietly
• neat, neatly
• bad, badly

### Spelling changes
• happy, happily (change y to i)
• angry, angrily
• easy, easily
• gentle, gently (drop the e, add y)
• terrible, terribly

### Some do not end in -ly
• well (the adverb of good): "She sings well."
• hard: "He works hard."
• fast: "The bus goes fast."
• straight: "Walk straight home."
Note: We do not say "She sings good". We say "She sings well". Good is an adjective; well is the adverb.

### Adjective or adverb?
• She is a careful driver. (careful describes the noun driver: adjective)
• She drives carefully. (carefully describes the verb drives: adverb)

### Sentences
1. "The patient breathed slowly."
2. "Please write neatly."
3. "The boy shouted loudly."
4. "The health worker explained clearly."

## PAGE 3: Adverbs of time (when)
Adverbs of time tell us when an action happens, or how often.

### When
• now, then, soon, later
• today, tomorrow, yesterday
• early, late
• already, yet, still
• first, next, finally

### How often
• always (every time)
• usually
• often
• sometimes
• seldom, rarely
• never (not at any time)

### Examples
• I will visit my aunt tomorrow.
• The bus arrived late.
• We always wash our hands before eating.
• The clinic is sometimes very busy.
• He never shares his razor.

### Where do they go in a sentence?
• Time adverbs like today and yesterday can go at the start or end of a sentence: "Yesterday we went to school." or "We went to school yesterday."
• Adverbs of frequency such as always and never usually go before the main verb: "I always brush my teeth." They go after the verb "to be": "She is never late."

### Try it
Use each of these in a sentence: soon, always, yesterday.
Examples: "The rain will stop soon." "My father always reads the newspaper." "Yesterday I visited the market."

## PAGE 4: Adverbs of place (where)
Adverbs of place tell where an action happens.

### Common adverbs of place
• here, there
• everywhere, nowhere, somewhere
• inside, outside
• upstairs, downstairs
• nearby, far, away
• up, down
• forward, backward
• home, abroad

### Examples
• Please come here.
• The children are playing outside.
• We looked everywhere for the key.
• The clinic is nearby.
• The birds flew away.

### Adverbs and prepositions
Some words, like inside and outside, can be adverbs or prepositions. When they stand alone, they are adverbs: "Wait outside." When they come before a noun, they are prepositions: "Wait outside the clinic." You will learn about prepositions in another lesson.

### Where in the sentence?
An adverb of place usually comes after the verb or after the object.
• The dog ran outside.
• She put the books there.

### Try it
Complete with an adverb of place: "The teacher told the children to wait ____." (outside, here or inside)

### Ask the question
To decide the kind, ask a question about the verb.
• How? gives manner.
• When? gives time.
• Where? gives place.

### Practice
Sort the adverbs in these sentences.
1. The nurse smiled kindly. (manner)
2. The clinic closes early. (time)
3. The patients waited inside. (place)
4. We must always wash our hands. (time)
5. She spoke softly. (manner)

### A sentence with all three
"Yesterday the farmer worked hard outside." Time: yesterday. Manner: hard. Place: outside.

### Games
Play "adverb charades". One learner acts a verb in a certain manner, such as "walk slowly", and the others guess the adverb. Then use the adverb in a sentence.

## PAGE 6: Using adverbs in speech and writing
### Poem with adverbs
Read this poem aloud.
"Slowly the sun climbs up the hill,
Softly the wind blows over the still.
Early the birds sing their song,
Everywhere the day moves along."
Adverbs: slowly, softly (manner); early (time); everywhere (place).

### Write your own
Write four sentences about your school day. Use a manner, a time and a place adverb at least once.
Example: "In the morning, we walk quickly to school. Our teacher greets us warmly inside the classroom. We always listen carefully."

### Common mistakes
• "She runs quick." Correct: She runs quickly.
• "He plays good." Correct: He plays well.
• "I will do it yesterday." Correct: I did it yesterday. (Match the adverb of time with the tense.)

### Summary
• An adverb tells more about a verb.
• Manner: how (slowly, well).
• Time: when or how often (soon, always).
• Place: where (here, outside).
• Many manner adverbs end in -ly.

## QUIZ
1. Which word in the sentence is an adverb? "The girl sang beautifully."
   A) girl
   B) sang
   C) beautifully
   D) The
   Answer: C
   Why: "Beautifully" tells how the girl sang.
2. Which is an adverb of place?
   A) quickly
   B) tomorrow
   C) outside
   D) always
   Answer: C
   Why: "Outside" tells where.
3. Which sentence is correct?
   A) He speaks good.
   B) He speaks well.
   C) He speaks goodly.
   D) He speak well.
   Answer: B
   Why: "Well" is the adverb that goes with the verb.
4. Choose the adverb of time: "We will visit the clinic tomorrow."
   A) We
   B) visit
   C) clinic
   D) tomorrow
   Answer: D
   Why: "Tomorrow" tells when.
5. Change these adjectives into adverbs: happy, gentle, careful. (3 marks)
   Answer: happily, gently, carefully.
   Why: One mark for each with correct spelling.
6. Write a sentence with an adverb of manner, an adverb of time and an adverb of place. (3 marks)
   Answer: For example: Yesterday the children played happily outside.
   Why: One mark for each adverb used correctly.
7. Explain the difference between "good" and "well". Give one sentence for each. (3 marks)
   Answer: Good is an adjective and describes a noun: "She is a good singer." Well is an adverb and describes a verb: "She sings well."
   Why: One mark for each rule and one for a sentence.
8. Sort these adverbs into manner, time and place: quietly, later, nearby, always, neatly, upstairs. (3 marks)
   Answer: Manner: quietly, neatly. Time: later, always. Place: nearby, upstairs.
   Why: One mark for each correct group.
