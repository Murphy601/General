## PAGE 1: Actions that are finished
Sometimes we speak about an action that has just been completed, or one that is finished but still matters now. For this we use the perfect aspect.

• "I have swept the compound." (I finished, and the compound is clean now.)
• "She has planted a tree." (She planted it, and it is there now.)

The word "perfect" here means "complete". A perfect verb form shows that an action is complete.

### How to form the present perfect
Use have or has + the past participle of the verb.
• I, you, we, they + have + past participle: We have finished.
• He, she, it, or one person or thing + has + past participle: She has finished. The bin has filled.

### The past participle
For regular verbs, the past participle is the same as the simple past. Add -ed:
• clean, cleaned: "I have cleaned the drain."
• plant, planted: "We have planted trees."
• walk, walked: "He has walked to school."
For irregular verbs, you have to learn the participle. We will look at these on the next page.

### Examples
• The children have swept the yard.
• Mother has washed the clothes.
• I have finished my homework.

### Try it
Say what you have done this morning, using "I have". Example: "I have brushed my teeth. I have eaten breakfast."

## PAGE 2: Irregular past participles
Some verbs change in unusual ways. Learn these common ones.

Base — Simple past — Past participle
• go — went — gone
• eat — ate — eaten
• see — saw — seen
• take — took — taken
• give — gave — given
• write — wrote — written
• do — did — done
• come — came — come
• run — ran — run
• begin — began — begun
• break — broke — broken
• drink — drank — drunk
• speak — spoke — spoken
• know — knew — known
• grow — grew — grown
• throw — threw — thrown
• fall — fell — fallen
• choose — chose — chosen
• buy — bought — bought
• bring — brought — brought
• make — made — made
• find — found — found
• sweep — swept — swept
• keep — kept — kept

### Note
Some verbs use the same form for the simple past and the past participle: bought, brought, made, found, swept, kept.

### Sentences
1. "I have eaten lunch."
2. "They have gone to the river."
3. "She has written a poem about the environment."
4. "The rain has fallen all morning."

### Common mistakes
• Wrong: "I have went." Right: "I have gone."
• Wrong: "She has ate." Right: "She has eaten."
• Wrong: "We have saw the show." Right: "We have seen the show."

## PAGE 3: Using the present perfect
### Uses
1. An action that has just finished: "I have just swept the floor."
2. An action that finished at an unstated time, with a result now: "I have lost my pen."
3. An experience up to now: "I have seen a giraffe."
4. Something that started in the past and continues: "We have lived here for five years."

### Time words often used
• just: I have just finished.
• already: She has already eaten.
• yet: (in questions and negatives) Have you finished yet? I have not finished yet.
• ever: Have you ever climbed a hill?
• never: I have never seen a leopard.
• since and for: I have lived here since 2018. I have lived here for five years.

### Do not use with a finished time word
We do not say "I have finished yesterday." Instead, use the simple past: "I finished yesterday."

### Negatives and questions
• Negative: have/has + not + participle: "I have not eaten." (haven't / hasn't)
• Question: Have/Has + subject + participle: "Have you cleaned the room?"
• Short answers: Yes, I have. / No, I have not.

### Try it
Answer: "Have you swept the floor?" "Yes, I have." Now ask your partner: "Has your mother gone to the market?" and record their answer.

## PAGE 4: The past perfect
The past perfect is used for an action that was completed before another action or time in the past.

### Form
had + past participle (the same for all subjects).
• I had finished.
• She had swept.
• They had left.

### Examples
• When I arrived at the stage, the bus had left. (The bus left first; then I arrived.)
• By the time the guests came, we had cleaned the house.
• She had eaten before she went to school.
• The teacher had already marked the books when we entered.

### Words often used
before, after, already, by the time, when, until.

### Negatives and questions
• The rain had not stopped when we left.
• Had you finished before the bell rang? Yes, I had. / No, I had not.

### Simple past or past perfect?
Use the past perfect for the earlier action, and the simple past for the later one.
• "I ate. Then the phone rang." → "I had eaten when the phone rang."

### Practise
Join with "had": "We planted trees. Then the rain came." → "We had planted trees before the rain came."

## PAGE 5: Present perfect or past perfect?
### Time clue
• Present perfect: connects the past with now. "The children have finished cleaning."
• Past perfect: connects two times in the past. "The children had finished cleaning when the teacher came."

### Read this text
"It is Friday afternoon. Our class has cleaned the compound. We have collected all the rubbish, and we have swept the paths. When Mrs. Ali came to inspect, we had already put the brooms away. She said, 'You have worked very well today.'"

### Find the forms
• Present perfect: has cleaned, have collected, have swept, have worked.
• Past perfect: had already put.

### Fill in the blanks
1. I (finish) my work, so I can go out now. (have finished)
2. By the time we reached the field, the match (start). (had started)
3. She (never see) a lion before yesterday. (had never seen)
4. They (just leave). (have just left)
5. He (eat) lunch before he went to play. (had eaten)

### Which is correct?
1. "I have seen him yesterday." Wrong. Right: "I saw him yesterday."
2. "The bus left before I arrived." Better: "The bus had left before I arrived."

### Songs and games
Sing a song with have or had, such as "Have you ever seen...?" or play a game where each pupil says what they have done today.

## PAGE 6: Practice and summary
### Complete with the correct form
1. We ____ (plant) ten trees this year. (have planted)
2. She ____ (write) a letter before she went to bed. (had written)
3. ____ you ever ____ (visit) the market at night? (Have, visited)
4. They ____ not ____ (come) yet. (have, come)
5. The bell rang after we ____ (finish) sweeping. (had finished)

### Make sentences
Write four sentences: two with "have/has" and two with "had". Use these verbs: clean, eat, go, see.
Example: "I have cleaned my room. She has gone to the shop. He had eaten before he left. We had seen the giraffe before the rain came."

### Type a list
On a computer or tablet, type six sentences about your morning using the perfect aspect. Read them to your partner.

### Summary
• Present perfect: have/has + past participle. It links the past to now.
• Past perfect: had + past participle. It shows the earlier of two past actions.
• Learn irregular participles: gone, eaten, seen, written, done.
• Do not use the present perfect with a finished time such as yesterday.

## QUIZ
1. Which is a correct present perfect sentence?
   A) She have finished.
   B) She has finished.
   C) She had finish.
   D) She has finish.
   Answer: B
   Why: With "she" we use "has" and the past participle "finished".
2. What is the past participle of "eat"?
   A) ate
   B) eated
   C) eaten
   D) eating
   Answer: C
   Why: "Eat" changes to "ate" in the simple past and "eaten" as the participle.
3. Which sentence uses the past perfect?
   A) I have finished.
   B) I finish.
   C) I had finished before he came.
   D) I am finishing.
   Answer: C
   Why: "Had finished" is the past perfect and the sentence shows an earlier action.
4. Choose the correct sentence.
   A) They have gone to the river.
   B) They have went to the river.
   C) They has gone to the river.
   D) They have go to the river.
   Answer: A
   Why: The past participle of "go" is "gone" and "they" takes "have".
5. Write the past participle of: write, see, take. (3 marks)
   Answer: written, seen, taken.
   Why: One mark for each.
6. Rewrite using the past perfect: "The bus left. Then I arrived." (2 marks)
   Answer: The bus had left before I arrived. (or When I arrived, the bus had left.)
   Why: One mark for "had left" and one mark for the linking word.
7. Explain the difference between "I have eaten" and "I had eaten when he came." (3 marks)
   Answer: "I have eaten" tells us I finished eating and it matters now. "I had eaten when he came" tells us I finished eating before he came in the past.
   Why: One mark for each meaning and one mark for the time link.
8. Write four sentences about your morning, two using "have/has" and two using "had". (4 marks)
   Answer: For example: I have brushed my teeth. She has swept the floor. We had eaten before we left. He had washed his face before school.
   Why: One mark for each correct sentence.
