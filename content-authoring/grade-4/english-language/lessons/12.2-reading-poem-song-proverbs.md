## PAGE 1: Stress and rhythm in reading
Poems, songs and passages have a beat, just as a drum does. Rhythm is the pattern of strong and weak beats in the words. Stress is the extra force we put on some syllables or words.

### Word stress
In a word with more than one syllable, one syllable is stronger.
• EN-vi-ron-ment (the first syllable)
• com-POUND (the second syllable)
• PRO-tect (the first)
• pro-TECT (the second, when it is a verb)
• RUB-bish (the first)

### Sentence stress
We stress important words: "We must KEEP our COMpound CLEAN."
Small words such as "the", "a" and "to" are said quickly and weakly.

### Reading a poem with rhythm
1. Read the poem quietly once to hear the story.
2. Clap or tap the beat.
3. Read with the beat, stressing the strong syllables.
4. Pause at the end of lines with punctuation.
5. Show feelings with your voice.

### Rhyme
Words that end with the same sound rhyme: ground and sound; tree and free; green and clean.

### A short line to practise
"Sweep, sweep, sweep the ground, / Keep the rubbish off the ground." Tap on each strong word: SWEEP, SWEEP, SWEEP the GROUND.

### Try it
Say your name and clap the syllables. "Ke-vin" has two claps; "Wan-ji-ku" has three.

## PAGE 2: A poem about a clean environment
Read the poem below, aloud, with a partner.

Our Clean Compound

Every morning, I take my broom,
I sweep the yard and the living room.
I put the rubbish in the pit,
And keep the bin closed, lid on it.

The tree gives shade, the grass is green,
The path is tidy, swept and clean.
Many hands make light work, they say,
So we all help, each day by day.

Little by little, the pile gets small,
Soon there's no rubbish left at all.
A stitch in time saves nine, I know,
So I clean today, and let it show.

### Questions
1. What does the speaker do each morning? (Takes a broom and sweeps the yard and room.)
2. Where does the speaker put the rubbish? (In the pit.)
3. What does the tree give? (Shade.)
4. Find the words that rhyme with "broom". (room)
5. Which lines contain proverbs? (Many hands make light work; A stitch in time saves nine.)

### Feelings in the poem
The speaker feels proud and responsible. The poem is cheerful and encourages the reader to help.

## PAGE 3: A song
Songs are poems set to music. They have verses and often a chorus, which is repeated.

Here is a simple song to sing to a marching tune, or to say in rhythm:

Verse 1
Sweep the yard, sweep the yard,
Every day, work hard.
Bring the broom, bring the bin,
Let the cleaning work begin.

Chorus
Clean, clean, keep it clean,
Make our compound bright and green.
Clean, clean, keep it clean,
Best in all the land we've seen.

Verse 2
Save the water, save the tree,
Care for all that we can see.
Rubbish in the pit it stays,
Covered up on all our days.

### Singing tips
• Sing together, keeping the same beat.
• Sing clearly so that the words are heard.
• Let the chorus be louder than the verse.
• Use actions: sweeping, carrying, planting.

### Questions
1. What is the chorus? (The part repeated after each verse.)
2. What does the song ask us to do? (Sweep the yard, keep it clean, save water and trees.)
3. Find two pairs of rhyming words. (bin / begin; clean / green)

### Talk about it
Why do people sing while they work? (It makes the work more fun and keeps everyone in rhythm.)

## PAGE 4: What is a proverb?
A proverb is a short, well-known saying that gives advice or states a truth. Proverbs often use pictures from everyday life, so we cannot take them word for word.

### Proverbs and their meanings
• Many hands make light work. When many people help, a job is easier.
• A stitch in time saves nine. Fixing a small problem early stops it from becoming a big one.
• Little by little fills the measure. Small efforts add up. (A Swahili proverb, "Haba na haba hujaza kibaba", carries the same idea.)
• Prevention is better than cure. It is better to stop a problem than to solve it later.
• Cleanliness is next to godliness. Being clean is very important. (Some people say this to encourage hygiene.)
• Where there is a will, there is a way. If you really want to do something, you will find a way.

### Match the proverb with the situation
1. The whole class sweeps the field in ten minutes. (Many hands make light work.)
2. You mend a small hole in your bag before it gets bigger. (A stitch in time saves nine.)
3. You collect a small piece of rubbish every day, and soon the compound is clean. (Little by little fills the measure.)
4. You keep the drain clear so that it does not flood. (Prevention is better than cure.)

### Using proverbs
Use a proverb to support your point. For example: "We should all help to clean the school compound. Many hands make light work."

### Try it
Choose a proverb. Explain it in your own words and give an example from your life.

## PAGE 5: A passage with proverbs
Read the passage. Find the proverbs.

The Clean-Up Day

Our school had a big clean-up day last Saturday. The head teacher, Mrs. Nyambura, stood before us and said, "Many hands make light work. Today we will clean our school together."

Each class was given a job. Class Four collected rubbish from the compound. Class Five swept the paths. Class Six planted young trees. The teachers brought brooms, bins and buckets of water.

Kamau found a big hole in the fence. "We should fix this now," he said. "A stitch in time saves nine." The teacher agreed and asked the caretaker to help mend it.

By noon, the compound was neat, the paths were clear and ten young trees were planted. Mrs. Nyambura smiled. "You have done very well. Little by little, we have made our school beautiful."

We ate boiled maize and drank water under the old tree. Kamau said, "I hope we can keep it clean." Wanjiru replied, "We will. Prevention is better than cure. If we throw no rubbish, we will not need another big clean-up."

### Answer these
1. When did the school hold its clean-up day? (Last Saturday.)
2. What job did Class Six do? (Planted young trees.)
3. Which proverb did Kamau use, and why? (A stitch in time saves nine, because he wanted to fix the hole in the fence before it got worse.)
4. Which proverb did Wanjiru use, and what did she mean? (Prevention is better than cure; if they do not throw rubbish around, they will not need another big clean-up.)

## PAGE 6: Answering questions and role play
### Factual questions
The answer is in the text.
• Who was the head teacher? (Mrs. Nyambura.)
• What did the teachers bring? (Brooms, bins and buckets of water.)

### Inferential questions
The answer is worked out from clues.
• Was the clean-up a success? (Yes. The compound was neat and trees were planted.)
• What kind of person is Kamau? (Observant and responsible, because he noticed the hole and suggested fixing it.)

### Role play
Act out the story in a group. Assign parts: the head teacher, Kamau, Wanjiru and a narrator. Say each proverb with feeling. Speak up so that everyone hears.

### Read with stress and rhythm
Read the head teacher's words: "Many HANDS make LIGHT work." Notice that the stressed words are "hands", "light" and "work".

### Watch and note
If you can, watch a video of a poem being recited. Note specific details, such as the speaker's voice, gestures and speed.

### Summary
• Rhythm is the beat; stress is the force on syllables or words.
• Poems and songs use rhyme and repetition.
• Proverbs give advice; explain them in plain words.
• Answer factual questions from the text and inferential ones from clues.

## QUIZ
1. What is a proverb?
   A) A long story
   B) A short, well-known saying that gives advice or a truth
   C) A kind of song
   D) A word with a similar meaning
   Answer: B
   Why: Proverbs are short sayings that teach lessons.
2. What does "Many hands make light work" mean?
   A) Big hands are strong
   B) A job is easier when many people help
   C) Work should be light
   D) Hands are heavy
   Answer: B
   Why: It means that when many people share the job, it becomes easier.
3. In the poem "Our Clean Compound", which word rhymes with "green"?
   A) tree
   B) clean
   C) shade
   D) grass
   Answer: B
   Why: "Green" and "clean" end with the same sound.
4. What is a chorus in a song?
   A) The first line
   B) The part that is repeated after each verse
   C) The name of the singer
   D) The last word
   Answer: B
   Why: The chorus is repeated between verses.
5. Explain the proverb "A stitch in time saves nine." (2 marks)
   Answer: Fixing a small problem early stops it from becoming a bigger problem that takes more work to solve.
   Why: One mark for fixing early and one mark for avoiding bigger work.
6. Give a situation in which the proverb "Prevention is better than cure" applies. (2 marks)
   Answer: For example: Washing your hands before eating prevents illness, which is better than getting sick and needing medicine.
   Why: One mark for a situation and one mark for linking it to the proverb.
7. What kind of person is Kamau in the passage? Give a reason from the text. (3 marks)
   Answer: Kamau is observant and responsible. He noticed a hole in the fence and said it should be fixed at once.
   Why: One mark for each quality and one for the evidence.
8. Read this line and mark the words you would stress: "We must keep our compound clean." (3 marks)
   Answer: We must KEEP our COMpound CLEAN. (Important words: keep, compound, clean.)
   Why: One mark for each important word stressed.
