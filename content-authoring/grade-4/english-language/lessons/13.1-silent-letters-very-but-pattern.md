## PAGE 1: Listening intensively
Intensive listening means listening very carefully for specific things: a sound, a word, a phrase or a sentence. You do not just hear; you search for what you need.

### When do we listen intensively?
• When a teacher gives instructions.
• When we take a message on the phone.
• When we listen to a story and must answer questions.
• When we listen to announcements at the market or at school.

### Strategies
1. Sit still and face the speaker.
2. Know what you are listening for. Before you start, ask, "What do I need to find?"
3. Jot down key words.
4. Do not interrupt; wait for the end.
5. Ask for repetition if needed: "Could you say that again, please?"

### Listening script: read aloud to your class
"Mama Halima sells vegetables at the market. On Monday she sold ten bunches of kale, and on Tuesday she sold fifteen. She sells tomatoes at fifty shillings a kilo. She saves ten shillings from each sale in a tin, so that she can pay her son's school fees."

### Listen for details
• How many bunches of kale on Monday? (ten)
• What is the price of a kilo of tomatoes? (fifty shillings)
• Why does she save money in a tin? (to pay her son's school fees)

### The telephone game
Sit in a circle. The first learner whispers a sentence to the next. Each person whispers what they heard. The last person says it aloud. Compare it with the original. This shows how careful listening matters.

## PAGE 2: The letter x and the sounds /m/, /n/ and /ŋ/
### The letter x
The letter x usually says /ks/, the sounds "k" and "s" together.
• box, fox, six, mix, tax
• taxi, next, relax
Sometimes x says /gz/, as in exam, example, exact.
At the beginning of a word, x can say /z/, as in xylophone.

### Money words with x
tax, fix (to repair), mix, six, box, exchange, expensive, extra.

### Sentences
1. "Six boxes of tomatoes cost six hundred shillings."
2. "The tax is extra."
3. "The taxi took us next to the exit."

### The sounds /m/, /n/ and /ŋ/
These are nasal sounds: air passes through the nose.
• /m/: lips closed. mother, money, market, sum, ram
• /n/: tongue tip touches the ridge behind the teeth. no, note, ten, sun, ran
• /ŋ/: back of tongue touches the soft roof. sing, song, long, ring, king, bank, think

### Contrast
• sum / sun / sung
• ram / ran / rang
• sin / sing
The last sound is the difference.

### Notice
The sound /ŋ/ is written "ng" or "n" before k: sing, bank, think. Do not add a hard "g" at the end of "sing". Say "sing", not "sing-guh".

### Sentence
"The king put money in a ring in the bank." (nasals: king, money, ring, in, bank)

## PAGE 3: Silent letters
Some letters are written, but not said. They are silent.

### Silent h
• honest (say "onest")
• hour (say "our")
• honour (say "onour")

### Silent b
• debt (say "det")
• doubt
• lamb, comb, climb, thumb, bomb

### Silent k
• knee, know, knife, knock, knot, knit
The k is silent before n at the start of a word.

### Silent w
• write, wrong, wrist, wrap, answer, two
The w is silent in wr at the start.

### Silent t
• listen, castle, whistle, fasten, often (some people say the t)

### Silent l
• walk, talk, chalk, calf, half

### Silent gh
• night, light, high, daughter

### Other silent letters
• island (silent s), Wednesday (silent d), cupboard (silent p)

### The r in "park"
In careful British English, the r in "park" and "car" is not pronounced. In Kenya, many speakers do say the r. Follow your teacher's model, and listen for both.

### Money words with silent letters
debt, honest, listen.
Example: "An honest shopkeeper listens to the customer and does not cheat."

### Try it
Read the words aloud. Cross out the silent letter: knife, honest, debt, write, lamb, listen, hour.

## PAGE 4: The pattern "very ... but ..."
This pattern shows a good point and then a difficulty. The word "very" strengthens an adjective or adverb, and "but" introduces the contrast.

### Form
subject + be + very + adjective, but + subject + verb ...
• "The bicycle is very nice, but it is too expensive."
• "The mango was very sweet, but it was small."
• "The market is very busy, but the prices are low."
• "She is very kind, but she is strict about money."
• "He worked very hard, but he did not earn much."

### Notice
• Use a comma before "but".
• The first part is usually positive (very good, very nice), and the second part gives a limit or difficulty.
• It can also work the other way: "The shirt is very cheap, but it is not strong."

### In dialogue about money
Buyer: "This bag is very beautiful, but it costs too much."
Seller: "I can lower the price a little."
Buyer: "Thank you. It is very kind of you, but I only have five hundred shillings."

### Say it with expression
Raise your voice slightly on "very", pause a little at the comma, and then say the "but" part in a lower tone.

### Practice
Complete the sentences.
1. The phone is very good, but ____ (it costs a lot).
2. The bus is very fast, but ____ (it is crowded).
3. I am very happy, but ____ (I am tired).

### Try it
Make three sentences about shopping with "very ... but ...". Say them to your partner.

## PAGE 5: Vocabulary about money
• tax: money that people pay to the government
• pay: to give money for something
• business: buying and selling things to make a living
• collect: to gather or receive
• money: coins and notes used to buy things
• honest: telling the truth and not cheating
• profit: money gained when you sell for more than you paid
• salary: money paid to a worker regularly
• fine: money paid as a punishment for breaking a rule
• loss: when you sell for less than you paid, or lose money
• law: a rule made by the government
• government: the group of people who lead a country
• buy: to get something by paying
• sell: to give something for money
• market: a place where people buy and sell
• fee: money paid for a service, such as school or parking
• borrow: to take something and give it back later
• credit: buying now and paying later
• bank: a place that keeps money safely
• income: money received, for example from work

### Sentences
1. "Mama Halima sells vegetables and makes a small profit."
2. "A teacher earns a salary every month."
3. "Honest people do not cheat in business."
4. "The government uses tax to build roads and schools."
5. "You must pay a fine if you break the law."

### Matching game
Match the word to its meaning: profit, borrow, fee, income.
• money paid for a service (fee)
• money received from work (income)
• take something and return it later (borrow)
• gain when you sell for more than you paid (profit)

## PAGE 6: Listening and speaking practice
### A story to listen to
Read this aloud to your class or partner. Listeners note answers.

"Otieno sells sugar cane near a bus stage. Each stick costs ten shillings. On Friday he sold thirty sticks. He put the money in a small box and counted it twice. A woman gave him a fifty-shilling note by mistake, thinking it was a twenty. Otieno noticed and gave her the correct change. 'You are very honest,' she said. Otieno smiled. 'It is very easy to be honest, but it is hard to earn back trust if you cheat.'"

### Questions
1. Where does Otieno sell sugar cane? (near a bus stage)
2. How much does each stick cost? (ten shillings)
3. What mistake did the woman make? (She gave him a fifty-shilling note thinking it was twenty.)
4. Why was Otieno honest? (He knew that it is hard to earn back trust if you cheat.)
5. Find the "very ... but ..." pattern. ("It is very easy to be honest, but it is hard to earn back trust.")

### Summary
• Intensive listening searches for specific information.
• x says /ks/; /m/, /n/ and /ŋ/ are nasal sounds.
• Silent letters: honest, debt, knee, write, listen, lamb.
• The pattern: "It is very ___, but ___."
• Money words: tax, profit, salary, fine, fee, income.

## QUIZ
1. Which word has a silent k?
   A) knee
   B) kind
   C) key
   D) king
   Answer: A
   Why: In "knee" the k is not pronounced.
2. In which word is the letter b silent?
   A) baby
   B) debt
   C) bag
   D) bus
   Answer: B
   Why: "Debt" is said "det".
3. Which sentence uses the pattern "very ... but ..." correctly?
   A) The shirt is very nice, but it is too expensive.
   B) The shirt very nice but expensive.
   C) Very shirt is nice, but.
   D) The shirt is but very nice, it is expensive.
   Answer: A
   Why: It has "is very" + adjective, then "but" with the difficulty.
4. What does "profit" mean?
   A) Money lost in a sale
   B) Money gained when you sell for more than you paid
   C) A tax
   D) A gift
   Answer: B
   Why: Profit is the gain from selling for more than the cost.
5. Cross out the silent letter in each word: write, lamb, honest, listen. (2 marks)
   Answer: write (w), lamb (b), honest (h), listen (t)
   Why: Half a mark each, total 2 marks.
6. Make two sentences with "very ... but ..." about shopping. (2 marks)
   Answer: For example: The mango is very sweet, but it is small. The bag is very nice, but it is expensive.
   Why: One mark for each correct sentence.
7. Explain the meaning of "tax" and "fine". (3 marks)
   Answer: Tax is money paid to the government to help run the country. A fine is money paid as a punishment for breaking a rule or law.
   Why: One mark for each meaning and one mark for showing a difference.
8. Read the story of Mama Halima. How much did she sell kale on Monday and how many on Tuesday? (2 marks)
   Answer: She sold ten bunches on Monday and fifteen on Tuesday.
   Why: One mark for each number.
