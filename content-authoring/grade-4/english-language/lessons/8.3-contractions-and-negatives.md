## PAGE 1: Shortening words with an apostrophe
When we speak, we often join two words into one shorter word. In writing, we show this with an apostrophe ('). The short form is called a contraction. The apostrophe shows where letters have been left out.

• I am becomes I'm (the letter a is left out)
• you are becomes you're
• they are becomes they're
• it is becomes it's
• we will becomes we'll
• I have becomes I've

### Where the apostrophe goes
Put the apostrophe exactly where the missing letter or letters were.
• do not becomes don't (the o in "not" is missing)
• cannot becomes can't (the letters n and o are missing)
• let us becomes let's (the u is missing)

### When to use contractions
Contractions are natural in speech, in friendly letters and in stories that show people talking. In very formal writing, such as an official report, writers often use the full forms. Your teacher will tell you which to use.

### Try it
Say "do not" and "don't" aloud. Which is faster? Now find the apostrophe in this sentence: "I don't know where they've gone." The two contractions are don't (do not) and they've (they have).

## PAGE 2: Contractions with "not"
Negative contractions join a helping verb and the word not.

• is not: isn't
• are not: aren't
• was not: wasn't
• were not: weren't
• do not: don't
• does not: doesn't
• did not: didn't
• have not: haven't
• has not: hasn't
• had not: hadn't
• can not: can't (written as cannot in full)
• could not: couldn't
• should not: shouldn't
• would not: wouldn't
• will not: won't

### Watch for these
• "will not" becomes "won't", not "willn't".
• "cannot" becomes "can't".
• "am not" is shortened to "I'm not". We do not write "amn't".

### Examples
• The tractor isn't working today.
• We weren't late for school.
• My brother doesn't like porridge.
• They didn't finish their homework.
• I can't lift the sack.

### Practice
Write the short form of: they are not, she has not, we did not, you will not. Answers: they aren't, she hasn't, we didn't, you won't.

## PAGE 3: Contractions with pronouns and the "it's / its" trap
Some contractions join a pronoun with a helping verb.

### With "is" or "has"
• he is, he has: he's
• she is, she has: she's
• it is, it has: it's
• that is: that's
• what is: what's
• there is: there's
The meaning depends on the sentence: "He's hungry" means he is hungry; "He's eaten" means he has eaten.

### With "are"
• we are: we're
• you are: you're
• they are: they're

### With "will" and "have"
• I will: I'll
• you will: you'll
• I have: I've
• they have: they've

### Important warning: it's and its
• it's = it is or it has. "It's raining."
• its = belonging to it. "The cow licked its calf."
Test: try replacing with "it is". If the sentence still makes sense, use it's.
• "The dog wagged its tail." (Not "it is tail", so use its.)
• "It's a fine day." ("It is a fine day", so it's is right.)

### Also do not confuse
• you're (you are) and your (belonging to you)
• they're (they are), their (belonging to them) and there (a place)

## PAGE 4: Positive and negative statements
A positive statement says that something is, was or happens. A negative statement says that something is not, was not or does not happen.

### Positive
• The farmer has a tractor.
• We planted beans.
• She is happy.

### Negative
• The farmer doesn't have a tractor.
• We didn't plant beans.
• She isn't happy.

### How to make a negative sentence
1. If there is a helping verb (is, are, was, were, have, has, had, can, will, should), add not after it, or use its contraction: She is not tired. She isn't tired.
2. If there is no helping verb (simple present or simple past), use do, does or did with not, and change the main verb back to its base form:
• He works. He doesn't work.
• They play. They don't play.
• I ate. I didn't eat. (Not "I didn't ate".)

### More examples
• The cow gives milk. The cow doesn't give milk.
• We saw pests. We didn't see pests.
• They will come. They won't come.

### Changing negatives to positives
• I haven't seen the seeds. I have seen the seeds.
• The shed isn't locked. The shed is locked.

### Practise
Change these to negatives: (a) She sells eggs. (b) They picked coffee. (c) We can dig. Answers: (a) She doesn't sell eggs. (b) They didn't pick coffee. (c) We can't dig.

## PAGE 5: Some cautions about negatives, and asking questions
### No double negatives
In English, one negative word is enough.
• Wrong: I don't have no pencil.
• Right: I don't have a pencil. Or: I have no pencil.
• Wrong: She didn't see nobody.
• Right: She didn't see anybody. Or: She saw nobody.
Use "any" words with not: anything, anybody, any. Use "no" words without not: nothing, nobody, no.

### Questions that get yes or no answers
Positive question: Do you like beans? Yes, I do. / No, I don't.
Negative question: Don't you like beans? Yes, I do. / No, I don't.
Notice: the answer is about the fact. If you like beans, say "Yes, I do."

### Question pairs to practise with a partner
• Is the maize ready? Yes, it is. / No, it isn't.
• Are you tired? Yes, I am. / No, I'm not.
• Did you feed the goats? Yes, I did. / No, I didn't.
• Can she drive the tractor? Yes, she can. / No, she can't.

### Why sentence structure matters
If you say "I don't know", you mean you lack the knowledge. If you say "I know", you have it. A missing not can change the whole message.

Draw two columns headed Positive and Negative. Write ten pairs of sentences about a farm. Display them on the wall.

## PAGE 6: Reading and writing practice
### Read this dialogue
Farmer: Hello, Amina. Isn't your father at the farm today?
Amina: No, he isn't. He's at the market.
Farmer: Didn't he sell the maize yesterday?
Amina: Yes, he did. He sold four sacks. There aren't any more left.
Farmer: I'm glad. It's a good price this year.

### Find the contractions
isn't (is not), he's (he is), didn't (did not), aren't (are not), I'm (I am), it's (it is).

### Exercise
Rewrite in full: (a) They're planting seeds. (b) She's not here. (c) We haven't finished. Answers: (a) They are planting seeds. (b) She is not here. (c) We have not finished.

Rewrite with a contraction: (a) I will help you. (b) It is a fine crop. (c) He does not eat meat. Answers: (a) I'll help you. (b) It's a fine crop. (c) He doesn't eat meat.

### Summary
• A contraction shortens two words; the apostrophe replaces missing letters.
• will not is won't; cannot is can't.
• it's means it is; its shows belonging.
• Use do, does or did to make negatives from main verbs.
• Avoid double negatives.

## QUIZ
1. What is the contraction of "they are"?
   A) they'r
   B) theyr
   C) they're
   D) thay're
   Answer: C
   Why: The apostrophe replaces the letter a in "are".
2. Which is the contraction of "will not"?
   A) willn't
   B) won't
   C) wont
   D) wil'nt
   Answer: B
   Why: "Will not" changes to "won't".
3. Which sentence uses "its" correctly?
   A) The goat hurt it's leg.
   B) The goat hurt its leg.
   C) Its raining today.
   D) I think its a goat.
   Answer: B
   Why: Its shows belonging. In the other options it should be "it is" or the apostrophe should not be used.
4. Which is the correct negative of "He works on the farm"?
   A) He doesn't works on the farm.
   B) He don't work on the farm.
   C) He doesn't work on the farm.
   D) He isn't work on the farm.
   Answer: C
   Why: With "doesn't" the main verb returns to its base form: work.
5. Write the short forms of: did not, I am, let us. (3 marks)
   Answer: didn't, I'm, let's.
   Why: One mark for each correct contraction with the apostrophe in the right place.
6. Change to a negative statement: "We planted beans in March." (2 marks)
   Answer: We didn't plant beans in March.
   Why: One mark for didn't and one mark for using the base form "plant".
7. Correct the sentence: "I don't have no seeds." Explain your correction. (3 marks)
   Answer: I don't have any seeds. (or I have no seeds.) English uses only one negative word in a statement like this.
   Why: One mark for a correct sentence and two marks for the explanation.
8. Explain the difference between "it's" and "its" and give one sentence for each. (4 marks)
   Answer: It's means "it is" or "it has": It's raining. Its shows belonging: The cow licked its calf.
   Why: One mark for each meaning and one for each correct sentence.
