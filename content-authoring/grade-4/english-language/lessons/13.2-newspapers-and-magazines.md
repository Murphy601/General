## PAGE 1: Why read newspapers and magazines?
Newspapers and magazines are reading materials that give news, stories, facts and fun. Newspapers come out daily or weekly. Magazines are usually published weekly or monthly and have more pictures and longer features. Both can be printed on paper or read on a phone, tablet or computer.

### Why read them?
• To find out what is happening in our town, country and the world.
• To learn new words in real-life writing.
• To find information on health, sports, money, farming and more.
• To enjoy stories, puzzles, jokes and pictures.
• To practise reading on your own, which builds fluency.

### Choosing what to read
Ask yourself:
1. Is it suitable for my age?
2. Is it interesting to me?
3. Is it easy enough to read?
4. Is it true and fair? (A good newspaper checks its facts.)
Ask an adult to help you choose. A children's magazine or a children's page in a newspaper is a good start.

### When to read
Set a regular time: after homework, on the weekend or during a school reading club. Read for about fifteen minutes a day. Reading regularly is better than reading a lot once.

### Try it
Bring a page from a newspaper or a children's magazine (with permission). Say what it is about in one sentence.

### The parts explained
• Name (masthead): the title of the newspaper at the top.
• Date: shows when it was printed.
• Headline: a short, bold title that tells what the story is about.
• Byline: the name of the writer.
• Article or story: the news text, often in columns.
• Picture and caption: the caption is a short line that explains the picture.
• Advertisement (advert): a notice that tries to sell something.
• Sports page, weather report, letters to the editor, and classified adverts (small notices such as jobs or items for sale).

### Note
The newspaper name in the figure is made up for this lesson. Real newspapers have their own names.

### Try it
Find the headline and one caption in a real newspaper. Say what each tells you.

## PAGE 3: Parts of a magazine
A magazine has a cover, pages of features, pictures and often puzzles.

### Parts of a magazine
• Cover: the front page with the title, the date or issue and a main picture.
• Contents page: lists the articles and their page numbers.
• Articles or features: longer texts on one subject, such as animals, sports, health or money.
• Interviews: questions and answers with a person.
• Comics, jokes and puzzles: for fun.
• Letters and pages by readers.
• Adverts.

### Using the contents page
To find an article, look at the contents page for its title, note the page number and turn to that page.

### Newspaper or magazine?
• Newspapers: news, published often, printed on thin paper, fewer colour pictures.
• Magazines: features, published less often, glossy paper, many pictures.

### Skimming and scanning
• Skimming: reading quickly to get the main idea. Look at headings, pictures and first sentences.
• Scanning: looking quickly for one detail, such as a name, a number or a date.

Skim the page first. Then scan for the information you need.

### Try it
Look at a magazine. Use the contents page to find an article on sports. What page is it on?

## PAGE 4: A sample article
Read this made-up article, written as an example of a newspaper report on a school event.

School Learners Learn to Save Money
By Staff Reporter

Pupils of a primary school in a town in Kenya have started a savings club. The club began this term after a class lesson about money.

Each Friday, members bring a small amount of money, such as ten or twenty shillings. A teacher writes down each pupil's name and how much they have saved in a book. The money is kept safely in a bank account for the school club.

"I want to buy a school bag with my savings," said a Grade 4 member. "It is better to save a little each week than to waste it on sweets."

The teacher in charge said the club teaches pupils to be careful with money and to be honest. "Every child receives a receipt for the money they bring," she said. "That is how we know that everyone's money is safe."

Parents have praised the idea. Members will get their savings back at the end of the year.

### Answer these
1. What is the headline? (School Learners Learn to Save Money)
2. When do members bring money? (Each Friday.)
3. Where is the money kept? (In a bank account for the school club.)
4. Why does the club give receipts? (To show that each child's money is safe.)
5. What does the teacher say the club teaches pupils? (To be careful with money and to be honest.)

### Note
This article is an invented example, not a real report.

## PAGE 5: Working out word meanings and forming opinions
### Inferring meaning
Use the words around a new word to work out its meaning.
• "The money is kept safely in a bank account." A bank account is a place in a bank to keep money.
• "Parents have praised the idea." From "Parents ... the idea" and the story, praised means said good things about it.
• "Every child receives a receipt." A receipt is a paper showing that money was received.

### Sample words to explore
savings, account, receipt, budget, price, cost, cheap, expensive, discount, income.

### Fact or opinion?
• Fact: "Each Friday, members bring money." (You can check this.)
• Opinion: "It is better to save a little each week." (This is what someone thinks.)
Newspapers give facts in news reports, and opinions in the opinion or letters pages.

### Share your opinion
After you read, say what you think:
• "I liked the article because it taught me to save."
• "I think the idea is good, because ..."
Listen respectfully when others disagree.

### Retell what you read
Tell a friend the main points of an article using your own words:
1. Who is the article about?
2. What happened?
3. Where and when?
4. Why is it important?

### Be careful of what you read online
Not everything online is true. Stay on sites your teacher or parent recommends. Never share personal details.

## PAGE 6: Reading club and practice
### Set up a reading club
1. Choose a day and time each week.
2. Bring a newspaper, magazine or book.
3. Read silently for ten minutes.
4. Each member tells the group one interesting thing they read.
5. Keep a list of what the club has read.
6. You can also meet online with a teacher's permission.

### Make your own page
In groups, make a class newspaper page. Include:
• a name for your newspaper
• a headline
• a short article of 4 or 5 sentences
• a picture with a caption
• a weather report or a "Did you know?" fact
Display it on the wall.

### Read for enjoyment
Choose a children's magazine story. Read it slowly, then more quickly, then tell it to a partner.

### Summary
• Newspapers and magazines give news, features, pictures and fun.
• Newspapers have a masthead, headline, byline, article, picture and caption.
• Magazines have a cover and contents page.
• Skim for the main idea and scan for details.
• Choose materials that suit your age, and share what you read.

## QUIZ
1. What is a headline?
   A) The writer's name
   B) A short, bold title that tells what a news story is about
   C) An advert
   D) A picture
   Answer: B
   Why: A headline tells the reader what a story is about.
2. What is a caption?
   A) A short line that explains a picture
   B) The name of the newspaper
   C) A kind of advert
   D) The last paragraph
   Answer: A
   Why: Captions describe pictures.
3. In a magazine, which page lists the articles and their page numbers?
   A) The cover
   B) The advert page
   C) The contents page
   D) The comic page
   Answer: C
   Why: The contents page tells you where to find each article.
4. What does "scanning" mean?
   A) Looking quickly for one detail such as a name or a number
   B) Reading every word slowly
   C) Copying the text
   D) Drawing a picture
   Answer: A
   Why: Scanning means searching for specific information.
5. Name three parts of a newspaper. (3 marks)
   Answer: For example: masthead (name), headline, byline, article, picture, caption, advert, weather report.
   Why: One mark for each.
6. Give the difference between a fact and an opinion, with an example of each. (3 marks)
   Answer: A fact can be checked, for example "The club meets on Fridays." An opinion is what someone thinks, for example "Saving is a good idea."
   Why: One mark for each meaning and one for examples.
7. Give two reasons why it is good to read newspapers and magazines. (2 marks)
   Answer: For example: to learn what is happening around us; to learn new words.
   Why: One mark for each reason.
8. Write a short headline and two sentences for a news story about your school. (3 marks)
   Answer: For example: Headline: "Pupils Win Football Match". Sentences: "The school team won 2 to 1 on Friday. The pupils were happy." Answers vary.
   Why: One mark for a suitable headline and one mark for each correct sentence.
