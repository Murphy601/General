## PAGE 1: Taking turns in a conversation
A conversation has two sides. One person speaks while the other listens, and then they swap. This is called turn-taking. If two people speak at the same time, neither of them is heard, and nobody learns anything.

Good listeners follow four habits:
• Look at the speaker's face.
• Keep quiet until the speaker has finished a whole idea.
• Nod or say small words such as "I see" to show you are following.
• Wait for a pause, then say your own part.

Good speakers also help. They speak clearly, they do not talk for too long, and they ask the other person a question so the conversation can move back and forth.

### Useful phrases
• Starting: "Good afternoon, may I ask you something?"
• Agreeing: "Yes, I think so too."
• Adding: "May I add something?"
• Giving the turn: "What do you think?"
• Waiting: "Please go on, I am listening."

### Why it matters
When we wait for our turn, we show respect. The other person feels that their ideas are important, and we understand them better. On a phone call or in an online chat, turn-taking is even more important, because we cannot see the other person's face. In a debate at school, the teacher may give each speaker one minute. Each speaker stops when the time is up so that everybody has a chance.

### Try it
Sit in pairs. One learner talks for one minute about a favourite game. The other listens without speaking and then repeats one thing that was said. Swap roles.

## PAGE 2: A two-way dialogue about staying safe
Read this dialogue aloud with a partner. Notice how each person speaks once and then waits.

Mwajuma: Hello, Kamau. Where were you yesterday afternoon?
Kamau: I was at the cyber café near the market. I bought data bundles for my uncle's phone.
Mwajuma: Did you go online there?
Kamau: Yes, I did. I watched a short video about safe internet use.
Mwajuma: What did you learn?
Kamau: I learnt that a stranger online may not be who he says he is. I never give my name, my school or my address to someone I do not know.
Mwajuma: That is wise. I always ask my mother before I open a new page.
Kamau: Good. What do you do when a message makes you feel afraid?
Mwajuma: I show it to a trusted adult at once.

### Look at the tenses
Some sentences talk about what always happens now. They use the present tense: "I never give my name", "I always ask my mother". Other sentences talk about something that has finished. They use the past tense: "I bought data bundles", "I watched a video", "I learnt", "Where were you?".

### Present or past?
• We use the present tense for habits and facts: "I ask my mother."
• We use the past tense for finished actions: "I asked my mother yesterday."
Words such as yesterday, last week and earlier tell us to use the past. Words such as always, every day and never usually go with the present.

### Try it
Act out the dialogue with a partner. Then change the place from a cyber café to a school library and change the questions and answers to fit.

## PAGE 3: The sounds /ɛə/ and /j/
Sounds are different from letters. Some sounds are written between slanted lines so we can talk about how a word is said and not how it is spelled.

### The sound /ɛə/
This sound starts like the "e" in "bed" and glides to a soft "uh". It is the sound in:
• air, chair, hair, stairs, pair, pear
• care, share, bare, square, rare
• bear, wear, there, where

Notice that the same sound can be spelled in different ways: air, are, ear, ere. Listen for the sound, not the spelling.

### The sound /j/
This sound is the "y" sound at the start of "yes". It is made with the middle of the tongue lifting towards the roof of the mouth. It is found in:
• yes, yellow, yam, year, young, yard
• you, your, yoghurt, yesterday
Some words spelled with the letter u also have the /j/ sound: use, unit, university, huge, computer.

### Say it clearly
1. "Where is the chair?" "The chair is there, on the square."
2. "Take care of your hair."
3. "Yesterday the young man ate a yellow yam."
4. "You should share your yoghurt."

### Common mistake
Some learners say "yes" as "jes" or "ess". Start with a smile and lift the tongue, then say "yes".

## PAGE 4: The sounds /ʤ/ and /ʒ/
### The sound /ʤ/
This sound is written "j" in the notes. It begins with a stop, like a small explosion, and then releases a buzzing sound. It is in:
• jug, jam, job, joke, July, jacket
• giant, gentle, giraffe, magic (letter g before e, i or y)
• page, bridge, judge, orange, village

### The sound /ʒ/
This is a soft, buzzing sound with no stop at the start. It is like the sound in the middle of "vision". It is not very common. You find it in:
• television, vision, decision
• measure, treasure, pleasure
• usual, casual

### How they differ
Say "jam" and feel the tongue stop and release. Then say "vision" and feel the air flow smoothly the whole time. The first sound is /ʤ/, the second is /ʒ/.

### Sentences to practise
1. "The judge told a joke about a jug of orange juice."
2. "The giant giraffe jumped over the bridge."
3. "It is usual to watch television for pleasure, but we must not waste our leisure time."
4. "It was a wise decision to measure the treasure."

### Tip
The letter j is almost always /ʤ/ in English. The letter g says /ʤ/ before e, i or y in many words, but not in all (get, give, girl).

## PAGE 5: Vocabulary for the theme: staying safe online
Words are tools. The more words you know, the more clearly you can speak. Learn these words and their meanings.

• crime: an act that breaks the law
• trust: to believe that someone is honest and will not harm you
• stranger: a person you do not know
• share: to let someone else have or see something; online, it means to post or send something
• care: to look after something or someone with attention
• leisure: free time, when you rest or play
• safe: not in danger
• cyber café: a place where people pay to use computers and the internet
• data bundles: an amount of internet you buy for a phone or computer
• laptop: a small computer that you can carry

### Words in sentences
1. "A stranger should not know where you live."
2. "We use our leisure time to read, play and rest."
3. "Thieves who steal online commit a crime."
4. "Only trust people who have earned it."
5. "Please take care of the school laptop."

### Choral verse
Read this verse together in a group. Use your face and your hands to match the words. Speak up on the last line.

Think before you click,
Ask a grown-up quick.
Names and homes we do not share,
Strangers online, beware!

### Nonverbal signs
Smile when you greet. Make eye contact. Use a raised hand for "stop" and a slow nod for "yes". Your face and hands tell listeners how you feel.

## PAGE 6: Putting it together
Now combine everything you have learnt: turn-taking, tenses, sounds and vocabulary.

### Interview task
Work in pairs. One learner is the reporter and the other is a learner who visited a cyber café.
Reporter questions:
• Where did you go?
• What did you do there?
• What do you usually do to stay safe online?
• What should we never share?
Answers should use the past tense for what happened ("I went", "I watched") and the present tense for habits ("I never share").

### Sound game
Say each group and clap once for every word that has the sound in the heading.
• /ɛə/: chair, sheep, care, bird, stairs
• /j/: yellow, young, bell, yam, milk
• /ʤ/: jam, judge, cat, giant, bridge
• /ʒ/: television, tree, measure, treasure, hand

### Remember
• Wait for your turn and never shout over the speaker.
• Use the present tense for habits and the past tense for finished actions.
• Listen for sounds, not spellings.
• Use vocabulary words in complete sentences.

## QUIZ
1. Which action shows good turn-taking?
   A) Speaking at the same time as your friend
   B) Waiting until the speaker finishes an idea
   C) Looking away while someone talks
   D) Speaking only in whispers
   Answer: B
   Why: Turn-taking means one person speaks while the other listens, and then they swap.
2. Which sentence is in the past tense?
   A) I always ask my mother.
   B) I never share my address.
   C) I bought data bundles yesterday.
   D) I ask a trusted adult.
   Answer: C
   Why: "Bought" and "yesterday" show a finished action.
3. Which word has the sound /ɛə/?
   A) Chair
   B) Chin
   C) Chop
   D) Church
   Answer: A
   Why: "Chair" ends with the /ɛə/ sound. The other words have a short vowel or a different vowel sound.
4. Which word has the sound /ʒ/ in the middle?
   A) Jug
   B) Treasure
   C) Yellow
   D) Bridge
   Answer: B
   Why: "Treasure" has the soft buzzing /ʒ/ sound. "Jug" and "bridge" have /ʤ/. "Yellow" has /j/.
5. What does "stranger" mean? (2 marks)
   Answer: A person you do not know.
   Why: One mark for "person", one mark for "you do not know".
6. Write two polite phrases you can use during a conversation. (2 marks)
   Answer: For example: "May I add something?" and "Please go on, I am listening."
   Why: One mark for each polite and correct phrase.
7. Rewrite this sentence in the past tense: "I watch a video at the cyber café." (2 marks)
   Answer: I watched a video at the cyber café.
   Why: One mark for "watched" and one mark for keeping the rest of the sentence correct.
8. List three good listening habits. (3 marks)
   Answer: For example: look at the speaker; keep quiet until the speaker has finished; nod to show you follow.
   Why: One mark for each correct habit.
9. Write four words that begin or contain the sound /j/. (2 marks)
   Answer: For example: yes, yellow, young, yam.
   Why: Half a mark for each correct word, giving a total of 2 marks.
