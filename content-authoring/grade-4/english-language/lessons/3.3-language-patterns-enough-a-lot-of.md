## PAGE 1: A poem with two patterns
Read this poem aloud with a friend. Then read it again and listen for the words "enough" and "a lot of".

### Good Manners
We have enough time to say hello,
We have enough words to say thank you.
There are a lot of ways to be kind,
And a lot of friends who are kind to you.

We have enough hands to help a neighbour,
Enough smiles to share around.
Our school has a lot of children,
And a lot of happy sounds.

### Find the patterns
In this poem you can find two language patterns. A language pattern is a way of putting words together that we use again and again.
• Pattern 1: enough + noun + to + verb. Example: enough time to say hello.
• Pattern 2: a lot of + noun. Example: a lot of children.

### Word class check
A noun is the name of a person, a place, an animal or a thing. In pattern 1 the word after "enough" is a noun: time, words, hands, smiles. The word "to" is followed by a verb such as say, help or share. A verb is a doing word. When "to" is followed by a verb in this way, we call the two words together an infinitive: to say, to help, to share.

## PAGE 2: The pattern enough + noun + to + verb
We use this pattern to say that we have as much as we need, or as many as we need, for a certain purpose.

### The pattern
subject + have/has + enough + noun + to + verb
• We have enough chairs to sit on.
• There is enough water to drink.
• Mother bought enough flour to make chapati for everyone.
• The bus has enough seats to carry all the children.

### Study the parts
• enough: as much or as many as needed.
• noun: chairs, water, flour, seats.
• to + verb: tells what we need it for: to sit on, to drink, to make, to carry.

### Both types of nouns
Enough works with plural nouns that we can count (enough chairs) and with nouns we cannot count (enough water, enough rice, enough time).

### Word order
Enough comes before the noun: enough food. It does not come after: not "food enough". However, with adjectives it comes after: "tall enough".
• She is old enough to go to school. (adjective + enough + to)
• The table is big enough to seat six people.
• Correct: enough milk. Incorrect: milk enough.

### Try it
Complete these: (1) We have enough plates ___ (eat) the meal. (2) There is enough rice ___ (feed) the guests. Answers: to eat; to feed.

## PAGE 3: Saying that there is not enough
We can also say that we do not have what we need. Use "not enough" or "do not have enough".

### Negative sentences
• I do not have enough money to buy a bicycle.
• There are not enough cups to serve all the visitors.
• We do not have enough time to finish the game.
• The room has not got enough space to hold the whole class.

### Questions
• Do we have enough chairs to seat the guests?
• Is there enough water to wash the plates?
• Does she have enough money to pay for the book?

Answers can be short: "Yes, we do." "No, there is not."

### Using the pattern politely
This pattern is useful when we speak about sharing.
• "Do you have enough pencils to share with the class?"
• "I have enough sweets to give one to each friend."
• "We have enough food to welcome the visitors."

### Fix the mistakes
• "We have enough chair to sit." → We have enough chairs to sit on.
• "I have money enough to buy bread." → I have enough money to buy bread.
• "There is enough rice for eat." → There is enough rice to eat.

### Speak it
In pairs, take turns to ask and answer. "Do you have enough time to read a book?" "Yes, I have enough time to read one chapter."

## PAGE 4: The pattern a lot of and a lot
"A lot of" means a large number or amount. It works with plural nouns and with nouns we cannot count.

### a lot of + noun
• There are a lot of children on the field. (plural, countable)
• We have a lot of homework. (cannot be counted)
• Our village has a lot of rain in April. (cannot be counted)
• She has a lot of friends.

### a lot without a noun
Sometimes "a lot" stands alone at the end of a sentence with no noun after it. It tells us how much or how often.
• Thank you a lot.
• She reads a lot.
• My grandmother talks a lot.
• I like mandazi a lot.

### Where to use it
"A lot of" is used mostly in positive sentences.
• We have a lot of books.
In questions and negative sentences we often use "many" with counted nouns and "much" with nouns we cannot count. You will learn more about these words later this year.
• Do you have many books? No, I do not have many books.

### Note on spelling
"A lot" is two words, never one. Write "a lot of", not "alot of".

### Try it
Write in the correct order: (1) a lot of / stories / knows / Grandfather. → Grandfather knows a lot of stories. (2) plays / lot / Kevin / a / football. → Kevin plays football a lot.

## PAGE 5: Using both patterns together
Use the patterns to talk about the family and school. Read the examples aloud.

### Examples
• Our family has a lot of visitors on Sunday. We have enough chairs to seat them.
• Mother cooked a lot of rice. There is enough food to feed everyone.
• The school has a lot of trees, and there is enough shade to sit under.
• We do not have enough time to play, but we read a lot.

### Sentence building
Choose a beginning and an ending to make new sentences.
1. We have enough paper ... to draw pictures.
2. The shop has enough bread ... to sell to all customers.
3. Wanjiku has a lot of ... beads.
4. The teacher reads ... a lot to us.

### Written exercise
Fill in each blank with enough, a lot of or a lot.
1. There are ___ cows in the shed. (a lot of)
2. We have ___ pencils to share. (enough)
3. He smiles ___. (a lot)
4. Is there ___ tea to serve everyone? (enough)
5. The compound has ___ flowers. (a lot of)

### Poem activity
In a group, write four lines about your school. Use "enough ... to" in two lines and "a lot of" in two lines. Recite them to the class.

### Remember
• enough + noun + to + verb: we have what we need.
• not enough: we lack what we need.
• a lot of + noun: many or much. "A lot" stands alone at the end of a sentence.

## QUIZ
1. Choose the correct sentence.
   A) We have chairs enough to sit on.
   B) We have enough chairs to sit on.
   C) We have enough chairs for sit on.
   D) We have enough chair to sit on.
   Answer: B
   Why: In this pattern enough comes before the noun, and to is followed by a verb.
2. Which word completes the sentence? "There is enough water ___ drink."
   A) for
   B) to
   C) at
   D) by
   Answer: B
   Why: The pattern is enough + noun + to + verb.
3. Which sentence uses "a lot" correctly, without a noun after it?
   A) She has a lot books.
   B) She reads a lot.
   C) She reads alot of.
   D) She a lot reads.
   Answer: B
   Why: A lot can stand at the end of a sentence to show how often she reads.
4. Which is a correct negative sentence?
   A) There is not enough cups.
   B) There are not enough cups to serve the visitors.
   C) There are enough not cups.
   D) There not are enough cups.
   Answer: B
   Why: Cups is plural, so we use are, and the negative is are not enough.
5. What does "a lot of" mean in "We have a lot of rain in April"?
   A) None
   B) Very little
   C) A large amount
   D) Exactly ten litres
   Answer: C
   Why: A lot of means a large number or amount.
6. Complete: "Mother bought enough flour ___ chapati for the guests." (2 marks)
   Answer: to make
   Why: 1 mark for to and 1 mark for a suitable verb such as make or cook.
7. Correct these two sentences: (a) "I have money enough to buy bread." (b) "She has alot of friends." (2 marks)
   Answer: (a) I have enough money to buy bread. (b) She has a lot of friends.
   Why: 1 mark for each correction.
8. Write two sentences about your school: one with "enough ... to" and one with "a lot of". (3 marks)
   Answer: For example: We have enough desks to seat every learner. The school has a lot of trees.
   Why: 1 mark for the enough pattern, 1 mark for the a lot of pattern and 1 mark for correct punctuation.
9. Explain why "enough" comes before a noun but after an adjective. Give an example of each. (3 marks)
   Answer: With a noun, enough comes first: enough food. With an adjective, enough comes after it: old enough. This is how English places the word in each pattern.
   Why: 1 mark for each example and 1 mark for the idea of position before nouns and after adjectives.
