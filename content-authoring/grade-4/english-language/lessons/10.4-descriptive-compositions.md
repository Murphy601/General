## PAGE 1: What is a descriptive composition?
A descriptive composition paints a picture with words. Instead of telling a story with events, you describe a person, a place, an object or an activity so clearly that the reader can imagine it.

### Compare
• Narrative: "We cleaned the classroom and then went home."
• Descriptive: "The classroom is bright and tidy. The desks stand in neat rows, and the floor shines after being mopped."

### What can you describe?
• A person: my mother, my teacher.
• A place: my school, our compound, the market.
• An activity: how we clean our house.
• An object: my school bag.

### Tense
Descriptions are often written in the present tense, as in "The floor shines." If you describe a past memory, use the past tense.

### Why is it useful?
Good descriptions help readers see, hear, smell, feel and taste what you write about. They make your writing interesting and clear.

### Try it
Look at an object in your hand, such as a pencil or a water bottle. Say three sentences about it. What does it look like? What does it feel like? What is it used for?

## PAGE 2: Using your five senses
Good descriptive writers use all five senses.

• Sight: colours, shapes, size ("a tall, green tree")
• Sound: "the dripping tap", "the loud laughter"
• Smell: "the fresh smell of soap"
• Touch: "smooth, cool water", "rough brush"
• Taste: "sweet mango", "bitter medicine"

### Describing words (adjectives)
Choose exact adjectives instead of plain words.
• Plain: nice room
• Better: bright, tidy room
• Plain: dirty clothes
• Better: muddy, crumpled clothes
• Plain: good smell
• Better: fresh, sweet smell of soap

### Example: describing a clean bathroom
"The bathroom is small but spotless. The walls are white, and the floor is dry. A bar of soap sits on the shelf, giving off a fresh smell. Clean towels hang on the hook. Water drips softly from the tap."

### Senses in this description
Sight: small, spotless, white. Smell: fresh smell. Sound: drips softly. Touch: dry, clean towels.

### Try it
Choose a place in your home, such as the kitchen. Write one sentence using each of two different senses.

### Each paragraph has one main idea
• Paragraph 1 introduces the topic in one or two sentences.
• Paragraph 2 gives the details.
• Paragraph 3 closes with a feeling or a message.

### Topic sentences
The first sentence of a paragraph, the topic sentence, tells what the paragraph is about.
• "I keep myself clean every day."
• "Our compound is tidy."

### Connecting words
Use words like first, next, also, then, and finally to link sentences. In descriptions, place words such as beside, near, behind and in front of help the reader see where things are.

### Write four descriptive sentences
Topic: How I keep myself clean.
1. I wash my face with soap and cold water every morning.
2. I scrub my hands after using the toilet.
3. I brush my teeth twice a day and my mouth feels fresh.
4. I wear clean clothes, so I feel comfortable and confident.

## PAGE 4: A model composition
Here is a model of about 90 words. It describes how a child keeps clean.

How I Keep Myself Clean

I keep myself clean every day because good hygiene protects me from illness. My family lives in a small house near Naivasha.

Every morning, I wash my face with cold water and soap. I brush my teeth until they shine. Before I eat, I scrub my hands and rinse them well. After school, I take a bath and put on clean clothes. My nails are short, so no dirt can hide beneath them.

I feel fresh and healthy when I am clean. I hope everyone will keep clean too.

### Study the model
• Paragraph 1 introduces the topic and the reason.
• Paragraph 2 gives details of actions: wash, brush, scrub, bath.
• Paragraph 3 gives the feeling and a wish.
• It uses the present tense.
• It uses describing words such as cold, clean, short, fresh, healthy.
• Sentences vary in length.

### A checklist
Does it have two or three paragraphs? Are there details for the senses? Is the length right? Does it flow? Is it neat?

### Another topic
"My Clean Classroom": describe the floor, the desks, the walls and the board. Say how pupils keep the classroom clean.

## PAGE 5: Writing your own
### Steps
1. Choose a topic: "How we clean our house", "My school compound" or "My favourite place".
2. Talk about it with a partner for two minutes.
3. Make a plan with a paragraph for each part.
4. Write a first draft.
5. Read it aloud.
6. Improve: add sensory words, fix punctuation.
7. Count the words: aim for 80 to 100.
8. Write a neat final copy.

### Ideas for "How we clean our house"
• Who cleans?
• What tools do you use: broom, mop, bucket, cloth?
• What do you clean first? Then what?
• How does the house look and smell after?

### Sample opening lines
• "Every Saturday morning, our house is full of noise and energy."
• "The smell of soap and wet cement fills our home on cleaning day."

### Sample closing lines
• "By noon, the house is shining, and we feel proud."
• "I love a clean home because it makes me happy and healthy."

### Avoid
• Repeating the same word too often: "nice, nice, nice".
• Sentences without capital letters or full stops.
• A list of unconnected sentences. Link them into paragraphs.

## PAGE 6: Proofread, display and share
### Proofreading
Proofreading means checking your work for mistakes.

Check for:
1. Spelling: use a dictionary for words you are unsure about.
2. Capital letters at the start of sentences and for names.
3. Full stops, commas and question marks.
4. Tense: keep it the same.
5. Word count: 80 to 100.

### Swap and check
Swap your composition with a partner. Read theirs and give one compliment and one suggestion, such as "Add one more detail about smell."

### Display and type
Write a clean copy for the classroom wall. If a computer or tablet is available, type your composition with help from an adult. Check that the text is spaced well and each paragraph starts on a new line.

### A checklist
• My title is clear.
• I described using at least three senses.
• I have two or three paragraphs.
• I used capital letters and full stops correctly.
• My composition is 80 to 100 words.

### Summary
• A descriptive composition paints a picture with words.
• Use your five senses and exact describing words.
• Organise it into paragraphs: introduction, details, conclusion.
• Proofread and share.

## QUIZ
1. What is a descriptive composition?
   A) A story with a problem and a solution
   B) A piece of writing that paints a picture of a person, place, object or activity
   C) A letter to a friend
   D) A list of numbers
   Answer: B
   Why: Descriptive writing describes things so that readers can picture them.
2. Which sentence uses the sense of smell?
   A) The wall is white.
   B) The soap gives off a fresh smell.
   C) The tap drips.
   D) The towel is rough.
   Answer: B
   Why: "Fresh smell" refers to smell.
3. What is a topic sentence?
   A) The last sentence of a paragraph
   B) The sentence that tells what the paragraph is about
   C) A sentence with a question
   D) A sentence that repeats the title
   Answer: B
   Why: A topic sentence states the main idea of the paragraph.
4. How many words should a descriptive composition at this level have?
   A) 20 to 30
   B) 80 to 100
   C) 200 to 300
   D) 500
   Answer: B
   Why: The composition for this level is 80 to 100 words.
5. Replace each plain word with a better describing word: nice room, dirty clothes. (2 marks)
   Answer: For example: bright, tidy room; muddy, crumpled clothes.
   Why: One mark for each suitable improvement.
6. Name the five senses. (2 marks)
   Answer: Sight, sound, smell, touch and taste.
   Why: Award 2 marks for all five, 1 mark for three or four.
7. Write four descriptive sentences about your classroom. (4 marks)
   Answer: Any four correct sentences with describing words, capital letters and full stops.
   Why: One mark for each correct sentence.
8. Plan a composition called "My School Compound". Write what you will put in each paragraph. (3 marks)
   Answer: Paragraph 1: what the compound is and where it is. Paragraph 2: details, such as trees, playground and buildings. Paragraph 3: how I feel about it.
   Why: One mark for each paragraph plan.
