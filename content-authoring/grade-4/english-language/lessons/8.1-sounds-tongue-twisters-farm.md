## PAGE 1: Saying words clearly
When you speak clearly, people understand you the first time. When words are mumbled or sounds are mixed up, the listener may hear a different word. "Ship" and "sheep", "very" and "berry", and "wet" and "vet" are different words, because a single sound is different.

This lesson helps you say five sound groups correctly:
• the vowel glide /ʊə/ as in "poor"
• the consonants /v/ as in "van" and /w/ as in "water"
• the consonant /s/ as in "sun"
• the consonant /ʧ/ as in "chair"

### Tips for clear speech
1. Open your mouth enough. Small mouth movement makes muddy sound.
2. Speak slowly at first, then speed up.
3. Listen to a good speaker, such as your teacher, a radio presenter or a recording, and copy them.
4. Practise a little every day.
5. Use a mirror to watch your lips and tongue.

### The farm theme
This unit uses words from the farm. Say these words aloud: farm, farmer, field, harvest, pests, plant, indigenous, factory, fertilizer, tractor, plough, shed.

### Try it
Say the word "harvest" three times. Which sounds are at the beginning and the end? The first sound is /h/, and the word ends with /st/.

## PAGE 2: The sounds /v/ and /w/
Many learners mix up /v/ and /w/. They are made differently.

### /v/
Bite your lower lip lightly with your upper teeth. Blow air out and use your voice. It buzzes. Words with /v/:
• van, vet, very, visit
• river, seven, harvest, vegetables
• love, five, have, give

### /w/
Round your lips as if you are going to whistle. Do not touch your teeth. Move your lips to the next sound. Words with /w/:
• water, window, wheat, week
• weed, wall, worm
• away, sweet, twelve
The letters wh at the start of many words are also said as /w/: what, when, where, which, why.

### Compare
• vine / wine
• vet / wet
• veal / wheel
• very / wary

### Practice sentences
1. "The farmer visited the valley in the village."
2. "We watered the weeds and the vegetables."
3. "Very fresh vegetables were weighed at the wide market."

## PAGE 3: The sounds /s/ and /ʧ/, and the diphthong /ʊə/
### The sound /s/
Put your tongue near the ridge behind your top teeth and blow air. It is like a snake. Do not use your voice. Words: sun, seed, soil, sell, sack, grass, house, face.

### The sound /ʧ/
This sound is a mix of /t/ and /ʃ/. It is written "ch" or sometimes "tch". Words: chair, cheese, chicken, church, chop, children, match, catch, teacher.

Compare /s/ and /ʧ/:
• sip and chip
• sin and chin
• seat and cheat
• sore and chore

### The sound /ʊə/
This is a vowel glide. It starts with the lips rounded, as in "book", and moves to a soft "uh". Words: poor, sure, tour, pure, cure, mature, secure, manure.
Note: some speakers say "poor" like "pour" (with /ɔː/). Both may be heard. Follow your teacher's model.

### Practice sentences
1. "Sure, the soil is poor, but the manure will cure it."
2. "The farmer chose cheap cheese and chicken at the chemist's shop."
3. "Chinelo sold six sacks of sweet corn."

## PAGE 4: Tongue twisters
A tongue twister is a group of words that are hard to say fast because the sounds are alike. They are fun, and they train your tongue and lips.

### How to practise
1. Say it very slowly, sounding each word.
2. Say it at a normal speed.
3. Say it faster without making mistakes.
4. Say it three times with no pause.

### Tongue twisters to try
• Vera viewed seven very green vegetables in the village.
• Which wheat was washed in the wet water?
• Sam sold six sacks of sweet sugar cane.
• The chief chose cheap cheese and chunky chicken.
• Poor Sure Ruth toured the pure, secure farm.

### Make your own
Choose one sound, such as /w/. List ten words with the sound. Put five or six in a short sentence.
Example with /w/: "We watched worms wriggle when the wind whistled."
Then test it on a friend. Can they say it three times fast?

### Note about fair play
Tongue twisters are a game. When your friend makes a mistake, do not laugh unkindly. Encourage them and try again together.

## PAGE 5: Vocabulary from the farm
Match the word with its meaning.
• farm: land used to grow crops or keep animals
• farmer: a person who works on a farm
• field: an open piece of land where crops grow
• harvest: to gather crops that are ready; also the crops gathered
• pests: small animals or insects that damage crops
• plant: a living thing that grows in soil; also, to put seeds in soil
• indigenous: belonging naturally to a place
• factory: a building where things are made or processed, such as tea
• fertilizer: something added to soil to help plants grow
• tractor: a strong vehicle that pulls farm machines
• plough: a tool or machine used to turn over soil before planting
• shed: a small building for storing tools or sheltering animals

### Use the words
1. "The farmer used a plough to prepare the field."
2. "Pests ate the leaves of the young cabbages."
3. "Indigenous vegetables grow well in our county."
4. "The tea is taken to the factory after the harvest."

### Word in context
Fill the gaps: "We keep the tools in the ____. Farmers add ____ to soil so the maize grows well." Answers: shed, fertilizer.

### Pronunciation reminder
"Indigenous" has four syllables: in-DIJ-i-nus. "Fertilizer" has four: FUR-ti-lie-zer. Clap the syllables as you say each word.

## PAGE 6: Listening for sounds and speaking clearly
### Listening script
Read this aloud to a partner, and let them listen for the sounds.
"Very early in the week, the farmer went to the field. He saw seven weeds near the wheat. He cut them with a hoe and put them in a sack. Then he visited the shed to fetch some manure. Last of all, he cheerfully sat under a tree and ate cheese and bread with his children."

### Tasks
1. Write down three words with /w/. (week, went, weeds, wheat)
2. Write two words with /v/. (very, seven, visited)
3. Write two with /ʧ/. (cheese, children, cheerfully)
4. Write one word with /ʊə/. (manure)

### Role of pronunciation
Correct sounds help us to be understood and to listen well. When you listen, you can pick out sounds, words and meaning. When you speak, others get your message.

### Summary
• /v/ uses the teeth and lip; /w/ uses rounded lips only.
• /s/ is a hiss; /ʧ/ is like "ch" in "chair".
• /ʊə/ glides from "oo" to "uh".
• Practise tongue twisters slowly, then fast.
• Farm words: plough, harvest, pests, fertilizer, shed.

## QUIZ
1. Which word begins with the sound /w/?
   A) van
   B) wheat
   C) vet
   D) very
   Answer: B
   Why: "Wheat" starts with /w/, made with rounded lips. The others start with /v/.
2. Which word has the sound /ʧ/?
   A) cheese
   B) seed
   C) soil
   D) sun
   Answer: A
   Why: "Cheese" begins with "ch", the /ʧ/ sound.
3. Which pair are different only because of /v/ and /w/?
   A) sip and chip
   B) vet and wet
   C) sit and sat
   D) chair and share
   Answer: B
   Why: "Vet" has /v/ and "wet" has /w/. The other letters are the same.
4. What does "indigenous" mean?
   A) Very expensive
   B) Coming from far away
   C) Belonging naturally to a place
   D) Damaged by pests
   Answer: C
   Why: Indigenous plants and animals are native to a place.
5. Write two words from the farm that contain the sound /s/. (2 marks)
   Answer: For example: seed, soil, sack, grass, harvest (the "s" in harvest is /s/).
   Why: One mark for each correct word.
6. Compose a tongue twister of at least eight words using the sound /w/. (3 marks)
   Answer: For example: "We watched worms wriggle when the wind whistled."
   Why: One mark for at least eight words, one mark for at least five /w/ words, one mark for a sentence that makes sense.
7. Explain how the sound /v/ is made differently from /w/. (2 marks)
   Answer: For /v/ the upper teeth touch the lower lip and the voice buzzes; for /w/ the lips are rounded and do not touch the teeth.
   Why: One mark for each correct description.
8. Give the meaning of each word and use it in a sentence: (a) harvest, (b) pests. (4 marks)
   Answer: (a) Harvest: to gather crops that are ready. "We harvest maize in August." (b) Pests: small animals or insects that damage crops. "Pests ate the cabbages."
   Why: Two marks for each word: one for the meaning and one for a correct sentence.
