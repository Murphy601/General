## PAGE 1: What are double consonants?
Some words have the same consonant letter twice in a row. These are called double consonants.

• letter: l-e-t-t-e-r (double t)
• happy: h-a-p-p-y (double p)
• summer: s-u-m-m-e-r (double m)
• yellow: y-e-l-l-o-w (double l)
• dinner: d-i-n-n-e-r (double n)

The double letter usually makes just one sound. In "letter" we say "let-er", not "let-ter" with two t sounds.

### Why they matter
If you leave out a double letter, or add one wrongly, the word is misspelt. Compare: "hopping" and "hoping". "Hopping" has a double p and means jumping on one foot. "Hoping" has one p and means wishing.

### Which consonants double?
Most consonants can be doubled: b, d, f, g, l, m, n, p, r, s, t, z. In English, we almost never double h, j, q, v, w, x or y.

### Money words with double consonants
• coffee (ff), account (cc), accountant (cc), dollar (ll)
• address (dd, ss), bill (ll), sell (ll), borrow (rr)
You will meet more of these later in the lesson.

### Try it
Write the words "letter", "happy" and "summer" in your book. Circle the double letters. Say each word slowly in syllables: let-ter, hap-py, sum-mer.

## PAGE 2: Double letters in the middle of words
Many words have double consonants in the middle, where the first syllable ends with a short vowel.

### Word lists
• bb: rabbit, hobby, pebble, bubble
• dd: ladder, middle, address, puddle
• ff: coffee, office, different, traffic
• gg: bigger, luggage, egg (at the end), jogging
• ll: yellow, pillow, bullet, dollar, silly
• mm: summer, hammer, comma, common
• nn: dinner, funny, tunnel, running
• pp: happy, apple, supper, puppy
• rr: mirror, borrow, tomorrow, carrot
• ss: lesson, message, missing, blossom
• tt: letter, butter, button, bottle, kitten
• zz: puzzle, buzzing, pizza, dizzy

### Careful
Village, comfort and money do not have double consonants. Learn each word as you meet it, and check with a dictionary.

### Sentences
1. "My rabbit ate a carrot in the middle of the garden."
2. "We had coffee at the office."
3. "The puppy chased a kitten across the puddle."
4. "Please write your address at the bottom of the letter."

### Trick: hear the short vowel
When a vowel is short and the next syllable begins with a consonant, the consonant is often doubled: rab-bit, lad-der, sum-mer.

### Try it
Cover the list and write ten words with double letters from memory. Then check.

## PAGE 3: Double letters at the end of words
Some short words end with a double consonant: ff, ll, ss, zz.

• ff: off, stuff, cliff, staff, sniff
• ll: ball, bell, tall, well, sell, bill, pull, small
• ss: class, glass, grass, boss, kiss, miss, dress, less
• zz: buzz, fizz, jazz
• Also: egg, add, odd, inn, ebb

### The "floss" rule
Words of one syllable that end in f, l or s after a short vowel often double the final letter: staff, bell, class. (Not all words follow this: "gas", "bus", "yes", "if", "of" do not.)

### Examples in money
• sell, bill, till (the drawer in a shop), fill
• "Please sell me this ball."
• "The bill for the meal is here."

### Adding endings
When the word already ends in a double letter, keep it: sell, selling; pass, passing; miss, missing; add, adding. Do not drop a letter.

### Sentences
1. "The class had a small ball and a tall bell."
2. "The grass was cut off the field."
3. "We sell balls at the school shop."

### Try it
Write five words ending in ll and five ending in ss. Read them aloud.

## PAGE 4: Doubling before adding -ing or -ed
When you add -ing or -ed to a short verb that ends in one vowel followed by one consonant, you double the last consonant. This keeps the vowel short.

### Examples
• stop: stopping, stopped
• run: running
• hop: hopping, hopped
• sit: sitting
• swim: swimming
• shop: shopping, shopped
• plan: planning, planned
• chat: chatting, chatted
• drop: dropping, dropped
• skip: skipping, skipped

### When we do not double
• If the word ends in two consonants: walk, walking; help, helping.
• If there are two vowels before the last consonant: rain, raining; read, reading.
• If the word ends in w, x or y: play, playing; snow, snowing; fix, fixing.
• If the last syllable is not stressed: open, opening; visit, visiting.

### Compare
• hop / hopping (short vowel, doubled)
• hope / hoping (long vowel, silent e dropped, no doubling)
• plan / planned

### Sentences about money
1. "We are shopping for school shoes."
2. "She stopped at the stall to buy mangoes."
3. "He is planning to save fifty shillings."

### Practice
Write the -ing form of: sit, stop, run, help, plan, visit, swim.
Answers: sitting, stopping, running, helping, planning, visiting, swimming.

## PAGE 5: Unscrambling and sentences
### Jumbled letters
Put the letters in order to make a word with a double consonant.
1. e-r-t-l-e-t → letter
2. p-p-h-a-y → happy
3. m-r-s-u-e-m → summer
4. l-l-o-w-y-e → yellow
5. i-n-n-e-r-d → dinner
6. z-z-p-u-l-e → puzzle
7. t-u-b-t-e-r → butter
8. d-r-a-s-d-e-s → address

### Jigsaw words
Cut card into pieces. On each piece, write part of a word: "let-", "-ter", "hap-", "-py", "sum-", "-mer". Join to make words.

### Complete the word
Fill in the missing double letters.
1. ha__y (pp)
2. di__er (nn)
3. le__er (tt)
4. ye__ow (ll)
5. me__age (ss)
6. bu__on (tt)
Answers: happy, dinner, letter, yellow, message, button.

### Sentences
Use each of these in a sentence: letter, dinner, yellow, address, rabbit.
• "I wrote a letter to my aunt."
• "We ate dinner at seven."
• "A yellow bird sat on the branch."
• "Please write your address."
• "The rabbit has soft fur."

### Dictation
Ask a friend to read out: "The happy rabbit ate a carrot at dinner. The little kitten sat on the yellow pillow."
Check your spelling and correct each error.

## PAGE 6: Money words and summary
### Money words with double consonants
• account: a record of money in a bank (cc)
• accountant: a person who keeps money records (cc)
• borrow: to take and return (rr)
• dollar: a unit of money in some countries (ll)
• bill: a paper that shows what you owe (ll)
• sell: to give in return for money (ll)
• coffee: a drink; also a crop farmed in Kenya (ff)
• office: a place where people work (ff)
• afford: to have enough money to buy something (ff)
• allowance: money given regularly (ll)
• address: where you live or where to send a letter (dd, ss)
• success: doing what you wanted to do (cc, ss)

### Sentences
1. "My aunt is an accountant at a bank."
2. "I can afford this book because I saved money."
3. "He sold his coffee at the market."
4. "Mother gave me an allowance for the week."

### Spelling strategy
1. Say the word in syllables.
2. Look for a short vowel before a consonant, and check whether it is doubled.
3. Write it three times.
4. Cover it and write again.
5. Check with a dictionary.

### Summary
• Double consonants appear in the middle and at the end of words.
• The double letter makes one sound.
• Double the last consonant before -ing or -ed if the word has a short vowel and ends in one consonant (stop, stopping).
• Learn each word and use a dictionary.

## QUIZ
1. Which word has a double consonant?
   A) letter
   B) later
   C) paper
   D) river
   Answer: A
   Why: "Letter" has a double t.
2. Which spelling is correct?
   A) happpy
   B) hapy
   C) happy
   D) hapie
   Answer: C
   Why: "Happy" is spelt h-a-p-p-y with a double p.
3. What is the -ing form of "stop"?
   A) stoping
   B) stopping
   C) stopeing
   D) stoppping
   Answer: B
   Why: We double the last consonant of a short verb that ends in one vowel plus one consonant.
4. Which word is spelt correctly?
   A) adress
   B) address
   C) addres
   D) adres
   Answer: B
   Why: "Address" has a double d and a double s.
5. Fill in the double letters: di__er, ye__ow, bu__on. (3 marks)
   Answer: dinner, yellow, button.
   Why: One mark for each.
6. Write the -ing form of: run, sit, swim. (3 marks)
   Answer: running, sitting, swimming.
   Why: One mark for each.
7. Explain the difference in meaning between "hopping" and "hoping". Use each in a sentence. (3 marks)
   Answer: "Hopping" means jumping, for example "The rabbit is hopping." "Hoping" means wishing, for example "I am hoping to win."
   Why: One mark for each meaning and one mark for correct sentences.
8. Write four sentences using words with double consonants that you learnt. (4 marks)
   Answer: Any four correct sentences with correctly spelt double consonant words.
   Why: One mark for each correct sentence.
