## PAGE 1: Reading a greeting card
French is spoken in many countries around the world, including France, Belgium, Canada, and countries in Africa such as Senegal, Côte d'Ivoire and Rwanda. Children in these countries also send cards with greetings. On a greeting card you might read the words Bonjour or Salut.

Reading aloud in French is different from reading in English. Some letters at the end of a word are silent. For example, in Salut, the final t is not said. In Bonjour, the final r is said, but the n is nasal.

### A card to read
Bonjour, Amina !
Salut, Kamau !
Bonsoir, Madame !
Au revoir, Monsieur !

Madame is used for a lady. Monsieur is used for a man. Both are polite words, like Madam and Sir.

### Say it
Madame = Mrs, Madam         (ma-DAM)
Monsieur = Sir, Mr          (muh-SYUH)

### Try it
Read each line of the card aloud. Point to each word with your finger as you read it. Then read the card to a partner.

## PAGE 2: Silent letters and sounds
When you read French aloud, you must know which letters are not said. This is an important skill in reading. The last letter of many French words is silent, especially s, t, d and x.

### Words to read
salut = hi, bye             (sa-LÜ) the t is silent
merci = thank you           (mair-SEE)
bonsoir = good evening      (bon-SWAR)
au revoir = goodbye         (oh ruh-VWAR)
je = I                      (zhuh)

Notice that in au revoir, the letters au make the sound oh. The letters oi in revoir make the sound wa.

### Letter groups
au = oh        as in au revoir
oi = wa        as in bonsoir, revoir
ou = oo        as in bonjour, jour
on = nasal o   as in bonjour, bon

### Practise
Read these words slowly and then faster: bonjour, bonsoir, au revoir, merci, salut. Ask a friend to listen. If they hear a word wrong, read it again.

## PAGE 3: Reading names
In French, names are read with the French sounds. When reading a name from a name card, look at each letter group. For a name like Amina, you read ah-mee-NAH, saying each letter with its plain sound. Keep the vowels short and clear, and do not add extra sounds.

### Name game
Write your name on a card. Put all the cards in a bag. Each person picks a card and reads the name aloud. Then they greet that person: Bonjour, Amina ! The owner of the name answers: Bonjour !

### Meet some friends
Read this short text aloud.
Salut ! Je m'appelle Otieno. Et toi ? Comment tu t'appelles ?
Bonjour ! Je m'appelle Njeri. Enchantée !

Enchanté means Pleased to meet you. A boy says enchanté. A girl says enchantée. They are said the same way: on-shon-TAY.

### Say it
Et toi ? = And you?              (ay TWA)
Comment tu t'appelles ? = What is your name?
Je m'appelle ... = My name is ...

## PAGE 4: Reading with the alphabet
You can also read the letters of the alphabet on flashcards. Show a card with the letter B. Say bay. Show a card with the letter J. Say zhee. You can then read a word by naming each letter.

### Spell-and-read game
Look at this word: SALUT. Its letters are ess, ah, el, ü, tay. When you put the sounds together, you read sa-LÜ.

Now look at this word: MERCI. Its letters are em, uh, air, say, ee. Together you read mair-SEE.

### Flashcards
Work in pairs. One person shows a picture of a greeting. The other reads the word. Use pictures of a sun rising for bonjour, a moon for bonsoir and a waving hand for au revoir.

### Phrase reading
When you read a whole line, do not stop after each word. Read chunks: Bonjour, // je m'appelle Amina. // Et toi ? The mark // shows where you take a short breath. Keep your voice friendly.

### Try it
Read the four chunks above aloud three times. Each time, try to be smoother.

## PAGE 5: Read, check and summarise
Reading aloud is a skill that grows with practice. Choose a friendly voice, speak clearly, and read at a steady speed. If you make a mistake, smile and read the word again.

### Read this dialogue in pairs
Amina: Bonjour, Kamau !
Kamau: Salut, Amina ! Ça va ?
Amina: Oui, ça va. Et toi ?
Kamau: Ça va bien, merci. Au revoir !
Amina: Au revoir !

Ça va ? means How are you? Oui means yes. Bien means well.

### Common mistakes
• Saying the last t in salut. It stays silent.
• Reading bonjour like English bon jaw. The ou is oo, and the n is soft.
• Reading fast without breath marks. Read in chunks.

### Summary
You can now read greetings on cards, recognise silent letters, use letter groups au, oi and ou, read names with French sounds and read a short dialogue aloud. Keep practising at home with a family member.

## QUIZ
1. Which letter is silent in the word salut?
   A) s
   B) a
   C) l
   D) t
   Answer: D
   Why: The final t of salut is not pronounced. We say sa-LÜ.
2. What sound do the letters au make in au revoir?
   A) ow as in cow
   B) oh
   C) ah
   D) ay
   Answer: B
   Why: In French, au is said oh.
3. Which word do we use to address a man politely?
   A) Madame
   B) Monsieur
   C) Salut
   D) Merci
   Answer: B
   Why: Monsieur means Sir or Mr.
4. What does Ça va bien, merci mean?
   A) I am well, thank you
   B) Goodbye, my friend
   C) What is your name?
   D) See you tomorrow
   Answer: A
   Why: Ça va bien means I am fine and merci means thank you.
5. How do you say oi in bonsoir?
   A) oy
   B) oh
   C) wa
   D) ee
   Answer: C
   Why: The letters oi are said wa, as in bon-SWAR.
6. Write the French for Goodbye, Sir. (2 marks)
   Answer: Au revoir, Monsieur.
   Why: 1 mark for Au revoir and 1 mark for Monsieur.
7. Read this line and write the respelling of the first word: Bonjour, je m'appelle Njeri. (2 marks)
   Answer: bon-ZHOOR
   Why: 1 mark for zh at the start of the second syllable and 1 mark for oor.
8. Explain what the mark // means when reading a phrase aloud. (2 marks)
   Answer: It shows a short pause to take a breath between chunks so the reading sounds smooth.
   Why: 1 mark for pause and 1 mark for smooth reading.
9. Write a dialogue of three short lines between two friends meeting. (3 marks)
   Answer: Salut ! Ça va ? Oui, ça va bien, merci.
   Why: 1 mark for a greeting, 1 mark for the question and 1 mark for the answer.
