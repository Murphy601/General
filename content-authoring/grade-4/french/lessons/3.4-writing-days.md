## PAGE 1: Spelling the seven days
To write the days in French, you must know how each one is spelled. Study this list and copy it into your exercise book.

lundi
mardi
mercredi
jeudi
vendredi
samedi
dimanche

### Spelling hints
• Six days end in di: lundi, mardi, mercredi, jeudi, vendredi, samedi.
• Only dimanche ends in che.
• Mercredi has the letters c-r-e-d-i in the middle. Say mer-cre-di as you write it.
• Vendredi has the letters d-r-e-d-i in the ending. Say ven-dre-di.
• Jeudi begins with j and eu.

### Small letters
In French, the days of the week are written with a small letter at the start, unless they are at the beginning of a sentence. In English we write Monday with a capital M. In French we write lundi with a small l.

### Try it
Look at the list for one minute. Cover it. Write the seven days in order from memory. Then check with the list. How many did you write correctly?

## PAGE 2: Writing on the board in turns
Writing on the board is a fun class activity. Each learner takes a turn to write one day of the week. Do not interrupt the person who is writing. Wait until they finish, then help gently if they need it.

### Group activity
The first person writes lundi. The second writes mardi. The third writes mercredi. Continue until you have all seven days on the board. Then read the days aloud together.

### Tips for neat writing
• Write letters of the same size.
• Leave a small space between words.
• Start at the left of the board and write in a straight line.
• Hold the chalk or marker lightly.

### Patience
Everyone writes at a different speed. Some learners need more time. Show respect and patience by giving each person time. This is a value called tolerance, and it makes our class a happy place.

### Try it
Choose a day and write it on the board in large, neat letters. Then say the day aloud in French.

## PAGE 3: Filling the gaps
In gap-fill exercises, you complete a text with the missing word. Look at the days in order to find the missing one.

### Exercise 1: Missing days
lundi, ______, mercredi, jeudi
vendredi, ______, dimanche
Answers: mardi and samedi.

### Exercise 2: Missing word in a text
Le ______, je vais à l'école. (day: lundi)
Le samedi, je ______. (verb: joue)
Le dimanche, je vais à l'______. (place: église)

### Exercise 3: Yesterday and tomorrow
Aujourd'hui, c'est mardi. Demain, c'est ______.
Aujourd'hui, c'est jeudi. Hier, c'était ______.

Careful: the second sentence uses c'était, which means it was. It has an accent aigu. Answers: mercredi and mercredi.

### Tip
Read the sentence aloud in your head. Ask yourself, does it make sense? If not, try a different word.

### Try it
Write two of your own gap-fill sentences for a partner and check their answers.

## PAGE 4: Writing sentences about the week
Now you write sentences about what you do on different days. Use this pattern.

Le + day, je + verb.
Le lundi, je lis.
Le mardi, je chante.
Le samedi, j'aide ma mère.
Le dimanche, je me repose.

### Punctuation
Every sentence ends with a full stop, a question mark or an exclamation mark. In French, put a space before a question mark or exclamation mark: Quel jour est-ce ? Bonjour !

### A short text about my week
Je m'appelle Njeri. Le lundi, je vais à l'école. Le mercredi, je joue avec mes amis. Le samedi, j'aide ma mère. Le dimanche, je vais à l'église. J'aime ma semaine !

J'aime means I like. Avec mes amis means with my friends.

### Try it
Write three sentences about your own week in French. Use the pattern Le ..., je ... Read them to a friend.

## PAGE 5: Managing time and checking work
A week has seven days, and each day gives you time to study, help at home, rest and play. Write a small timetable to plan your time well.

lundi      école
mardi      école
mercredi   école
jeudi      école
vendredi   école
samedi     jouer
dimanche   se reposer

### Checking your writing
1. Did I use a small letter for each day?
2. Are all seven days spelled correctly?
3. Does each sentence start with a capital letter and end with a full stop?
4. Can someone else read my handwriting easily?

### Common mistakes
• Writing Lundi with a capital letter in the middle of a sentence.
• Writing mercredi as mecredi or mercredy.
• Writing vendredi as vandredi.
• Missing the space before ? and !

### Summary
You can now spell the seven days, write them in order, fill gaps in short texts, write simple sentences about the week and plan your time. Legible handwriting makes your writing easy to read.

## QUIZ
1. Which is the correct spelling of Wednesday in French?
   A) mecredi
   B) mercredi
   C) mercredy
   D) merkredi
   Answer: B
   Why: Wednesday is spelled m-e-r-c-r-e-d-i.
2. Which day comes after mardi?
   A) lundi
   B) mercredi
   C) jeudi
   D) vendredi
   Answer: B
   Why: The order is lundi, mardi, mercredi.
3. How do we normally write the days in French?
   A) with a capital letter
   B) with a small letter
   C) all in capitals
   D) with an accent
   Answer: B
   Why: French days are written with a small letter inside a sentence.
4. Which day ends in che?
   A) samedi
   B) lundi
   C) dimanche
   D) jeudi
   Answer: C
   Why: Dimanche is the only day that ends in che.
5. Which sentence is correct?
   A) Le lundi, je vais à l'école.
   B) Le lundi je vais a l'ecole
   C) le lundi Je vais à l'école
   D) Le lundi, je vais à l'école,
   Answer: A
   Why: It has the capital letter at the start, the accents and the full stop.
6. Fill in the missing days: lundi, ______, mercredi, ______, vendredi. (2 marks)
   Answer: mardi, jeudi
   Why: 1 mark for each correct day.
7. Write in French: On Saturday, I play. (2 marks)
   Answer: Le samedi, je joue.
   Why: 1 mark for le samedi and 1 mark for je joue.
8. Write two sentences in French about two days of your week. (4 marks)
   Answer: Le lundi, je vais à l'école. Le dimanche, je me repose.
   Why: 2 marks for each correct sentence.
9. Explain why it is important to give others enough time when they write on the board. (2 marks)
   Answer: It shows respect and patience, and everyone can write neatly.
   Why: 1 mark for respect and 1 mark for a reason.
