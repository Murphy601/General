## PAGE 1: Spelling food words
To write about food, copy each word with its little word. Check every letter and accent.

le pain          bread
le riz           rice
le lait          milk
le jus           juice
le thé           tea
l'eau            water
la banane        banana
la mangue        mango
l'orange         orange
la tomate        tomato
la carotte       carrot
le poisson       fish
le poulet        chicken
le gâteau        cake

### Spelling hints
• Thé has an accent aigu on the é.
• Gâteau has an accent circonflexe on the â.
• Poisson has oi and ss. Say pwa-son as you write it.
• Carotte has a double t.
• Orange starts with o and has no h.

### Try it
Study the list for two minutes. Cover it. Write eight words from memory. Then check your spelling.

## PAGE 2: Drawing and labelling
A labelled picture helps you remember words. Draw a food item and write its French name beside it, with le, la or l'.

### Example labels
A drawing of a banana        la banane
A drawing of a glass of milk    le lait
A drawing of a tomato          la tomate
A drawing of a fish            le poisson

### Making a food chart
A chart is a table or list. Make a chart with three columns in your exercise book.

Petit déjeuner (breakfast)   Déjeuner (lunch)   Dîner (dinner)

Under each column, write the foods you eat at that time. For example:
Petit déjeuner: le pain, le thé
Déjeuner: le riz, les haricots
Dîner: le poisson, la banane

### Try it
Make your own food chart. Label each food in French and colour the pictures. Show your chart to your group and read the words aloud.

## PAGE 3: Filling gaps
Complete each sentence with a word from the list.

Word bank: une, un, des, mange, bois, aime

1. C'est ______ banane.
2. C'est ______ gâteau.
3. Ce sont ______ mangues.
4. Je ______ du pain.
5. Je ______ de l'eau.
6. J'______ le lait.

Answers: 1. une  2. un  3. des  4. mange  5. bois  6. aime

Note: In sentence 6, the word j' comes before aime. Write it as J'aime, without a space.

### Tip
Look at the noun before you choose un or une. Banane and mangue need une. Gâteau needs un. In the sentences with mange and bois, du means some. Use du before a masculine food or drink you do not count, such as du pain and du thé. Use de l' before eau: de l'eau.

### Try it
Write three more gap sentences for a partner. Give them a word bank of four words.

## PAGE 4: Writing short texts
Write short texts about the foods you eat at home. Use simple sentences.

Le matin, je mange du pain. Je bois du thé.
À midi, je mange du riz et des haricots.
Le soir, je mange du poisson. J'aime les bananes.

### Use capital letters
Start each sentence with a capital letter. End it with a full stop. Names of people and places have capital letters, but foods do not.

### A menu
A menu is a list of foods on offer. Write a small menu.
Menu du jour
le riz
les haricots
le poisson
une banane
de l'eau

Du jour means of the day.

### Try it
Write your own menu for lunch with four foods. Add a title. Read it aloud to your class.

## PAGE 5: Check, share and summarise
Good writers check their work carefully. Read your sentences aloud and check for spelling and accents.

### Checklist
1. Are the food words spelled correctly?
2. Did I use un, une or des correctly?
3. Do I have accents on thé and gâteau?
4. Is there a capital letter at the start and a full stop at the end?
5. Can my friend read my handwriting?

### Common mistakes
• Writing le banane. Banane is feminine, so write la banane.
• Forgetting the accent on thé.
• Writing des gâteau for many cakes. Add s: des gâteaux.

For many cakes, the plural of gâteau is gâteaux. The plural of banane is bananes.

### Summary
You can spell food and drink words, label pictures, make a food chart, fill gaps, write short texts and a menu. Take pride in your writing and share it with others.

## QUIZ
1. Which is the correct spelling of tea?
   A) the
   B) thé
   C) te
   D) tée
   Answer: B
   Why: Tea is thé, with an accent aigu.
2. Which word fits: C'est ______ gâteau.
   A) une
   B) un
   C) des
   D) la
   Answer: B
   Why: Gâteau is masculine, so we use un.
3. What is the plural of gâteau?
   A) gâteaus
   B) gâteaux
   C) gâteau
   D) gateaux
   Answer: B
   Why: Words ending in eau add x in the plural.
4. Which sentence means I drink water?
   A) Je mange de l'eau.
   B) Je bois de l'eau.
   C) J'aime le pain.
   D) Je mange du riz.
   Answer: B
   Why: Je bois means I drink.
5. What is a menu?
   A) a list of foods on offer
   B) a kind of drink
   C) a type of fruit
   D) a plate
   Answer: A
   Why: A menu lists foods.
6. Write in French: a banana and a mango. (2 marks)
   Answer: une banane et une mangue
   Why: 1 mark for each correct phrase.
7. Write the French names of three foods you can see in the kitchen at home. (3 marks)
   Answer: Any three correct, for example le riz, le pain, la tomate.
   Why: 1 mark for each correct word with its le or la.
8. Write two sentences in French about what you eat and drink in the morning. (4 marks)
   Answer: Le matin, je mange du pain. Je bois du thé.
   Why: 2 marks for each correct sentence.
9. Explain why we must write accents on words such as thé and gâteau. (2 marks)
   Answer: Accents are part of the spelling and show how to say the letter.
   Why: 1 mark for part of the spelling and 1 mark for pronunciation.
