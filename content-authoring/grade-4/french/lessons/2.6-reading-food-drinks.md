## PAGE 1: Reading food names
Read each food and drink name aloud, matching the word to a picture. First look at the picture, then read the word underneath. Use the little word le, la or l' with each name.

le pain        bread           le riz         rice
la banane      banana          la mangue      mango
la tomate      tomato          la carotte     carrot
le poisson     fish            le poulet      chicken
le lait        milk            le jus         juice
le thé         tea             l'eau          water

### Letter groups to help you read
• ai says eh, as in lait.
• oi says wa, as in poisson.
• ou says oo, as in poulet.
• eau says oh, as in gâteau and eau.
• The final letters s, t and x are usually silent: riz, lait, pain.

### Read and match
Match each name to its picture in your mind: le lait (a glass of milk), le jus (a cup of juice), la mangue (a yellow-orange fruit). Read aloud as you match.

### Try it
Take turns with a partner. One learner points to a picture, the other reads the word aloud. Change roles after six words.

## PAGE 2: Reading short food sentences
Read these sentences aloud. Each one uses words you already know.

C'est un gâteau.            It is a cake.
C'est une banane.           It is a banana.
Ce sont des oranges.        They are oranges.
J'aime le lait.             I like milk.
Je mange une mangue.        I eat a mango.
Je bois un jus.             I drink a juice.

Je mange means I eat (zhuh MONZH). Je bois means I drink (zhuh BWA).

### Notice the s
In Ce sont des oranges, the s in des and in oranges is not said. But before the vowel in oranges, the s of des is linked to the next word and sounds like z: day-zo-RONZH. This is called liaison, and it helps the sentence flow.

### Try it
Read each sentence three times: slowly, at normal speed, and with a happy voice. Ask a partner to listen and tell you which sound was hardest.

Bon pour la santé means good for health. À manger peu means to eat little.

### Read the sentences
Les fruits et les légumes sont bons pour la santé.
Les bonbons ne sont pas bons pour les dents.

Les dents means the teeth (lay DON). Sont means are. Ne ... pas means not.

### Try it
Read the two lists aloud. Then say one sentence: La carotte, c'est bon pour la santé.

## PAGE 4: A short text about a meal
Read this text aloud. Take turns to read one sentence each.

Bonjour ! Je m'appelle Wanjiru. Le matin, je mange du pain et je bois du thé. À midi, je mange du riz et des haricots. Le soir, je mange du poisson et une banane. J'aime l'eau. C'est bon pour la santé !

### New words
le matin = in the morning
à midi = at midday (a mee-DEE)
le soir = in the evening
du = some (used with foods you cannot count)

### Comprehension
1. Que mange Wanjiru le matin ? She eats bread and drinks tea.
2. Que mange-t-elle à midi ? Rice and beans.
3. Que boit-elle ? Tea and water.
4. Is water good for your health? Yes.

Que mange ... ? means What does ... eat? Que boit ... ? means What does ... drink?

### Word search
Find five foods in this text: pain, thé, riz, haricots, poisson, banane. Circle them.

## PAGE 5: Foods from around the world
People eat different foods in different countries. Read about what a child eats in France and in Kenya.

En France, un enfant mange du pain et du fromage. En Kenya, un enfant mange du maïs et des haricots. Les deux sont bons !

Fromage means cheese (fro-MAZH). Les deux sont bons means Both are good.

### Be respectful
We should never laugh at another person's food. Foods are part of culture. Speak with respect about your food and other people's food.

### Reading tips
Pause where there is a comma. Slow down for new words. If you meet a word you do not know, read the whole sentence and use the picture to guess.

### Common mistakes
• Reading eau as ee-au. It is said oh.
• Reading final s in pois or des. It is usually silent.
• Not pausing where there is a comma.

### Summary
You can read the names of foods and drinks, read short sentences with C'est un, Ce sont des and J'aime, group foods into healthy and unhealthy, read a text about meals and talk with respect about foods from many places.

## QUIZ
1. What does le poisson mean?
   A) chicken
   B) fish
   C) rice
   D) bread
   Answer: B
   Why: Le poisson is fish.
2. How do you say eau?
   A) ee-oh
   B) oh
   C) ow
   D) ay-oo
   Answer: B
   Why: The letters eau say oh.
3. What does Je bois un jus mean?
   A) I eat a mango.
   B) I drink a juice.
   C) I like juice.
   D) I buy a juice.
   Answer: B
   Why: Je bois means I drink.
4. What does bon pour la santé mean?
   A) very tasty
   B) good for health
   C) cheap
   D) too sweet
   Answer: B
   Why: La santé means health.
5. What does à midi mean?
   A) at night
   B) in the morning
   C) at midday
   D) in the evening
   Answer: C
   Why: Midi means midday.
6. Translate into English: Je mange du riz et des haricots. (2 marks)
   Answer: I eat rice and beans.
   Why: 1 mark for I eat and 1 mark for rice and beans.
7. Read: Le matin, je mange du pain et je bois du thé. What does the speaker eat and drink in the morning? (2 marks)
   Answer: The speaker eats bread and drinks tea.
   Why: 1 mark for bread and 1 mark for tea.
8. Give two reasons why we should respect other people's foods. (2 marks)
   Answer: Foods are part of a person's culture, and respect keeps friendships happy.
   Why: 1 mark for each reason.
9. Write two sentences in French, one about a healthy food and one about a food to eat little. (4 marks)
   Answer: La banane, c'est bon pour la santé. Les bonbons, ce n'est pas bon pour la santé.
   Why: 2 marks for each correct sentence.
