# German Grade 4: notes for human checking

Coverage: 27 lessons, one per sub-strand 1.1-1.9, 2.1-2.9, 3.1-3.9 (the KICD design numbering). All manifest topics have a lesson, so no retire.json is needed.

Please double-check:
- Pronunciation guides are simple English approximations (for example "GOO-ten MOR-gen") and are not IPA.
- The Kikuyu/Luo greetings were removed on purpose; only Kiswahili "Jambo/Habari" is named as an example.
- Curriculum typos corrected: "heiss" is taught as heiß (Swiss writers use ss); "Öbst" is taught as Obst; "Sclafzimmer" as Schlafzimmer; "M-I-CH-AE-L" is taught as M-I-CH-A-E-L (Michael has no umlaut).
- Days: Sonnabend (northern German for Saturday) is not taught; only Samstag.
- Kenyan weather text (2.8): rainy season "March to May" and dry "June to September" is a simplified example; Kenya also has short rains (about Oct-Dec). The lesson says so.
- Tongue twisters come from the design (Montags macht mir meine muntere Mutter mittags meistens Mus; Am zehnten zehnten zehn Uhr...) plus well-known Fischers Fritz fischt frische Fische. Two short twisters (birthday "Bei Bens Geburtstag backt Bella bunten Butterkuchen", weather "Wenn der Wind weht, weht er weit", toes "Zehn Zehen zappeln zusammen") were composed for the course; a German speaker could confirm they read naturally.
- Songs: only the first line/idea of "Kopf, Schulter, Knie und Fuß" and "Zum Geburtstag viel Glück" are named; no full copyrighted lyrics were reproduced. Poems in the lessons are original short texts.
- Food blends (Banareis, Orapf, Colmil, Mangmil) are playful made-up words from the design, not real German words; the lesson says so.
- Grammar kept light: dative after in/auf/unter/neben (dem/der) is taught as a pattern with der/das vs die nouns; verbs sein/haben and ich-forms only, plus a few er/sie forms (kocht, liest, schläft).
- Ordinals 1st-10th are included in 3.5 (link to dates in Mathematics).
- Bank: 640+ MCQ and 175+ short items, partly generated from a checked vocabulary table (meaning, article, plural, numbers, months, days) and partly hand-written; the vocabulary table is worth a quick native-speaker skim.
