## PAGE 1: Birthday words and why clear speech matters
A birthday is a happy day. In German, birthday is der Geburtstag. Say it: dair geh-BOORTS-tahk. It is a joined word: die Geburt means birth and der Tag means day.

When you say a word clearly, everyone understands you. Articulating means moving your lips, tongue and teeth so each sound comes out clearly. If you mumble, "Geburtstag" can sound like a jumble. If you articulate, your friend hears every sound.

### Birthday wishes
Alles Gute zum Geburtstag! = All the best for your birthday! Say: AL-les GOO-te tsoom geh-BOORTS-tahk.
Herzlichen Glückwunsch! = Congratulations! Best wishes! Say: HERTS-likh-en GLÜK-vunsh.
Ich bin heute neun. = Today I am nine.
Ich bin heute zehn. = Today I am ten.

### First warm-up
Say "Geburtstag" slowly, one part at a time: Ge - burts - tag. Then say it faster, keeping each part clear.

## PAGE 2: Party words
Repeat after your teacher and point at the picture.

der Kuchen = cake. Say: KOO-khen. Notice the ch after u: a scratchy sound in the throat.
die Torte = fancy cake, gateau. Say: TOR-te.
die Kerze = candle. Say: KER-tse. The plural is die Kerzen.
das Geschenk = present. Say: geh-SHENK.
der Luftballon = balloon. Say: LOOFT-bal-lon.
die Party = party. Say: PAR-tee.
das Lied = song. Say: leet. The letters ie sound like "ee".
die Gäste = the guests. Say: GES-te. The ä sounds like the e in "bed".

### Sound spotting
• Kuchen: ch as in a soft cough.
• Kerze: z = ts.
• Geschenk: sch = sh.
• Lied: ie = ee.
• Gäste: ä = short e.

## PAGE 3: Songs and poems for birthdays
The German birthday song most children know begins "Zum Geburtstag viel Glück". It wishes the birthday child a lot of luck. Ask your teacher or a recording to play it, and sing along. Many children also sing "Happy Birthday" in English.

Viel Glück = lots of luck. Say: feel glük.

### Reading the sound flow
When you sing or recite, keep these things in mind:
• Sing every part of the long word Geburtstag.
• Keep the rhythm the same as the music.
• Open your mouth on vowels, and use your lips and tongue on consonants.

### A short poem to recite
Ich bin heute neun.
Ich habe einen Kuchen.
Ich habe ein Geschenk.
Alles Gute!

Meaning: Today I am nine. I have a cake. I have a present. All the best!

Say the poem slowly, then again more smoothly. Make your voice go up a little when you say the last line, because it is a happy wish.

### Group task
Two groups say alternate lines. Then everyone says "Alles Gute!" together with a big smile.

## PAGE 4: A birthday tongue twister
Tongue twisters train your articulation. They repeat similar sounds so your tongue gets stronger.

Bei Bens Geburtstag backt Bella bunten Butterkuchen.

Meaning: At Ben's birthday Bella bakes colourful butter cake.

### Word help
bei = at. backt = bakes. bunten = colourful. Butterkuchen = butter cake. Say the "b" like in English: lips together, then pop.

### Practice steps
1. Say only "Bei Bens Geburtstag" ten times.
2. Say only "backt Bella bunten" ten times.
3. Add "Butterkuchen" and say the whole line three times.
4. Race your partner: who can say it clearly in one breath?

### Fluency
Fluency means smooth speech without stopping too much. To become fluent, practise a little each day. Say the tongue twister while walking or washing your hands.

Note: clear is better than fast. Speed will come.

## PAGE 5: Talking about my birthday
Now use your practice to give a short talk. You can video yourself or say it to a partner.

### A model talk
Hallo! Ich heiße Baraka. Ich bin heute zehn. Ich habe einen Kuchen und Kerzen. Meine Familie ist da. Wir singen und tanzen. Danke!

Word help: wir = we. singen = sing. tanzen = dance. Say: TAN-tsen.

### Make your own
Replace the name and age. Say what you like: Ich habe einen Kuchen. Wir spielen. Wir singen.

### Checklist for a good talk
• Did I say the ch in Kuchen softly?
• Did I say z as ts in Kerzen and tanzen?
• Did I say sch as sh in Geschenk?
• Did I speak slowly and look at my audience?

### Remember
• Good pronunciation helps people understand you.
• Practise tongue twisters slowly first.
• Alles Gute zum Geburtstag! is the standard birthday wish.

Common mistake: saying "Geburtstag" as "Gebortstag". The vowel is u, said like "oo".

## QUIZ
1. What does "der Geburtstag" mean?
   A) Cake
   B) Birthday
   C) Present
   D) Party
   Answer: B
   Why: der Geburtstag is the German word for birthday.
2. Which phrase do you say to wish someone a happy birthday?
   A) Guten Abend
   B) Auf Wiedersehen
   C) Alles Gute zum Geburtstag
   D) Gute Nacht
   Answer: C
   Why: Alles Gute zum Geburtstag means All the best for your birthday.
3. How is the letter z pronounced in "Kerze"?
   A) like z in "zebra"
   B) like "ts"
   C) like "sh"
   D) like "k"
   Answer: B
   Why: German z sounds like ts, so Kerze is KER-tse.
4. Which word means "candle"?
   A) die Kerze
   B) das Geschenk
   C) der Kuchen
   D) das Lied
   Answer: A
   Why: die Kerze is candle. Geschenk is present, Kuchen is cake and Lied is song.
5. Write the German words for cake, present and candle, each with der, die or das. (3 marks)
   Answer: der Kuchen, das Geschenk, die Kerze.
   Why: 1 mark for each noun with the correct article.
6. Explain what "articulating" means and why it is important in a language. (2 marks)
   Answer: Articulating means saying each sound clearly using lips, tongue and teeth. It matters because clear sounds help people understand you.
   Why: 1 mark for meaning, 1 mark for reason.
7. Write a short birthday message in German that says you are ten and wish a friend well. (3 marks)
   Answer: For example: Ich bin heute zehn. Alles Gute zum Geburtstag!
   Why: 1 mark for saying age correctly, 1 mark for the wish, 1 mark for capital letters and punctuation.
8. Give two tips for practising a tongue twister. (2 marks)
   Answer: Start slowly and add a few words at a time; repeat it often and speed up gradually.
   Why: 1 mark for each sensible tip.
