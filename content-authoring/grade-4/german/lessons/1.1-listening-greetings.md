## PAGE 1: Why we greet, and how German sounds
In Kenya we greet with care. In Kiswahili you may say "Jambo" or "Habari?", in English "Hello", and in your mother tongue you have your own special greetings. A greeting shows respect and says, "I see you." People all over the world greet each other, in many different languages.

German is the language of Germany. It is also spoken in Austria and in a large part of Switzerland. Millions of people speak it. In this course you will learn to listen to German, to say it, to read it and to write it.

When you listen to something new, you do not need to understand every word. You listen for the gist. The gist is the main idea. Ask yourself: Who is talking? Is it morning or evening? Are they saying hello or goodbye? Are they happy?

### Your first German word
Hallo means hello. It is used with friends, classmates and family.
Say it like this: HAH-lo.

### Try it
Listen to your teacher say "Hallo". Repeat it three times. Then greet a friend in German and in your mother tongue. Notice which greeting feels longer, and which sounds softer.

## PAGE 2: Greetings for different times of the day
In German we choose a greeting to match the time of day. Listen for a word like Morgen, Tag or Abend. That word tells you the gist: it tells you the time.

### Greetings and how to say them
Guten Morgen = good morning. Say: GOO-ten MOR-gen.
Guten Tag = good day, hello (used in the daytime). Say: GOO-ten TAHK.
Guten Abend = good evening. Say: GOO-ten AH-bent.
Gute Nacht = good night. Say: GOO-te NAKHT. Only say this when someone is going to bed.

Notice that the "g" in Morgen sounds hard, like the g in "go". The letter "ch" in Nacht is a breathy sound made at the back of the mouth, a little like clearing your throat softly. Practise it slowly.

## PAGE 3: Saying goodbye and being polite
Every meeting ends with a goodbye. Here are the German ways to say it. Some are for friends and some are polite for older people.

### Goodbyes
Auf Wiedersehen = goodbye. Say: owf VEE-der-zay-en.
Tschüss = bye (friendly, for friends). Say: chooss, with your lips round as if you are going to whistle.
Bis bald = see you soon.
Bis morgen = see you tomorrow.

### Polite words
Danke = thank you. Say: DAHN-ke.
Bitte = please, and also "you are welcome". Say: BIT-te.
Ja = yes. Say: yah.
Nein = no. Say: nine, like the number nine in English.
Entschuldigung = sorry, excuse me.

### Gist practice
Read these short lines to a partner and ask, "Is this hello or goodbye?"
1. Hallo, Tom!
2. Tschüss, Anna!
3. Guten Morgen!
4. Auf Wiedersehen!
5. Bis morgen!

Answers: hello, goodbye, hello, goodbye, goodbye.

## PAGE 4: Introducing yourself
To say your name you can use two sentences. The teacher asks a question and you answer with a sentence.

Wie heißt du? = What is your name? Say: vee HYST doo.
Ich heiße Amina. = My name is Amina. Say: ikh HY-se.
Ich bin Amina. = I am Amina.

The letter combination "ei" sounds like the English word "eye". That is why heiße sounds like HY-se. The letter ß is called "Eszett". It sounds like a strong "ss".

### Asking how someone is
Wie geht's? = How are you? Say: vee GAYTS.
Gut, danke. = Fine, thank you.
Sehr gut. = Very good.
Es geht. = It is so-so.
Nicht so gut. = Not so good.

### A mini-dialogue
Amina: Hallo! Wie heißt du?
Otieno: Hallo! Ich heiße Otieno. Und du?
Amina: Ich heiße Amina. Wie geht's?
Otieno: Gut, danke.

Practise this dialogue in pairs, then change the names to your own and to those of a neighbour.

## PAGE 5: Listening for the gist and speaking with respect
Now you will hear a very short text. Your teacher (or a classmate) reads it aloud twice. The first time, just listen. The second time, listen for the answers.

### Listening text
Guten Morgen! Ich heiße Neema. Ich bin neun. Wie geht's? Mir geht es gut, danke. Auf Wiedersehen!

### Gist questions
1. Is Neema saying hello or goodbye at the start? Hello.
2. What is the time of day? Morning (Morgen).
3. What is her name? Neema.
4. How is she feeling? Good (gut).

### How to imitate well
• Listen first and do not rush.
• Copy the melody of the sentence, not just the words.
• Speak clearly so that your partner can hear each sound.
• Look at the person you greet and wait for your turn.

### Common mistakes
Do not say "Gute Tag": it is Guten Tag. Do not say "Gute Nacht" when you arrive in the morning. Saying "Tschüss" to a very old person may sound too casual, so use Auf Wiedersehen.

## QUIZ
1. Which greeting do you use in the morning?
   A) Gute Nacht
   B) Guten Abend
   C) Guten Morgen
   D) Tschüss
   Answer: C
   Why: Morgen means morning, so Guten Morgen is the morning greeting.
2. What does "Auf Wiedersehen" mean?
   A) Thank you
   B) Goodbye
   C) Please
   D) Good evening
   Answer: B
   Why: Auf Wiedersehen is the polite way to say goodbye.
3. You hear "Ich heiße Peter." What is the gist?
   A) Peter is saying goodbye
   B) Peter is saying his name
   C) Peter is saying thank you
   D) Peter is saying it is night
   Answer: B
   Why: Ich heiße means "my name is".
4. Which is the correct answer to "Wie geht's?"
   A) Guten Tag
   B) Ich heiße Anna
   C) Gut, danke
   D) Auf Wiedersehen
   Answer: C
   Why: Wie geht's asks how you are, and Gut, danke means "Fine, thank you".
5. Write the German words for "good morning" and "good evening". (2 marks)
   Answer: Guten Morgen and Guten Abend.
   Why: One mark for each greeting spelled correctly.
6. Write two sentences in German to tell someone your name and ask how they are. Use "Ich heiße" and "Wie geht's?". (3 marks)
   Answer: Ich heiße (your name). Wie geht's?
   Why: 1 mark for Ich heiße plus a name, 1 mark for Wie geht's, 1 mark for the capital letters and question mark.
7. Explain what it means to listen for the gist. (2 marks)
   Answer: It means listening for the main idea, such as who is speaking and what is happening, instead of trying to understand every word.
   Why: 1 mark for main idea, 1 mark for not needing every word.
8. When should you say "Gute Nacht" and when should you not? (3 marks)
   Answer: Say it when someone is going to bed at night. Do not say it as a hello in the morning or afternoon.
   Why: 1 mark for going to bed, 1 mark for not a hello, 1 mark for a clear example.
