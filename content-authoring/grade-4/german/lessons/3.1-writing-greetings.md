## PAGE 1: Why we write things down
Writing helps us remember, share and communicate with people who are far away. We write letters, SMS messages, WhatsApp messages and emails. In this lesson we write greetings in German and use correct punctuation.

### Kenyan greetings first
Write down a greeting from your own community, for example "Habari?" in Kiswahili. Find out greetings from different parts of the country and write them in your book. Kenya has many communities, and each greets in its own way. Greetings show respect and unity.

### German greetings to write
Hallo
Guten Morgen
Guten Tag
Guten Abend
Gute Nacht
Auf Wiedersehen
Tschüss

### Writing rules for greetings
1. Start every greeting with a capital letter.
2. After a name, use a comma or an exclamation mark: Hallo, Amina! or Hallo Amina!
3. End with an exclamation mark or a full stop.
4. Take care with ü in Tschüss and ß in heißen.

## PAGE 2: Capital letters and punctuation in German
### Capital letters
• The first word of a sentence: Guten Morgen!
• All nouns: der Tag, die Schule, das Haus.
• Names: Amina, Otieno, Kenia.
• The polite word Sie (You) is always written with a capital letter. You will learn it later.

Words such as ich (I) are NOT written with a capital letter unless they start a sentence. This is different from English! Write: Heute bin ich müde. (Today I am tired.) The "ich" stays small in the middle of a sentence.

### Punctuation
. (der Punkt) after a statement: Ich heiße Amina.
? (das Fragezeichen) after a question: Wie heißt du?
! (das Ausrufezeichen) after a greeting or a wish: Guten Morgen!
, (das Komma) after a name that you call: Hallo, Amina!

### Practice
Correct these sentences. Write them out again with the right capital letters and punctuation.
1. guten morgen amina
2. wie heißt du
3. ich heiße otieno

Answers:
1. Guten Morgen, Amina!
2. Wie heißt du?
3. Ich heiße Otieno.

## PAGE 3: Filling in dialogues
A dialogue has two speakers. Each line starts with the speaker's name and a colon.

### Incomplete dialogue
Amina: ___, Otieno!
Otieno: Guten Morgen, Amina! Wie ___?
Amina: Gut, ___. Und dir?
Otieno: Auch gut. Auf ___!

Choose from: Guten Morgen, geht's, danke, Wiedersehen.

### Answers
Amina: Guten Morgen, Otieno!
Otieno: Guten Morgen, Amina! Wie geht's?
Amina: Gut, danke. Und dir?
Otieno: Auch gut. Auf Wiedersehen!

New words: Und dir? = And you? Say: oont deer. Auch = also. Say: owkh.

### Tips
1. Read the whole dialogue first.
2. Find the missing word that makes sense.
3. Check the spelling of every word.
4. Copy the punctuation marks.

## PAGE 4: Greetings for the times of the day
### Match the greeting to the picture
Draw four small pictures in your book: a rising sun (morning), a bright sun (day), a low sun (evening) and a moon (night). Write the correct German greeting below each drawing.

1. Rising sun: Guten Morgen!
2. Bright sun: Guten Tag!
3. Low sun: Guten Abend!
4. Moon: Gute Nacht!

### Write the sentences
Complete these with the right greeting.
• Es ist sechs Uhr am Morgen. Ich sage: ____.
• Es ist zwölf Uhr am Mittag. Ich sage: ____.
• Es ist acht Uhr am Abend. Ich sage: ____.
• Ich gehe ins Bett. Ich sage: ____.

Answers: Guten Morgen! Guten Tag! Guten Abend! Gute Nacht!

Words: Es ist sechs Uhr = It is six o'clock. Uhr = o'clock. Say: oor.

### Spelling check
Watch these spellings: Guten (not Gutten), Abend (not Abent), Tschüss (with ü and double s), Wiedersehen (ie and ee).

### Reminder
Gute Nacht is only said at bedtime.

## PAGE 5: Writing messages to friends
### Digital messages
Many people send greetings by SMS, WhatsApp or email. Here is a short example message.

Hallo Amina!
Guten Morgen! Wie geht's?
Ich bin Otieno.
Tschüss!

### Writing your own message
1. Write the greeting: Hallo ...!
2. Write a question: Wie geht's?
3. Say your name: Ich heiße ...
4. End with a goodbye: Tschüss! or Bis morgen!

### Writing in pairs
With a partner, write a short dialogue of four lines. Then read it aloud to the class. Check: capital letters, punctuation, spelling.

### Safety when writing online
Only send messages to people you know. Ask an adult before you use a phone or computer. Do not share personal information such as your address with strangers.

### Remember
• Capital letters for nouns, names and the first word.
• ich is small in the middle of a sentence.
• Use ? for questions and ! for greetings.

Common mistake: writing "guten tag" without capitals. It must be Guten Tag.

## QUIZ
1. Which greeting is correctly written?
   A) guten morgen
   B) Guten morgen
   C) Guten Morgen
   D) guten Morgen
   Answer: C
   Why: The first word and the noun Morgen both start with capital letters.
2. Which punctuation mark ends the question "Wie heißt du"?
   A) .
   B) !
   C) ?
   D) ,
   Answer: C
   Why: A question ends with a question mark.
3. Where do we use the comma in "Hallo Amina"?
   A) After Amina
   B) Between Hallo and Amina
   C) Before Hallo
   D) Nowhere is possible
   Answer: B
   Why: We may write Hallo, Amina! with a comma after Hallo.
4. Which is the correct spelling?
   A) Tschus
   B) Tchüss
   C) Tschüss
   D) Tschuess with ß
   Answer: C
   Why: The correct spelling is Tschüss, with ü and double s.
5. Rewrite correctly: "guten abend otieno" (2 marks)
   Answer: Guten Abend, Otieno!
   Why: 1 mark for capital letters, 1 mark for the punctuation.
6. Complete the dialogue: "Wie ___? Gut, ___." Use geht's and danke. (2 marks)
   Answer: Wie geht's? Gut, danke.
   Why: 1 mark for each correct word.
7. Write a four-line dialogue in German between two friends who meet in the morning and say goodbye. (4 marks)
   Answer: For example: A: Guten Morgen, Amina! B: Guten Morgen, Otieno! Wie geht's? A: Gut, danke. B: Tschüss!
   Why: 1 mark for the greeting, 1 mark for the question, 1 mark for the answer, 1 mark for correct punctuation.
8. Give two safety rules for sending messages online. (2 marks)
   Answer: Write only to people you know; ask an adult before using devices; do not share private information.
   Why: 1 mark for each sensible rule.
