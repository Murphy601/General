## PAGE 1: Reading the days as a rhythm
Fluency in reading means reading smoothly, at a good speed, and in the right rhythm. The days of the week are a good practice text because they follow a pattern.

### Read aloud with a beat
Montag, Dienstag, Mittwoch,
Donnerstag, Freitag,
Samstag, Sonntag.
Das ist die Woche!

Clap once for each day. Read the days in a group first, then alone.

### Words with a similar shape
Six days end in -tag: Montag, Dienstag, Donnerstag, Freitag, Samstag, Sonntag. When you see -tag at the end of a word, say TAHK. Look at the start of the word to see which day it is. Mon-, Dienst-, Donners-, Frei-, Sams-, Sonn-.

### Reading tip
Read the first part quickly and then the last part slowly. That is a good way to read long German words that are joined together: Don-ners-tag, Wo-chen-en-de.

The word for weekend, Wochenende, is Woche (week) plus Ende (end).

## PAGE 2: Punctuation marks that guide reading aloud
Many punctuation marks help us read aloud with feeling. You already know the full stop, comma, question mark and exclamation mark. Here are two more.

### More marks
der Doppelpunkt (:) = colon. It comes before a list or a speech. Say: DOP-pel-poonkt.
der Bindestrich (-) = hyphen. It joins parts of a word.
die Anführungszeichen (" ") = quotation marks. They show the words someone says. Say: AN-fü-roongs-tsy-khen.

### Where do you pause?
• Full stop: pause long, drop your voice.
• Comma: pause short.
• Colon: pause a little and announce what comes next.
• Question mark: raise your voice.
• Exclamation mark: strong voice.

### Example
Heute ist Montag. Wer hat Schule? Wir alle! Am Montag lerne ich: Mathe, Kiswahili und Deutsch.

Meaning: Today is Monday. Who has school? We all do! On Monday I learn: maths, Kiswahili and German.

Read this aloud and mark where each pause and voice change happens. Notice that Deutsch (German) begins with a capital letter, and so does Kiswahili.

## PAGE 3: Tongue twister and song reading
A tongue twister is good fluency practice.

Am zehnten zehnten zehn Uhr zehn zogen zehn zahme Ziegen zehn Zentner Zucker zum Zoo.

### Step-by-step reading
1. Read it once in your head.
2. Read it slowly aloud.
3. Read it faster, three times.
4. Read it to a partner and get feedback: "Gut gemacht!" (Well done!) or "Noch einmal!" (One more time!).

Say: Gut gemacht = goot geh-MAKHT. Noch einmal = nokh INE-mahl.

### Reading a class song
Your teacher may have a song about the days of the week (many German children's songs use the word Wochentage, which means weekdays). Read the words on paper and follow them as you sing. Point to each word as it is sung. That helps you connect the sounds with the written words.

### Feedback
When a friend reads to you, say one thing they did well (for example, "You stopped at the full stops") and one thing to improve (for example, "Say z as ts").

## PAGE 4: Reading about the week
Read this text about a school week. It is written for you to read aloud.

### Text: Meine Woche
Am Montag gehe ich zur Schule. Am Dienstag spiele ich mit Freunden. Am Mittwoch lese ich ein Buch. Am Donnerstag helfe ich zu Hause. Am Freitag singe ich in der Schule. Am Samstag spiele ich Fußball. Am Sonntag gehe ich in die Kirche.

New words: Freund = friend (die Freunde = friends). Say: froynt. Buch = book. Say: bookh. Schule = school. Say: SHOO-le. Kirche = church. Say: KEER-khe (soft ch).

### Read again with a partner
Partner A reads a sentence. Partner B reads the next sentence. Keep the rhythm going. If you meet a hard word, break it up: Frei-tag, Sams-tag.

### Note on word order
In these sentences the verb always comes second: Am Montag gehe ich. Am Freitag singe ich. When the sentence starts with Am plus a day, the verb comes next, and then ich. Watch for it as you read.

## PAGE 5: Reading and talking about my week
Now discuss your own week. Speak in English or use simple German with a partner, following the pattern.

### Pattern
Am Samstag spiele ich Fußball.
Am Sonntag gehe ich in die Kirche.
Am Freitag lese ich.

### Peer pressure talk
Your week includes choices. Sometimes friends ask you to do something you know is not right, like skipping homework. Talk with your partner (in English): How can you say no politely? What can you do instead? The German word for no is nein. You can say "Nein, danke." (No, thank you.) Say: nine DAHN-ke.

### Fluency checklist
• Read clearly, keep a steady speed.
• Stop at full stops and pause at commas.
• Use the right voice for ? and !.
• Look at your partner from time to time.

### Remember
• -tag is said TAHK.
• A colon (:) announces a list.
• All days have capital letters.

Common mistake: reading Dienstag as "Die-ns-tag" with the wrong ie sound. ie is "ee": DEENS-tahk.

## QUIZ
1. What is the German name for "colon"?
   A) der Punkt
   B) der Doppelpunkt
   C) das Komma
   D) das Fragezeichen
   Answer: B
   Why: der Doppelpunkt is the colon (:).
2. How do you say the ending "-tag" in Montag?
   A) tag
   B) tahk
   C) tayg
   D) tage
   Answer: B
   Why: In German the g at the end sounds like k and the a is long: TAHK.
3. What does "Am Samstag spiele ich Fußball" mean?
   A) On Saturday I play football
   B) On Sunday I watch football
   C) On Friday I read
   D) Today I play chess
   Answer: A
   Why: Am Samstag = on Saturday, spiele ich = I play, Fußball = football.
4. What does "Wochenende" mean?
   A) Weekday
   B) Weekend
   C) Week
   D) Tomorrow
   Answer: B
   Why: Woche (week) plus Ende (end) gives weekend.
5. Read this sentence aloud and write how your voice changes: "Wer hat Schule? Wir alle!" (2 marks)
   Answer: Voice goes up at the question mark and shows strong feeling at the exclamation mark.
   Why: 1 mark for each correct voice change.
6. Write the punctuation mark for each: full stop, question mark, exclamation mark. (3 marks)
   Answer: . ? !
   Why: 1 mark for each correct mark.
7. Write in German what you do on Sunday. Use "Am Sonntag ...". (3 marks)
   Answer: For example: Am Sonntag gehe ich in die Kirche.
   Why: 1 mark for Am Sonntag, 1 mark for a correct verb with ich, 1 mark for a sensible ending with capital letter and full stop.
8. Explain why breaking a long word into parts helps you to read it aloud. Use "Donnerstag" as an example. (2 marks)
   Answer: Small parts are easier to say, for example Don-ners-tag, and then you can join them smoothly.
   Why: 1 mark for the reason, 1 mark for a correct example.
