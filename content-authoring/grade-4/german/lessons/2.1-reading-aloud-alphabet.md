## PAGE 1: The German alphabet
The German alphabet has the same 26 letters as the English one. It also has three special letters with two dots: ä, ö, ü, and the special letter ß. Names of the letters in German are different from English names. Learn them in the order of the alphabet song.

### Letter names
A = ah   B = bay   C = tsay   D = day   E = ay   F = eff   G = gay
H = hah  I = ee    J = yot    K = kah   L = ell  M = emm   N = enn
O = oh   P = pay   Q = koo    R = err   S = ess  T = tay   U = oo
V = fow  W = vay   X = iks    Y = ÜP-si-lon  Z = tset

Notice: A is ah, E is ay, I is ee, U is oo. The vowels are a, e, i, o, u. The consonants are the other letters.

## PAGE 2: The special letters
### Umlauts: ä, ö, ü
An umlaut is a vowel with two dots above it.
ä sounds like the "e" in bed (Mädchen = MAYD-khen, girl; the word Bär = bear, sounds like "bair").
ö is made by saying "ay" with round lips. Say: schön (nice) = shurn, like the "ur" in "fur" without the r.
ü is made by saying "ee" with round lips. Say: Tür (door) = tür, as if you are saying "ee" and whistling.

When you cannot type the dots, write ae for ä, oe for ö and ue for ü. So Küche can be written Kueche. That is why your name in the curriculum example, Michael, might be spelled with AE when the letter Ä is needed.

### The letter ß
ß is called Eszett (ess-tset). It sounds like a strong ss. Examples: heißen = HY-sen (to be called), Fuß = foos (foot), groß = grohs (big). In writing, ß comes after a long vowel or after a double vowel sound like ei, au, eu.

### Try it
Say each word with your teacher: Mädchen, schön, Tür, groß, heißen.

## PAGE 3: Sound patterns you must know
Some letter groups make special sounds. Reading aloud gets easy once you know them.

### Key patterns
ei = eye. Example: nein (no) = nine, Eis (ice cream) = ice.
ie = ee. Example: Bier is not for children, but Tier (animal) = teer, Liebe (love) = LEE-be.
eu = oy. Example: heute (today) = HOY-te, Euro = OY-ro.
au = ow. Example: Haus (house) = house, Baum (tree) = bowm.
sch = sh. Example: Schule (school) = SHOO-le, Schwester = SHVES-ter.
ch = a breathy sound: after a, o, u it is like "kh" (Nacht, Buch = bookh), after e, i it is softer (ich, Milch).
sp/st at the start of a word = shp / sht. Example: Sport = shport, Stuhl = shtool.
z = ts. Example: Zahl (number) = tsahl.
w = v. Example: Wasser = VAS-ser.
v = f in most German words. Example: Vater = FAH-ter, viel = feel.
j = y. Example: ja = yah, Januar = YAN-oo-ar.
s before a vowel = z. Example: Sonne = ZON-ne.

Learn these and you can read many new words correctly!

## PAGE 4: Reading greetings aloud
Now practise reading greetings aloud. Read slowly, sound by sound, then smoothly.

### Read these
Hallo! Guten Morgen! Guten Tag! Guten Abend! Gute Nacht! Auf Wiedersehen! Tschüss!

### Read and spot the sound pattern
• Guten Morgen: g hard, r soft.
• Guten Tag: g at the end of Tag sounds like k.
• Auf Wiedersehen: au = ow, ie = ee, ei is not here, but sehen has long e.
• Tschüss: tsch = ch, ü = round lips.

### Taking turns
Read in a circle. Each learner reads one greeting, then the next learner reads a different one. Listen to the reader without interrupting. If someone makes a mistake, help them by saying the word correctly, and they repeat it. That is a good way to respect turn-taking.

### Matching greetings to pictures
Look at the four boxes in your book (morning sun, midday sun, evening sun and moon). Read the greeting and point to the picture.

## PAGE 5: Reading your name letter by letter
In German you often spell your name aloud, like on the telephone. Use the letter names.

### Model: M-I-CH-AE-L
The name Michael is spelled: M (emm), I (ee), C (tsay), H (hah), A (ah), E (ay), L (ell). Some people say "tsay-hah" for CH. Name: MI-kha-el.

### Model: Tom
T (tay), O (oh), M (emm) = Tom.

### Model: Zawadi
Z (tset), A (ah), W (vay), A (ah), D (day), I (ee) = Zawadi.

### Your turn
Ask: Wie schreibt man deinen Namen? = How is your name written? Say: vee shrybt man DY-nen NAH-men. Then spell your name with German letter names. Your partner writes the letters and reads the name aloud to check.

### Remember
• The German alphabet has 26 letters plus ä, ö, ü and ß.
• ei = eye, ie = ee, eu = oy, au = ow, sch = sh, z = ts, w = v.
• Read slowly first, then smoothly.

Common mistake: reading Vater as "Vater" with English v. In German it is FAH-ter.

## QUIZ
1. How is the German letter "ß" called?
   A) Beta
   B) Eszett
   C) Umlaut
   D) Ypsilon
   Answer: B
   Why: ß is called Eszett and sounds like ss.
2. How is "ie" said in "Tier"?
   A) eye
   B) ee
   C) oy
   D) ow
   Answer: B
   Why: ie sounds like ee, so Tier is teer.
3. How is "z" said in "Zahl"?
   A) z as in zebra
   B) ts
   C) sh
   D) k
   Answer: B
   Why: German z is ts, so Zahl is tsahl.
4. Which spelling shows "Küche" when you cannot type ü?
   A) Kuche
   B) Kueche
   C) Kuuche
   D) Kiche
   Answer: B
   Why: ü can be written as ue, so Küche becomes Kueche.
5. Read and write the sound of these letter groups: ei, eu, au, sch. (4 marks)
   Answer: ei = eye, eu = oy, au = ow, sch = sh.
   Why: 1 mark for each correct sound.
6. Spell the name "Tom" using German letter names. (2 marks)
   Answer: T (tay), O (oh), M (emm).
   Why: 1 mark for the sounds of T and O, 1 mark for M and putting them in order.
7. Explain how to say the ü in "Tür". (2 marks)
   Answer: Say "ee" but make your lips round as if whistling.
   Why: 1 mark for "ee" sound, 1 mark for round lips.
8. Give three greetings you can read aloud and say when you use each. (3 marks)
   Answer: Guten Morgen in the morning, Guten Abend in the evening, Auf Wiedersehen when leaving.
   Why: 1 mark for each greeting with a correct time or use.
