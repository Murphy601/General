## PAGE 1: Special days in our homes
Families and communities enjoy special days. There are birthdays, weddings, graduations, harvest festivals, Christmas, Eid al-Fitr and Eid al-Adha, and many more. On these days we wear nice clothes, cook special food, sing, dance and meet our relatives and friends. Special days bring people together and help us to live in peace with one another.

Think about a celebration that you have attended. Who was there? What did you eat? What did people do? Tell your partner about it. When your partner talks, listen carefully and wait until they finish. Waiting for your turn is part of good conversation.

In this lesson you will learn Arabic words for celebrations. You will also learn some polite phrases to congratulate people and how to take turns when talking. A conversation is like a game of catch: one person speaks, and then they pass the turn to another.

### Warm up
Name two celebrations in your community. Say what food or drink is served at each. Say it first in English or Kiswahili, then look for the Arabic words on the next page.

## PAGE 2: Celebration words
Here are words you need for a party or a festival. Say them after your teacher.

• kaʿka = كَعْكَةٌ = cake

• ʿasiir = عَصِيرٌ = juice

## PAGE 3: Congratulating people
People say special words on happy occasions. Learn these and use them when you meet friends at a celebration.

### Phrases for celebrations
• ʿiid mubaarak = عِيدٌ مُبَارَكٌ = Blessed festival (Happy Eid)
• kulla ʿaamin wa-antum bi-khayr = كُلَّ عَامٍ وَأَنْتُمْ بِخَيْرٍ = May you be well every year
• ʿiid miilaad saʿiid = عِيدُ مِيلَادٍ سَعِيدٌ = Happy birthday
• mabruuk = مَبْرُوكٌ = Congratulations
• shukran = شُكْرًا = Thank you
• ahlan wa sahlan = أَهْلًا وَسَهْلًا = Welcome

### Asking simple questions
• hal ʿindaka hafla? = هَلْ عِنْدَكَ حَفْلَةٌ؟ = Do you have a party?
• naʿam = نَعَمْ = yes
• laa = لَا = no

The word hal at the start of a sentence turns it into a yes or no question. Answer with naʿam or laa.

### Practise
In groups of three, practise saying "Happy birthday" to a friend, and reply with shukran. Change roles so everyone has a turn to give and to receive the wishes.

## PAGE 4: Taking turns in a conversation
A good talker is also a good listener. Here are the rules for turn taking.

### Rules of good turn taking
1. Listen until the speaker has finished.
2. Do not shout or speak at the same time.
3. Look at the person who is talking.
4. Say one idea, then give another person a chance.
5. If you must interrupt, use polite words.

### Polite words in Arabic
• ʿafwan = عَفْوًا = excuse me, pardon
• law samahta = لَوْ سَمَحْتَ = if you allow, excuse me (to a boy or man; to a girl say law samahti = لَوْ سَمَحْتِ)
• min fadlika = مِنْ فَضْلِكَ = please (to a boy; min fadliki to a girl)
• tafaddal = تَفَضَّلْ = please, go ahead (to a boy)
• dawrii = دَوْرِي = my turn
• dawruka = دَوْرُكَ = your turn (to a boy; dawruki to a girl)

### Try it
Sit in a circle. Pass a small object, such as a ball of paper. Only the person holding it may speak. When they finish, they say dawruka and pass it on. If you need to speak but do not have the object, say ʿafwan and wait for the answer tafaddal.

## PAGE 5: A conversation about a celebration
Read this short conversation. Two friends, Amina and Ahmad, speak about a birthday.

### Dialogue
Ahmad: as-salaamu ʿalaykum. hal ʿindaki hafla?
Amina: wa ʿalaykumu s-salaam. naʿam, ʿindii haflatu ʿiidi miilaad.
Ahmad: ʿiid miilaad saʿiid!
Amina: shukran. tafaddal, kul kaʿka.

The last sentence: kul kaʿka (كُلْ كَعْكَةً) means "Eat cake." This is a friendly invitation.

### Arabic script of the first part
هَلْ عِنْدَكِ حَفْلَةٌ؟ (Do you have a party? asked to a girl)
نَعَمْ، عِنْدِي حَفْلَةُ عِيدِ مِيلَادٍ (Yes, I have a birthday party)

### Your turn
Make your own conversation about a celebration you attended. Use hal ʿindaka or hal ʿindaki, naʿam, shukran, and one word for food or drink. Present it to the class. The audience listens quietly and claps at the end.

## PAGE 6: Celebration songs and summary
Songs make celebrations happy. In groups, sing a celebration song in Arabic. The simplest song is a repeated wish that everybody can join: ʿiid miilaad saʿiid, ʿiid miilaad saʿiid. Clap to the beat of each word. You may record your group with a phone or tablet if your teacher allows it, and keep the recordings in a class digital folder.

### Why special days matter
Special days help us to thank God, to share with others, to remember our culture and to make new friends. They teach us to live together in peace.

### Summary
• Celebration words: ʿiid, hafla, hadiyya, kaʿka, baaluun, ʿasiir.
• Greeting phrases: ʿiid mubaarak, mabruuk, shukran.
• hal makes a yes or no question. Answer with naʿam or laa.
• Take turns: listen, wait, use polite words like ʿafwan and tafaddal.

### Common mistakes
• Talking while another person is speaking.
• Forgetting to say shukran after a kind wish.
• Using -ka for a girl. Use -ki for a girl.

## QUIZ
1. What does ʿiid mubaarak mean?
   A) Blessed festival
   B) Good night
   C) My turn
   D) Excuse me
   Answer: A
   Why: ʿiid is a festival and mubaarak means blessed.
2. Which word means "gift"?
   A) كَعْكَةٌ
   B) بَالُونٌ
   C) هَدِيَّةٌ
   D) شَمْعَةٌ
   Answer: C
   Why: هَدِيَّةٌ (hadiyya) means gift. The others are cake, balloon and candle.
3. What does naʿam mean?
   A) No
   B) Yes
   C) Please
   D) Thank you
   Answer: B
   Why: naʿam is yes. The word for no is laa.
4. Which word do you say to give your friend a turn to speak?
   A) دَوْرِي
   B) دَوْرُكَ
   C) شُكْرًا
   D) عَفْوًا
   Answer: B
   Why: dawruka means "your turn". dawrii means "my turn".
5. What does the word hal do at the start of a sentence?
   A) It makes the sentence negative
   B) It means "the"
   C) It makes a yes or no question
   D) It means "and"
   Answer: C
   Why: hal turns a sentence into a yes or no question, for example hal ʿindaka hafla?
6. Write three Arabic celebration words (English letters are fine) with meanings. (3 marks)
   Answer: For example ʿiid (festival), kaʿka (cake), hadiyya (gift).
   Why: 1 mark for each word with the right meaning.
7. Give three rules for good turn taking in a conversation. (3 marks)
   Answer: Listen until the speaker finishes; do not talk at the same time; use polite words to interrupt; give others a chance to speak.
   Why: 1 mark for each correct rule, up to 3.
8. Write two polite Arabic words or phrases you can use to interrupt or to invite someone to speak. Give the meanings. (2 marks)
   Answer: For example ʿafwan (excuse me) and tafaddal (please, go ahead).
   Why: 1 mark for each correct polite word with meaning.
9. Explain why special occasions are important for living together in peace. (2 marks)
   Answer: They bring people together, help us to share food and joy, and teach us to respect one another.
   Why: 1 mark for bringing people together or sharing and 1 mark for respect or peace.
