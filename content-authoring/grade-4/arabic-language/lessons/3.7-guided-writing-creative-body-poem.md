## PAGE 1: Writing creatively about the body
Creative writing means writing with imagination and care. It may be a story, a description, a song or a poem. Creative writing lets us share our ideas and feelings with others. It also helps us to practise the words and sentence patterns we have learnt.

In this lesson you will write about the parts of the body in neat, legible Arabic handwriting. You will make flashcards, draw and colour body parts, write simple sentences and copy a short poem of one stanza. A stanza is a group of lines in a poem, like a paragraph in a story.

You already know many words about the body: head, eye, ear, nose, mouth, hand, leg, tooth and more. You also know the sentence patterns "I see with my eye" and "I write with my hand". Use them here.

### What you will do
• List body vocabulary on flashcards.
• Draw and colour parts of the body and put them in a portfolio.
• Write simple sentences about body parts.
• Copy and perform a short poem.

### Why creative writing is fun
When we write creatively, we choose our own words and make something new. Even a short line can be creative. When you write "My eye sees", you are telling a small truth in a beautiful way.

### Talk about it
Which part of your body do you like most? Why? Tell your partner.

### Warm up
Touch your nose and say anf. Touch your hand and say yad. Touch your ear and say udhun. Say the words in Arabic script if you can read them: أَنْفٌ, يَدٌ, أُذُنٌ.

## PAGE 2: Vocabulary flashcards
Make a set of flashcards for the parts of the body. Write each Arabic word neatly on one side of a card, and its sound and meaning on the other.

### Words for flashcards
• رَأْسٌ = raʾs = head
• وَجْهٌ = wajh = face
• عَيْنٌ = ʿayn = eye
• أُذُنٌ = udhun = ear
• أَنْفٌ = anf = nose
• فَمٌ = fam = mouth
• سِنٌّ = sinn = tooth
• يَدٌ = yad = hand
• رِجْلٌ = rijl = leg
• قَدَمٌ = qadam = foot

### Steps
1. Cut cards of the same size.
2. Write each Arabic word large and neat on the front.
3. Write the sound and the meaning on the back.
4. Draw a small picture beside the Arabic word, on the front.
5. Store the cards in an envelope.

### Neat and legible writing check
Before you stick or store a card, check the writing.
• Are all the letters the correct shape?
• Are the dots in place?
• Are the vowel marks above and below the correct letters?
• Is the word on the baseline?

### Vocabulary bank
Put all the flashcards together as a class vocabulary bank. Use them in a game: one learner shows a card, and the others say the word. The first to answer correctly takes the next card.

## PAGE 3: Draw and colour the body
Drawing helps you to notice each part of the body carefully. It also makes the writing more interesting.

### Steps for the portfolio
1. Draw a simple picture of a person. It can be a stick figure with a big head.
2. Colour the body and its parts.
3. Draw a line from each part of the body to a label.
4. Write the Arabic name at the end of the line.
5. Put your picture in the class portfolio so that others can see it.

### Draw parts separately
You may also draw one large picture of one part, such as a hand with five fingers or an eye with eyelashes. Write its name under it in Arabic. The word for finger is إِصْبَعٌ (isbaʿ).

### Group portfolio
Sit in groups. Each learner chooses one part to draw. Put the pictures together in order from the head to the feet. Make a cover with the title جِسْمِي (jismii, my body).

### Kind feedback
When you look at another learner's picture, say what is good about it. Then say one thing they could add.

### Take care of your body
Talk with your group about how we take care of each part of the body. We wash our hands, we clean our teeth, we protect our eyes from dust and we eat well.

## PAGE 4: Writing simple sentences about the body
Use the sentence patterns you know to write about your body. Think–pair–share: think about a sentence, share it with your partner, then share with the class.

### Model sentences
• رَأْسِي كَبِيرٌ = raʾsii kabiir = My head is big.
• يَدِي صَغِيرَةٌ = yadii Sagiira = My hand is small.
• أَكْتُبُ بِيَدِي = aktubu bi-yadii = I write with my hand.
• أَغْسِلُ وَجْهِي = aghsilu wajhii = I wash my face.
• أَغْسِلُ يَدِي = aghsilu yadii = I wash my hand.
• أُنَظِّفُ أَسْنَانِي = unadhdhifu asnaanii = I clean my teeth.

### Note on gender
Raʾs (head) is masculine, so the describing word is kabiir. Yad (hand) is feminine, so the describing word is Sagiira. This is the same rule you learnt about describing household items.

### Write your own
Choose three parts of your body. Write one sentence for each, using the model. Check the spelling against the flashcards.

### Read and share
Read your sentences to your partner. Let your partner correct one thing and thank them. Copy your final sentences neatly onto a clean page.

### Tip
If you are not sure of a word, look at the flashcards or ask your teacher. It is better to check than to guess.

### In English letters
ʿaynii taraa
udhunii tasmaʿu
anfii yashummu
famii yatakallamu

### Meaning
My eye sees.
My ear hears.
My nose smells.
My mouth speaks.

### Steps for copying
1. Read the poem aloud line by line.
2. Copy the first line from the right. Leave a small space between words.
3. Check the dots and marks. Compare with the model.
4. Copy the other lines the same way.
5. Read your copy aloud to make sure it sounds right.

### Try to add a line
Try to write one more line using the same pattern: body part with ii, then the action. For example: يَدِي تَكْتُبُ (yadii taktubu, my hand writes).

## PAGE 6: Perform, display and summary
Poems are meant to be spoken and shared. Prepare your poem to perform at the school function or the interclass festival.

### Perform the poem
1. Practise reading the poem together with a steady rhythm.
2. Add actions: point to your eye, ear, nose and mouth as each is named.
3. Speak clearly and look at the audience.
4. Bow and say shukran (thank you) at the end.

### Display your writing
Put your best copy on the class wall. Others will read it, and you will feel proud. You can add a small drawing of each part beside the line.

### Artistic expression
Creative writing shows who you are. Art, poems and songs all express ideas and feelings. Do not be afraid to try, even if your first try is not perfect.

### Summary
• Creative writing uses imagination in words.
• Make flashcards and a body vocabulary bank.
• Draw and label the parts of the body.
• Write simple sentences, matching describing words to gender.
• A stanza is a group of lines in a poem.
• Copy and perform the poem with neat handwriting and clear voice.

### Common mistakes
• Mismatching describing words, such as kabiir with yad.
• Forgetting vowel marks and dots when copying.
• Reading the poem in a flat, quiet voice.

## QUIZ
1. What is a stanza?
   A) A group of lines in a poem
   B) A kind of word
   C) A drawing
   D) A spelling test
   Answer: A
   Why: A stanza is a group of lines in a poem, like a paragraph in a story.
2. What does أَغْسِلُ وَجْهِي mean?
   A) I wash my face.
   B) I write with my hand.
   C) I clean my teeth.
   D) My head is big.
   Answer: A
   Why: aghsilu means I wash and wajhii means my face.
3. Which describing word matches the feminine word yad (hand)?
   A) كَبِيرٌ
   B) صَغِيرَةٌ
   C) قَوِيٌّ
   D) جَدِيدٌ
   Answer: B
   Why: Yad is feminine, so it takes the describing word ending in -a, such as Sagiira.
4. Which line of the poem means "My nose smells"?
   A) عَيْنِي تَرَى
   B) أُذُنِي تَسْمَعُ
   C) أَنْفِي يَشُمُّ
   D) فَمِي يَتَكَلَّمُ
   Answer: C
   Why: أَنْفِي means my nose and يَشُمُّ means smells.
5. What should you write on the back of a body flashcard?
   A) The sound and the meaning
   B) Only your name
   C) Nothing
   D) A long story
   Answer: A
   Why: The Arabic word is on the front. The back has the sound and the meaning.
6. Write three body parts in Arabic sounds with their meanings. (3 marks)
   Answer: For example raʾs (head), yad (hand), fam (mouth).
   Why: 1 mark for each correct word with meaning.
7. Write one sentence in Arabic sounds about your body and translate it. (2 marks)
   Answer: For example aktubu bi-yadii. I write with my hand.
   Why: 1 mark for a correct Arabic sentence and 1 mark for the translation.
8. Explain what neat and legible writing means, and why it is important in a poem you display. (3 marks)
   Answer: Neat and legible writing has clear letters, correct dots and marks, and spacing that others can read easily. It matters so that readers understand and enjoy the poem.
   Why: 1 mark for clear letters, 1 mark for dots and marks or spacing and 1 mark for the reader's understanding.
9. Give two ways of taking care of your body. (2 marks)
   Answer: For example wash hands and face, clean teeth, eat well, sleep.
   Why: 1 mark for each sensible way.
