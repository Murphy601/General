## PAGE 1: Reading a simple text
So far you have read words and single sentences. Now you will read a short text with several sentences. A text is a group of sentences that go together to tell us something. When you read a text, you must do three things: read the words correctly, read at a good speed, and understand what the text is about.

Reading speed is important for understanding. If you read too slowly, you forget the beginning of the sentence before you reach the end. If you read too fast, you skip words and make mistakes. A good speed helps the ideas to flow like a story.

The theme of this lesson is the body. Before you read, think about what you already know. In earlier lessons you learnt the words raʾs (head), ʿayn (eye), udhun (ear), anf (nose), fam (mouth), yad (hand) and rijl (leg). Say these words to a friend. You will meet them again in the text.

### Reading habits of a good reader
• Read the title first.
• Predict what the text will say.
• Read silently once, then aloud.
• Underline any word you do not know.
• Use the sentence around the word to work out its meaning.
• Check in a dictionary if you still need help.

### Talk about it
Why do you think reading is important? Give two reasons to your partner.

## PAGE 2: The text "My body"
Read this text aloud. Your teacher will read it first. Then read it in your own time, from right to left.

### My body
هَذَا جِسْمِي. لِي رَأْسٌ وَيَدَانِ وَرِجْلَانِ. أَكْتُبُ بِيَدِي. أَسْمَعُ بِأُذُنِي. جِسْمِي قَوِيٌّ.

### In English letters
haadhaa jismii. lii raʾsun wa yadaani wa rijlaani. aktubu bi-yadii. asmaʿu bi-udhunii. jismii qawiyy.

### In English
This is my body. I have a head, two hands and two legs. I write with my hand. I hear with my ear. My body is strong.

### Read it in three ways
1. Read the text slowly with your teacher.
2. Read it at a normal speed with a partner, sentence by sentence.
3. Read it alone, as if you were telling a friend about your body.

### Notice
The word lii (لِي) means "I have" or "to me". So lii raʾs means "I have a head". The new words in this text are jism (جِسْمٌ, body), qawiyy (قَوِيٌّ, strong), and the special endings -aani in yadaani and rijlaani. You will learn about these endings in the next page.

## PAGE 3: Two of something
In Arabic there is a special ending for exactly two of something. It is called the dual. The ending is -aani (انِ).

### Dual words
• yad = يَدٌ = one hand
• yadaani = يَدَانِ = two hands
• rijl = رِجْلٌ = one leg
• rijlaani = رِجْلَانِ = two legs
• ʿayn = عَيْنٌ = one eye
• ʿaynaani = عَيْنَانِ = two eyes
• udhun = أُذُنٌ = one ear
• udhunaani = أُذُنَانِ = two ears

Look at the words. The ending -aani is added to the word and it always means two. English adds an s for more than one, but Arabic has a different ending for two.

### Say and act
Point to your two eyes and say ʿaynaani. Point to your two hands and say yadaani. Touch your two ears and say udhunaani.

### Using the dual in a sentence
• lii ʿaynaani = لِي عَيْنَانِ = I have two eyes.
• lii udhunaani = لِي أُذُنَانِ = I have two ears.
• lii yadaani = لِي يَدَانِ = I have two hands.

### Try it
Look at a partner and say what your partner has. For example: laka ʿaynaani (لَكَ عَيْنَانِ), "You have two eyes."

## PAGE 4: Inferring the meaning of a new word
Sometimes you meet a word you do not know. Do not stop reading. Use clues to infer, or work out, its meaning.

### Clues to use
• The sentence around the word.
• The picture, if there is one.
• A word you already know inside the sentence.
• The topic of the text.

### Example
Read: أَكْتُبُ بِيَدِي. جِسْمِي قَوِيٌّ.
Suppose you do not know قَوِيٌّ (qawiyy). The sentence is about the body. The person is telling us something good about their body. From the clue words you might guess "healthy" or "strong". Good guess! The real meaning is strong.

### Work with a partner
Read these sentences and guess the meaning of the word with the underline in English letters.
• أَنَا أَرَى بِعَيْنِي = anaa araa bi-ʿaynii. If you know ʿayn (eye), you can guess that araa means "see".
• أَنَا أَتَكَلَّمُ بِفَمِي = anaa atakallamu bi-famii. If you know fam (mouth), you can guess that atakallamu means "speak".

### Use a dictionary
A dictionary lists words in alphabetical order. To find a word, use its first letter and follow the order alif, baa, taa, thaa. You may also use a picture dictionary or ask your teacher. Build a vocabulary bank: a page in your notebook with each new word, its sound and its meaning.

## PAGE 5: Reader's theatre
A reader's theatre is a group performance where readers read aloud from a script. You do not need to act or memorise. You read with expression. Your voice does the acting.

### Script: The parts of the body
There are five readers. Each reader has a line.

Reader 1 (narrator): هَذَا جِسْمِي.
Reader 2: أَنَا الْعَيْنُ. أَرَى.
Reader 3: أَنَا الْأُذُنُ. أَسْمَعُ.
Reader 4: أَنَا الْيَدُ. أَكْتُبُ.
Reader 5: أَنَا الْفَمُ. أَتَكَلَّمُ.
All: جِسْمِي قَوِيٌّ.

### In English letters
Reader 1: haadhaa jismii.
Reader 2: anaa l-ʿayn. araa.
Reader 3: anaa l-udhun. asmaʿu.
Reader 4: anaa l-yad. aktubu.
Reader 5: anaa l-fam. atakallamu.
All: jismii qawiyy.

### Meaning
This is my body. I am the eye. I see. I am the ear. I hear. I am the hand. I write. I am the mouth. I speak. My body is strong.

### Performing
Practise three times. Use a different voice for each part. Speak clearly, loud enough and at a steady speed. After the performance, clap for each other.

## PAGE 6: Summary and vocabulary bank
Reading grade-appropriate texts means reading words and sentences that you can already understand with a little help. The more you read, the more words you know.

### Make a vocabulary bank
Make a list of the new words from this lesson.
• jism = جِسْمٌ = body
• qawiyy = قَوِيٌّ = strong
• yadaani = يَدَانِ = two hands
• rijlaani = رِجْلَانِ = two legs
• lii = لِي = I have

Add other words that you meet as you read. You can add a small drawing beside each word.

### Summary
• Read a text at a steady, comfortable speed.
• Use clues to infer the meaning of unfamiliar words.
• The dual ending -aani means two.
• Use a dictionary or ask for help when clues are not enough.
• A reader's theatre uses voices to bring a text to life.

### Common mistakes
• Reading so fast that you skip words.
• Stopping at every new word instead of using clues.
• Forgetting that -aani means exactly two.

## QUIZ
1. What does the word جِسْمٌ mean?
   A) Head
   B) Body
   C) Hand
   D) Leg
   Answer: B
   Why: جِسْمٌ (jism) means body.
2. What does the ending -aani mean in yadaani?
   A) My
   B) The
   C) Exactly two
   D) Very big
   Answer: C
   Why: The ending -aani shows that there are two of something, so yadaani means two hands.
3. What does lii ʿaynaani mean?
   A) I have two eyes.
   B) I see with my eye.
   C) You have two eyes.
   D) The eye is big.
   Answer: A
   Why: lii means I have and ʿaynaani means two eyes.
4. Which of these helps you to infer the meaning of a new word?
   A) The words around it in the sentence
   B) The colour of the page
   C) The number of the page
   D) The size of the letters
   Answer: A
   Why: The surrounding sentence gives clues about the meaning.
5. What does qawiyy mean?
   A) Small
   B) Strong
   C) Hot
   D) Cold
   Answer: B
   Why: قَوِيٌّ means strong.
6. Write the Arabic sounds for "two hands" and "two ears" and give the meaning of each. (4 marks)
   Answer: yadaani (two hands) and udhunaani (two ears).
   Why: 1 mark for each correct word and 1 mark for each correct meaning.
7. Translate into English: aktubu bi-yadii. (2 marks)
   Answer: I write with my hand.
   Why: 1 mark for "I write" and 1 mark for "with my hand".
8. Describe two ways to work out the meaning of a word you do not know. (2 marks)
   Answer: Use the sentence around the word, use a picture or a word you know, or look it up in a dictionary.
   Why: 1 mark for each suitable method.
9. Explain what happens in a reader's theatre. (3 marks)
   Answer: Learners read a script aloud in a group. Each has a part. They use their voices with expression instead of acting or memorising.
   Why: 1 mark for reading a script, 1 mark for each having a part and 1 mark for using the voice.
