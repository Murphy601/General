## PAGE 1: Writing about the weather
Weather is all around us. We see the sun, the clouds, the wind and the rain. The weather affects what we wear, what we eat, how we travel and what farmers plant. When the rains come, crops grow. When the sun is very hot, we look for shade and drink more water.

In this lesson you will write about the weather in Arabic. You will list weather features, match pictures to their names, write simple descriptions and draw and label the weather on a poster. You will use neat and legible handwriting.

### Why is the weather important?
• Farmers depend on rain to grow food.
• Travellers need to know if the roads will be dry or muddy.
• Children need to know what to wear to school.
• Everyone needs clean water, which often comes from rain.

### Words you know
• شَمْسٌ = shams = sun
• سَحَابٌ = sahaab = clouds
• رِيحٌ = riih = wind
• مَطَرٌ = matar = rain
• سَمَاءٌ = samaa' = sky
• حَارٌّ = haarr = hot
• بَارِدٌ = baarid = cold

### Talk about it
How does weather affect your daily life? Give two examples, such as what you wear when it rains. Share with your partner.

### Warm up
Look outside. Which weather words can you see? Say them in Arabic.

## PAGE 2: List the weather features
Work with a partner to list weather features. Write each Arabic word neatly and check it with the list.

### Steps
1. Write the word شَمْسٌ. Say: shams, sun.
2. Write the word سَحَابٌ. Say: sahaab, clouds.
3. Write the word رِيحٌ. Say: riih, wind.
4. Write the word مَطَرٌ. Say: matar, rain.
5. Read your list aloud to your partner.

### Neat handwriting check
• Start each word on the right.
• Write the letters on the baseline.
• Put the dots and vowel marks in place.
• Leave a small space between words.

### More weather words
• جَوٌّ = jaww = weather
• جَمِيلٌ = jamiil = beautiful
• مُمْطِرٌ = mumtir = rainy
• مُشْمِسٌ = mushmis = sunny
• غَائِمٌ = ghaa'im = cloudy

### Be fair
Give your partner equal time to write and read. Take turns for each word. Being fair means everyone learns.

### Learn by comparing
Compare your list with another pair. Did you write every word? Look at the spelling of any word that is different. Correct it together.

## PAGE 3: Match the pictures to the names
Matching pictures to words helps you to remember them.

### Activity
Imagine four weather pictures: a sun, a cloud with rain falling, a cloud, and curved lines that show wind. Match each picture with its Arabic name.
1. The sun picture
2. The rain picture
3. The cloud picture
4. The wind picture

Names: a) رِيحٌ  b) شَمْسٌ  c) سَحَابٌ  d) مَطَرٌ

Answers: 1 with b (shams), 2 with d (matar), 3 with c (sahaab), 4 with a (riih).

### Match with your own drawings
Draw four small weather pictures on separate cards. Write the Arabic words on other cards. Mix them. A partner matches the pictures to the words. Then swap.

### Sentences for pictures
Write a short sentence for each picture with the pattern haadhihi ... (هَذِهِ ...), which means "This is ...".
• هَذِهِ شَمْسٌ = haadhihi shams = This is the sun.
• هَذِهِ سَحَابَةٌ = haadhihi sahaaba = This is a cloud.
• هَذَا مَطَرٌ = haadhaa matar = This is rain.
• هَذِهِ رِيحٌ = haadhihi riih = This is wind.

Notice haadhaa for a masculine word like matar and haadhihi for a feminine word like shams, sahaaba and riih.

### Check
Read each pair: picture, Arabic words, meaning.

### Meanings
• ash-shamsu haarra = The sun is hot.
• al-jawwu ghaa'im = The weather is cloudy.
• al-jawwu mumtir = The weather is rainy.
• ar-riihu qawiyya = The wind is strong.

The new word is qawiyya (قَوِيَّةٌ), strong, for a feminine noun such as riih.

### Write your own
Choose one weather feature. Look out of the window or think of yesterday. Write one sentence in Arabic about it. Use the models above.

### Copy neatly
Copy the four sentences in your book. Keep the spacing even. Read them aloud.

## PAGE 5: Draw and label weather on a poster
A poster shows information in a clear, attractive way. Make a poster with your group.

### Steps
1. Divide the poster into four parts: sun, clouds, rain and wind.
2. Draw a large picture for each part.
3. Colour it with bright colours.
4. Write the Arabic word for each picture underneath, in big, neat letters.
5. Write one short sentence for each picture.
6. Give your poster a title: الطَّقْسُ (at-taqs, the weather).
7. Post it on the wall of your school.

### Check
Before you post it, check the spelling, dots and vowel marks. Ask another group to check it as well.

### Care for the environment
Weather and the environment affect each other. Cutting many trees can make the land drier. Planting trees helps to bring shade and clean air. Add one line to your poster about caring for the environment: نُحَافِظُ عَلَى الْبِيئَةِ (nuhaafidhu ʿalaa l-bii'a, we take care of the environment).

### Learn from posters
Look at the posters of other groups. Read the words aloud. Tell the group one thing that you like about their poster.

## PAGE 6: Why Arabic writing is special
Writing in Arabic is different from writing in English or Kiswahili. That is part of what makes it special.

### What is unique?
• It is written from right to left.
• It has no capital letters.
• Most letters join and change shape.
• Dots are very important.
• Short vowels are shown with small marks above and below the letters.
• The alphabet has 28 letters, including sounds that English does not have.

### Summary
• List weather features: shams, sahaab, riih, matar.
• Match pictures with words.
• Describe with short sentences: ash-shamsu haarra, al-jawwu baarid.
• Use haadhaa for masculine and haadhihi for feminine words.
• Make a poster with drawings, labels and sentences.

### Common mistakes
• Using haadhaa with a feminine word like shams.
• Forgetting a dot, so the letter changes.
• Writing the labels from left to right.

## QUIZ
1. What does the word مَطَرٌ mean?
   A) Wind
   B) Sun
   C) Rain
   D) Sky
   Answer: C
   Why: مَطَرٌ (matar) means rain.
2. Which sentence means "The wind is strong"?
   A) الشَّمْسُ حَارَّةٌ
   B) الْجَوُّ غَائِمٌ
   C) الرِّيحُ قَوِيَّةٌ
   D) الْجَوُّ مُمْطِرٌ
   Answer: C
   Why: الرِّيحُ is the wind and قَوِيَّةٌ means strong.
3. Which word do you use to say "This is ..." before a feminine word such as shams?
   A) هَذَا
   B) هَذِهِ
   C) مَنْ
   D) أَيْنَ
   Answer: B
   Why: haadhihi is used with feminine words. haadhaa is for masculine words.
4. What does al-jawwu mumtir mean?
   A) The weather is sunny.
   B) The weather is rainy.
   C) The weather is cold.
   D) The weather is cloudy.
   Answer: B
   Why: mumtir means rainy.
5. Which of these is unique about Arabic writing?
   A) It has capital letters
   B) It is written from right to left
   C) It is written from top to bottom
   D) It has 26 letters
   Answer: B
   Why: Arabic is written from right to left and has 28 letters and no capitals.
6. Write four weather features in Arabic sounds with meanings. (4 marks)
   Answer: shams (sun), sahaab (clouds), riih (wind), matar (rain).
   Why: 1 mark for each correct word with its meaning.
7. Write one Arabic sentence about the weather in sounds and translate it. (2 marks)
   Answer: For example al-jawwu baarid. The weather is cold.
   Why: 1 mark for a correct sentence and 1 mark for the translation.
8. Describe three steps for making a weather poster. (3 marks)
   Answer: Draw and colour pictures of the sun, clouds, rain and wind; write the Arabic word under each; add a sentence and a title, then display it.
   Why: 1 mark for each valid step.
9. Explain how the weather affects our daily life. (2 marks)
   Answer: Rain helps crops to grow and gives us water, while very hot sun makes us look for shade. We choose clothes and plan travel by the weather.
   Why: 1 mark for one valid effect and 1 mark for a second effect.
