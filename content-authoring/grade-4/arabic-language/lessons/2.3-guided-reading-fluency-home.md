## PAGE 1: Reading about the home
In guided reading, your teacher helps you to read a short text step by step. First you look at the text together. Then you read aloud, then you read with a partner, and finally you read on your own. Guided reading helps you to read more accurately and smoothly.

Every text has a theme. The theme is the main topic. The theme of today's text is the home. When we read a text on a theme, we expect to find words about that theme. For a text about the home, we expect words such as house, room, kitchen, table, chair and cup.

### What you will do
• Pick out vocabulary about the home from a short passage.
• Read words about household items with correct pronunciation.
• Give and receive kind feedback on reading.
• Spell some words in a spelling bee.

### Before you read
Look at the title and any pictures. Think about what you already know. Say aloud three things you expect to find in a story about a home. This is called predicting. Then read to check your predictions.

### Warm up
Say the words for the following things in Arabic, using what you learnt in the speaking lessons: house, door, window, table, chair, bed, book, pen. Can you remember the words without looking?

## PAGE 2: The passage
Read this short passage slowly. Your teacher will read it first, and then you will read after your teacher.

### Ahmad's house
هَذَا بَيْتُ أَحْمَدَ. الْبَيْتُ كَبِيرٌ. فِي الْبَيْتِ غُرْفَةٌ وَمَطْبَخٌ. فِي الْمَطْبَخِ طَاوِلَةٌ وَكُرْسِيٌّ. عَلَى الطَّاوِلَةِ كُوبٌ.

### The same passage in English letters
haadhaa baytu Ahmad. al-baytu kabiir. fii l-bayti ghurfa wa matbakh. fii l-matbakhi taawila wa kursii. ʿalaa t-taawilati kuub.

### The passage in English
This is Ahmad's house. The house is big. In the house there is a room and a kitchen. In the kitchen there is a table and a chair. On the table there is a cup.

### Read step by step
1. Listen to your teacher read the whole passage.
2. Read after your teacher, one sentence at a time.
3. Read with a partner, taking turns by sentence.
4. Read alone, with a finger under each word, starting from the right.

Notice that the passage uses words you already know: bayt, ghurfa, matbakh, taawila, kursii and kuub. It also uses the position words fii (in) and ʿalaa (on).

## PAGE 3: Picking out the theme words
Now pick out the words that belong to the theme, the home. Read the passage again and find them.

### Theme words in the passage
• بَيْتٌ = bayt = house
• غُرْفَةٌ = ghurfa = room
• مَطْبَخٌ = matbakh = kitchen
• طَاوِلَةٌ = taawila = table
• كُرْسِيٌّ = kursii = chair
• كُوبٌ = kuub = cup

### Words that are not theme words
The words كَبِيرٌ (kabiir, big), فِي (fii, in), عَلَى (ʿalaa, on) and وَ (wa, and) are also in the passage, but they are not names of things in the home. They help us to describe or link the words.

### Work in teams
Each team gets a copy of the passage. Underline all the theme words in one colour. Then make a list on paper, with the Arabic word, its sound in English letters and its meaning. Compare your list with another team. Did you find all six words?

### Why are lists of words useful?
A word list helps you to remember new words. Later you can use it for a spelling contest or to write your own sentences.

## PAGE 4: Reading with correct pronunciation
Read the words below aloud. Focus on the sounds.

### Pronunciation guide
• baab, bayt, kuub: read these with clear b and long vowels. In كُوبٌ the letter waaw after the damma makes a long uu.
• taawila: the first letter is the heavy Taa (ط). Say it with a full sound and long aa after it.
• matbakh: it ends with khaa, the throat sound.
• kursii: the letters يّ at the end make a long ii. Say kur-sii.
• ghurfa: the letter ghayn is like a soft gargle.

### Peer assessment
When your partner reads, listen and give feedback. Use this checklist. Give a tick for each thing your partner did well.

• Each word was pronounced correctly.
• The heavy and throat sounds were clear.
• The reading was smooth, not choppy.
• The partner read from right to left and did not skip a word.

### Give kind and honest feedback
Be honest but kind. Say what went well first, for example: "You read taawila well." Then say one thing to improve: "Try to make the kh in matbakh stronger." Being honest helps your friend to grow.

### Try it
Read the passage to your partner. Your partner marks the checklist and gives feedback. Then you swap roles. Read again and try to improve.

## PAGE 5: A spelling bee

### More words to spell
• باب = baab: baa, alif, baa
• بيت = bayt: baa, yaa, taa
• كوب = kuub: kaaf, waaw, baa
• قلم = qalam: qaaf, laam, miim
• سرير = sariir: siin, raa, yaa, raa

Here the words are written without short vowel marks, as they usually appear in books. You have to know the vowels from the word itself.

### How to run the spelling bee
1. Split into two teams.
2. The teacher shows a picture, for example a chair.
3. A player from Team A says the Arabic word and spells it, saying the letter names.
4. If correct, Team A earns a point. If not, Team B may try.
5. Take turns until each learner has played.

## PAGE 6: Practise and review
Read the passage again as a class, then read the passage in pairs. Each pair reads it once and gives feedback to another pair.

### Extend the passage
Working in a team, write two more sentences about Ahmad's house, using words you know. For example, you can say al-baytu jamiil (الْبَيْتُ جَمِيلٌ), "The house is beautiful." Or you can say ʿalaa s-sariiri kitaab (عَلَى السَّرِيرِ كِتَابٌ), "On the bed there is a book."

### Summary
• Guided reading is reading with help from your teacher.
• A theme is the main topic of a text. Theme words belong to it.
• Read with correct pronunciation: heavy Taa, throat khaa, long vowels.
• Peer assessment should be honest and kind.
• In a spelling bee we say the letters in order from right to left.

### Common mistakes
• Missing out a word while reading.
• Reading with no pauses or too quickly.
• Saying the letters of a word from left to right when spelling.

## QUIZ
1. What is the theme of the passage "Ahmad's house"?
   A) Food
   B) The home
   C) The weather
   D) The body
   Answer: B
   Why: The passage talks about a house, its rooms and things inside it.
2. Which of these words is a theme word from the passage?
   A) كُرْسِيٌّ
   B) شُكْرًا
   C) مَرْحَبًا
   D) جَدٌّ
   Answer: A
   Why: كُرْسِيٌّ (kursii, chair) is an item in the home. The other words are a thanks, a greeting and grandfather.
3. In the passage, what is on the table?
   A) A book
   B) A cup
   C) A chair
   D) A key
   Answer: B
   Why: The last sentence says ʿalaa t-taawilati kuub, "on the table there is a cup".
4. In what order do you say the letters when you spell an Arabic word aloud?
   A) From left to right
   B) From right to left
   C) From the middle
   D) In any order
   Answer: B
   Why: Arabic is read and written from right to left, so we spell in that order.
5. Which letter begins the word taawila?
   A) ت
   B) ط
   C) ث
   D) د
   Answer: B
   Why: طَاوِلَةٌ begins with the heavy letter Taa (ط).
6. Spell the word باب by naming its letters in order. (2 marks)
   Answer: baa, alif, baa.
   Why: 1 mark for the correct letters and 1 mark for the correct order.
7. List three theme words from the passage with their meanings. (3 marks)
   Answer: For example bayt (house), matbakh (kitchen), taawila (table).
   Why: 1 mark for each correct word with meaning.
8. Give two rules for good peer assessment of reading. (2 marks)
   Answer: Be honest and kind, and say what went well before saying one thing to improve.
   Why: 1 mark for each suitable rule.
9. Explain why it is helpful to make a list of theme words after reading. (2 marks)
   Answer: It helps us to remember the words and to use them later in speaking, spelling and writing.
   Why: 1 mark for remembering and 1 mark for using the words later.
