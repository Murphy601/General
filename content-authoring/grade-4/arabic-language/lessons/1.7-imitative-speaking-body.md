## PAGE 1: My body
Your body is a wonderful gift. You see with your eyes, hear with your ears, smell with your nose, taste with your tongue and touch with your hands. You run with your legs and speak with your mouth. Every part has its job, and all the parts work together.

We must look after the body so that it stays healthy. We wash our hands and faces, brush our teeth, eat good food, drink clean water, sleep well and play outdoors.

In this lesson you will learn Arabic words for the parts of the body. You will listen, imitate (copy) and sing. You will also learn instructions such as "raise your hand" and "touch your nose" and you will act them out. When you copy a word, copy the sound, the speed and the feeling.

### Warm up
Point to each part of your body as your teacher says its name in English. Then say it in Kiswahili. Soon you will say it in Arabic too.

### Why copy carefully?
A small change in a sound can change the meaning of a word. Careful copying makes your Arabic clear to the listener. Practise in pairs and correct each other kindly.

## PAGE 2: Parts of the head
Learn the parts of the head and face first. Say each word after your teacher, three times.

### Head and face
• raʾs = رَأْسٌ = head
• wajh = وَجْهٌ = face
• shaʿr = شَعْرٌ = hair
• ʿayn = عَيْنٌ = eye (ʿaynaan = two eyes)
• udhun = أُذُنٌ = ear
• anf = أَنْفٌ = nose
• fam = فَمٌ = mouth
• sinn = سِنٌّ = tooth
• lisaan = لِسَانٌ = tongue

Notice the sound ʿ in ʿayn. It comes from deep in the throat. If it is difficult, keep listening and trying. Notice the sound dh in udhun. It is like th in the English word "this".

### Activity
Work in pairs. One learner touches a part of the face. The other says the Arabic name. Take turns. Count the correct words you say without help.

## PAGE 4: What do we do with our body?
Each part of the body has a job. Say these sentences and act them out.

### Sentences
• anaa araa biʿaynii = أَنَا أَرَى بِعَيْنِي = I see with my eye.
• asmaʿu bi-udhunii = أَسْمَعُ بِأُذُنِي = I hear with my ear.
• ashummu bi-anfii = أَشُمُّ بِأَنْفِي = I smell with my nose.
• atakallamu bi-famii = أَتَكَلَّمُ بِفَمِي = I speak with my mouth.
• aktubu bi-yadii = أَكْتُبُ بِيَدِي = I write with my hand.

The little word bi (بِ) means "with". The ending -ii means "my", as you learnt in the family lesson.

### Speak about yourself
Point to your own head and say haadhaa raʾsii (هَذَا رَأْسِي), "This is my head." Point to your hand and say haadhihi yadii (هَذِهِ يَدِي), "This is my hand." Do not worry if your Arabic is not perfect. Copy your teacher's words and keep trying.

### Take care
We look after the body by washing, eating well and resting. Talk with a partner about how you take care of your eyes and teeth. Then say the Arabic names of those parts.

## PAGE 5: Instructions and the language game
Instructions help us to learn words by doing. When you hear an instruction, act it out.

### Body instructions (to a boy)
• irfaʿ yadaka = ارْفَعْ يَدَكَ = raise your hand
• aghmiD ʿaynayka = أَغْمِضْ عَيْنَيْكَ = close your eyes
• ilmis anfaka = اِلْمَسْ أَنْفَكَ = touch your nose
• ilmis raʾsaka = اِلْمَسْ رَأْسَكَ = touch your head
• iftaH famaka = افْتَحْ فَمَكَ = open your mouth

To a girl, the last sound changes to -ki. For example, ilmisii anfaki (اِلْمَسِي أَنْفَكِ) means "touch your nose" to a girl.

### Game: Simon Says
One learner is the leader. The leader says an instruction and acts it. The group copies only if the leader starts with the word qaala (he said). For example, if the leader says qaala ilmis anfaka, everyone touches their nose. Any learner who moves without the start word sits out for one round. This game trains your ears, so listen carefully.

### Work in pairs
One partner gives three different instructions. The other acts and then says shukran. Then swap.

## PAGE 6: A rhyme and summary
Here is a short rhyme for you to sing. Each line says what a part of the body does. Clap to the beat.

ʿaynii taraa
udhunii tasmaʿu
anfii yashummu
famii yatakallamu

عَيْنِي تَرَى
أُذُنِي تَسْمَعُ
أَنْفِي يَشُمُّ
فَمِي يَتَكَلَّمُ

It means: my eye sees, my ear hears, my nose smells, my mouth speaks. Sing it with actions. Point to each part as you sing.

### Summary
• Body words: raʾs, ʿayn, udhun, anf, fam, yad, rijl.
• Use bi- for "with" and -ii for "my".
• Instructions: irfaʿ, ilmis, aghmiD, iftaH.
• Copy sounds carefully, and take care of your body.

### Common mistakes
• Saying the throat sound ʿ as a plain a.
• Saying udhun with d instead of dh.
• Using the boy instruction when speaking to a girl.

## QUIZ
1. What does the word يَدٌ (yad) mean?
   A) Leg
   B) Hand
   C) Head
   D) Eye
   Answer: B
   Why: يَدٌ means hand.
2. Which word means "nose"?
   A) أَنْفٌ
   B) فَمٌ
   C) أُذُنٌ
   D) رَأْسٌ
   Answer: A
   Why: أَنْفٌ (anf) means nose. فَمٌ is mouth, أُذُنٌ is ear, رَأْسٌ is head.
3. What does the little word bi mean in bi-yadii?
   A) my
   B) with
   C) and
   D) in
   Answer: B
   Why: bi means "with", so bi-yadii means "with my hand".
4. What does ilmis anfaka tell you to do?
   A) Open your mouth
   B) Close your eyes
   C) Touch your nose
   D) Raise your hand
   Answer: C
   Why: ilmis means touch and anfaka means your nose.
5. Which ending means "my"?
   A) -ii
   B) -ka
   C) -ki
   D) -bi
   Answer: A
   Why: The ending -ii means "my", as in yadii (my hand).
6. Write four parts of the body in Arabic sounds (English letters) with meanings. (4 marks)
   Answer: For example raʾs (head), ʿayn (eye), fam (mouth), yad (hand).
   Why: 1 mark for each correct word with meaning.
7. Translate: asmaʿu bi-udhunii. (2 marks)
   Answer: I hear with my ear.
   Why: 1 mark for "I hear" and 1 mark for "with my ear".
8. Give two ways in which you can take care of your body. (2 marks)
   Answer: For example wash your hands and face, brush your teeth, eat good food, sleep well.
   Why: 1 mark for each sensible way.
9. Explain how the game Simon Says helps you to learn instructions. (2 marks)
   Answer: You must listen carefully to the words and act at the right time, so you remember the words and their meanings.
   Why: 1 mark for listening carefully and 1 mark for linking action to meaning.
