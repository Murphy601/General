## PAGE 1: Learning to write Arabic letters
Writing Arabic is a beautiful skill. Every letter is a small shape, and when the letters join, they make flowing words. To write well you need patience, a sharp pencil and slow, careful practice.

Here are the basic rules.
• Arabic is written from right to left. Start on the right side of the page and move to the left.
• All the letters of a word are written without lifting your pencil, if they join. Then you add the dots and marks at the end.
• Letters sit on an imaginary line called the baseline. Most letters rest on it. The letter alif stands tall above it.
• Dots are part of the letter. They must be in the right place and the right number.
• The short vowel marks (harakat) are written above or below the letters, after the letter is written.

You already know the 28 letters by their names. Now you will write them neatly and legibly, which means so that anyone can read them. Neatness matters because a wrong shape or a missing dot can change the meaning of a word.

### Get ready
• Sit straight with both feet on the floor.
• Hold your pencil lightly.
• Turn your exercise book so that you can write from right to left without smudging.
• Use a book with lines to help you keep the letters on the baseline.

### Talk about it
Why does neat writing matter? Tell your partner about a time when you could not read someone's writing.

## PAGE 2: Letters change shape

The short line ـ is a stretch that shows where a letter joins to another. It is called a tatweel and it is only used to show joining.

### Letters that do not join
Six letters join only to the letter before them (on the right) and never to the letter after them (on the left): alif (ا), daal (د), dhaal (ذ), raa (ر), zaay (ز) and waaw (و). After these letters, the next letter starts fresh, in its "start" form. This is why raa in Figure 1 has no start or middle form.

### Other pairs
• ص and ض: ض has one dot above.
• ط and ظ: ظ has one dot above.
• ع and غ: غ has one dot above.
• ن has one dot above and ي has two dots below.

### Practise
Trace the letters in a group with your finger in the air, then on your desk, then on paper. Always say the letter name as you write, and count the dots aloud.

## PAGE 4: Trace, then write
The best way to learn a letter is to trace it, then copy it, then write it alone. Follow these steps for each letter.

### Steps
1. Trace your teacher's model with a pencil, again and again, until the shape is smooth.
2. Copy the letter on the same line, a few times, from right to left.
3. Write the letter from memory. Then compare it with the model.
4. Add the dots and check the number and place.
5. Write the letter in the four forms, if it has them.

### Neat and legible writing
• Keep letters the same size.
• Keep them on the baseline.
• Leave a small space between words.
• Do not rush. Speed will come with practice.

### Chart of Arabic characters
Team up to prepare a chart of the Arabic alphabet for the language corner. Divide the letters among the group. Each learner writes their letters large, neatly, in colour, with the letter's name below. Put the letters in the correct order and display the chart. You can add the four forms for the letters that join.

### Check each other
Swap your work with a partner. Look for the shape, the dots and the baseline. Tell your partner one good thing and one thing to fix.

## PAGE 5: Completing words with missing letters
In this activity a letter is missing from a word. You must write the missing letter with its vowel mark. Use the sound of the word to find the letter. The missing letters are nuun, raa, Saad and faa.

### Exercise 1: Missing nuun (ن)
_ُور
The word is nuur, which means light. Write the missing nuun with its Damma: نُور

### Exercise 2: Missing raa (ر)
شُكْ_ا
The word is shukran, thank you. The missing letter is raa. It carries the tanwiin mark (two small slanted lines above), and the alif follows it. Write: شُكْرًا

### Exercise 3: Missing Saad (ص)
_َبَاحٌ
The word is Sabaahun, morning. Write the missing letter Saad with its fatHa: صَبَاحٌ

### Exercise 4: Missing faa (ف)
_َمٌ
The word is fam, mouth. Write the missing letter faa with its fatHa: فَمٌ

### Your turn
Cover the answers and try again. Say the word aloud before you write the letter. Then check with your teacher's model. If a word is wrong, rewrite it three times.

### Tip
The vowel mark goes on the letter you write. If the sound is "u", write the Damma above. If the sound is "a", write the fatHa above. If the sound is "i", write the kasra below.

## PAGE 6: Copying greetings and self-introduction
Copy these words and sentences neatly from a display. Keep the same size and stay on the baseline.

### Words to copy
• مَرْحَبًا = marhaban = hello
• أَهْلًا = ahlan = welcome
• شُكْرًا = shukran = thank you
• عَفْوًا = ʿafwan = you are welcome
• سَلَامٌ = salaam = peace
• صَبَاحُ الْخَيْرِ = Sabaahu l-khayr = good morning

### Sentences to copy
• اسْمِي أَحْمَدُ = ismii Ahmad = My name is Ahmad.
• أَنَا تِلْمِيذٌ = anaa tilmiidh = I am a pupil.

### Steps
1. Read the word aloud.
2. Look at each letter and its shape.
3. Write it once, slowly.
4. Check against the model. Write it two more times if needed.

### Summary
• Write from right to left. Keep letters on the baseline.
• Letters have up to four forms: alone, start, middle, end.
• Six letters do not join to the next letter: alif, daal, dhaal, raa, zaay, waaw.
• Dots decide the letter, so write them carefully.
• Neatness and legibility matter.

### Common mistakes
• Writing from left to right.
• Missing or misplacing the dots.
• Making letters too large or too small so that they float off the line.

## QUIZ
1. In which direction is Arabic written?
   A) From left to right
   B) From right to left
   C) From top to bottom only
   D) From the middle outward
   Answer: B
   Why: Arabic is written from right to left.
2. Which letter has three dots above it?
   A) ب
   B) ت
   C) ث
   D) ج
   Answer: C
   Why: thaa (ث) has three dots above. Baa has one dot below and taa has two dots above.
3. Which of these letters does NOT join to the letter after it?
   A) ب
   B) س
   C) ر
   D) ن
   Answer: C
   Why: raa is one of the six letters that join only to the letter before them.
4. Which word is made when the missing letter nuun is written in _ُور (light)?
   A) نُور
   B) بُور
   C) تُور
   D) يُور
   Answer: A
   Why: The word nuur (light) starts with nuun (ن).
5. What is the difference between the letters ف and ق?
   A) ف has one dot above and ق has two dots above
   B) ف has two dots above and ق has one dot above
   C) They have no dots
   D) ف has a dot below
   Answer: A
   Why: faa has one dot above and qaaf has two dots above.
6. List the six Arabic letters that do not join to the next letter. (3 marks)
   Answer: alif, daal, dhaal, raa, zaay and waaw.
   Why: 3 marks for all six; 2 marks for four or five; 1 mark for two or three.
7. Complete the word: شُكْ_ا with the missing letter, and give the meaning. (2 marks)
   Answer: شُكْرًا (shukran), which means thank you.
   Why: 1 mark for raa (ر) and 1 mark for the meaning.
8. Explain why dots are important in Arabic writing. (2 marks)
   Answer: Several letters have the same shape, and only the dots tell them apart. A wrong dot changes the letter and the meaning of the word.
   Why: 1 mark for same shape and 1 mark for a change in meaning.
9. Give three characteristics of neat and legible Arabic writing. (3 marks)
   Answer: Letters of the same size, letters sitting on the baseline, correct dots and small spaces between words.
   Why: 1 mark for each valid characteristic.
