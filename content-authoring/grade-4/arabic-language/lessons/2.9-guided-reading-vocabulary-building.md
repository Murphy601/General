## PAGE 1: Why build vocabulary?
Vocabulary is the collection of words that you know. The more words you know, the more you can understand and say. A learner with a rich vocabulary reads more easily, speaks with more confidence and writes better sentences.

Imagine you are learning to play football. You need to know the names of the parts of the pitch: the goal, the centre and the touchline. Learning a language is similar. You need the names of things and the words that tell where they are.

In this lesson we build vocabulary on a theme. The theme is the home, and the special focus is where household items are placed. We will select thematic words from a text, read them aloud, and prepare a vocabulary bank.

### What is a thematic word?
A thematic word belongs to a theme, which is the main subject of a text. In a text about the home, thematic words include house, room, kitchen, table, chair, cup and bed. Words like "and" and "the" are not thematic words. They are small helping words.

### Why is it important to keep household items safe?
Household items are useful, but some can cause harm if they are left in the wrong place. A knife should be kept out of reach of small children. A key should be kept in a safe place so that it does not get lost. A lamp must be kept away from water. Knowing where things are helps us to find them and to keep everyone safe.

### Warm up
Look around the classroom. Name an item and say where it is in English or Kiswahili. For example, "The chalk is on the table."

## PAGE 2: A text about where things are
Read this text aloud in Arabic. Your teacher will read first.

### Text: Where are things?
الْكِتَابُ عَلَى الطَّاوِلَةِ. الْقَلَمُ فِي الْحَقِيبَةِ. الْكُرْسِيُّ عِنْدَ الْبَابِ. الْقِطَّةُ تَحْتَ السَّرِيرِ. الْمِصْبَاحُ فَوْقَ الطَّاوِلَةِ. الْمِفْتَاحُ فِي الْخِزَانَةِ.

### In English letters
al-kitaabu ʿalaa t-taawila. al-qalamu fii l-haqiiba. al-kursiyyu ʿinda l-baab. al-qittatu taHta s-sariir. al-misbaahu fawqa t-taawila. al-miftaahu fii l-khizaana.

### In English
The book is on the table. The pen is in the bag. The chair is at the door. The cat is under the bed. The lamp is above the table. The key is in the cupboard.

### Read together
Read the text three times: the first time slowly with your teacher, the second time in pairs and the third time alone. Point to each word as you read. If you find a difficult word, put a small dot under it in pencil and practise it later.

### Check understanding
Answer these questions in English or with an Arabic word.
• Where is the pen? (in the bag)
• Where is the cat? (under the bed)
• What is above the table? (the lamp)
• Where is the key? (in the cupboard)

## PAGE 3: Selecting thematic words
Now find the thematic words in the text.

### Items (nouns)
• كِتَابٌ = kitaab = book
• طَاوِلَةٌ = taawila = table
• قَلَمٌ = qalam = pen
• حَقِيبَةٌ = haqiiba = bag
• كُرْسِيٌّ = kursii = chair
• بَابٌ = baab = door
• قِطَّةٌ = qitta = cat
• سَرِيرٌ = sariir = bed
• مِصْبَاحٌ = misbaah = lamp
• مِفْتَاحٌ = miftaah = key
• خِزَانَةٌ = khizaana = cupboard

### Position words (prepositions)
• عَلَى = ʿalaa = on
• فِي = fii = in
• عِنْدَ = ʿinda = at
• تَحْتَ = taHta = under
• فَوْقَ = fawqa = above

### Sort the words
Make two lists in your book. Put the items in the first list and the position words in the second. Tell your partner what each word means.

### Help to remember
Group the words by place. In the bedroom: sariir, khizaana. In the kitchen: taawila, kursii. At the door: baab, miftaah. Grouping helps your memory.

## PAGE 4: A reading circle
A reading circle is a small group who sit together to read aloud. Each learner has a turn. Here is how to run one.

### Steps
1. Sit in a circle of four to six learners.
2. The first learner reads the first word from the list of thematic words.
3. The next learner repeats the word and reads the second word.
4. Keep going around the circle until all words are read.
5. Then read the whole text sentence by sentence, each learner reading one sentence.

### Rules
• Listen when others read. Do not laugh at mistakes.
• Help by reading the word slowly and letting your friend repeat.
• Take only your turn. Give others time to read.
• Use your finger to follow the words.

### Extra practice
Play a game called "Find the position". Your teacher reads out a position word, such as taHta. The learners quickly find it in the text and put a finger on it. The first learner with the correct finger reads the whole sentence aloud.

### Why do reading circles help?
Each learner reads aloud, listens to others and gets support. Everyone practises pronouncing the words and grows more confident.

## PAGE 5: Preparing a vocabulary bank
A vocabulary bank is a place where you keep all the words you have learnt on a theme. Follow these steps to make a class vocabulary bank of words about the home.

### Steps
1. Write each word on a card, in large clear Arabic letters.
2. On the back, write the sound in English letters and the meaning.
3. Draw a small picture on the front to help you remember.
4. Put the cards in a box or on a chart.
5. Use the cards to test yourself and your friends.

### Sentences with the cards
Pick two cards and make one sentence with them. For example, from kitaab and taawila: al-kitaabu ʿalaa t-taawila. Pick a position word to join the items.

### Display
Stick the flashcards on the wall of the classroom in groups, such as items in the kitchen and items in the bedroom. Read them as you pass each day.

## PAGE 6: Words for keeping things safe
Reading can also teach us to be careful. Here are two safety sentences in Arabic. Read them with your teacher.

### Safety sentences
• الْمِفْتَاحُ فِي الْخِزَانَةِ = al-miftaahu fii l-khizaana = The key is in the cupboard.
• لَا تَلْعَبْ بِالسِّكِّينِ = laa talʿab bi-s-sikkiin = Do not play with the knife. (This is said to a boy. Girls are told laa talʿabii.)

The new words are sikkiin (سِكِّينٌ, knife) and laa (لَا, no, do not).

### Talk about it
Choose two items in your home that must be kept in a safe place. Say where you keep them in Arabic, using fii or ʿalaa or fawqa.

### Summary
• Vocabulary is all the words you know. Thematic words belong to a theme.
• Sort words into items and position words.
• A reading circle gives every learner a turn to read.
• A vocabulary bank keeps words on cards for practice.
• Keeping household items in the right place keeps us safe.

### Common mistakes
• Putting position words in the list of items.
• Skipping a learner in the reading circle.
• Writing the Arabic word from left to right on a card.

## QUIZ
1. Which of these is a thematic word for the home?
   A) كِتَابٌ
   B) شُكْرًا
   C) وَ
   D) لَا
   Answer: A
   Why: كِتَابٌ (book) is an item found in a home. The others are thanks, "and" and "no".
2. What does fawqa mean?
   A) Under
   B) In
   C) Above
   D) On
   Answer: C
   Why: فَوْقَ means above.
3. What does al-miftaahu fii l-khizaana mean?
   A) The key is in the cupboard.
   B) The chair is at the door.
   C) The book is on the table.
   D) The pen is in the bag.
   Answer: A
   Why: miftaah is key, fii is in, and khizaana is cupboard.
4. How does a reading circle work?
   A) Each learner takes a turn to read aloud
   B) Only the teacher reads
   C) Learners read silently and never speak
   D) One learner reads the whole book
   Answer: A
   Why: In a reading circle, learners sit together and each has a turn to read aloud.
5. What should you write on the back of a vocabulary flashcard?
   A) The sound and the meaning
   B) Only your name
   C) Nothing
   D) The date
   Answer: A
   Why: The Arabic word goes on the front. The sound and the meaning go on the back.
6. Sort these words into items and position words: كِتَابٌ, عَلَى, طَاوِلَةٌ, تَحْتَ. (4 marks)
   Answer: Items: kitaab (book), taawila (table). Position words: ʿalaa (on), taHta (under).
   Why: 1 mark for each word placed correctly.
7. Translate: al-qittatu taHta s-sariir. (2 marks)
   Answer: The cat is under the bed.
   Why: 1 mark for "the cat" and 1 mark for "under the bed".
8. Give two reasons why we should keep household items in safe places. (2 marks)
   Answer: So that we can find them easily, and so that nobody is harmed, for example by a knife.
   Why: 1 mark for each valid reason.
9. Describe how to make a vocabulary bank for words about the home. (3 marks)
   Answer: Write each Arabic word on a card with a picture, write the sound and meaning on the back, and keep the cards in a box or on a chart to practise.
   Why: 1 mark for the word and picture, 1 mark for sound and meaning on the back and 1 mark for keeping and using the cards.
