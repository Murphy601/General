## PAGE 1: Pictures and words together
Pictures and words work well together. A picture shows us what something looks like. A word tells us its name. When we draw a picture and write its name beside it, we say the same thing in two ways, and this helps us to remember the word.

Long ago, before people had many written words, they used drawings to send messages. Today, we still use pictures on road signs, on food packets and in books for children. A picture can be understood by people who speak different languages. That is why pictures are important in communication.

In this lesson you will draw, colour and label household items and rooms. You will match pictures to their Arabic names and write simple words that describe the items.

### What you will do
• List household items and rooms found at home.
• Draw, colour and label some of them.
• Share your work for review and place it in a class portfolio.
• Match pictures with names.
• Write simple describing words.

### Talk about it
Why do people use pictures to communicate? Think of a road sign, a picture on a packet of biscuits or a picture on a toilet door. What message does each give?

### Warm up
Look around your home in your mind. Which rooms can you see? Which things are in each room? Tell your partner, using English, Kiswahili or Arabic words that you know.

## PAGE 2: Items and rooms to draw
Make a list of household items and rooms for your drawings. Here are the Arabic words that you know.

### Rooms and places
• بَيْتٌ = bayt = house
• غُرْفَةٌ = ghurfa = room
• مَطْبَخٌ = matbakh = kitchen
• حَمَّامٌ = hammaam = bathroom
• حَدِيقَةٌ = hadiiqa = garden

### Items
• سَرِيرٌ = sariir = bed
• كُرْسِيٌّ = kursii = chair
• طَاوِلَةٌ = taawila = table
• بَابٌ = baab = door
• نَافِذَةٌ = naafidha = window
• مِصْبَاحٌ = misbaah = lamp
• سَاعَةٌ = saaʿa = clock
• كُوبٌ = kuub = cup
• صَحْنٌ = sahn = plate
• مِلْعَقَةٌ = milʿaqa = spoon

### Choose your items
In your group, choose three to five items from the lists. You will draw and label them. Try to choose items that you see in a Kenyan home, such as a chair, a table and a cup.

### Tip
Discuss what each item is used for. A table is used for eating and writing. A bed is for sleeping. A lamp is for light. A clock shows the time. A cup holds a drink. Say these in English or Kiswahili with your group.

## PAGE 3: Draw, colour and label
Follow these steps for each drawing.

### Steps
1. Draw the item large, in pencil. Keep the lines simple.
2. Colour it neatly with crayons or colour pencils.
3. Write its Arabic name under it in neat handwriting, from right to left.
4. Write the meaning in English under the Arabic name, in small letters.
5. Check the spelling against the list.

### Share and review
Put your drawings on the wall or on your desk. Walk around and look at the work of others. Leave a kind comment, such as "Well coloured" or "Clear writing". Check that each name matches its picture.

### Class portfolio
A portfolio is a folder of your best work. Work together to organise the pictures in order, for example rooms first, then items. Give the portfolio a cover and a title. Keep it in the language corner.

## PAGE 4: Matching pictures and names
Matching helps you to remember words and to think carefully.

### Activity 1: Match the picture to the word
Imagine three pictures. Match each picture to its Arabic name.
1. A picture of a chair
2. A picture of a door
3. A picture of a bed

Names: a) بَابٌ  b) سَرِيرٌ  c) كُرْسِيٌّ

Answers: 1 with c (kursii), 2 with a (baab), 3 with b (sariir).

### Activity 2: Match the room to the item
1. The kitchen: matbakh
2. The bathroom: hammaam
3. The garden: hadiiqa

Think about which items belong in each room. A cup and a spoon belong in the kitchen. A bed belongs in the bedroom.

### Activity 3: Match in pairs
Use your own drawings. Cut out the drawings and the names separately. Shuffle them. Ask a friend to match each drawing to its name. Take turns.

### Check
Read each pair aloud: picture, then Arabic word, then meaning.

## PAGE 5: Words that describe
Basic describing words help us to say more about a picture. In Arabic, the describing word comes after the name and is usually matched to it in gender.

### Describing words
• كَبِيرٌ / كَبِيرَةٌ = kabiir / kabiira = big
• صَغِيرٌ / صَغِيرَةٌ = Sagiir / Sagiira = small
• جَدِيدٌ / جَدِيدَةٌ = jadiid / jadiida = new
• نَظِيفٌ / نَظِيفَةٌ = nadhiif / nadhiifa = clean
• جَمِيلٌ / جَمِيلَةٌ = jamiil / jamiila = beautiful

The first form is for a masculine word, and the second form, ending in -a, is for a feminine word. Words like kursii (chair) and baab (door) are masculine. Words like taawila (table) and naafidha (window) are feminine.

### Examples
• الْبَابُ كَبِيرٌ = al-baabu kabiir = The door is big.
• الطَّاوِلَةُ صَغِيرَةٌ = at-taawilatu Sagiira = The table is small.
• النَّافِذَةُ نَظِيفَةٌ = an-naafidhatu nadhiifa = The window is clean.
• الْكُرْسِيُّ جَدِيدٌ = al-kursiyyu jadiid = The chair is new.

### Write your own
In pairs, write three simple describing sentences about your drawings, using the model. Check that the describing word matches the gender of the item.

## PAGE 6: Writing simple words and summary
Now write simple words to describe the items and rooms in the house.

### Steps
1. Choose a drawing from your portfolio.
2. Write the name of the item on a line in neat Arabic letters.
3. Write a describing word next to it: for example طَاوِلَةٌ جَمِيلَةٌ (a beautiful table).
4. Read your words to a friend.
5. Look after your household items and keep them clean, then draw a picture of a clean house.

### Why do we take care of household items?
Household items last longer if we take care of them. We must clean them, keep them dry and put them back in the right place. When we take care of items, we save money and keep our homes safe.

### Summary
• Pictures and words work together to communicate.
• Draw, colour, label and share your drawings.
• Match each picture to its Arabic name.
• Describing words come after the name, and match its gender: kabiir for masculine, kabiira for feminine.
• A portfolio keeps your best work.

### Common mistakes
• Writing the label from left to right.
• Using a masculine describing word for a feminine item, such as kabiir with taawila.
• Forgetting to check the spelling against the list.

## QUIZ
1. What is the Arabic word for "door"?
   A) بَابٌ
   B) سَرِيرٌ
   C) كُوبٌ
   D) مَطْبَخٌ
   Answer: A
   Why: بَابٌ (baab) means door.
2. Which word means "clean" for a masculine item?
   A) نَظِيفٌ
   B) جَدِيدٌ
   C) صَغِيرٌ
   D) كَبِيرٌ
   Answer: A
   Why: نَظِيفٌ (nadhiif) means clean.
3. Which word matches a feminine noun like taawila (table)?
   A) كَبِيرٌ
   B) كَبِيرَةٌ
   C) جَدِيدٌ
   D) نَظِيفٌ
   Answer: B
   Why: A feminine noun takes the describing word ending in -a, so kabiira.
4. What does at-taawilatu Sagiira mean?
   A) The table is small.
   B) The table is big.
   C) The chair is new.
   D) The door is clean.
   Answer: A
   Why: at-taawila is the table and Sagiira means small.
5. What is a class portfolio?
   A) A folder of the class's best work
   B) A kind of desk
   C) A test
   D) A picture on the wall only
   Answer: A
   Why: A portfolio is a collection of work, kept together and shown to others.
6. Match: chair, bed, kitchen with their Arabic names (in English letters). (3 marks)
   Answer: chair is kursii, bed is sariir, kitchen is matbakh.
   Why: 1 mark for each correct match.
7. Write one describing sentence in Arabic sounds about a table, and give its meaning. (2 marks)
   Answer: For example at-taawilatu jadiida. The table is new.
   Why: 1 mark for a correct sentence with a matching describing word and 1 mark for the meaning.
8. Explain how drawing and labelling a picture helps you to learn a new word. (2 marks)
   Answer: The picture shows what the word means and writing the label helps you to remember its spelling.
   Why: 1 mark for the picture linking to meaning and 1 mark for practising spelling.
9. State three steps for drawing and labelling a household item. (3 marks)
   Answer: Draw the item, colour it, then write its Arabic name and meaning under it and check the spelling.
   Why: 1 mark for each valid step.
