## PAGE 1: Why do we greet one another?
Every morning in Kenya we say Jambo, Habari or Shikamoo to the people we meet. A greeting is a small gift of kindness. It tells the other person, "I see you and I respect you." People all over the world do the same thing in their own languages.

Arabic is one of the great languages of the world. Millions of people speak it, from Egypt and Morocco to Saudi Arabia and Oman. In Kenya, many people hear Arabic when they pray, and many Kenyan words come from Arabic, such as kitabu (book), shukrani (thanks) and safari (journey).

In this lesson you will learn to greet someone in Arabic, to say your name, and to answer politely. You will also learn to pronounce each word carefully. Arabic is written from right to left, which is the opposite of English. Your finger will start on the right side of the page.

### Think and share
• How do you greet your teacher?
• How do you greet a friend?
• Why is a greeting the first thing we say when we meet?

Say your answers to a partner. Listening to your partner is also part of speaking well.

## PAGE 2: The first Arabic greetings
The most famous Arabic greeting is as-salaamu ʿalaykum. It means "peace be upon you". The answer is wa ʿalaykumu s-salaam, which means "and upon you be peace". Muslims and many other Arabic speakers use these words all day long.

Here are the greetings you must know. Each line shows the sound in English letters, the Arabic, and the meaning.

### Greeting words
• as-salaamu ʿalaykum = السَّلَامُ عَلَيْكُمْ = peace be upon you
• wa ʿalaykumu s-salaam = وَعَلَيْكُمُ السَّلَامُ = and upon you peace (the reply)
• marhaban = مَرْحَبًا = hello
• ahlan wa sahlan = أَهْلًا وَسَهْلًا = welcome
• sabaahu l-khayr = صَبَاحُ الْخَيْرِ = good morning
• sabaahu n-nuur = صَبَاحُ النُّورِ = good morning (the reply, "a morning of light")
• masaa'u l-khayr = مَسَاءُ الْخَيْرِ = good evening
• maʿa s-salaama = مَعَ السَّلَامَةِ = goodbye ("go with safety")

### Try it
Say each word slowly, then faster. Greet your partner with as-salaamu ʿalaykum and let your partner reply. Then swap places.

## PAGE 3: Saying who you are
To introduce yourself you need only a few words. Ask a friend: maa ismuka? (مَا اسْمُكَ؟) which means "What is your name?" You answer with ismii and then your name: ismii Ahmad (اسْمِي أَحْمَدُ) means "My name is Ahmad."

The small word anaa (أَنَا) means "I". You can say anaa tilmiidh (أَنَا تِلْمِيذٌ) for "I am a pupil" if you are a boy, and anaa tilmiidha (أَنَا تِلْمِيذَةٌ) if you are a girl.

To say where you come from, use min, which means "from". Anaa min Kiiniya (أَنَا مِنْ كينيا) means "I am from Kenya." You can also say your age. The question is kam ʿumruka? (كَمْ عُمْرُكَ؟) meaning "How old are you?" One answer is ʿumrii tisʿu sanawaat (عُمْرِي تِسْعُ سَنَوَاتٍ), "I am nine years old." For ten, say ʿumrii ʿashru sanawaat (عُمْرِي عَشْرُ سَنَوَاتٍ).

### Pattern to remember
ismii + your name
anaa + tilmiidh or tilmiidha
anaa min + your country

## PAGE 4: Talking to boys and to girls
Arabic has different word endings when you speak to a boy and when you speak to a girl. This is called gender. Do not worry, it is only a small sound at the end of the word.

### Asking "How are you?"
• To a boy: kayfa haaluka? = كَيْفَ حَالُكَ؟
• To a girl: kayfa haaluki? = كَيْفَ حَالُكِ؟

### Asking "What is your name?"
• To a boy: maa ismuka? = مَا اسْمُكَ؟
• To a girl: maa ismuki? = مَا اسْمُكِ؟

The ending -ka is for a boy and the ending -ki is for a girl. The answer is the same for both: anaa bi-khayr, al-hamdu lillaah (أَنَا بِخَيْرٍ، الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ). It means "I am well, thanks be to God." You can add shukran (شُكْرًا), which means "thank you". The reply to thanks is ʿafwan (عَفْوًا), which means "you are welcome".

Greeting customs are a little different in different places, for example whether people shake hands. Show respect by greeting with a smile and polite words, and follow the customs of the people you are with. This is a good way to treat boys and girls, men and women equally.

### Reading it in English sounds
Ahmad: as-salaamu ʿalaykum.
Amina: wa ʿalaykumu s-salaam.
Ahmad: maa ismuki?
Amina: ismii Aminatu.

### Role play
Work in pairs. One of you is Ahmad and one is Amina, or use your own names. Greet, ask the name, answer, and say goodbye with maʿa s-salaama. Then change roles and try a new pair of friends.

## PAGE 6: Pronunciation tips and summary
Good pronunciation helps people understand you. Follow these tips.

### Listen first, then copy
Listen to your teacher or to a recording. Repeat the word three times. Say it slowly, then at normal speed.

### Long and short vowels
A long aa (as in sabaah) is held for about twice as long as a short a. Try marhaban (short) and salaam (long aa).

### Two sounds that are new to you
• The sound kh is made at the back of the throat, like a soft clearing of the throat. It is in khayr (good).
• The sound ʿ is a deep throat sound. It is in ʿalaykum. Listen to your teacher and copy the sound carefully. Even if it is difficult at first, keep trying.

### Summary
• Greet with as-salaamu ʿalaykum and reply wa ʿalaykumu s-salaam.
• Introduce yourself with ismii and anaa.
• Use -ka for a boy and -ki for a girl.
• Say shukran to thank and ʿafwan to reply.

### Common mistakes
Saying the words too fast, mixing up -ka and -ki, and forgetting to reply to a greeting. A greeting always needs an answer.

## QUIZ
1. What does as-salaamu ʿalaykum mean?
   A) Good night
   B) Peace be upon you
   C) Thank you
   D) What is your name
   Answer: B
   Why: It is the standard Arabic greeting and means "peace be upon you".
2. What is the correct reply to as-salaamu ʿalaykum?
   A) wa ʿalaykumu s-salaam
   B) shukran
   C) maʿa s-salaama
   D) sabaahu l-khayr
   Answer: A
   Why: The reply is wa ʿalaykumu s-salaam, "and upon you be peace".
3. Which question do you ask a girl to find out her name?
   A) maa ismuka?
   B) maa ismuki?
   C) kam ʿumruka?
   D) anaa min Kiiniya
   Answer: B
   Why: The ending -ki is used when speaking to a girl, so maa ismuki? asks her name.
4. What does maʿa s-salaama mean?
   A) Welcome
   B) Good morning
   C) Goodbye
   D) I am a pupil
   Answer: C
   Why: It is the way to say goodbye, "go with safety".
5. Which Arabic word means "I"?
   A) شُكْرًا
   B) أَنَا
   C) مَرْحَبًا
   D) عَفْوًا
   Answer: B
   Why: أَنَا (anaa) means "I". The other words mean thank you, hello and you are welcome.
6. Write two greetings in Arabic sounds (English letters) and give their meanings. (2 marks)
   Answer: For example, as-salaamu ʿalaykum (peace be upon you) and sabaahu l-khayr (good morning).
   Why: 1 mark for each greeting with its correct meaning.
7. Introduce yourself using ismii and anaa. Write what you would say. (3 marks)
   Answer: For example: ismii Wanjiku. Anaa tilmiidha. Anaa min Kiiniya. (My name is Wanjiku. I am a pupil. I am from Kenya.)
   Why: 1 mark for ismii with a name, 1 mark for anaa tilmiidh or tilmiidha correct for the speaker, 1 mark for a correct meaning or sentence about the country.
8. Explain why we say kayfa haaluka to a boy but kayfa haaluki to a girl. (2 marks)
   Answer: Arabic uses the ending -ka when speaking to a boy or man and -ki when speaking to a girl or woman.
   Why: 1 mark for saying the ending changes, 1 mark for matching -ka to a boy and -ki to a girl.
9. Ahmad says to you kayfa haaluka? Write a full polite reply and say what it means. (3 marks)
   Answer: anaa bi-khayr, al-hamdu lillaah. It means "I am well, thanks be to God."
   Why: 1 mark for anaa bi-khayr, 1 mark for al-hamdu lillaah, 1 mark for the meaning.
