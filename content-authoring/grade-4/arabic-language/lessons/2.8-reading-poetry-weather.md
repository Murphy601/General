## PAGE 1: What is a poem?
A poem is a short piece of writing that uses words in a special way. Poems have lines, and the lines often have a rhythm (a beat) and sometimes end with the same sound. This is called rhyme. Poems can make us feel happy, calm, excited or thoughtful.

Poetry is very important in the Arabic language. For hundreds of years, Arabic speakers have loved poems and songs. People recited poems at gatherings, at celebrations and when they wanted to remember something important. In Kenya, too, we enjoy poems, songs and rhymes in Kiswahili, English and mother-tongue languages. A poem is a good way to learn a language because the rhythm helps us to remember words.

In this lesson you will read a short poem about the weather. You will read it aloud in a group, use gestures, facial expressions and movements when you recite it, and pick out weather words for your vocabulary bank.

### Think about it
• Do you know a poem or a rhyme in Kiswahili or English?
• What makes it easy to remember?
• How do you say it: quietly, loudly, quickly or slowly?

### Warm up
Clap a steady beat with your hands. Say the days of the week to the beat. Feel the rhythm. Poems use rhythm in the same way.

## PAGE 2: The poem "The Sky"
Read this short poem about the sky and the weather. It is written for learners at your level.

### Poem
الشَّمْسُ فِي السَّمَاءِ
وَالْغَيْمُ فِي السَّمَاءِ
وَالْمَطَرُ مِنَ السَّمَاءِ
مَا أَجْمَلَ الْبِيئَةَ

### In English letters
ash-shamsu fii s-samaa'i
wal-ghaymu fii s-samaa'i
wal-maTaru mina s-samaa'i
maa ajmala l-bii'a

### In English
The sun is in the sky,
and the clouds are in the sky,
and the rain is from the sky.
How beautiful the environment is!

### Notice the rhyme
The first three lines all end with the sound -maa'i (السَّمَاءِ). This is rhyme. The fourth line is the surprise, the happy ending.

### New words
• ghaym = غَيْمٌ = clouds
• min = مِنْ = from
• maa ajmala = مَا أَجْمَلَ = how beautiful (a cry of wonder)
• bii'a = بِيئَةٌ = environment

The word wa (وَ) at the start of a line means "and". Write it joined to the next word: وَالْغَيْمُ.

## PAGE 3: Reading the poem aloud
When you read a poem aloud, follow these steps.

### Steps
1. Listen to your teacher read the poem twice.
2. Read it line by line after your teacher.
3. Read it with a partner. One reads line 1 and line 3. The other reads line 2 and line 4.
4. Read it in a group, all together.
5. Read the last line with a happy voice.

### Voice tips
• Read the first three lines in an even rhythm, like a gentle beat.
• Slow down a little on the last word of each line so that the rhyme can be heard.
• Say the fourth line with wonder, as if you have just seen a beautiful sight.
• Keep the same speed for everyone in the group.

### Read in a group
Divide the class into three groups. Group 1 reads line 1, Group 2 reads line 2, Group 3 reads line 3, and everyone says line 4 together. Practise until it is smooth and steady.

### Feedback
After a group recites, the other groups say one thing that went well and one thing to improve, such as "Speak a little louder" or "Wait for the last word of each line."

## PAGE 4: Non-verbal cues
Non-verbal cues are the messages we give without words. They include gestures (hand movements), facial expressions and body movement. When we recite a poem, non-verbal cues bring the poem to life and help the audience understand.

### Actions for the poem
• Line 1, shams (sun): lift both arms above your head and make a big circle.
• Line 2, ghaym (clouds): move your hands slowly in soft circles in front of you.
• Line 3, maTar (rain): raise your hands and wiggle your fingers downwards.
• Line 4, maa ajmala l-bii'a: open your arms wide and smile.

### Facial expressions
Smile in the first line, look calm in the second line, look curious in the third and look delighted in the last line.

### Body movement
Stand straight. Do not fidget. Move only when your action matches a word. Face the audience.

### Practise
Recite the poem with actions in groups of four. Each learner takes one line and performs the action for it. Rotate roles. Ask the audience to guess which weather word you are showing.

### Remember
The actions must match the words. If you are saying "rain", your hands must show rain, not sun.

## PAGE 5: Weather vocabulary bank
Poems are full of useful words. Pick out the weather words from the poem and add them to a vocabulary bank.

### Words from the poem
• shams = شَمْسٌ = sun
• samaa' = سَمَاءٌ = sky
• ghaym = غَيْمٌ = clouds
• maTar = مَطَرٌ = rain
• bii'a = بِيئَةٌ = environment

### Other weather words you know
• riiH = رِيحٌ = wind
• Haarr = حَارٌّ = hot
• baarid = بَارِدٌ = cold
• jaww = جَوٌّ = weather, air

### How to build the bank
1. Write each word neatly on a card in Arabic script.
2. Write its sound in English letters underneath.
3. Write the meaning in English.
4. Draw a small picture beside it.
5. Keep the cards in a box, or stick them on a chart for the language corner.

Use your cards for games. Pick a card and say a sentence about the weather, for example al-jawwu Haarr (الْجَوُّ حَارٌّ).

### Talk about it
Why do we need to look after the environment? How does the environment give us the weather we need for our crops and our water? Share ideas in your group.

## PAGE 6: Staging the poem and summary
You can perform your poem at an inter-class festival or a school gathering.

### Prepare a stage performance
1. Choose a group of four to six learners.
2. Decide who says which line and who does which action.
3. Stand in a line facing the audience.
4. Say the poem together, with actions.
5. Bow together at the end.

### Poem tips
• Face the audience and speak loudly enough.
• Stay together on the beat.
• Smile at the end.

### Summary
• A poem has rhythm and often rhyme.
• Read a poem aloud line by line, keeping a steady beat.
• Non-verbal cues are gestures, facial expressions and body movement.
• The actions must match the meaning of the words.
• A vocabulary bank keeps the new words for the future.

### Common mistakes
• Reading a poem too fast and losing the rhythm.
• Doing an action that does not match the word.
• Forgetting to look at the audience.

## QUIZ
1. What is rhyme?
   A) Words with the same ending sound
   B) A very long word
   C) A kind of number
   D) A drawing
   Answer: A
   Why: Rhyme is when words or lines end with the same sound, like the lines ending in السَّمَاءِ.
2. What does maa ajmala l-bii'a mean?
   A) Where is the environment?
   B) How beautiful the environment is!
   C) I plant a tree.
   D) The rain is heavy.
   Answer: B
   Why: maa ajmala is a cry of wonder meaning "how beautiful" and al-bii'a is the environment.
3. Which word means "clouds"?
   A) شَمْسٌ
   B) مَطَرٌ
   C) غَيْمٌ
   D) رِيحٌ
   Answer: C
   Why: غَيْمٌ (ghaym) means clouds. شَمْسٌ is sun, مَطَرٌ is rain, and رِيحٌ is wind.
4. Which of these is a non-verbal cue?
   A) A hand movement that shows rain
   B) The spelling of a word
   C) A dictionary
   D) The title of the poem
   Answer: A
   Why: Gestures, facial expressions and body movements are non-verbal cues.
5. What does the word min mean in maTaru mina s-samaa'i?
   A) in
   B) from
   C) and
   D) on
   Answer: B
   Why: min means from, so "the rain is from the sky".
6. Write four weather words from the poem in Arabic sounds with meanings. (4 marks)
   Answer: shams (sun), samaa' (sky), ghaym (clouds), maTar (rain).
   Why: 1 mark for each correct word with its meaning.
7. Describe the actions you could use for the lines about the sun and the rain. (2 marks)
   Answer: For the sun I lift my arms and make a big circle. For the rain I raise my hands and wiggle my fingers downwards.
   Why: 1 mark for each suitable action that matches the word.
8. Explain how you can make a recitation of a poem more interesting for the audience. (3 marks)
   Answer: Use a clear voice and steady rhythm, use gestures and facial expressions that match the words, and look at the audience.
   Why: 1 mark for voice or rhythm, 1 mark for gestures or expressions and 1 mark for looking at the audience.
9. Give two reasons why poems are useful when learning a language. (2 marks)
   Answer: The rhythm and rhyme help us to remember words, and poems help us to practise pronunciation and expression.
   Why: 1 mark for each valid reason.
