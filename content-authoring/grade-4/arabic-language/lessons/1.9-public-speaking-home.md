## PAGE 1: Speaking to a group
Public speaking means speaking to a group of people, such as your class, your school assembly or a competition. Many people feel shy at first. That is normal. With practice and a plan, you can speak with confidence.

A good speaker uses three things. The first is correct pronunciation, so that people can understand each word. The second is a good tone. Tone is the way your voice sounds: friendly, calm, clear and strong enough to be heard at the back. The third is a clear plan, so the speech has a beginning, a middle and an end.

Speeches can be given in any language. In this lesson you will give a short speech about your home in simple Arabic. You will use the words you have learnt in this term: rooms, household items and position words.

### Why do we practise speaking?
• To share information clearly with others.
• To gain confidence.
• To learn new words.
• To make our school proud in competitions.

### Talk about it
Have you ever spoken in front of a class? How did you feel? Tell your partner one thing that helped you to feel brave.

## PAGE 2: Key words to retell
Before you speak, you need the words. Look at the list. Say each word, then retell the word to a friend by giving its meaning.

### Key words about the home
• bayt = بَيْتٌ = house
• ghurfa = غُرْفَةٌ = room
• matbakh = مَطْبَخٌ = kitchen
• hammaam = حَمَّامٌ = bathroom
• hadiiqa = حَدِيقَةٌ = garden
• baab = بَابٌ = door
• naafidha = نَافِذَةٌ = window
• taawila = طَاوِلَةٌ = table
• kursii = كُرْسِيٌّ = chair
• sariir = سَرِيرٌ = bed

### Key phrases
• haadhaa baytii = هَذَا بَيْتِي = This is my house.
• fii baytii matbakh = فِي بَيْتِي مَطْبَخٌ = In my house there is a kitchen.
• al-kitaabu ʿalaa t-taawila = الْكِتَابُ عَلَى الطَّاوِلَةِ = The book is on the table.

### Retelling game
Sit in pairs. One learner says an Arabic word. The other learner says the meaning and adds a position word, for example kursii, the chair, and then fii l-ghurfa (فِي الْغُرْفَةِ, in the room). Then swap.

## PAGE 3: Planning a short speech
A speech has three parts.

1. The opening. Greet the audience and say who you are.
2. The middle. Give the main information. For a speech about the home, say what rooms and things are in it and where they are.
3. The closing. Thank the audience.

The closing phrase shukran li-istimaaʿikum means "thank you for listening".

A short speech does not need to be long. Four to six sentences are enough at this stage. What matters is that every sentence is clear and that you say it with confidence. Write your plan on a small card with one key word for each part, and keep the card in your hand as a reminder. Do not read every word from the card. Look at your listeners, speak, and glance at the card only when you need it.

## PAGE 4: A model speech
Read this model speech. It is short so that you can learn it.

### The speech
as-salaamu ʿalaykum. ismii Ahmad. anaa fii S-Saffi r-raabiʿ. haadhaa baytii. fii baytii matbakh wa ghurfatu nawm wa hammaam. al-kitaabu ʿalaa t-taawila. shukran li-istimaaʿikum.

### In Arabic script
السَّلَامُ عَلَيْكُمْ. اسْمِي أَحْمَدُ. أَنَا فِي الصَّفِّ الرَّابِعِ. هَذَا بَيْتِي. فِي بَيْتِي مَطْبَخٌ وَغُرْفَةُ نَوْمٍ وَحَمَّامٌ. الْكِتَابُ عَلَى الطَّاوِلَةِ. شُكْرًا لِاسْتِمَاعِكُمْ.

### In English
Peace be upon you. My name is Ahmad. I am in Grade 4. This is my house. In my house there is a kitchen, a bedroom and a bathroom. The book is on the table. Thank you for listening.

Notice how the speech joins words with wa (and). The new words are Saff (صَفٌّ, class) and raabiʿ (رَابِعٌ, fourth), so "Grade 4" is "the fourth class".

### Make it yours
Change the name, the rooms and the items. Say what is in your home. You may keep some of the model sentences exactly as they are.

## PAGE 5: Tone, pace and body language
Your voice and your body both speak. Here is how to use them well.

### Voice
• Speak loudly enough so that the last row can hear you.
• Speak slowly and clearly. Pronounce each word.
• Let your voice fall at the end of each statement and pause between sentences.
• Use a friendly tone. Smile a little when you greet and when you thank.

### Body
• Stand straight with your feet steady.
• Look at your audience, not at the floor.
• Use your hands to point to things, for example to show a door or a table.

### Handle nerves
• Take a deep breath before you begin.
• Practise many times, first alone and then with a friend.
• Remember that the audience wants you to do well.

### Listening to the speaker
The audience also has a job. Listen quietly, look at the speaker, and clap at the end. Give kind feedback afterwards.

### Class practice
Speak in groups of four. Each learner gives the speech while the others watch their tone, pace and body language. Use the checklist: loud enough, clear, steady, friendly, eye contact.

## PAGE 6: Speeches and competitions
You are now ready to give your own speech. Choose three or four rooms or items and put them in the speech pattern. Practise until you can say it without looking at your paper.

### Steps
1. Write your speech in Arabic script or in English letters, using words from this term.
2. Read it aloud three times.
3. Say it to a friend and ask for feedback.
4. Say it in front of the class.

Some schools hold speaking competitions between classes or with other schools. Join in with your team. Speaking contests help you to grow in confidence, and they bring pupils together.

### Summary
• Public speaking means speaking to a group.
• Use correct pronunciation, a good tone and a clear plan: opening, middle, closing.
• Greet with as-salaamu ʿalaykum. Thank with shukran li-istimaaʿikum.
• Use eye contact, a steady pace and calm body language.
• Practise many times before you speak.

### Common mistakes
• Speaking too fast because you are nervous.
• Looking down at your paper all the time.
• Forgetting to greet and to thank the audience.

## QUIZ
1. What does shukran li-istimaaʿikum mean?
   A) Please come in
   B) Thank you for listening
   C) Good morning
   D) Where is the book
   Answer: B
   Why: shukran means thank you and istimaaʿ means listening, so the phrase means "thank you for listening".
2. Which part of a speech is the greeting?
   A) The opening
   B) The middle
   C) The closing
   D) The question
   Answer: A
   Why: We greet the audience and say who we are in the opening.
3. What does haadhaa baytii mean?
   A) This is my house.
   B) This is my sister.
   C) I like the kitchen.
   D) The book is on the table.
   Answer: A
   Why: haadhaa means "this" and baytii means "my house".
4. Which of these helps a speaker most?
   A) Speaking very fast
   B) Looking at the floor
   C) Speaking clearly and looking at the audience
   D) Whispering
   Answer: C
   Why: Clear speech and eye contact help the audience to understand and to trust the speaker.
5. What does the word ghurfa mean?
   A) Door
   B) Room
   C) Garden
   D) Table
   Answer: B
   Why: غُرْفَةٌ (ghurfa) means room.
6. Name the three parts of a short speech and say what each one does. (3 marks)
   Answer: Opening: greet and say who you are. Middle: give the main information. Closing: thank the audience.
   Why: 1 mark for each part with its job.
7. Write two Arabic sentences that you can use in a speech about your house and translate them. (4 marks)
   Answer: For example haadhaa baytii (This is my house) and fii baytii matbakh (In my house there is a kitchen).
   Why: 1 mark for each correct Arabic sentence and 1 mark for each correct translation.
8. Give three tips that help a speaker use tone and body language well. (3 marks)
   Answer: Speak loudly enough and clearly; look at the audience; stand straight; smile when greeting.
   Why: 1 mark for each suitable tip.
9. Explain why it is good to practise a speech before giving it. (2 marks)
   Answer: Practice helps you to remember the words, pronounce them correctly and feel less nervous.
   Why: 1 mark for remembering or pronouncing and 1 mark for confidence.
