## PAGE 1: Syllables are the building blocks
A syllable is a small beat of sound in a word. When you clap the word "Kenya", you clap two times: Ke-nya. When you clap "banana", you clap three times: ba-na-na. Every word is made of one or more syllables.

In Arabic, a syllable often starts with a consonant letter and a short vowel mark, such as بَ (ba), تِ (ti) or كُ (ku). When we read, we say each syllable and then join them together into a word. Joining sounds into a whole word is called blending.

Blending is an important reading skill. If you can blend, you can read new words that you have never seen before. This is called decoding, which means working out a word from its letters and marks.

### Clap the syllables
Clap and say the following:
• ba-ba (two claps)
• ka-ta-ba (three claps)
• ma-ka-ta-ba (four claps)

Now say the same words fast and smooth: baba, kataba, makataba. The claps disappear, but the beats are still inside the word.

### Talk about it
Say your name and clap the syllables. How many are there? Compare with your friends.

Read the syllables from right to left: ka, ta, ba. Then blend them: kataba. The Arabic word is كَتَبَ and it means "he wrote".

### Steps for blending
1. Look at the whole word and find its letters.
2. Read each letter with its vowel mark.
3. Say the syllables slowly: ka - ta - ba.
4. Say them again, faster, with no gaps.
5. Check that the word makes sense.

### More words to blend
• kha + ra + ja = kharaja = خَرَجَ = he went out
• dha + ha + ba = dhahaba = ذَهَبَ = he went
• ja + la + sa = jalasa = جَلَسَ = he sat
• fa + ta + Ha = fataHa = فَتَحَ = he opened
• sha + ri + ba = shariba = شَرِبَ = he drank

## PAGE 3: Reading strategies
Good readers use three strategies. Use them together.

### Strategy 1: Decoding
Look at each letter and its mark, say the sound, and blend. For example, in the word سَمَكٌ you see siin with fatHa (sa), miim with fatHa (ma), kaaf with Damma-tanwiin (kun). You blend sa-ma-kun. This is samakun, which means fish.

### Strategy 2: Cues
Cues are clues that help you to guess a word. A picture beside the word is a cue. The sentence around the word is a cue. If the picture shows a book and the word starts with kaaf, the word may be كِتَابٌ (kitaab).

### Strategy 3: Word spacing
In Arabic, the letters inside a word join, and there is a small space between one word and the next. Look for the spaces. They help you to see where each word begins and ends. Some letters, such as alif, daal, dhaal, raa, zaay and waaw, do not join to the letter after them. A small gap after them does not mean a new word.

### Practice in pairs
Read this line slowly with your partner. Use all three strategies.
مَرْحَبًا يَا أَحْمَدُ
The word يَا (yaa) is used to call someone, like "O" or "hey". The line means "Hello, Ahmad."

## PAGE 4: Family words in syllables
Now blend words about the family. The syllables are shown with a hyphen. At the end of each word the small sound -un comes from the tanwiin mark.

### Family words
• a-bun = أَبٌ = father
• um-mun = أُمٌّ = mother
• a-khun = أَخٌ = brother
• ukh-tun = أُخْتٌ = sister
• jad-dun = جَدٌّ = grandfather
• jad-da-tun = جَدَّةٌ = grandmother
• am-mun = عَمٌّ = father's brother
• khaa-lun = خَالٌ = mother's brother
• ib-nun = ابْنٌ = son
• bin-tun = بِنْتٌ = daughter, girl

### Words with shadda
The mark ّ over a letter means the letter is said twice. In um-mun, we say the mim twice. The word breaks into two syllables: um and mun. The same happens in jad-dun and jad-da-tun.

### Read aloud
Take turns to read each word from a family display. Every learner reads three words. When your friend reads, listen and help kindly if the word is hard. Read each word twice, first slowly and then smoothly.

## PAGE 5: Jumbled syllables
A good way to practise blending is to put jumbled syllables in the right order to make a word. Try these. The answers are at the end of the page.

### Puzzle 1: Arrange the syllables
1. tun + ukh
2. da + jad + tun
3. lun + khaa
4. nun + ib
5. bun + a
6. ta + ka + ba

### Answers
1. ukh-tun = أُخْتٌ (sister)
2. jad-da-tun = جَدَّةٌ (grandmother)
3. khaa-lun = خَالٌ (mother's brother)
4. ib-nun = ابْنٌ (son)
5. a-bun = أَبٌ (father)
6. ka-ta-ba = كَتَبَ (he wrote)

### Puzzle 2: Make your own
In pairs, choose three family words. Split each word into syllables and write each syllable on a card. Shuffle the cards. Ask another pair to put them back in order and read the word aloud.

### Tip for solving
Start by looking for the syllable that begins with the letter you hear at the start of the family word. The end syllable often carries the -un sound.

## PAGE 6: Reading sentences about the family
Now use the words in short sentences. Read each sentence, then say its meaning.

### Sentences
• هَذَا أَبِي = haadhaa abii = This is my father.
• هَذِهِ أُمِّي = haadhihi ummii = This is my mother.
• أَخِي صَغِيرٌ = akhii Sagiir = My brother is small.
• أُخْتِي جَمِيلَةٌ = ukhtii jamiila = My sister is beautiful.
• جَدِّي كَبِيرٌ = jaddii kabiir = My grandfather is old (big in age).

The new words are Sagiir (صَغِيرٌ, small), jamiila (جَمِيلَةٌ, beautiful, for a girl or woman) and kabiir (كَبِيرٌ, big or old).

### Read aloud fluently
Read a sentence three times. First point to each word and read slowly. Next read in syllables. Then read the whole sentence smoothly.

### Summary
• A syllable is a beat of sound. Blending joins syllables into a word.
• Strategies: decoding, cues and word spacing.
• Shadda means say the letter twice.
• Practise with family words: ab, umm, akh, ukht, jadd, jadda.

### Common mistakes
• Skipping a syllable when you read fast.
• Forgetting to say a shadda letter twice.
• Reading from left to right.

## QUIZ
1. How many syllables are in the word kataba (ka-ta-ba)?
   A) 2
   B) 3
   C) 4
   D) 1
   Answer: B
   Why: The word is ka + ta + ba, which makes three syllables.
2. What does كَتَبَ mean?
   A) He wrote
   B) He sat
   C) He opened
   D) He drank
   Answer: A
   Why: كَتَبَ (kataba) means he wrote.
3. What does the mark shadda tell you?
   A) The letter is silent
   B) The letter is said twice
   C) The vowel is long
   D) The word is finished
   Answer: B
   Why: A shadda means the letter is doubled, as in um-mun.
4. Which is the correct way to blend the syllables ukh + tun?
   A) tunukh
   B) ukhtun
   C) khtunu
   D) unukht
   Answer: B
   Why: The syllables follow in order: ukh then tun, which makes ukhtun (sister).
5. Which reading strategy uses a picture or the sentence to help you guess a word?
   A) Decoding
   B) Word spacing
   C) Cues
   D) Clapping
   Answer: C
   Why: Cues are clues such as pictures or the words around a word.
6. Blend these syllables and give the meaning: kha + ra + ja. (2 marks)
   Answer: kharaja, which means he went out.
   Why: 1 mark for the blended word and 1 mark for the meaning.
7. Put the syllables in order and write the word with its meaning: da + jad + tun. (3 marks)
   Answer: jad-da-tun (grandmother).
   Why: 1 mark for the correct order, 1 mark for reading the whole word and 1 mark for the meaning.
8. Explain the three steps you would use to read a new Arabic word. (3 marks)
   Answer: Find the letters and marks; read each syllable; blend the syllables and check the word makes sense.
   Why: 1 mark for each correct step.
9. Read this sentence and write its meaning in English: هَذِهِ أُمِّي. (2 marks)
   Answer: haadhihi ummii. This is my mother.
   Why: 1 mark for the correct reading and 1 mark for the meaning.
