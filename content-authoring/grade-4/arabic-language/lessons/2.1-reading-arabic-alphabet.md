## PAGE 1: Meet the Arabic alphabet
The Arabic alphabet has 28 letters. Arabic is written and read from right to left. To read a line, start at the right side and move your eyes to the left. When you open an Arabic book, the front cover is on what looks like the back of an English book.

Arabic letters are different from English letters in some ways.
• There are no capital letters and small letters. Each letter has only one style.
• Most letters join to the letters next to them, so a letter can look a little different at the start, the middle and the end of a word.
• Some letters have dots. The dots matter a lot, because a dot can change one letter into another.
• The letters are mostly consonants. Small marks called harakat are written above or below the letters to show the short vowels.

You may already know some sounds from Kiswahili. Many Kiswahili words came from Arabic. Dhahabu (gold) comes from an Arabic word with the sound dh. Kheri (good fortune) has the kh sound. Ghali (expensive) has gh. Thelathini (thirty) has th. In this lesson you will meet the letters that make those sounds.

### Try it
Hold your finger under the line below and move it from right to left: ب ت ث. Say baa, taa, thaa as you move.

## PAGE 3: Sounds of the letters, part one
Here are the first fourteen letters and their sounds. The English word in brackets helps you to find a similar sound.

### Letters 1 to 14
• alif = ا = a long "aa" sound (as in "father") when it carries a vowel
• baa = ب = b (bag)
• taa = ت = t (tap)
• thaa = ث = th (think)
• jiim = ج = j (jug)
• Haa = ح = a breathy h, made from the middle of the throat
• khaa = خ = kh, like clearing your throat softly; it is like the ch in the Scottish word "loch"
• daal = د = d (dog)
• dhaal = ذ = dh (this)
• raa = ر = r, a quick rolled r
• zaay = ز = z (zebra)
• siin = س = s (sun)
• shiin = ش = sh (shop)
• Saad = ص = a heavy s, said with the tongue low and the mouth round

Notice that pairs like baa, taa and thaa have the same body shape and differ only by their dots. Baa has one dot below. Taa has two dots above. Thaa has three dots above. That is why dots are so important.

### Practice
Say the sounds down the list, then say them from the bottom up. Point to each letter in Figure 1 as you say it.

## PAGE 4: Sounds of the letters, part two
Here are the remaining fourteen letters.

### Letters 15 to 28
• Daad = ض = a heavy d
• Taa = ط = a heavy t
• DHaa = ظ = a heavy dh
• ʿayn = ع = a deep sound made in the throat; there is no English letter like it
• ghayn = غ = gh, like gargling water softly
• faa = ف = f (fan)
• qaaf = ق = q, a deep k made far back in the throat
• kaaf = ك = k (kite)
• laam = ل = l (lamp)
• miim = م = m (mat)
• nuun = ن = n (net)
• haa = ه = a light h (hat)
• waaw = و = w (water) or a long "uu"
• yaa = ي = y (yes) or a long "ii"

The letters Saad, Daad, Taa and DHaa are called heavy letters. Say them with the back of the tongue raised and a full sound. Their light partners are siin, daal, taa and dhaal.

### Hamza
There is one more sign, called hamza (ء). It is a short stop in the throat, like the pause in "uh-oh". It often sits on alif, as in أَنَا (anaa, I) and أَبٌ (ab, father).

### Match the sounds
Your teacher says a sound. Point to the correct letter in Figure 1. Try khaa, shiin, qaaf, ghayn.

## PAGE 5: Short vowels and blending
The letters give the consonant sounds. To read the vowels you need the marks called harakat.

### The four marks
• fatHa = a small slanted line above the letter = short "a"
• kasra = a small slanted line below the letter = short "i"
• Damma = a small curl like a tiny waaw above the letter = short "u"
• sukuun = a small circle above the letter = no vowel

### Blend a letter with a vowel
• بَ = ba
• بِ = bi
• بُ = bu
• بْ = b with no vowel

Do the same with other letters: تَ ta, ثِ thi, جُ ju, دَ da, سِ si, مُ mu.

### Long vowels
The letters alif, waaw and yaa make the vowel long when they follow a matching short vowel.
• بَا = baa
• بُو = buu
• بِي = bii

Now blend digraph-like sounds: ثَا (thaa), قَا (qaa), صَا (Saa), خَا (khaa). Say them in a group. Your teacher points at each syllable and you say it.

### Read a syllable display
مَ مِ مُ (ma, mi, mu)
نَ نِ نُ (na, ni, nu)
لَا لِي لُو (laa, lii, luu)

## PAGE 6: Reading greeting words
Now you can read some greeting words. Read each word slowly, one syllable at a time. Then read the whole word.

### Words to read
• مَرْحَبًا = mar-ha-ban = hello
• أَهْلًا = ah-lan = welcome
• شُكْرًا = shuk-ran = thank you
• عَفْوًا = af-wan = you are welcome
• سَلَامٌ = sa-laa-mun = peace
• صَبَاحٌ = Sa-baa-Hun = morning

The two little strokes at the end of a word, as in مَرْحَبًا and شُكْرًا, are called tanwiin. They make the sound -an at the end of the word. At the end of سَلَامٌ the double damma makes the sound -un.

### Read sentences
Read these sentences on greetings and self-introduction:
• أَنَا أَحْمَدُ = anaa Ahmad = I am Ahmad
• اسْمِي أَمِينَةُ = ismii Aminatu = My name is Amina
• السَّلَامُ عَلَيْكُمْ = as-salaamu ʿalaykum = Peace be upon you

Read them in pairs and take turns. If you make a mistake, go back and read again. Reading again is how everybody improves.

## PAGE 7: Make a letter bank and summary
A letter bank is a collection of the alphabet letters that you make and keep in the classroom. Here is how to make one as a class.

### Steps
1. Give each group four or five letters.
2. Write each letter big and neat on a card.
3. Write its name in English letters below it.
4. Draw or write a picture or a word that starts with the letter, for example baa for بَابٌ (door), shams for شَمْسٌ (sun), and kitaab for كِتَابٌ (book).
5. Arrange all the cards in alphabetical order in the class portfolio.

### Why is Arabic different?
Arabic reads from right to left, has no capital letters, has letters that join, and uses marks for the short vowels. English reads from left to right and uses capital letters. Many sounds of Arabic, like Haa and ʿayn, do not exist in English.

### Summary
• The Arabic alphabet has 28 letters and is read from right to left.
• Dots change letters: baa, taa and thaa differ only by dots.
• The marks fatHa, kasra, Damma and sukuun show short vowels or no vowel.
• Alif, waaw and yaa make long vowels.
• Tanwiin gives the sounds -an, -un, -in at the end of a word.

### Common mistakes
• Reading from left to right.
• Saying heavy letters like light letters.
• Forgetting the dots or putting them in the wrong place.

## QUIZ
1. In which direction is Arabic read?
   A) Left to right
   B) Top to bottom only
   C) Right to left
   D) In a circle
   Answer: C
   Why: Arabic is written and read from right to left.
2. How many letters are in the Arabic alphabet?
   A) 26
   B) 28
   C) 30
   D) 24
   Answer: B
   Why: The Arabic alphabet has 28 letters.
3. Which letter has one dot below it?
   A) ب
   B) ت
   C) ث
   D) خ
   Answer: A
   Why: baa has one dot below. Taa has two dots above and thaa has three dots above.
4. What does the mark kasra tell you?
   A) The sound i
   B) The sound a
   C) The sound u
   D) There is no vowel
   Answer: A
   Why: A kasra is a small line below the letter and gives the short vowel i.
5. How do you say the syllable بُ?
   A) ba
   B) bi
   C) bu
   D) baa
   Answer: C
   Why: The mark Damma gives the short vowel u, so بُ is bu.
6. Say what is different about capital letters in Arabic, and name any four Arabic letters. (2 marks)
   Answer: Arabic has no capital letters. Four letters are, for example, baa, taa, miim and nuun.
   Why: 1 mark for saying Arabic has no capital letters and 1 mark for four correct letter names.
7. Read and write in English letters: أَهْلًا and شُكْرًا. Give the meaning of each. (4 marks)
   Answer: ah-lan (welcome) and shuk-ran (thank you).
   Why: 1 mark for each correct reading and 1 mark for each correct meaning.
8. Explain two ways in which the Arabic alphabet is different from the English alphabet. (2 marks)
   Answer: Arabic is read from right to left, and it has no capital letters. Also letters join, and marks show the short vowels.
   Why: 1 mark for each correct difference.
9. A letter bank has a card for the letter ب. Write what you would put on the card. (3 marks)
   Answer: The letter ب written neatly, its name baa, and a word that starts with it, such as بَابٌ (baab, door).
   Why: 1 mark for the letter, 1 mark for the name, 1 mark for a suitable word.
