## PAGE 1: What is good handwriting?
Handwriting is the way you write with your hand. Good handwriting is neat and legible. Legible means that other people can read it easily. If your writing is clear, your teacher, your friends and your family can understand your ideas without guessing.

Good handwriting in Arabic has some special things to look for. Let us list them.

### Characteristics of good handwriting
• The letters have the correct shape.
• The letters are about the same size.
• The letters sit on the baseline.
• The dots and vowel marks are in the right place and the right number.
• There is a small, even space between words.
• The writing is clean, with no smudges and no untidy crossing out.

### Why neat handwriting matters
Untidy handwriting can change the meaning of words. In Arabic, if you write the dots badly, baa (ب), taa (ت) and thaa (ث) may look the same. A reader might read the wrong word. Neat handwriting also shows respect for the reader.

### Discuss with a partner
Look at a page of your own writing. Circle one letter that you wrote well. Underline one that you could improve. Tell your partner why.

### Get ready to write
Sit up straight, place the book flat, hold the pencil with a relaxed grip and rest your hand on the paper. Write slowly at first. Good writers become fast only after they become neat.

### Size
Letters should be the same height, except for tall letters like alif (ا). Do not write some letters big and others tiny.

### Spacing
Leave a small space between the words, about the width of one letter. Do not leave a big gap between the letters inside a word. Letters that join must be joined smoothly.

### Position
Letters like baa, taa and siin sit on the line. Letters like jiim, Haa and khaa have a part that dips a little below it. Dots and vowel marks sit above or below the letter, not in the middle of the next one.

### Tip
Use a ruled exercise book. If you do not have one, make a light pencil line and write on it.

## PAGE 3: Tracing letters neatly
Tracing helps your hand to remember a shape. Trace each letter with a sharp pencil, following the model line carefully.

### Steps for tracing
1. Look at the whole shape first.
2. Put your pencil at the start of the model on the right.
3. Trace slowly. Keep the pencil on the model.
4. Trace the same letter three times.
5. Now write it without the model.

### Letters of family words
Practise these letters, which you need for the family words:
أ ب ت ج د ر س ع ل م ن خ ة

The last one, ة, is called taa marbuta. It appears only at the end of words such as جَدَّةٌ and is said like a short a or a soft t. Trace each letter and say its name aloud.

### Check your work
After tracing, ask yourself:
• Are my letters the right shape?
• Are they the same size?
• Are they on the line?
• Do my dots look like small, neat points?

### Practise with joining
Write the word أَبٌ (ab). It has two letters, alif and baa. Alif does not join to the next letter, so leave a small space between them and write the baa on the baseline. Write it five times.

### Peer support
Show your writing to a friend. Point to the best word. Kind words help a friend to try again.

## PAGE 4: Copying family words
Now copy the names of family members neatly. Look at each word carefully, then write it.

### Words to copy
• أَبٌ = ab = father
• أُمٌّ = umm = mother
• أَخٌ = akh = brother
• أُخْتٌ = ukht = sister
• جَدٌّ = jadd = grandfather
• جَدَّةٌ = jadda = grandmother
• عَمٌّ = ʿamm = father's brother
• خَالٌ = khaal = mother's brother
• ابْنٌ = ibn = son
• بِنْتٌ = bint = daughter

### A sentence to copy
هَذِهِ أُسْرَتِي = haadhihi usratii = This is my family.

The word أُسْرَتِي contains the word usra (family) and the ending -ii (my).

### How to copy well
1. Say the word aloud.
2. Look at each letter and each mark.
3. Cover the model and write the word once.
4. Uncover and compare.
5. Correct any part that is different and write the word again.

### Common difficulty: the shadda
In أُمٌّ and جَدٌّ, the mark over the last letter looks like a small w. It is the shadda. Write it above the letter, and put the tanwiin mark next to it. Do not forget it, because it tells the reader to say the letter twice.

## PAGE 5: Display and peer review
Displaying your work makes you proud and helps you to learn from others. Choose your best page of family words. Write it on a clean sheet, copy it as neatly as you can, and put your name on the back. Your teacher will help you to display it in the language corner.

### How to review a friend's work
When you look at a friend's display, use the checklist.
1. Are the letters the correct shape?
2. Are the letters the same size?
3. Are they on the line?
4. Are the dots and marks correct?
5. Is the spacing between words even?

### Feedback rules
• Say something good first.
• Give one tip for improvement.
• Use kind words.
• Do not laugh at anyone's work.

### Being open to feedback
When someone gives you a tip, say thank you (shukran, شُكْرًا). Then try again. Everybody can improve their handwriting, even those who already write well.

### Show respect for your work
Keep your books clean. Do not tear pages. Do not scribble. A clean page is easier to read and is a sign of care.

## PAGE 6: A handwriting contest and summary
A handwriting contest is a fun way to practise. It is not about speed. It is about neatness and correct presentation.

### How to run it
1. Choose three family words for everyone to write, for example أَبٌ, أُمٌّ and أُخْتٌ.
2. Give everyone the same time, five minutes.
3. A panel of two learners and the teacher looks at the writing.
4. They give marks for shape, size, spacing, dots and neatness.
5. Everyone gets a certificate of participation, and the best writers are praised.

### Presentation of written work
Neat presentation means: the date and title at the top, letters on the line, no smudges, and clear spacing.

### Summary
• Good handwriting is neat and legible.
• Keep letters the same size and on the baseline.
• Leave small, even spaces between words.
• Trace letters, then copy words, then write from memory.
• Give kind, helpful peer feedback.

### Common mistakes
• Writing letters in different sizes.
• Forgetting the shadda or the dots.
• Leaving large gaps inside a word.
• Rushing and making the writing untidy.

## QUIZ
1. What does legible mean?
   A) Very small
   B) Easy to read
   C) Very fast
   D) Written in colour
   Answer: B
   Why: Legible writing can be read easily by others.
2. On which line should most Arabic letters sit?
   A) The baseline
   B) The margin
   C) The top line only
   D) Below the page
   Answer: A
   Why: Letters rest on the baseline, and only tall letters like alif rise above.
3. Which of these is a characteristic of good handwriting?
   A) Letters of different sizes
   B) Correct dots and even spacing
   C) Smudges on the page
   D) Big gaps inside words
   Answer: B
   Why: Good handwriting has correct shapes, dots and even spacing.
4. Which family word is written أُخْتٌ?
   A) ukht
   B) akh
   C) ab
   D) umm
   Answer: A
   Why: أُخْتٌ is ukht, sister.
5. What does the shadda mark tell the reader?
   A) The letter is said twice
   B) The letter is silent
   C) The vowel is long
   D) The word is finished
   Answer: A
   Why: A shadda doubles the sound of the letter, as in umm.
6. Write أُمٌّ and جَدَّةٌ and give their meanings. (4 marks)
   Answer: أُمٌّ (umm) means mother and جَدَّةٌ (jadda) means grandmother.
   Why: 1 mark for each neat correct word and 1 mark for each meaning.
7. List three characteristics of good handwriting. (3 marks)
   Answer: Neat letters of the correct shape, letters of the same size on the baseline, correct dots and marks, even spacing.
   Why: 1 mark for each valid characteristic.
8. Explain why dots must be written carefully in Arabic. (2 marks)
   Answer: Several letters differ only by their dots, so wrong dots can change the letter and the word.
   Why: 1 mark for saying letters differ only by dots and 1 mark for a change in meaning.
9. Give two rules for giving peer feedback on handwriting. (2 marks)
   Answer: Say something good first, then give one tip for improvement, using kind words.
   Why: 1 mark for each valid rule.
