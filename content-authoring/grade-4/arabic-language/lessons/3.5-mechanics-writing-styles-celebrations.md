## PAGE 1: The art of Arabic writing
Arabic writing is not only a way to record words. It is also an art. For over a thousand years, scribes and artists have written Arabic in many beautiful styles. You can see Arabic calligraphy (beautiful writing) on the walls of mosques, in old books, on coins, on posters and even on modern buildings. Some Arabic calligraphy is also found at the Kenyan coast, on old buildings and doors in places like Lamu and Mombasa.

A writing style is a special way of shaping the letters. The letters remain the same, and the words are the same, but the look is different. It is like the way people have different handwriting in English, or the difference between print and cursive.

In this lesson you will learn the names of some Arabic writing styles, write vocabulary about family celebrations, and write simple sentences correctly. You will also learn how to arrange words in sentences and to fill gaps with the right words.

### What you will learn
• The names and uses of some common Arabic writing styles.
• How to write vocabulary about celebrations neatly.
• How to build simple sentences and check them.
• How to explain what happens at your own family celebration.

### Talk about it
Have you seen Arabic writing on a building, a door, a poster or a book? What did it look like? Tell your partner.

### Warm up
Look at these two writing styles in English: print and cursive. Write your name in both. Which is easier to read? Which is faster to write? Arabic styles also have different purposes.

## PAGE 2: Some Arabic writing styles
There are many Arabic writing styles. Here are five that you should know by name.

### Styles and their uses
• Naskh (النَّسْخ) is a clear and round style. It is used in most printed books, newspapers and school textbooks. It is the easiest style to read and is the one you are learning.
• Ruqʿah (الرُّقْعَة) is a short, quick style, with small, simple shapes. It is used for everyday handwriting, for notes and in many schools.
• Thuluth (الثُّلُث) is a grand style with large, graceful curves. It is often used for titles and for decorations on mosques.
• Kufic (الْكُوفِيّ) is an old, angular style with straight lines. It is used in decoration and on old manuscripts.
• Diwani (الدِّيوَانِي) is a flowing, elegant style, with curves that run together. It was used in official documents in the Ottoman period.

### Which style do we use?
Your school books use Naskh. For your writing in class, you will use a neat, simple hand that looks like Naskh. That is the best style to build a good foundation.

### Different styles, same word
In every style the word stays the same. The letters change their shape a little, but the sounds and the meaning do not change. That is why we can read Arabic in any style once we know the letters.

### Appreciating styles
Look at books, posters and calendars for examples of Arabic writing. Tell your group which style you think it is. Discuss why an artist might choose a grand style for a mosque title and a quick style for notes.

## PAGE 3: Vocabulary for celebrations
Write these words neatly, several times each, so that you know them well. Use neat writing in Naskh style.

### Words to write
• عِيدٌ = ʿiid = festival
• حَفْلَةٌ = hafla = party
• هَدِيَّةٌ = hadiyya = gift
• كَعْكَةٌ = kaʿka = cake
• بَالُونٌ = baaluun = balloon
• شَمْعَةٌ = shamʿa = candle
• طَعَامٌ = taʿaam = food
• عَصِيرٌ = ʿasiir = juice
• صَدِيقٌ = sadiiq = friend
• أُغْنِيَةٌ = ughniya = song

### Think-pair-share
1. Think: Which three words would you use to describe your last family celebration?
2. Pair: Tell your partner.
3. Share: Write your words together on a sheet and read them to another pair.

### Writing tip
When you write a word, say the letters as you write them, from right to left. If you are unsure of a letter, look at the list. After you write, check that every dot and vowel mark is there.

### Add a picture
Beside each word, draw a small picture. This helps you to remember the meaning.

### Sentences to copy
• أَنَا أُحِبُّ الْحَفْلَةَ = anaa uhibbu l-hafla = I like the party.
• عِنْدِي هَدِيَّةٌ = ʿindii hadiyya = I have a gift.
• أُمِّي تَطْبُخُ الطَّعَامَ = ummii tatbukhu t-taʿaam = My mother cooks the food.
• نَحْنُ نَرْقُصُ = nahnu narqusu = We dance.
• الْأَصْدِقَاءُ يَأْكُلُونَ الْكَعْكَةَ = al-asdiqaa'u ya'kuluuna l-kaʿka = The friends eat the cake.

The word nahnu (نَحْنُ) means "we".

### Copy neatly
Copy each sentence twice in your book. Leave a small space between words. Check the marks and read the sentence aloud.

## PAGE 5: Jumbled words and gaps
### Arrange the words
Words in a sentence must come in the right order. Put these jumbled words in order.
1. الْحَفْلَةَ / أُحِبُّ / أَنَا
   Answer: أَنَا أُحِبُّ الْحَفْلَةَ (I like the party)
2. الْكَعْكَةَ / الْأَصْدِقَاءُ / يَأْكُلُونَ
   Answer: الْأَصْدِقَاءُ يَأْكُلُونَ الْكَعْكَةَ (The friends eat the cake)
3. تَطْبُخُ / أُمِّي / الطَّعَامَ
   Answer: أُمِّي تَطْبُخُ الطَّعَامَ (My mother cooks the food)

### Fill the gap
Use the word bank: الْكَعْكَةَ, الْعَصِيرَ, هَدِيَّةٌ.
1. نَأْكُلُ ____ فِي الْحَفْلَةِ. (We eat the ___ at the party.)
2. أَشْرَبُ ____. (I drink the ___.)
3. عِنْدِي ____. (I have a ___.)

Answers: 1 الْكَعْكَةَ, 2 الْعَصِيرَ, 3 هَدِيَّةٌ.

### Check your sentence
After you write, ask yourself:
• Did I write from right to left?
• Are the letters neat and joined?
• Are all the dots and marks in place?
• Does the sentence make sense?

### Work with a partner
Each of you writes one jumbled sentence for the other to solve. Swap and check the answers.

## PAGE 6: Writing about my family celebration
Now write two or three simple sentences about a family celebration you know, using the words and patterns of this lesson.

### Model
هَذِهِ حَفْلَةٌ. عِنْدِي هَدِيَّةٌ. أَنَا أُحِبُّ الْكَعْكَةَ.
haadhihi hafla. ʿindii hadiyya. anaa uhibbu l-kaʿka.
This is a party. I have a gift. I like the cake.

### Steps
1. Choose your celebration: a birthday, a wedding, an Eid or a Christmas celebration.
2. Choose three words from the list.
3. Write a sentence with each word, using the model.
4. Read your sentences to your partner and correct them together.
5. Copy them onto a clean page with neat handwriting.

### Explaining activities
Think about what people do at your celebration. They eat, drink, sing, dance and give gifts. Write one sentence about an activity. For example: نَحْنُ نَرْقُصُ (We dance).

### Summary
• Arabic writing styles include Naskh, Ruqʿah, Thuluth, Kufic and Diwani.
• We use a neat, simple Naskh-like style at school.
• A simple sentence tells who and what.
• Arrange jumbled words, fill gaps and check your sentences.
• Celebrations bring people together and are part of our culture.

### Common mistakes
• Mixing up the names of the styles.
• Putting the sentence words in the wrong order.
• Forgetting the marks and dots when copying.

## QUIZ
1. Which Arabic writing style is used in most printed books and newspapers?
   A) Naskh
   B) Kufic
   C) Diwani
   D) Thuluth
   Answer: A
   Why: Naskh is clear and round and is the style of most printed books.
2. Which style is quick and simple and is used for everyday handwriting?
   A) Thuluth
   B) Ruqʿah
   C) Kufic
   D) Diwani
   Answer: B
   Why: Ruqʿah is a short, quick style for notes and everyday writing.
3. What does nahnu narqusu mean?
   A) I like the party.
   B) We dance.
   C) I have a gift.
   D) My mother cooks.
   Answer: B
   Why: nahnu means we and narqusu means we dance.
4. Which sentence means "I have a gift"?
   A) عِنْدِي هَدِيَّةٌ
   B) أَنَا أُحِبُّ الْحَفْلَةَ
   C) نَحْنُ نَرْقُصُ
   D) أَشْرَبُ الْعَصِيرَ
   Answer: A
   Why: ʿindii means "I have" and hadiyya means gift.
5. Which word fills the gap: أَشْرَبُ ____ (I drink the juice)?
   A) الْكَعْكَةَ
   B) الْعَصِيرَ
   C) الْحَفْلَةَ
   D) بَالُونٌ
   Answer: B
   Why: We drink juice (ʿasiir), not cake, party or balloon.
6. Put these words in order and translate: الْحَفْلَةَ / أُحِبُّ / أَنَا. (3 marks)
   Answer: أَنَا أُحِبُّ الْحَفْلَةَ. I like the party.
   Why: 1 mark for the first word, 1 mark for the correct order and 1 mark for the translation.
7. Name three Arabic writing styles and give one use of one of them. (3 marks)
   Answer: For example Naskh, Ruqʿah and Kufic. Naskh is used in printed books.
   Why: 1 mark for each style named (up to 2) and 1 mark for a correct use.
8. Write two Arabic celebration words with their meanings. (2 marks)
   Answer: For example hadiyya (gift) and kaʿka (cake).
   Why: 1 mark for each correct word with meaning.
9. Write two sentences in Arabic sounds about a celebration and translate them. (4 marks)
   Answer: For example haadhihi hafla. This is a party. anaa uhibbu l-kaʿka. I like the cake.
   Why: 1 mark for each correct sentence and 1 mark for each correct translation.
