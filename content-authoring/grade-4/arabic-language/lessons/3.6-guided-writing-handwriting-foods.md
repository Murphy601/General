## PAGE 1: Writing about foods and drinks
In this lesson you will write about foods and drinks in neat Arabic handwriting. You will name common foods in your area, draw and label them, and copy simple sentences with correct word spacing and correct shaping of letters.

Shaping of characters means making each letter in its correct shape. Every letter has a shape that readers know. If you change the shape, the reader may read a different letter. Word spacing means the small space between words. If words are too close, they look like one long word. If they are too far apart, the sentence looks broken.

Foods and drinks are a good theme for practice because you already know many words: bread, rice, milk, water, fish, eggs, bananas and more. You also know how to say "I like", "I eat" and "I drink".

### What you will do
• List the names of foods and drinks in your locality.
• Draw and label foods on a chart.
• Copy sentences with good spacing and shape.
• Rearrange jumbled words to make a sentence.
• Share your work on the class wall.

### Talk about it
Which foods and drinks are common where you live? Tell your partner three of them and say which is your favourite. Then look at the Arabic words on the next page.

### Warm up
Air-write in the air with your finger the word خُبْزٌ (khubz, bread), starting from the right. Say each letter name as you write: khaa, baa, zaay.

## PAGE 2: Foods and drinks vocabulary
Read these words aloud and then write them neatly. Notice how each word begins on the right side.

### Foods
• خُبْزٌ = khubz = bread
• أَرُزٌّ = aruzz = rice
• ذُرَةٌ = dhura = maize
• سَمَكٌ = samak = fish
• لَحْمٌ = lahm = meat
• بَيْضٌ = bayd = eggs
• مَوْزٌ = mawz = bananas
• بُرْتُقَالٌ = burtuqaal = oranges

### Drinks
• مَاءٌ = maa' = water
• حَلِيبٌ = haliib = milk
• شَايٌ = shaay = tea
• عَصِيرٌ = ʿasiir = juice

### Write the words
Write each word three times. After each line, check the shape of every letter against the printed word. Are the dots right? Are the vowel marks right? Is the spacing even?

### Choose foods from your county
Foods vary from place to place. Some counties eat a lot of maize, others eat more rice or fish. Say which foods from this list are common where you live.

### Tip
Write slowly. It is better to write four words carefully than ten words carelessly.

### Steps
1. Choose four or five foods or drinks from your county.
2. Draw each one on a chart, big enough to be seen from the back of the room.
3. Write its Arabic name neatly under each picture, from right to left.
4. Write the English or Kiswahili name in small letters beside it.
5. Colour the drawings and decorate the chart.
6. Post it on the class wall for peer review.

### Peer review of the chart
Walk around the room. Read each chart aloud. Give a compliment and one suggestion. Check the spelling against the vocabulary list.

## PAGE 4: Good word spacing and letter shapes
### Word spacing rules
• Leave a gap about as wide as one letter between words.
• Do not put gaps inside a word between letters that join.
• After letters that do not join (alif, daal, dhaal, raa, zaay, waaw), there is a natural small gap in the word. Keep it small so that it is not mistaken for a space between words.
• Start a new word from the right side after the space.

### Shaping letters
• Keep the letters in a word joined smoothly on the baseline.
• Write the tall letters (alif and laam) straight and tall.
• Write the letters with a curve, such as siin and miim, with a smooth curve, not a sharp corner.
• Put the dots and vowel marks above or below the right letter.

### Compare and correct
Look at your own line of writing. Circle a word that was too close to the next word. Circle a letter whose shape was not clear. Rewrite these two carefully. Show your teacher.

### Self-check checklist
• Are all my letters the correct shape?
• Are the spaces between words even?
• Are my dots and marks correct?
• Is my writing on the baseline?

## PAGE 5: Copying and rearranging sentences
### Sentences to copy
Copy these sentences neatly. Watch the spacing and the letter shapes.

• أُحِبُّ الْخُبْزَ = uhibbu l-khubza = I like bread.
• آكُلُ الْأَرُزَّ = aakulu l-aruzza = I eat rice.
• أَشْرَبُ الْحَلِيبَ = ashrabu l-haliiba = I drink milk.
• أَشْرَبُ الشَّايَ = ashrabu sh-shaaya = I drink tea.
• الْمَوْزُ لَذِيذٌ = al-mawzu ladhiidh = The banana is delicious.

### Steps for copying
1. Read the sentence twice, aloud.
2. Look at the first word and write it from the right.
3. Leave a small space and write the next word.
4. Check every word with the printed sentence.
5. Read what you wrote.

### Rearrange the jumbled words
Put the words in the right order to make a sentence.
1. الْخُبْزَ / آكُلُ
   Answer: آكُلُ الْخُبْزَ (I eat bread.)
2. الْعَصِيرَ / أَشْرَبُ
   Answer: أَشْرَبُ الْعَصِيرَ (I drink juice.)
3. لَذِيذٌ / السَّمَكُ
   Answer: السَّمَكُ لَذِيذٌ (The fish is delicious.)

### Share
Write your best sentences on a clean strip of paper. Stick them on the class talking wall. Read your friends' sentences and say what you like about them.

## PAGE 6: Writing about food and summary
Food is a gift. It gives us strength to learn and play. Write two or three sentences about your favourite food or drink. Use the models above and change the food.

### Model paragraph
أُحِبُّ الْخُبْزَ. أُحِبُّ الْحَلِيبَ. الْمَوْزُ لَذِيذٌ.
uhibbu l-khubza. uhibbu l-haliiba. al-mawzu ladhiidh.
I like bread. I like milk. The banana is delicious.

### Food in our country
Kenya grows many foods, like maize, beans, bananas, tea and coffee. Farmers work hard to grow them. We should not waste food. Eat what you take and be thankful.

### Summary
• Shaping of characters means writing each letter in its known shape.
• Leave one letter's width between words. Do not leave gaps inside a word.
• Keep letters on the baseline, and write dots and marks neatly.
• Copy sentences carefully and check them against the model.
• Displaying your work helps others and you to learn.

### Common mistakes
• Words too close together, so the sentence is hard to read.
• Wide gaps between the letters of one word.
• Writing dots too far from their letters.

## QUIZ
1. What does the word خُبْزٌ mean?
   A) Bread
   B) Rice
   C) Fish
   D) Milk
   Answer: A
   Why: خُبْزٌ (khubz) means bread.
2. What is meant by word spacing?
   A) The small space left between words
   B) The height of the letters
   C) The colour of the ink
   D) The number of dots
   Answer: A
   Why: Word spacing is the neat space between one word and the next.
3. Which sentence means "I drink milk"?
   A) آكُلُ الْأَرُزَّ
   B) أَشْرَبُ الْحَلِيبَ
   C) أُحِبُّ الْخُبْزَ
   D) السَّمَكُ لَذِيذٌ
   Answer: B
   Why: ashrabu means I drink and al-haliib means the milk.
4. Which of these is a fault in handwriting?
   A) Even spaces between words
   B) Letters on the baseline
   C) Wide gaps inside a word
   D) Correct dots
   Answer: C
   Why: Wide gaps inside a word make it look like several words.
5. Which choice is the complete sentence "I eat bread" in the word order taught in this lesson?
   A) الْخُبْزَ آكُلُ
   B) آكُلُ الْخُبْزَ
   C) آكُلُ
   D) الْخُبْزَ
   Answer: B
   Why: In the pattern taught here the verb aakulu (I eat) comes first, then al-khubza (the bread). C and D are only single words.
6. Write four foods or drinks in Arabic sounds with their meanings. (4 marks)
   Answer: For example khubz (bread), aruzz (rice), maa' (water), haliib (milk).
   Why: 1 mark for each correct word with meaning.
7. Rearrange the words and translate: الْعَصِيرَ / أَشْرَبُ. (2 marks)
   Answer: أَشْرَبُ الْعَصِيرَ, I drink juice.
   Why: 1 mark for the correct order and 1 mark for the translation.
8. Give three tips for neat handwriting with correct word spacing. (3 marks)
   Answer: Leave a gap about one letter wide between words, keep letters of a word joined smoothly, write on the baseline and place dots carefully.
   Why: 1 mark for each valid tip.
9. Describe how to make and use a food chart in the classroom. (3 marks)
   Answer: Draw foods, write each Arabic name under its picture in neat letters, colour it and post it on the wall for others to read and review.
   Why: 1 mark for drawing, 1 mark for labelling and 1 mark for posting or reviewing.
