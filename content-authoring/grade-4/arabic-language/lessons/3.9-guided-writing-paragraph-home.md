## PAGE 1: From words to sentences to a paragraph
You have learnt to write letters, then words, then short sentences. The next step is to put sentences together to make a paragraph. A paragraph is a group of sentences about one idea. When the sentences are in a good order and fit together, we say the paragraph is coherent. Coherent means it holds together and makes sense.

Think of building a wall. A single brick is a word. A row of bricks is a sentence. Many rows joined together make a wall, which is the paragraph. If the bricks are in the wrong places, the wall falls down. If the sentences are in the wrong order, the paragraph does not make sense.

In this lesson you will write simple sentences about household items in the right word order. You will then join them to make a short paragraph. You will also fix a jumbled paragraph by putting the sentences in a good order.

### A good paragraph
• Starts with an introduction that tells the topic.
• Has a middle with two or three details.
• Ends with a final sentence, such as a feeling or a conclusion.
• Uses words that connect ideas, such as "and".

### Why does coherence matter?
If a paragraph jumps from one idea to another, the reader gets lost. A coherent paragraph is like a clear road, where the reader knows where to go next.

### Talk about it
Tell your partner about your bedroom in three sentences. Does your partner understand where you began, what you described and how you ended?

## PAGE 2: Sentences in the right order
Sentences about household items follow simple patterns. Learn them well.

### Pattern 1: This is my ...
هَذَا بَيْتِي = haadhaa baytii = This is my house.
هَذِهِ غُرْفَتِي = haadhihi ghurfatii = This is my room.

### Pattern 2: The item is (describing word)
الْبَيْتُ كَبِيرٌ = al-baytu kabiir = The house is big.
الطَّاوِلَةُ صَغِيرَةٌ = at-taawilatu Sagiira = The table is small.

### Pattern 3: In the place there is an item
فِي الْمَطْبَخِ طَاوِلَةٌ = fii l-matbakhi taawila = In the kitchen there is a table.
فِي الْغُرْفَةِ سَرِيرٌ = fii l-ghurfati sariir = In the room there is a bed.

### Pattern 4: The item is (position)
الْكِتَابُ عَلَى الطَّاوِلَةِ = al-kitaabu ʿalaa t-taawila = The book is on the table.
الْقَلَمُ فِي الْحَقِيبَةِ = al-qalamu fii l-haqiiba = The pen is in the bag.

### Pattern 5: I like ...
أُحِبُّ بَيْتِي = uhibbu baytii = I like my house.

### Notice the order
In Patterns 2 and 4 the item comes first and the describing word or position comes after it. In Pattern 3, the place comes first, and the item follows.

## PAGE 3: Ordering the words
Sometimes the words of a sentence are mixed up. To fix them, ask: what is the sentence about, and what does it say about it? Put the topic first (the item), then the description or position.

### Jumbled words: put them in order
1. عَلَى / الْكِتَابُ / الطَّاوِلَةِ
   Answer: الْكِتَابُ عَلَى الطَّاوِلَةِ (The book is on the table.)
2. فِي / الْقَلَمُ / الْحَقِيبَةِ
   Answer: الْقَلَمُ فِي الْحَقِيبَةِ (The pen is in the bag.)
3. كَبِيرٌ / بَيْتِي
   Answer: بَيْتِي كَبِيرٌ (My house is big.)
4. طَاوِلَةٌ / فِي / الْمَطْبَخِ
   Answer: فِي الْمَطْبَخِ طَاوِلَةٌ (In the kitchen there is a table.)

### How to solve
1. Find the word that names the item, for example الْكِتَابُ.
2. Find the position word, such as عَلَى or فِي.
3. Put them together in the pattern.
4. Read aloud to check that it makes sense.

### Work in pairs
Each of you writes one jumbled sentence. Swap and solve. Check each other's answer.

### Writing tip
Say the sentence aloud before you write it. If it sounds right, write it. Then read it again.

### The paragraph in English letters
haadhaa baytii. baytii kabiir. fii baytii ghurfatu nawmin wa matbakhun wa hammaam. fii l-matbakhi taawila. uhibbu baytii.

### In English
This is my house. My house is big. In my house there is a bedroom, a kitchen and a bathroom. In the kitchen there is a table. I like my house.

### Parts
• Beginning: says the topic.
• Middle: gives details.
• End: says how the writer feels.

### Copy the paragraph
Copy the whole paragraph neatly. Leave the spaces between words. Begin from the right of each line. Read it aloud.

## PAGE 5: Rearranging a jumbled paragraph
A jumbled paragraph has all its sentences, but in the wrong order. Your job is to put them in a good order so that it makes sense.

### Jumbled sentences
A. أُحِبُّ غُرْفَتِي.
B. الْكِتَابُ عَلَى الطَّاوِلَةِ.
C. هَذِهِ غُرْفَتِي.
D. فِي الْغُرْفَةِ سَرِيرٌ وَطَاوِلَةٌ.

### Clues
• Which sentence tells the topic? C, "This is my room."
• Which sentences give details? D and B.
• Which sentence tells the feeling and ends the paragraph? A.
• Which detail should come first, the general one or the specific one? D is general, B is more specific.

### A good order
C, D, B, A:
هَذِهِ غُرْفَتِي. فِي الْغُرْفَةِ سَرِيرٌ وَطَاوِلَةٌ. الْكِتَابُ عَلَى الطَّاوِلَةِ. أُحِبُّ غُرْفَتِي.

This is my room. In the room there is a bed and a table. The book is on the table. I like my room.

### Why this order works
The paragraph begins with the topic, moves from general details to specific details, and ends with a feeling. Each sentence follows the one before it.

### Work in groups
Write each sentence on a separate strip. Mix the strips. Another group puts them in order and explains why. There may be more than one good order, but the paragraph must still make sense.

## PAGE 6: Write your own paragraph
Now write your own paragraph about a room or your house, using a display of pictures for ideas.

### Steps
1. Think: choose the room and three items.
2. Pair: tell your partner your ideas.
3. Share: write your paragraph, starting with هَذَا or هَذِهِ.
4. Add two or three detail sentences using fii, ʿalaa or a describing word.
5. End with أُحِبُّ ...
6. Read it aloud and check it.

### Checklist
• Did I write from right to left?
• Does the paragraph have a beginning, a middle and an end?
• Do the describing words match the items?
• Are the spelling, dots and marks correct?
• Is it neat?

### Keep household items safe
In your final sentence or in a separate line, you may say how to care for your things, in English if you cannot write it in Arabic. For example, "Keep the key in a safe place." Keeping items safe helps everyone in the home.

### Summary
• A paragraph is a group of sentences about one idea.
• A coherent paragraph has a beginning, a middle and an end.
• Use patterns: haadhaa baytii, al-baytu kabiir, fii l-matbakhi taawila, uhibbu baytii.
• Fix jumbled words and jumbled sentences by finding the topic first.
• Check your work for meaning, spelling and neatness.

### Common mistakes
• Putting the ending sentence first.
• Writing sentences that do not belong to the topic.
• Forgetting to make the describing word match the item.

## QUIZ
1. What is a paragraph?
   A) A group of sentences about one idea
   B) One long word
   C) A drawing
   D) A single letter
   Answer: A
   Why: A paragraph is a group of related sentences about one idea.
2. Which sentence would be the best beginning of a paragraph about a house?
   A) أُحِبُّ بَيْتِي
   B) هَذَا بَيْتِي
   C) فِي الْمَطْبَخِ طَاوِلَةٌ
   D) الْكِتَابُ عَلَى الطَّاوِلَةِ
   Answer: B
   Why: هَذَا بَيْتِي (This is my house) introduces the topic.
3. Which sentence is correctly arranged from the jumbled words فِي / الْقَلَمُ / الْحَقِيبَةِ?
   A) الْقَلَمُ فِي الْحَقِيبَةِ
   B) فِي الْقَلَمُ الْحَقِيبَةِ
   C) الْحَقِيبَةِ الْقَلَمُ فِي
   D) فِي الْحَقِيبَةِ الْقَلَمُ
   Answer: A
   Why: The item comes first, then the position word and the place: The pen is in the bag.
4. What does fii l-matbakhi taawila mean?
   A) In the kitchen there is a table.
   B) The book is on the table.
   C) I like the kitchen.
   D) The table is small.
   Answer: A
   Why: fii l-matbakhi means in the kitchen and taawila means a table.
5. Which sentence is a good ending for a paragraph about your room?
   A) أُحِبُّ غُرْفَتِي
   B) هَذِهِ غُرْفَتِي
   C) فِي الْغُرْفَةِ سَرِيرٌ
   D) الْكِتَابُ عَلَى الطَّاوِلَةِ
   Answer: A
   Why: أُحِبُّ غُرْفَتِي (I like my room) is a closing feeling that finishes the paragraph.
6. Put the words in order and translate: كَبِيرٌ / بَيْتِي. (2 marks)
   Answer: بَيْتِي كَبِيرٌ, My house is big.
   Why: 1 mark for the correct order and 1 mark for the translation.
7. Write three simple Arabic sentences about your room in sounds (English letters) with meanings. (3 marks)
   Answer: For example haadhihi ghurfatii (This is my room). fii l-ghurfati sariir (In the room there is a bed). uhibbu ghurfatii (I like my room).
   Why: 1 mark for each correct sentence with its meaning.
8. Explain what makes a paragraph coherent. (2 marks)
   Answer: The sentences are about one topic and are in a good order, so they fit together and make sense.
   Why: 1 mark for one topic and 1 mark for a logical order.
9. Arrange these sentences into a good paragraph and explain your order: uhibbu baytii / haadhaa baytii / baytii kabiir. (3 marks)
   Answer: haadhaa baytii. baytii kabiir. uhibbu baytii. The topic comes first, then a detail, then the feeling at the end.
   Why: 1 mark for the first sentence, 1 mark for the correct order overall and 1 mark for the explanation.
