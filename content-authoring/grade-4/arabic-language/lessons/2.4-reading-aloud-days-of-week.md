## PAGE 1: Reading the days
You learnt to say the days of the week in Arabic. Now you will read them in Arabic script. Reading accurately means reading each word correctly. Reading with fluency means reading smoothly, with a steady speed. Both are needed for a good reader.

Here are the seven days in the order Arabic speakers usually list them, beginning with Sunday.

الْأَحَدُ • الِاثْنَيْنِ • الثُّلَاثَاءُ • الْأَرْبِعَاءُ • الْخَمِيسُ • الْجُمُعَةُ • السَّبْتُ

Read them in order. Each has the word al- (the) at the start. The l of al- is not pronounced in some of them, and we say ath-thulaathaa' and as-sabt.

### The days in English letters
al-ahad, al-ithnayn, ath-thulaathaa', al-arbiʿaa', al-khamiis, al-jumuʿa, as-sabt

### Read in a chain
Read the seven days in a chain, one learner after another. Each learner reads one day. When you reach the end, start again with a new first reader. Try to keep the speed steady.

### Tip
Put your finger under each word and move it from right to left as you read. Do not go so fast that you skip a word, and do not go so slow that the words sound broken.

## PAGE 2: What the names mean
Knowing the meaning of words helps you to remember them. The first five day names in Arabic are related to numbers.

### Number clues
• al-ahad comes from a word for "one". It is the first day.
• al-ithnayn comes from a word for "two". It is the second day.
• ath-thulaathaa' comes from a word for "three". It is the third day.
• al-arbiʿaa' comes from a word for "four". It is the fourth day.
• al-khamiis comes from a word for "five". It is the fifth day.
• al-jumuʿa comes from a word meaning "gathering". Friday is the day when many Muslims gather for the special midday prayer.

The Arabic numbers are wahid (وَاحِدٌ, one), ithnaan (اثْنَانِ, two), thalaatha (ثَلَاثَةٌ, three), arbaʿa (أَرْبَعَةٌ, four) and khamsa (خَمْسَةٌ, five). You can hear how the days are related.

### Other useful words
• yawm = يَوْمٌ = day
• usbuuʿ = أُسْبُوعٌ = week
• al-yawm = الْيَوْمَ = today
• ghadan = غَدًا = tomorrow
• amsi = أَمْسِ = yesterday

Read each word and say its meaning aloud. Then use one in a sentence such as al-yawmu yawmu l-ithnayn (الْيَوْمُ يَوْمُ الِاثْنَيْنِ), "Today is Monday."

## PAGE 3: Listening at different paces
Fluent reading needs a good pace. Pace is the speed at which we read. Listen to your teacher or to an audio recording of the days read at three paces.

### Three paces
• Slow: for learning. Each syllable is clear.
• Normal: for reading to others. Words flow together.
• Fast: for expert readers. It must still be accurate.

### Practice
1. Listen to the slow reading. Repeat after it.
2. Listen to the normal reading. Repeat after it.
3. Try reading at normal speed on your own.

### Common problems
• Rushing and missing sounds.
• Stopping in the middle of a word.
• Reading each day in a different voice or speed.

### Training your eyes
Fluent readers look ahead at the next word while they say the current one. Try to look at the second day while you say the first day. It becomes easier with practice.

### Pairs
Read the seven days to a partner. Your partner listens and says "slow", "just right" or "too fast". Adjust your speed and read again.

## PAGE 4: Matching activities to days
Now read short sentences about what people do on different days. Read each sentence and say its meaning.

### Sentences
• أَذْهَبُ إِلَى الْمَدْرَسَةِ يَوْمَ الِاثْنَيْنِ = adhhabu ilaa l-madrasa yawma l-ithnayn = I go to school on Monday.
• أَقْرَأُ كِتَابًا يَوْمَ الْأَحَدِ = aqraʾu kitaaban yawma l-ahad = I read a book on Sunday.
• أَزُورُ جَدَّتِي يَوْمَ الْجُمُعَةِ = azuuru jaddatii yawma l-jumuʿa = I visit my grandmother on Friday.
• أُسَاعِدُ أُمِّي يَوْمَ السَّبْتِ = usaaʿidu ummii yawma s-sabt = I help my mother on Saturday.
• أَلْعَبُ يَوْمَ السَّبْتِ = alʿabu yawma s-sabt = I play on Saturday.

### New words
• madrasa = مَدْرَسَةٌ = school
• aqraʾu = أَقْرَأُ = I read
• azuuru = أَزُورُ = I visit
• usaaʿidu = أُسَاعِدُ = I help
• alʿabu = أَلْعَبُ = I play

### Match the activity to the day
Match each activity with its day, using the sentences above.
1. Visiting grandmother: Friday
2. Going to school: Monday
3. Reading a book: Sunday
4. Helping mother: Saturday

### Your own week
Say what you do on two days of your own week. You may use the sentences above as a model and change the day or the activity.

## PAGE 5: Flashcards and a reading contest
Flashcards help you to remember words. Make one card for each day.

### How to make flashcards
1. Cut seven cards of the same size.
2. On the front, write the Arabic day name in large, neat letters.
3. On the back, write the English name and the sound in English letters.
4. Decorate the card with a picture of an activity for that day.
5. Display the cards on the wall of the class in the right order.

### Use the cards
• Show a card to your partner. Your partner reads the day.
• Mix the cards. Put them in order and read each aloud.
• Hide one card. Ask your partner which day is missing.

### Reading contest
A reading contest is a friendly competition for fluency. Teams take turns to read a list of days and sentences.
• A judge listens for accuracy, pace and clear pronunciation.
• Each team scores points for each correct word and smooth reading.
• All teams help each other to improve.

The winning team is not only the fastest. It is the team that reads correctly, smoothly and with respect for the other teams.

## PAGE 6: Reading for pleasure and summary
Reading is a joy. Read the days on a calendar at home. Read the Arabic names on a calendar or on a poster. Each time you read, you get better. Reading for pleasure makes you a strong reader for life.

### Give each other time
When you match activities and read in groups, give every learner enough time to think and read. Being patient with each other is a sign of kindness.

### Summary
• Read Arabic from right to left, with a finger under each word.
• The days: al-ahad, al-ithnayn, ath-thulaathaa', al-arbiʿaa', al-khamiis, al-jumuʿa, as-sabt.
• The first five names come from the numbers one to five.
• Practise at three paces: slow, normal, fast, and stay accurate.
• Use yawm before a day name to say "on that day".
• Flashcards and contests help us to practise.

### Common mistakes
• Mixing up al-arbiʿaa' (Wednesday) with al-ithnayn (Monday).
• Reading too fast and missing sounds.
• Forgetting that the week begins on Sunday in the Arabic list.

## QUIZ
1. Which day comes from the Arabic word for "one"?
   A) al-jumuʿa
   B) al-ahad
   C) as-sabt
   D) al-khamiis
   Answer: B
   Why: al-ahad comes from a word meaning one and is the first day of the week.
2. Which day is الْخَمِيسُ?
   A) Tuesday
   B) Wednesday
   C) Thursday
   D) Saturday
   Answer: C
   Why: al-khamiis is Thursday, the fifth day.
3. What does yawm mean?
   A) Week
   B) Day
   C) School
   D) Tomorrow
   Answer: B
   Why: يَوْمٌ (yawm) means day. The word for week is usbuuʿ.
4. Which word means "today"?
   A) غَدًا
   B) أَمْسِ
   C) الْيَوْمَ
   D) أُسْبُوعٌ
   Answer: C
   Why: الْيَوْمَ (al-yawm) means today. غَدًا is tomorrow and أَمْسِ is yesterday.
5. What does azuuru jaddatii yawma l-jumuʿa mean?
   A) I play on Friday.
   B) I visit my grandmother on Friday.
   C) I go to school on Friday.
   D) I read a book on Friday.
   Answer: B
   Why: azuuru means I visit, jaddatii means my grandmother and yawma l-jumuʿa means on Friday.
6. Write the seven days in Arabic sounds, in order. (4 marks)
   Answer: al-ahad, al-ithnayn, ath-thulaathaa', al-arbiʿaa', al-khamiis, al-jumuʿa, as-sabt.
   Why: 4 marks for all seven in order; 3 marks for five or six; 2 marks for three or four; 1 mark for one or two.
7. Explain what "reading with fluency" means. (2 marks)
   Answer: It means reading smoothly at a steady speed, with correct words and few stops.
   Why: 1 mark for smoothly or steady speed and 1 mark for accuracy.
8. Match these activities to the days in the sentences of this lesson: going to school, reading a book, visiting a grandmother. (3 marks)
   Answer: Going to school: Monday. Reading a book: Sunday. Visiting a grandmother: Friday.
   Why: 1 mark for each correct match.
9. Describe how you can make and use flashcards to learn the days. (3 marks)
   Answer: Write the Arabic day on the front and the English and sound on the back; use the cards to read, put them in order and to test a partner.
   Why: 1 mark for making the cards, 1 mark for reading or ordering them and 1 mark for testing a partner.
