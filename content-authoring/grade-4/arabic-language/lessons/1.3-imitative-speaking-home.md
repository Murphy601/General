## PAGE 1: My home
Your home is the place where you eat, sleep, study and play. Some homes are built of stone, some of timber and iron sheets, and some of mud with a thatched roof. Every home has rooms or spaces and many useful things inside it.

In imitative speaking we listen carefully and copy what we hear. Your teacher says a word, and you repeat it with the same sounds, the same speed and the same tone. Copying well is how babies learn to speak, and it is also the best way to start a new language.

In this lesson you will learn Arabic words for rooms and household items. You will also learn small words called prepositions that tell us where something is. English prepositions are words like in, on, at, under and above. Arabic has its own words for them.

### Warm up
Look around your classroom or home. Point to a door, a window and a table. Say their names in Kiswahili and English. Soon you will say them in Arabic too.

### Copying rules
• Listen first with your eyes on the speaker.
• Repeat the word exactly.
• Say it three times: slowly, normally, then proudly.
• Help a friend if they make a mistake, and do it kindly.

## PAGE 2: Rooms of the house
Here are the Arabic words for the house and its parts. Copy each word after your teacher.

### Rooms and places
• bayt = بَيْتٌ = house
• ghurfa = غُرْفَةٌ = room
• ghurfatu n-nawm = غُرْفَةُ النَّوْمِ = bedroom
• ghurfatu l-juluus = غُرْفَةُ الْجُلُوسِ = sitting room
• matbakh = مَطْبَخٌ = kitchen
• hammaam = حَمَّامٌ = bathroom
• hadiiqa = حَدِيقَةٌ = garden
• baab = بَابٌ = door
• naafidha = نَافِذَةٌ = window

The word ghurfatu n-nawm is made from two words: ghurfa (room) and nawm (sleep). It means "the room of sleep", which is a bedroom.

### Say it in a sentence
haadhaa baytii (هَذَا بَيْتِي) means "This is my house." Notice that -ii again means "my".

### Activity
Sit in pairs. One of you points to a picture of a room or item. The other says the Arabic word. Take turns and count how many you know without help.

## PAGE 3: Household items
Now learn the names of some things found in a home.

### Things at home
• kursii = كُرْسِيٌّ = chair
• taawila = طَاوِلَةٌ = table
• sariir = سَرِيرٌ = bed
• kitaab = كِتَابٌ = book
• qalam = قَلَمٌ = pen
• misbaah = مِصْبَاحٌ = lamp
• saaʿa = سَاعَةٌ = clock
• khizaana = خِزَانَةٌ = cupboard
• kuub = كُوبٌ = cup
• sahn = صَحْنٌ = plate
• milʿaqa = مِلْعَقَةٌ = spoon
• miftaah = مِفْتَاحٌ = key

### The word "the"
In Arabic, the word for "the" is al- and it joins the front of a word. So kitaab (a book) becomes al-kitaab (the book). With some letters the l is not heard. We say at-taawila, not al-taawila, and as-sariir, not al-sariir. Just copy your teacher: at-taawila, as-sariir.

### Uses of household items
Talk with a partner. What do we use a cup for? A key? A lamp? Give your answers in English or Kiswahili and then say the Arabic word.

## PAGE 4: Where is it? Prepositions
A preposition tells us the position of one thing in relation to another. Look at the words below.

### Position words
• fii = فِي = in
• ʿalaa = عَلَى = on
• taHta = تَحْتَ = under
• fawqa = فَوْقَ = above
• ʿinda = عِنْدَ = at (near, by)
• amaama = أَمَامَ = in front of
• khalfa = خَلْفَ = behind
• bijaanibi = بِجَانِبِ = beside

## PAGE 5: Saying where things are
Now put a noun and a preposition together. To ask where something is, use ayna (أَيْنَ), which means "where". Ayna l-kitaab? (أَيْنَ الْكِتَابُ؟) means "Where is the book?"

### Model sentences
• al-kitaabu ʿalaa t-taawila = الْكِتَابُ عَلَى الطَّاوِلَةِ = The book is on the table.
• al-misbaahu fawqa t-taawila = الْمِصْبَاحُ فَوْقَ الطَّاوِلَةِ = The lamp is above the table.
• al-qittatu taHta s-sariir = الْقِطَّةُ تَحْتَ السَّرِيرِ = The cat is under the bed.
• al-qalamu fii l-haqiiba = الْقَلَمُ فِي الْحَقِيبَةِ = The pen is in the bag.
• anaa ʿinda l-baab = أَنَا عِنْدَ الْبَابِ = I am at the door.

New words here: qitta (قِطَّةٌ) is a cat and haqiiba (حَقِيبَةٌ) is a bag. The endings you hear (-u, -i) change slightly with the word's job in the sentence. At this level, copy what your teacher says.

### Pair game
Hide a small object such as a pencil. One learner asks ayna l-qalam? The other answers, for example al-qalamu taHta l-kursii. Take turns hiding and finding.

## PAGE 6: Care for household items and summary
Household items cost money and work. We must look after them so that they last. Wash cups and plates after eating, put books back on the shelf, switch off the lamp when you leave a room, and keep chairs and tables clean. Sharing care of items in the house is a way of saying thank you to the people who provide them.

Talk in a group: how can we take care of the items in our homes? Choose two ideas and say the names of the items in Arabic.

### Summary
• Copy new words carefully: listen, repeat, say it three times.
• Room words: bayt, ghurfa, matbakh, hammaam, hadiiqa.
• Item words: kursii, taawila, sariir, kitaab, qalam.
• Position words: fii (in), ʿalaa (on), taHta (under), fawqa (above), ʿinda (at).
• Ask ayna? to find out where something is.

### Common mistakes
• Mixing up fawqa (above) and taHta (under).
• Forgetting the al- for "the".
• Saying the heavy letters T, S and H like the soft ones. Listen closely to your teacher.

## QUIZ
1. What does the Arabic word بَيْتٌ mean?
   A) Door
   B) Kitchen
   C) House
   D) Chair
   Answer: C
   Why: بَيْتٌ (bayt) means house.
2. Which Arabic word means "on"?
   A) فِي
   B) تَحْتَ
   C) فَوْقَ
   D) عَلَى
   Answer: D
   Why: عَلَى (ʿalaa) means on. فِي is in, تَحْتَ is under and فَوْقَ is above.
3. What does ayna l-kitaab? mean?
   A) Where is the book?
   B) Who is the boy?
   C) What is your name?
   D) How are you?
   Answer: A
   Why: ayna means "where" and al-kitaab means "the book".
4. Which sentence means "The cat is under the bed"?
   A) الْقِطَّةُ فَوْقَ السَّرِيرِ
   B) الْقِطَّةُ تَحْتَ السَّرِيرِ
   C) الْقِطَّةُ فِي الْمَطْبَخِ
   D) الْقِطَّةُ عَلَى الْبَابِ
   Answer: B
   Why: تَحْتَ means under, السَّرِيرِ means the bed and الْقِطَّةُ means the cat.
5. In the room word ghurfatu n-nawm, what does the room stand for?
   A) A kitchen
   B) A bedroom
   C) A garden
   D) A bathroom
   Answer: B
   Why: nawm means sleep, so ghurfatu n-nawm is the bedroom.
6. Name three household items in Arabic (English letters are fine) and give the meanings. (3 marks)
   Answer: For example kursii (chair), taawila (table), sariir (bed).
   Why: 1 mark for each correct item with meaning.
7. Say where the lamp is when it is hanging over the table. Write the Arabic sentence in English letters and translate it. (3 marks)
   Answer: al-misbaahu fawqa t-taawila. The lamp is above the table.
   Why: 1 mark for fawqa, 1 mark for the complete sentence, 1 mark for the translation.
8. Explain how imitative speaking helps you learn Arabic words. (2 marks)
   Answer: You listen to a correct speaker and copy the sounds, speed and tone exactly, so you learn to pronounce the words in the right way.
   Why: 1 mark for listening and copying, 1 mark for correct pronunciation.
9. Give two ways of taking care of household items at home. (2 marks)
   Answer: For example wash cups and plates after use, and keep chairs and tables clean or put books back on the shelf.
   Why: 1 mark for each sensible way.
