## PAGE 1: What makes reading sound good?
Listen to a radio newsreader. The voice is clear, the speed is comfortable, and the voice goes up and down in a pleasant way. Now imagine someone reading each word alone with long gaps in between. It is difficult to listen to. The difference is fluency.

Two things help fluency very much. The first is pace, which is the speed of your reading. The second is verbal cues. Verbal cues are the ways your voice shows meaning: intonation (the rise and fall of your voice), speed and tonal variation (changes in loudness, softness and feeling).

In this lesson you will read short sentences about foods and drinks with a good pace and clear verbal cues. You will time each other and give kind feedback. Reading in a captivating way makes listeners want to hear more.

### What is a good pace?
• Not too fast: the listener must understand.
• Not too slow: the sentence must sound like a sentence.
• Steady: the speed should not jump up and down.

### Talk about it
Which foods are common in your area? Tell your partner. Then think how you would read a sentence like "I like mangoes!" How would your voice sound?

## PAGE 2: A reading text about food
Here is a short text. Read it in Arabic script from right to left.

### Text: I like food
أُحِبُّ الطَّعَامَ. آكُلُ الْخُبْزَ وَالْبَيْضَ. أَشْرَبُ الْحَلِيبَ. هَلْ تُحِبُّ الْمَوْزَ؟ نَعَمْ، أُحِبُّ الْمَوْزَ!

### In English letters
uHibbu T-Taʿaam. aakulu l-khubza wa l-bayDa. ashrabu l-Haliiba. hal tuHibbu l-mawz? naʿam, uHibbu l-mawz!

### In English
I like food. I eat bread and eggs. I drink milk. Do you like bananas? Yes, I like bananas!

### More sentences
• السَّمَكُ لَذِيذٌ = as-samaku ladhiidh = The fish is delicious.
• الْمَاءُ مُفِيدٌ = al-maa'u mufiid = Water is useful (good for you).
• الْعَصِيرُ بَارِدٌ = al-ʿasiiru baarid = The juice is cold.

The new words are ladhiidh (لَذِيذٌ, delicious) and mufiid (مُفِيدٌ, useful). Read each sentence three times. On the first reading, read slowly. On the second, read at a normal speed. On the third, add expression.

### Tip
Before you read aloud, read silently once. Find the words that are difficult and practise them.

The Arabic comma (،) looks like a reversed English comma. The Arabic question mark (؟) also looks like a reversed English one. The full stop (.) and exclamation mark (!) look the same.

### Practise
Read: هَلْ تُحِبُّ الْمَوْزَ؟ Let your voice rise at the end. Then read: نَعَمْ، أُحِبُّ الْمَوْزَ! Pause a little after the comma, and read the last part happily.

## PAGE 4: Timing and pace
Work in pairs for this timing activity. It builds pace and time management.

### How to do it
1. Choose a sentence, for example أَشْرَبُ الْحَلِيبَ (I drink milk).
2. One learner reads it aloud three times.
3. The partner counts the seconds with a clock or watch.
4. Write the time.
5. Swap roles.

### What to look for
Your aim is not to be the fastest. Your aim is to read at a steady speed with all the words correct. Compare your times. If you finish very quickly but make mistakes, slow down. If you take much longer than others, practise the words until they are smooth.

### The reading speed you want
Try to read the whole text "I like food" smoothly. It has five short sentences. A good reader can read it clearly in about twenty to thirty seconds at this stage. If you are slower, do not worry. Each time you practise, you get faster.

### Self-check
Ask yourself after each reading:
• Did I read every word?
• Did I stop too many times?
• Did my voice show that I understood?
• What can I do better next time?

## PAGE 5: Verbal cues and peer review
Verbal cues make your reading come alive. There are three main cues.

### Intonation
The rise and fall of the voice. Statements end with a fall. Questions end with a rise.

### Speed
Read exciting parts a little quicker and important parts a little slower. Slow down for a new or difficult word.

### Tonal variation
Change your voice to show feeling. Read a sentence about delicious fish with a happy voice: as-samaku ladhiidh! Read a sentence about cold juice with a shivering voice: al-ʿasiiru baarid!

### Peer review checklist
When your friend reads, use this checklist.
1. Every word was read correctly.
2. The pace was steady.
3. The voice went up for questions.
4. The voice paused at commas and stopped at full stops.
5. The voice showed feeling.

Tell your friend one good thing and one thing to improve. Speak kindly. Then read again and try the improvement.

### Free reading
When you have free time, read short Arabic texts on your own, for example a page of a picture book you made. Read aloud to yourself, and try to sound like a good storyteller.

## PAGE 6: Summary and foods in your locality
Food is important for life. When we read about food, we remember the many foods we have at home. Talk about the foods found in your county. Choose one food and write a short sentence in Arabic about it, using the pattern al-... ladhiidh (الـ ... لَذِيذٌ). For example: al-mawzu ladhiidh (الْمَوْزُ لَذِيذٌ), "The banana is delicious."

### Summary
• Fluency is smooth reading with a steady pace.
• Verbal cues are intonation, speed and tonal variation.
• The Arabic comma (،) and question mark (؟) are reversed.
• A question rises at the end. A statement falls.
• Time each other to improve pace, but accuracy matters most.
• Peer review must be kind.

### Common mistakes
• Reading everything in the same flat voice.
• Ignoring punctuation marks.
• Racing through the text and missing words.

## QUIZ
1. What is pace in reading?
   A) The speed of your reading
   B) The loudness of your voice
   C) The colour of the page
   D) The size of the letters
   Answer: A
   Why: Pace is how fast or slow we read.
2. What does the mark ؟ tell you to do?
   A) Stop and let the voice fall
   B) Pause briefly
   C) Let the voice rise as it is a question
   D) Read very fast
   Answer: C
   Why: ؟ is the Arabic question mark. A question ends with a rising voice.
3. What does as-samaku ladhiidh mean?
   A) The fish is delicious.
   B) The milk is cold.
   C) I like bread.
   D) Water is useful.
   Answer: A
   Why: as-samaku is the fish and ladhiidh means delicious.
4. Which of these is a verbal cue?
   A) The colour of the pen
   B) Intonation
   C) The length of the page
   D) The size of the letters
   Answer: B
   Why: Verbal cues include intonation, speed and tonal variation.
5. When you time a partner reading, what should be your main aim?
   A) To be the fastest at any cost
   B) To read at a steady pace with all words correct
   C) To read in a whisper
   D) To read without punctuation
   Answer: B
   Why: Timing helps with pace, but accuracy and smoothness matter most.
6. Give three points from the peer review checklist. (3 marks)
   Answer: For example every word correct; steady pace; voice rises for a question; pauses at commas.
   Why: 1 mark for each correct point.
7. Read and translate: هَلْ تُحِبُّ الْمَوْزَ؟ (2 marks)
   Answer: hal tuHibbu l-mawz? Do you like bananas?
   Why: 1 mark for the reading and 1 mark for the translation.
8. Explain how punctuation marks help a reader to read aloud well. (3 marks)
   Answer: They tell the reader when to pause, when to stop and when the voice should rise or show feeling, so the reading sounds natural and meaning is clear.
   Why: 1 mark for pausing or stopping, 1 mark for voice changes, 1 mark for clear meaning.
9. Write one sentence in Arabic sounds about a food in your area using ladhiidh, and translate it. (2 marks)
   Answer: For example al-mawzu ladhiidh. The banana is delicious.
   Why: 1 mark for a correct sentence and 1 mark for the translation.
