# Grade 4 Arabic Language: notes for human checking

## Coverage
- 27 lessons, one per sub-strand (1.1-1.9 Listening and Speaking, 2.1-2.9 Reading, 3.1-3.9 Writing). All manifest topics have a lesson, so no retire.json is needed.
- The design has no numbered Grammar strand. Grammar is taught inside the themes (gender endings -ka/-ki, "my" -ii, dual -aani, definite article al-, sun letters, describing-word agreement, question words).

## Conventions
- Arabic is shown in Arabic script with harakat, then a simple English-letter transliteration (aa/ii/uu for long vowels, ʿ for ayn, ' for hamza) and the English meaning. Capital T, S, H, D in some transliterations mark the heavy letters.
- Case endings (-u/-a/-i) are given in fully vowelled examples. The lessons tell learners to copy the teacher for these. A teacher may prefer to teach pausal forms without the final vowels.
- SVG text uses direction="rtl" and text-anchor. Joined forms in figures use the tatweel (for example بـ ـبـ ـب). No Arabic font was available in the authoring environment, so figures were NOT rendered; please view Figures in a browser once (2.1 alphabet grid, 2.2 blending, 3.1 letter forms and dots, 1.7 body, 1.4 days).

## Items that need a human Arabic teacher to confirm
1. Poems (2.8 "The Sky", and the body stanza in 1.7 / 2.7 / 3.7) were composed simply by the author for learners (not quoted from any source). Lines: الشَّمْسُ فِي السَّمَاءِ / وَالْغَيْمُ فِي السَّمَاءِ / وَالْمَطَرُ مِنَ السَّمَاءِ / مَا أَجْمَلَ الْبِيئَةَ and عَيْنِي تَرَى / أُذُنِي تَسْمَعُ / أَنْفِي يَشُمُّ / فَمِي يَتَكَلَّمُ. Please confirm they are acceptable.
2. 1.4: "ayyu yawmin al-yawm?" (أَيُّ يَوْمٍ الْيَوْمَ؟) for "What day is it today?". Common, but a teacher may prefer another phrasing.
3. 1.1: age sentences use ʿumrii tisʿu sanawaat (nine years) and ʿashru sanawaat (ten years). The name spelling كينيا (Kenya) is written without harakat. Names Amina/Ahmad are shown as أَمِينَةُ / أَحْمَدُ.
4. 1.5: "law samahta" and "tafaddal" as polite phrases; "kul kaʿka" (كُلْ كَعْكَةً) "Eat cake". "ʿafwan" is taught both as "you are welcome" and "excuse me" (both are standard).
5. 1.7: Simon Says uses the word qaala (he said) as the start word. This is only a class convention.
6. 2.4: day names are given with the standard forms الِاثْنَيْنِ and الْأَرْبِعَاءُ. The claim that the first five day names come from the numbers one to five, and that al-jumuʿa relates to "gathering", is standard etymology. No claim is made about the origin of as-sabt.
7. 2.7: dual endings (yadaani, rijlaani, ʿaynaani, udhunaani) are taught in the nominative form only.
8. 3.5: writing styles Naskh, Ruqʿah, Thuluth, Kufic and Diwani are described in one line each. No attempt is made to draw them.
9. Simplifications for children: "haadhaa/haadhihi" for this, "kabiir/kabiira" agreement, and "aakulu" (آكُلُ) spelling with madda are used without discussing hamza rules.

## Bank
- 670 MCQ and 254 short items. About 60 percent of the MCQs are generated from the lesson vocabulary and sentences (Arabic to English, English to Arabic, reading in English letters); the rest are written by hand. Vocabulary distractors avoid near-synonyms (for example hello/welcome, leg/foot, food/bread).
- Bank questions describe any text or picture in words. Some MCQs quote transliterated Arabic, and learners need the lessons' transliteration key.
